Summary

The **Holden Commodore VE** is an iconic sedan of the Australian market. Based on the Zeta platform (developed at a cost of one billion Australian dollars), it is the first Commodore designed entirely in Australia without using an Opel chassis. Offered as a sedan, station wagon (Sportwagon), and utility vehicle (Ute), it stands out for its RWD architecture (rear-wheel drive), excellent driving dynamics, and generous interior space. It received a major update in 2010 (Series II) introducing the Holden iQ multimedia system, E85 compatibility, and the widespread use of direct injection engines (SIDI). Although the Holden brand disappeared in 2020, spare parts support remains ensured by the GM network and numerous independent specialists for at least a decade.

Strengths 6

  • Excellent road handling (weight distribution close to 50/50)
  • Giant interior space and trunk volume (496L in sedan)
  • Exceptional reliability of the V8 blocks (LS/L family)
  • Availability and price of spare parts (GM network and independents)
  • Very robust 6-speed automatic gearbox (6L80E)
  • Unbeatable price/performance ratio on the used market

Weaknesses 6

  • Endemic timing chain stretch issue on the V6s (Alloytec and SIDI)
  • Average interior plastic quality and assembly (frequent rattles)
  • Very common failure of the climate control flap actuators (HVAC blend doors) requiring dashboard removal
  • Headliner sagging with heat and time
  • Rapid wear of the front suspension bushings (radius rod bushes)
  • Obsolete and fragile 4-speed automatic gearbox (4L60E) on early V6s

Check before buying

  • Listen carefully to the V6 engine when cold: any rattling or Check Engine light can indicate a stretched timing chain (expensive repair).
  • Test all climate control zones: verify that hot and cold air blow correctly from both sides (HVAC actuator failure).
  • Check the oil change history: a V6 without regular oil changes (max 10-15,000 km) should be avoided.
  • Inspect the front axle bushings (clunking noises over speed bumps) and asymmetrical tire wear.
  • Check the operation of the center screen (especially the Series II iQ system which can be buggy).

  • Generous Space and Comfort — With its giant cabin space and impressive boot volume, the Commodore VE is ideal for families and long journeys, ensuring optimal comfort.

  • Proven V8 Mechanical Robustness and Transmission — V8 engines are renowned for their exceptional reliability, complemented by a highly robust 6-speed automatic transmission (6L80E).

Alternatives

- **Australia / NZ**: Ford Falcon (FG/FG X Generation) - Its main rival, renowned for its indestructible "Barra" inline-6 engine. - **North America**: Dodge Charger, Chrysler 300C (spacious V6/V8 RWDs). - **Europe**: BMW 5 Series (E60/F10), Mercedes E-Class (W211/W212) - More luxurious but much more expensive to maintain. - **Japan**: Toyota Aurion (FWD, very reliable V6 but less dynamic).

Verdict

The Holden Commodore VE is an excellent cruiser, spacious and dynamic. According to RACQ/NRMA surveys, its overall reliability is rated 'Good', with a special mention for the robustness of the 6L80 gearbox and the V8 engines (LS2/LS3/L98). However, buying a V6 version (Alloytec or SIDI) requires extreme caution: the timing chain stretch issue is real and expensive. Demand an impeccable service book with very regular oil changes. The V8 versions, although thirstier, offer superior mechanical reliability and incomparable driving pleasure. Watch out for finishing details (headliner, climate control flaps) that age poorly.
