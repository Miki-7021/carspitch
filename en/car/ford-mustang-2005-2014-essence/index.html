Ford Mustang

S197 · 2005 – 2014

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The fifth-generation Ford Mustang (codename S197) marks the triumphant return of the "neo-retro" design, heavily inspired by the fastback models of the late 60s. Produced from 2005 to 2014, it is divided into two major phases: Phase 1 (2005-2009) with its historic engines (4.0L V6 and 4.6L V8), and Phase 2 (2010-2014) which introduces a deep facelift and, especially from 2011, modern and much more powerful engines (3.7L Cyclone V6 and 5.0L Coyote V8). Although its solid rear axle betrays an older design, the S197 is renowned for its overall robustness, ease of maintenance, and huge aftermarket parts market. According to TÜV Report and J.D. Power data, it sits in the average of its segment, with excellent mechanical reliability on recent blocks, although the comfort electronics (SYNC system) are often singled out.

Strengths 5

  • Very satisfactory overall mechanical reliability, particularly on the 5.0L Coyote and Boss 302 V8s (very few NHTSA complaints).
  • Tremec TR-6060 manual gearbox (on Shelby) reputed to be indestructible.
  • Reasonable maintenance cost and massive availability of spare parts (OEM and Aftermarket).
  • Timeless design and huge sympathy capital.
  • Major evolution of the chassis and interior finish from the 2010/2011 facelift.

Weaknesses 5

  • Chain tensioner problems on the 4.6L V8s (2005-2007) and brittle spark plugs (up to 2008).
  • MT82 (Getrag) manual gearbox on 2011+ models catchy when cold and sometimes fragile under intensive use.
  • Temperamental comfort electronics (first-generation SYNC multimedia system).
  • Solid rear axle limiting comfort on degraded roads and efficiency on the track (corrected on the following S550 generation).
  • Recurring paint defect (blisters) on the leading edge of the aluminum hood.

Check before buying

  • Imperatively verify that the Takata airbag recalls have been performed (deadly risk).
  • On the 4.6L V8s (2005-2007): listen carefully to the engine when cold to detect a timing chain rattle.
  • On 2011-2013 models with automatic gearbox: verify if the TCM module reprogramming has been done (NHTSA recall).
  • Inspect the front lip of the hood to detect paint blisters (aluminum oxidation).
  • Test cold gear shifting on models equipped with the MT82 manual gearbox (2011-2014).

Performance

  • Exhilarating Performance — With powerful V8 engines, notably the 412-420 hp 5.0L Coyote and the 444 hp 5.0L Boss 302, the Mustang S197 offers a sporty and dynamic driving experience, true to the spirit of "Pony Cars".

Design

  • Timeless Neo-Retro Design — Its iconic style, inspired by the legendary fastbacks of the 60s, gives the Mustang S197 immense appeal and an aesthetic that transcends eras without aging.

economy

  • Accessible Maintenance and Abundant Parts — Benefit from reasonable maintenance costs and a massive availability of spare parts, whether OEM or aftermarket, ensuring ease of maintenance and increased longevity.

driving_pleasure

  • Authentic Driving Experience — The Mustang S197 offers total immersion in the world of American muscle cars, with an enchanting engine sound and an assertive character that promises thrills on every journey.

modernity

  • Increased Modernity and Refinement (from 2011) — The restyled models from 2010/2011 benefit from a major evolution of the chassis and an improved interior finish, offering a more contemporary comfort and experience.

Alternatives

- **North America / Import**: Chevrolet Camaro (5th generation, very robust LS3 V8, superior independent chassis), Dodge Challenger (heavier, GT/Cruising oriented, reliable but thirsty Hemi V8s). - **Europe / Asia**: Nissan 350Z / 370Z (reliable naturally aspirated V6, sportier handling), BMW 3 Series Coupe E92 (335i or M3, superior finish but more temperamental maintenance costs and engine reliability).

Verdict

The Ford Mustang S197 is an excellent choice to enter the world of *Pony Cars*. If your budget allows, favor the Phase 2 models (2011-2014) equipped with the 5.0L Coyote V8 or the 3.7L V6: their engines are significantly more modern, powerful, and display remarkable reliability (very few complaints recorded by the NHTSA). The 2005-2010 models (4.6L V8) remain good passion buys, provided you check the history of the timing system (tensioners) and spark plugs. The absolute black spot of this generation remains the Takata airbag scandal: **never** buy an example without formal proof that this recall has been performed. Finally, expect sometimes dated and temperamental onboard electronics (SYNC).
