Summary

The second-generation MINI Countryman (F60) marks a major break from its predecessor (R60). Based on BMW Group's modular UKL2 platform (shared with the BMW X1 F48 and X2 F39), it grew considerably to become a true compact family SUV. Gone are the capricious 'Prince' engines developed with PSA: the F60 exclusively adopts BMW's modular B-series engines (B38, B48 for gasoline; B37, B47 for diesel), renowned for their excellent reliability. A facelift (LCI) occurred in mid-2020, bringing aesthetic touches, digital instrumentation, and an update of the engines to meet Euro 6d standards (integration of OPF particulate filters on gasoline engines). Overall, the F60 is a very accomplished vehicle, offering dynamic handling typical of the brand, although its increased weight is noticeable. It is one of the most reliable models in its category if maintenance is rigorously followed.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent overall reliability of BMW gasoline engines (B38 and B48)
  • Significantly improved finish and assembly quality compared to the R60 generation
  • Generous interior space and modularity (sliding rear bench on internal combustion versions)
  • Dynamic road handling and precise steering for an SUV
  • Smooth and responsive automatic gearboxes (Aisin torque converter or Getrag DKG)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very firm suspension, especially with 18 or 19-inch wheels and on JCW versions
  • Chronic fragility of engine mounts (bushings) across the range
  • Reduced boot volume on the plug-in hybrid version (Cooper SE)
  • High weight penalizing agility compared to other MINI models
  • Used purchase price and cost of options/spare parts (BMW pricing)

Check before buying

  • Vibrations at idle or clunking when shifting gears: sign of a faulty upper right engine mount (very common).
  • Coolant level on gasoline engines (B48): check for leaks at the water pump or thermostat housing.
  • Recall history on diesel engines (EGR cooler must have been replaced).
  • Battery State of Health (SOH) on the Cooper SE plug-in hybrid version.
  • Maintenance of the ALL4 all-wheel drive (transfer case and rear axle oil change every 80,000 km).

Verdict

The MINI Countryman II (F60) is an excellent used car choice, marking an era of maturity and reliability for the brand thanks to the adoption of BMW mechanical components. The gasoline engines (Cooper 1.5 and Cooper S 2.0) are particularly recommended: they are robust, powerful, and free from the flaws of the previous generation. The main mechanical issue is often limited to the upper engine mount, a known and relatively simple repair. The plug-in hybrid version (Cooper SE) is relevant for daily urban use, but prioritize post-2019 models (10 kWh battery). Diesels are best reserved for very high mileage drivers, provided the EGR recall has been performed. In summary: aim for a Cooper or Cooper S gasoline model with a clear service history.
