Summary

The Renault Modus Phase 2 (internal code JP0) marks a decisive turning point in the career of the small diamond-badged minivan. Launched in 2008, this facelift corrects the main flaw of Phase 1: a tiny boot. Renault thus introduced the Grand Modus, extended by 16 cm (4.03 m vs 3.87 m), finally offering cabin space and loading volume worthy of the name. Aesthetically, the front end is modernized (redesigned headlights, refined grille) and perceived quality is up. In terms of reliability, this Phase 2 is an excellent vintage: it benefits from the eradication of serious early-life issues that marred the 2004-2007 models (especially on 1.5 dCi diesels and electronics). It is a pragmatic, comfortable, and economical vehicle, particularly relevant in its Grand Modus version.

Strengths 6

  • Cabin space and boot volume (specific to Grand Modus)
  • Sliding and folding rear bench seat (exemplary modularity)
  • Excellent overall reliability on this Phase 2 (Phase 1's errors served as a lesson)
  • Extremely low fuel consumption of 1.5 dCi engines (often under 5L/100km)
  • Suspension comfort and decent sound insulation for the category
  • Very affordable insurance and maintenance costs

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic mechanical accessibility (changing a headlight bulb often requires removing the front bumper, although slightly improved on Phase 2)
  • Very upright driving position ('van' type), steering wheel adjustable only for height
  • DP0 automatic gearbox (on 1.6 16v) slow, thirsty, and unreliable
  • Hard interior plastics that scratch easily and age moderately
  • Boot of the 'short' Modus still too small (prefer the Grand Modus)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (strict deadline: 5 years or 120,000 km, breakage is fatal to the engine).
  • Ventilation/air conditioning operation (the air blower resistor frequently burns out, tedious repair).
  • On diesels (dCi): absence of 'Check Injection' or 'DPF' warning light, signs of a clogged EGR valve or a blocked DPF.
  • Operation of all 4 electric windows (plastic clips often break).
  • Condition of the stalks (headlights/indicators) and the steering wheel's rotary coupler (Airbag light on).

Official recalls

  • January 2008: Recall concerning a risk of power steering loss or steering column blockage (reprogramming or replacement).,- September 2009: Inspection and possible replacement of front seat belts (possible improper locking).,- February 2011: On the 1.2 TCe engine, risk of premature wear of valve half-cones which could lead to engine failure (concerns a small series produced in 2010).,- May 2012: Replacement of tailgate struts which may show signs of weakness and no longer hold the boot open.

What sets it apart 6

reliability

  • Enhanced and Proven Reliability — This Phase 2 of the Modus benefits from excellent overall reliability, with major improvements having eradicated the early issues of the 1.5 dCi diesel engines, ensuring lasting peace of mind.

economy

  • Exceptionally Low Fuel Consumption — The 1.5 dCi diesel engines are renowned for their exemplary fuel efficiency, often achieving consumption figures below 5L/100km, making it a very economical choice for daily use.
  • Very Affordable Running Costs — Beyond its low fuel consumption, the Modus Phase 2 stands out for its very affordable insurance and maintenance costs, contributing to a well-managed car budget.

practicality

  • Ingenious Modularity and Optimized Space — The Grand Modus offers generous interior space and a significant boot volume, complemented by a sliding and folding rear bench seat, ensuring remarkable adaptability to all your needs.

comfort

  • Pleasant Driving Comfort — Enjoy a well-balanced suspension comfort and correct sound insulation for its category, making daily commutes and getaways more serene and relaxing.

versatility

  • 1.5 dCi Diesel Engine: Robust and Versatile — The 1.5 dCi engine, particularly in its 85 or 90 hp versions, is a truly tireless workhorse, offering an excellent compromise between power and consumption, ideal for all types of journeys.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Opel Meriva A/B (suicide doors on B, very modular), Nissan Note I (technical cousin of the Modus, shares the same dCi engines), Citroën C3 Picasso (more cubic design, very spacious), Honda Jazz II (unbeatable petrol reliability, 'Magic Seats' modularity).
  • Asia / Japan: Honda Fit/Jazz, Toyota Ractis, Nissan Note.
  • North America: Model not sold. Segment alternatives: Honda Fit, Nissan Versa Note.

Verdict

The Renault Modus Phase 2 (2008-2012), particularly in its Grand Modus variant, is a highly recommendable used car purchase. Renault has corrected almost all the reliability flaws that ruined the reputation of Phase 1. The 1.5 dCi (85 or 90 hp) engine is at its peak here: freed from its injection and connecting rod problems, it proves to be a tireless workhorse, provided it has been rigorously maintained (regular oil changes, up-to-date belt) and not used exclusively in the city. If your use is purely urban, you should definitely opt for the excellent 1.2 TCe 100 hp or the modest 1.2 16v 75 hp. Simply avoid the automatic gearbox versions (4-speed automatic).
