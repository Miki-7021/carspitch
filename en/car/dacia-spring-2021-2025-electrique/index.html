Summary

The first-generation Dacia Spring (internal code: HFF) was launched in 2021, marking Dacia's entry into the electric vehicle market with a proposition focused on accessibility. Based on the Renault-Nissan group's CMF-A platform, it is closely derived from the Renault City K-ZE sold in China and the thermal Kwid sold in emerging markets. Positioned as Europe's cheapest electric car at its launch, the Spring primarily targets urban and peri-urban use. It benefited from a slight facelift in 2024 (new logo, some interior and exterior tweaks) but retains its powertrains and platform. Its technical simplicity is both an asset for reliability and a limitation in terms of performance, comfort, and passive safety (1 Euro NCAP star at its launch). Overall, it is an honest vehicle for its price, but with clear compromises and important points of vigilance.

Strengths 5

  • Very competitive purchase price (new and used)
  • Very low running and maintenance costs
  • Agility and compactness ideal for city driving
  • Mechanical simplicity of the electric powertrain
  • Good interior space for the segment and decent boot volume

Weaknesses 8

  • Limited range (especially in winter or on highways)
  • Modest performance (acceleration and pick-up, even in 65 hp version)
  • Very basic interior material quality and rudimentary finish
  • Passive safety (1-star Euro NCAP at launch)
  • Slow charging speed (AC and DC)
  • Recurring engine wiring harness issues (numerous recalls)
  • Reliability of the 12V battery and charging system
  • Limited suspension comfort and sound insulation

Check before buying

  • Complete history of manufacturer recalls (especially for the engine wiring harness and 12V battery) with proof of completion.
  • Condition of the 12V battery (voltage, age, signs of weakness).
  • Functionality of the charging system (AC and DC if available) on different charging stations.
  • Condition of the suspension and running gear (noises, play, premature wear).
  • Functionality of the onboard electronics (screen, connectivity, bugs).
  • Wear of tires and braking system (discs, pads).
  • Absence of abnormal noises from the electric motor or transmission.

It is crucial to verify that all applicable recalls have been carried out on the vehicle you are considering purchasing. Verdict

The first-generation Dacia Spring is an electric vehicle that delivers on its promise of accessibility. It is a relevant choice for those looking for a new or used electric car at an unbeatable price, primarily for urban and peri-urban use. Its mechanical simplicity is an asset for the reliability of the electric powertrain itself.

However, the verdict must be strongly qualified due to the numerous manufacturer recalls, particularly those concerning the engine wiring harness. This recurring problem is a major concern and must be imperatively checked. The reliability of the 12V battery is also a weak point that can immobilize the vehicle.

Buying advice: * If you buy a Spring, absolutely ensure that all manufacturer recalls have been carried out. Ask the seller for proof or check with a Dacia dealership using the vehicle's serial number. This is the most critical point. * Prioritize more recent models (after 2023) that may have benefited from factory fixes or had their recalls performed. * For purely urban use, the Electric 45 version is sufficient. For a bit more versatility and occasional forays onto expressways, the Electric 65 is preferable. * Do not expect great comfort, sporty performance, or a premium finish. The Spring is primarily an electric utility vehicle, designed to be economical.

Verdict: NEGOTIATE due to documented reliability issues from recalls and significant compromises. Purchase is possible if and only if the recall history is clear, all corrective actions have been taken, and the price is very attractive for a well-defined urban and peri-urban use.
