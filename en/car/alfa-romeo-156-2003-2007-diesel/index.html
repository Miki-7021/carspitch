Summary

The Alfa Romeo 156 Phase 2 (or facelift), redesigned by Giorgetto Giugiaro in 2003, brilliantly modernizes Walter de Silva's original lines. This generation (Type 932) stands out with a more aggressive front end (widened grille, new headlights) and a revamped interior with higher quality materials. Mechanically, this period marks the peak of the Fiat group's diesel engines with the introduction of the 16- and 20-valve Multijet (JTDm) blocks, offering first-rate drivability and performance. The range was also expanded with the Crosswagon Q4 versions (raised station wagon with AWD), offered exclusively in diesel. While the road handling remains an absolute benchmark (ultra-direct steering, incisive front axle), the 156 suffers from chronic fragility of its running gear and uneven build quality. It is an engineer's and enthusiast's car, which requires rigorous maintenance.

Strengths 5

  • Timeless design (especially in Phase 2 Giugiaro) and very successful station wagon (Sportwagon) lines
  • Exceptional chassis: double wishbone front axle offering ultra-precise steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock)
  • Overall reliability of the JTD and JTDm diesel engine blocks (indestructible cast iron blocks)
  • Drivability and sound of the 5-cylinder diesel (2.4 JTD/JTDm)
  • Crosswagon Q4 version very effective on snow (Torsen C transmission)

Weaknesses 5

  • Extremely fragile running gear: upper and lower wishbones, anti-roll bar bushings (squeaking/clunking noises every 60,000 km)
  • Catastrophic turning radius (worse than a large SUV), making city maneuvers tedious
  • Quality of interior plastics and assembly could be improved (frequent rattles)
  • Floor corrosion (often invisible under the underseal) on models driven in salted regions
  • Capricious electronics (airbag warning lights, TDC sensors, mass air flow sensors)

Check before buying

  • Front and rear suspension: Listen carefully for noises on rough roads ('old spring bed' type squeaks = upper wishbones; dull clunks = lower wishbones or anti-roll bar).
  • Engine temperature (Diesels): The needle should stabilize at 90°C after 10-15 minutes. If it stays at 70°C, the thermostat is dead.
  • Underbody: Imperative to inspect the front floors (under the driver's and passenger's feet) to hunt for perforating rust.
  • Timing belt history: On Twin Spark petrols, demand an invoice less than 3 years / 60,000 km old. On diesels, 5 years / 100,000 km (with water pump).
  • Clutch / Flywheel (JTDm): Test acceleration in 4th/5th gear at low RPM. If it slips or if strong vibrations are felt at idle, the dual-mass flywheel is tired.

Official recalls

  • Engine hood (2004): Risk of unexpected hood opening while driving due to a defect in the safety catch. Massive recall for lock replacement.
  • Brake hoses (2005): Risk of rubbing and rupture of a brake hose on certain diesel versions.
  • Suspension (2004): Inspection and possible replacement of lower suspension arms on a specific series.

What sets it apart 6

Design

  • Timeless and Elegant Design — Redesigned by Giorgetto Giugiaro, the Alfa Romeo 156 Phase 2 boasts an avant-garde line and a particularly successful Sportwagon silhouette, ensuring a distinctive and timeless aesthetic.

Reliability

  • Exceptional Diesel Engine Reliability — JTD and JTDm diesel engines are renowned for their legendary robustness. These cast-iron blocks are designed for remarkable longevity, often exceeding 300,000 km with proper maintenance.

Handling

  • Precise Driving and Sporty Dynamics — Its exceptional chassis, featuring a double wishbone front suspension, offers ultra-precise steering and incisive road handling, providing dynamic and sporty driving pleasure.

Performance

  • Captivating Performance and Sound — JTDm diesel engines, especially the 5-cylinder, deliver first-class performance, generous torque, and a distinctive sound, guaranteeing a sensation-rich driving experience.

Practicality

  • Versatility and Enhanced Traction — The Crosswagon Q4 version, with its Torsen C all-wheel drive, offers remarkable versatility and efficiency, ensuring excellent traction even on challenging surfaces like snow.

Economy

  • Optimized Energy Efficiency and Driving Pleasure — JTD and JTDm diesel engines are recognized for their fuel efficiency and excellent performance, offering a perfect balance between power, driving pleasure, and optimized fuel consumption.

Alternatives

  • Europe: BMW 3 Series (E46) (benchmark in RWD, high-performance diesels but intake swirl flap issues), Audi A4 (B6) (superior finish, robust but noisy TDIs), Peugeot 406 / 407 (excellent comfort/road handling compromise, reliable HDIs).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Accord (7th Generation) (exceptional reliability, very smooth 2.2 i-CTDi diesel engine), Mazda 6 (dynamic but major rust and DPF issues on diesels).

Verdict

The Alfa Romeo 156 Phase 2 is a sedan with character that should be bought with full knowledge of the facts. If you target a diesel engine (JTD or JTDm), you are making the choice of mechanical reason: these engines are among the most reliable of their generation, capable of exceeding 300,000 km without opening the block. The 1.9 JTDm 150 hp represents the best compromise. However, engine reliability must not overshadow the chronic fragility of the running gear (consider the suspension wishbones as consumables) and the risks of floor corrosion. Flee from neglected or 'tuned' examples. A model with a complete maintenance history, recent wishbones, and a functional thermostat will offer you unrivaled driving pleasure in this price range.
