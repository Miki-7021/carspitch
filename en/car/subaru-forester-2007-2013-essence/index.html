Subaru Forester

III (SH) (2007–2013) · 2007 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The third-generation Subaru Forester, known by the chassis code SH, marks a significant evolution from its predecessors. Launched in 2007, it abandoned the raised wagon look to adopt a silhouette closer to a traditional compact SUV, thus meeting global market expectations. Larger, more spacious, and offering generous ground clearance, it nevertheless retained the brand's fundamentals: symmetrical all-wheel drive (Symmetrical AWD) and Boxer (flat) engines, ensuring a low center of gravity and excellent road holding.

This generation was praised for its off-road capabilities, general reliability (for gasoline engines), and interior space. However, the introduction of Subaru's first Boxer Diesel engine brought its share of reliability challenges, particularly in the early years of production. The Forester SH benefited from a slight facelift in 2011, mainly aesthetic (grille, headlights, wheels) and with some minor interior and mechanical improvements.

Strengths 6

  • Highly effective and safe Symmetrical AWD all-wheel drive
  • Boxer engines offering a low center of gravity and good weight distribution
  • Excellent off-road capabilities for its category (ground clearance, approach/departure angles)
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume
  • Proven reliability of naturally aspirated gasoline engines (with maintenance)
  • Suspension comfort on degraded roads

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption for gasoline engines (especially turbo)
  • Problematic reliability of early versions of the Boxer Diesel engine (EE20)
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speeds
  • Interior plastic quality perceived as inferior to some competitors
  • Cost of parts and maintenance at Subaru (Boxer/AWD specificity)

Check before buying

  • **Gasoline Engines (EJ):** Presence of seeping or leaks at the cylinder head gaskets. Oil level and consumption. Timing belt replacement history.
  • **Diesel Engines (EE20):** Engine production date (avoid before mid-2010). Engine warning lights (DPF, EGR). Abnormal noises (crankshaft). Condition of the clutch and dual-mass flywheel.
  • **Turbo Engines (XT):** Turbo condition (shaft play, abnormal noises). History of very regular oil changes.
  • **General:** All-wheel drive operation (no noises or jerks). Condition of shock absorbers and bushings. Chassis and underbody corrosion (depending on region).
  • **Electronics:** Check the proper functioning of all equipment (air conditioning, windows, radio, etc.).

Verdict

The Subaru Forester III (SH) is an endearing vehicle, renowned for its adventurous qualities and unparalleled all-wheel drive in its category. For a worry-free purchase, **very clearly favor naturally aspirated gasoline engines (2.0i or 2.5i)**. They are generally robust, provided they receive regular maintenance, especially concerning the timing belt and monitoring of cylinder head gaskets at high mileage. The 2.5i offers superior driving pleasure for a moderate increase in consumption.

Turbo versions (XT) are powerful but require meticulous maintenance and respectful driving to avoid costly problems. They are reserved for connoisseurs or those with larger maintenance budgets.

**Regarding the Boxer Diesel engine (EE20), caution is advised.** Models produced before mid-2010 are to be **AVOIDED absolutely** due to crankshaft problems. If you opt for a post-2010 diesel, ensure an impeccable service history and predominantly road use to preserve the DPF. The risk of costly breakdowns remains higher than with gasoline versions.

In summary, the gasoline Forester SH is an excellent choice for anyone looking for a reliable, spacious, and capable SUV for all-road use. The diesel is a lottery, even if later model years are less risky. Negotiate the price based on history and engine.
