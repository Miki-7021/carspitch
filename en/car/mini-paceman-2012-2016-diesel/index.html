MINI Paceman

I (R61) · 2012 – 2016

NEGOTIATE Reliability 66/100 — moyen

Summary

The MINI Paceman (R61) is the 3-door 'Coupé' counterpart to the Countryman (R60). Launched in late 2012, it positions itself as a niche urban SAC (Sports Activity Coupé). It sacrifices the practicality of the Countryman (only 4 individual seats, sloping roofline, reduced trunk space) in favor of a more dynamic design and slightly sharper road handling thanks to a standard lowered suspension. Although it shares all of its underpinnings and powertrains with the Countryman, its distribution was much more limited, which makes it an atypical model on the used car market today. Overall reliability is average, highly dependent on rigorous maintenance, particularly on diesel (N47 family) and turbocharged gasoline (N18 family) engines.

Strengths 5

  • Atypical and exclusive exterior design (very rare on the roads)
  • Dynamic road handling (karting effect preserved despite the weight)
  • Original interior presentation with the central rail (Center Rail)
  • Efficiency of the ALL4 all-wheel drive (optional)
  • Driving pleasure of the Cooper SD engine (torquey and economical)

Weaknesses 6

  • Variable engine reliability (chain on N47 diesel, HP pump/oil consumption on N18 gasoline)
  • Limited rear passenger space (strict 2 seats) and difficult access
  • Reduced trunk volume (330 liters) compared to the Countryman
  • Very firm suspension comfort, especially with > 17-inch wheels and Runflat tires
  • Poor rear visibility
  • Quality of some interior plastics and parasitic noises (squeaks/rattles)

Check before buying

  • Timing chain noise: metallic rubbing or cold rattling (N47 Diesels and N18 Gasoline).
  • Maintenance history: avoid vehicles that followed the LongLife interval (30,000 km). Demand oil changes every 15,000 km.
  • Electronics operation: test all windows, lighting, and controls. The FRM (Footwell Module) is very sensitive to battery voltage drops and can burn out.
  • Particulate filter (DPF) on diesels: check for absence of warning light and ask if the vehicle has been driven a lot in the city (risk of clogging).
  • Clutch and flywheel: on diesels with manual gearbox, look for vibrations in the clutch pedal at the biting point.
  • Oil leaks: inspect the valve cover gasket, oil pan, and oil filter housing.

  • Dynamic "Go-Kart Feeling" Road Handling — Enjoy a sporty and agile driving experience, true to the MINI spirit, with precise road holding and responsive steering for maximum pleasure on every journey.

Safety

  • Enhanced Safety — Travel with peace of mind thanks to a high level of passive safety, attested by 5 stars in the Euro NCAP crash test, with 84% for adult protection and 71% for child protection.
  • Effective and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from a powerful and responsive braking system, offering short stopping distances and excellent pedal feel, for perfect control in all circumstances.

Practicality

  • All-Road Capabilities with ALL4 (optional) — For increased versatility and optimal grip on all surfaces, the ALL4 all-wheel drive (available as an option) ensures efficient and secure traction, even in challenging conditions.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Range Rover Evoque Coupé (more luxurious, more expensive, superior off-road capabilities), Nissan Juke (more affordable, 5-door but polarizing design), first-generation DS 4 (atypical premium positioning). - **North America**: Range Rover Evoque Coupe, Hyundai Veloster (for its atypical coupe aspect, although lower). - **Asia**: Nissan Juke, Toyota C-HR (appeared later, but targeting the same urban coupe SUV audience).

Verdict

The MINI Paceman is a purely passionate purchase. It is aimed at those who find the Countryman too family-oriented and the Hatch (3-door) too small. **Regarding diesel engines (Cooper D and SD)**, caution is advised. The BMW N47 engine offers excellent driving pleasure, particularly in the 143 hp SD version, but its Achilles' heel remains the timing chain. A purchase is only recommendable if the vehicle has an impeccable maintenance history (annual oil changes) and, ideally, if the timing chain kit has already been replaced with invoices to prove it. Avoid examples that have been driven exclusively in the city (clogged DPF/EGR). If you drive little, prefer a more reliable Cooper gasoline version (122 hp), or a Cooper S, assuming a premium maintenance budget.
