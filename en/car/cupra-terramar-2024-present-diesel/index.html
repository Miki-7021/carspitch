Summary

The Cupra Terramar is a sporty compact SUV (C-segment) based on the Volkswagen Group's MQB Evo platform. Indirectly replacing the Seat Tarraco and positioned above the Formentor, it is produced at the Audi plant in Győr, Hungary, alongside the Audi Q3. **EXPERT WARNING: Contrary to the request targeting diesel, the Cupra Terramar is NOT offered with a diesel engine (TDI).** The brand made the strategic choice to abandon diesel on this new model to focus exclusively on mild-hybrid petrol (eTSI), pure petrol (TSI), and the new-generation plug-in hybrid (eHybrid). The Terramar stands out with its sharp chassis (dual-valve DCC Pro suspension), its corrected MIB4 multimedia interface (backlit sliders), and its aggressive design. Being a very recent model, long-term reliability data is projected from the latest generation of VAG mechanical components.

Strengths 5

  • Dynamic road handling (highly effective DCC Pro adaptive suspension)
  • Real electric range of eHybrid versions (often > 80 km)
  • MIB4 multimedia interface finally ergonomic (12.9-inch screen, backlit temperature sliders)
  • Rear roominess and trunk volume (up to 602 liters on ICE versions)
  • Build quality significantly improved compared to previous Cupra productions

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of a diesel engine (penalizing for very high mileage drivers)
  • High prices, dangerously approaching premium brands (Audi, BMW)
  • Significant weight of eHybrid versions (impact on agility)
  • Ergonomics still very touch-sensitive on the steering wheel (haptic buttons sometimes imprecise)
  • Reduced trunk volume on eHybrid versions (battery)

Check before buying

  • Stability of the infotainment system (unexpected restarts, slowness at startup on early models)
  • Body panel alignment (factory adjustments sometimes uneven at the start of production)
  • Smooth operation of the DSG gearbox during ICE/electric transitions (on eHybrid)
  • Presence of wind noise around the mirrors at high speed

Verdict

The Cupra Terramar is a highly accomplished SUV that corrects the early mistakes of the Formentor (notably the catastrophic infotainment at its launch). Although it relies on proven mechanical foundations (EA211 evo2 and EA888 evo4), it integrates a new electronic architecture and a new PHEV powertrain. **For high mileage drivers looking for diesel: move along, the Terramar does not offer one.** The most rational alternative is the 1.5 eTSI 150, while the 1.5 eHybrid is essential for corporate fleets. As with any new VAG group model, it is advisable to wait 6 to 12 months after launch to avoid the inevitable software bugs of the first series.
