Summary

Volvo S60 Generation III (Z252)

The third-generation Volvo S60 marked a significant turning point for the Swedish brand. Built on the Scalable Product Architecture (SPA) platform, it was the first modern Volvo to completely ditch diesel engines, signaling the brand's commitment to electrification. It was also the first Volvo manufactured in the United States (Ridgeville, South Carolina).

Positioned as a premium compact executive sedan, it competes directly with the German triad (BMW 3 Series, Audi A4, Mercedes-Benz C-Class). The S60 stands out with its minimalist Scandinavian design, exceptional seat comfort, and class-leading active safety features. Over its lifecycle, the powertrain lineup transitioned from traditional turbocharged petrol engines (T-series) to 48V mild-hybrids (B-series) and high-performance plug-in hybrids (Recharge/T8). In 2022, the model received a mild facelift and transitioned from the Sensus infotainment system to a Google-based Android Automotive OS.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional front seat comfort (especially Inscription/Ultimate trims)
  • Class-leading active and passive safety systems (City Safety, Pilot Assist)
  • Stunning, timeless Scandinavian exterior and interior design
  • Excellent Bowers & Wilkins premium audio system (optional)
  • PHEV models offer sports-car acceleration with usable daily EV range (especially 2022+ extended range models)

Weaknesses 5

  • Trunk space is smaller than German rivals (and PHEV battery intrudes slightly)
  • Ride quality can be harsh on 19-inch or 20-inch wheels (R-Design/Polestar)
  • Transition to Android Automotive (2022+) brought early software bugs and removed some physical drive mode selectors
  • Front-wheel-drive bias on non-AWD models limits dynamic handling compared to a RWD BMW 3 Series
  • High repair costs for PHEV-specific components out of warranty

Check before buying

  • Infotainment stability: Ensure the screen doesn't freeze or reboot randomly (requires latest software update).
  • PHEV ERAD system: On T8/T6 Recharge models (2018-2021), listen for a high-pitched whine from the rear axle during regenerative braking.
  • HVCH (Heater): On PHEVs, test the cabin heater in pure electric mode. If it blows cold, the High Voltage Coolant Heater has failed.
  • Sunroof seals: Check for water ingress in the front footwells, indicating clogged sunroof drains.
  • Brake wear: Heavy PHEV models consume brake pads and rotors faster than standard ICE variants.

Verdict

The Volvo S60 III is an outstanding alternative to the default German compact executive sedans. Because it was launched in 2018, it benefited from Volvo having already ironed out the major engine flaws (oil consumption, spark plug cracking) that plagued the earlier SPA-platform cars (like the 2016 XC90).

**Buying Advice:** - For maximum reliability and low running costs, the **T4 or T5 (or later B4/B5)** are the sweet spots. They offer plenty of power and avoid the complexity of superchargers and high-voltage systems. - If buying a **PHEV (T6/T8 Recharge)**, highly prioritize the **2022+ 'Extended Range' models**. Not only do they offer a much larger battery (18.8 kWh) and better EV range, but they also feature a redesigned rear electric motor that eliminates the costly ERAD bearing failures seen on the 2018-2021 models. If looking at an early T8, ensure it has a comprehensive warranty.
