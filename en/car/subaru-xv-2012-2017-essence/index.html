Summary

The first-generation Subaru XV (code GP), known as the Crosstrek in North America, is a compact crossover derived directly from the Impreza hatchback. It stands out with its generous ground clearance (22 cm), its adventurous look with raw plastic cladding, and above all its symmetrical all-wheel drive (Symmetrical AWD) as standard on almost all versions. Positioned as a robust and pragmatic alternative to traditional urban SUVs, it shines with its off-road capabilities and active/passive safety. A slight facelift occurred in late 2015, improving soundproofing, interior material quality, and steering responsiveness. Overall, it is a very reliable vehicle with petrol engines, provided oil consumption is monitored on the early model years.

Strengths 5

  • Formidable efficiency of the Symmetrical AWD system
  • 22 cm ground clearance, worthy of a true off-roader
  • Overall reliability of post-2014 petrol engines
  • Robust chassis and secure road handling
  • Excellent peripheral visibility

Weaknesses 5

  • Limited trunk volume (380 liters) due to the rear differential
  • Soundproofing could be improved (wind and road noise), especially before the 2015 facelift
  • CVT gearbox generating an "elastic" effect (revving) under hard acceleration
  • Dated and slow infotainment system
  • Hit-or-miss reliability of the Boxer Diesel engine (EE20) in urban use

Check before buying

  • Oil consumption history on 2012-2014 petrol engines (ask if the piston rings were replaced under warranty).
  • Service book: the CVT gearbox and axle (differential) oil changes must have been done around 60,000 km.
  • Rear wheel bearings: tendency to become noisy (humming) from 80,000 km.
  • Air conditioning compressor clutch: check that the AC produces cold air continuously (frequent failure of the relay or magnetic clutch).
  • On Diesel versions: condition of the DPF (warning light on?) and absence of abnormal noises from the bottom end of the engine.

Official recalls

  • [2021-06-16] Brake light switch (Code SRT270 / 201907): The brake light switch may be defective, preventing the brake lights from illuminating and, on automatic transmission models, preventing the vehicle from starting or shifting out of Park. Risk of collision/injury.
  • [2019] Valve springs: On 2.0i (FB20) engines produced between 2012 and 2013, risk of valve spring breakage that can lead to stalling or engine failure.
  • [2019] Electronic Parking Brake (EPB) wiring harness: Risk of short circuit or malfunction of the electronic parking brake due to an improperly insulated wiring harness.
  • [2016] Secondary air pump relay: On turbo petrol models (WRX/Forester) and some XVs, risk of overheating of the emissions secondary air pump relay.

What sets it apart 7

Safety

  • Exemplary Overall Safety — The Subaru XV achieved a maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores for adult (89%) and child (82%) protection, confirming its commitment to passenger safety. It was also named an IIHS "Top Safety Pick".

Handling

  • Unrivaled Control and Grip — Thanks to its Symmetrical AWD system, the XV offers stable and predictable road handling with exceptional grip on all surfaces. Its body roll is perfectly controlled, ensuring a serene and reassuring drive, even in the most demanding conditions.

Braking

  • Confident and Effective Braking — The Subaru XV benefits from good braking performance, offering a confident and progressive feel. Braking distances are competitive for its category, enhanced by the inherent stability of its symmetrical all-wheel-drive system.

Performance

  • Remarkable Off-Road Capabilities — With its incredibly efficient Symmetrical AWD and a generous ground clearance of 22 cm, the Subaru XV is ready to tackle the toughest paths, offering capabilities worthy of a true adventurer.

Reliability

  • Proven Reliability of Petrol Engines — The Subaru XV's petrol engines, particularly post-2014 models, are renowned for their robustness and overall reliability, ensuring long-term peace of mind thanks to proven mechanics.

Practicality

  • Optimal Panoramic Visibility — The intelligent design of the Subaru XV offers excellent peripheral visibility, facilitating maneuvers and increasing safety by allowing the driver to better understand their surroundings.

Comfort

  • Robust Chassis for a Serene Drive — Its robust and well-designed chassis gives the Subaru XV secure and stable road handling, contributing to a comfortable and reassuring driving experience on all types of roads.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Dacia Duster (for the affordable 4x4 off-roader aspect), Nissan Qashqai, Suzuki Vitara (excellent AllGrip system), Skoda Yeti.
  • North America: Jeep Renegade, Honda HR-V, Mazda CX-3, Chevrolet Trax.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Vezel (HR-V), Toyota C-HR, Mazda CX-3.

Verdict

The first-generation Subaru XV is an excellent choice for those who genuinely need a high-performance all-wheel drive system (mountains, snow, dirt roads) without the bulk of a large SUV. With a petrol engine (1.6i or 2.0i), it is an extremely robust vehicle, provided you favor a post-2014 model to avoid the excessive oil consumption issues of the early model years. The CVT gearbox requires smooth driving and rigorous maintenance (oil change every 60,000 km). The Diesel engine (2.0D) should be avoided if you drive in the city, as repair costs related to the DPF and EGR can quickly exceed the value of the vehicle.
