Summary

The fourth-generation Mercedes-Benz A-Class (W177 for the compact hatchback, V177 for the sedan) marks a major technological breakthrough with the introduction of the MBUX infotainment system. Positioned as the benchmark premium compact against the Audi A3 and BMW 1 Series, it offers significantly improved finish quality compared to the previous generation (W176). A facelift occurred in late 2022, bringing mild hybridization (48V MHEV) to the petrol engines, the removal of the central touchpad, and a software update. Although global demand is shifting towards petrol and hybrid, the diesel offering (which is of particular interest here) remains one of the most accomplished on the market, with highly efficient engines (the reworked Renault-sourced OM608, then the excellent 100% Mercedes OM654q). Overall generation rating: Very satisfactory, despite early electronic bugs.

Strengths 5

  • Diesel offering (OM654q) among the best on the market: quiet, torquey, and extremely fuel-efficient
  • Spectacular onboard technology and MBUX system
  • Significantly improved interior finish quality compared to the W176
  • Very sound road handling and suspension comfort (especially with adaptive damping)
  • Exceptional aerodynamics benefiting sound insulation on the highway

Weaknesses 5

  • Frequent electronic bugs on 2018-2019 models (screens, driving aids)
  • 7G-DCT gearbox (on smaller engines) sometimes hesitant and causing jerks in urban driving
  • Average rear passenger space and boot volume for the category
  • Very high maintenance costs within the Mercedes network
  • Cylinder head issue on the 2.0L petrol engine (M260) in early years

Check before buying

  • History of MBUX system software updates (black screens?)
  • On diesels: absence of engine light related to the AdBlue/NOx system
  • On OM654q diesels: listen to the engine when cold (absence of pronounced knocking in the upper engine)
  • On 2.0L petrol models (220, 250, 35 AMG) before 2021: check if the cylinder head has been replaced
  • Smooth operation of the automatic gearbox (no jerks when shifting from D to R)
  • Wear of front tires (front axle sometimes heavy on diesel versions)

Official recalls

  • - **2019 (OM608 Diesel)**: Risk of oil leak at the turbocharger oil supply line. Concerns models manufactured in 2018.
  • - **2019 (Airbags)**: Possible incorrect deployment of the passenger airbag due to non-compliant fastening.
  • - **2020 (eCall)**: The automatic emergency call system may transmit an incorrect position. Software update required.
  • - **2021 (Steering)**: Power steering wiring harness may rub and get damaged, leading to loss of assistance.
  • - **2022 (Panoramic roof)**: The polycarbonate panel between the windshield and the sunroof may detach while driving (bonding defect).
  • - **2023 (Transmission)**: On some front-wheel drive versions, risk of right driveshaft breakage due to a manufacturing defect.

Verdict

The Mercedes A-Class W177 is a highly desirable premium compact that has successfully corrected the comfort and finish flaws of its predecessor. **If you are looking for a diesel**, it is an excellent choice. The small 1.5L (180d until 2020) is an ultra-reliable workhorse, ideal for tight budgets. However, the **2.0L diesel (OM654q in 200d or 220d)** is the true gem of this range: it offers superior driving pleasure, remarkable reliability, and negligible fuel consumption on the highway. Avoid 2.0L petrol models (220/250) from 2018-2019 without warranty due to cylinder head risks. Prioritize a post-2020 model to avoid the early electronic bugs of the MBUX system.
