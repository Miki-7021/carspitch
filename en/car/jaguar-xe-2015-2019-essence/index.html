Jaguar XE

I (X760) · 2015 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation **Jaguar XE (X760)** (phase 1) was launched to compete with the German benchmarks in the D-segment (BMW 3 Series, Mercedes C-Class). Based on the iQ[Al] platform made of 75% aluminum, it stands out for its exceptional road handling, precise steering, and sharp front axle (double wishbone). While its dynamic qualities are undeniable, it suffers from cramped rear passenger space, average trunk volume, and an aging infotainment system (InControl) prone to bugs on the early model years. Mechanically, the 2015-2019 period marks the transition between Ford-sourced gasoline engines (EcoBoost) and Jaguar Land Rover's new modular "Ingenium" engine family. **Overall rating: 7/10 (Gasoline) / 4/10 (Diesel)**.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis (75% aluminum) and benchmark road handling
  • Precise steering offering excellent feel
  • Elegant and distinctive exterior design
  • Drivability and sound of the V6 Supercharged engine
  • Suspension comfort (especially with adaptive damping)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very restricted rear passenger space (legroom and headroom)
  • Disappointing trunk volume and accessibility
  • Quality of certain interior plastics unworthy of the premium segment (phase 1)
  • Slow and bug-prone InControl multimedia system
  • Disastrous reliability of the Ingenium diesel engines (chain, oil dilution)

Check before buying

  • Clear maintenance history (frequent oil changes are essential)
  • Coolant level (especially on the 3.0L V6)
  • Metallic noise on cold start (sign of timing chain wear)
  • Operation of the reversing camera (fragile trunk wiring harness)
  • Absence of jerks from the 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (a gearbox oil change is recommended around 80,000 - 100,000 km)

Official recalls

  • - **[2026-06-19] / [2025-05-13] Takata Airbags (Rappel Conso):** The propellant in the driver's airbag may deteriorate over time. Risk of metal fragments being projected in the event of deployment.
  • - **[2025-01-16] / [2023-07-20] Reversing camera (NHTSA 25V017000 / 23V501000):** On 2019-2020 models, wiring damage in the trunk can prevent the reversing camera image from displaying.
  • - **[2024-09-06] Turbo oil leak (Rappel Conso):** A leak from the turbocharger oil supply pipe can lead to deposits on the road (concerns 2021-2025 model years, to be checked if replaced late on phase 1).
  • - **[2022-09-02] Seatbelt pretensioners (Rappel Conso):** The pretensioner tubes may have been damaged during assembly.
  • - **[2022-07-01] Fuel return pipe (Rappel Conso):** Defect in the support wire mesh within the rubber return pipe.
  • - **[2021-04-08] Body structure (NHTSA 21V248000):** On certain 2017-2018 and 2020 models that have undergone a rear rail repair, missing spot welds can weaken the structure.
  • - **[2018-02-02] Fuel rail (NHTSA 18V090000):** On 2018 models equipped with the 2.0L gasoline engine, the fuel rail end caps may leak, leading to a fire risk.
  • - **[2017-10-26] Instrument cluster (NHTSA 17V678000):** On 2018 models, the dashboard may blank out intermittently.
  • - **[2017-04-27] Diesel fuel leak (NHTSA 17V286000):** On 2017 models equipped with the 2.0L diesel engine, the fuel return pipe may leak.

Verdict

The Jaguar XE is a sedan with character, designed for driving enthusiasts. Its chassis is one of the best in its class. However, buying it used requires great caution. **You must avoid the diesel engines (2.0d Ingenium)**, whose timing chain and oil dilution problems are ruinous. On the other hand, **the gasoline versions (our target) are much more recommendable**. The 2.0t (whether Ford or Ingenium sourced) offers a good compromise, while the 3.0L V6 is a future collector's item, provided its cooling system is preventively made reliable. Buy a gasoline model with a flawless maintenance history and be prepared to forgive mediocre rear passenger space in favor of driving pleasure.
