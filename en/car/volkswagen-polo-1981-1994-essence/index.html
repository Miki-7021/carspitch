Summary

The Volkswagen Polo II (code name 86C) is an iconic city car from the 80s and early 90s. Replacing the first generation, it stood out with its atypical body styles: the 'Steilheck' (small estate or van profile), the 'Coupé' (sloping rear window), and the 'Classic' (three-box sedan, formerly Derby). In 1990, it underwent a major facelift (often called 'Polo 2F' for *Facelift*), modernizing its front end, bumpers, dashboard, and generalizing electronic injection. Designed with a pragmatic approach, it is an extremely light car (often under 800 kg), equipped with very simple mechanics. Today, it has acquired a highly sought-after *Youngtimer* status, particularly in its GT versions and the legendary G40 with its supercharger. While its basic mechanical reliability is excellent, it suffers from typical ailments of its era: a strong susceptibility to corrosion and capricious carburetors on early models.

Strengths 6

  • Featherlight (700 to 830 kg) making driving fun even with low power
  • Exceptional mechanical accessibility (lots of space under the hood)
  • Reliability of bare engine blocks (very robust cast iron blocks)
  • Availability and ridiculously low price of spare parts (excluding G40 and specific bodywork)
  • Surprising boot volume on the 'Steilheck' (van) version
  • Injection versions (post-1990) very reliable for daily use

Weaknesses 6

  • Destructive corrosion (an ailment of the era, particularly before 1990)
  • Non-existent passive and active safety (no ABS, no airbags, light braking)
  • Pierburg carburetors become very capricious with age
  • Spartan comfort and almost no sound insulation
  • Maintenance cost of the supercharger on the G40 version
  • Original braking lacking bite and endurance

Check before buying

  • Battery tray: THE critical point. It rusts, perforates, and water flows directly onto the fuse box and into the passenger compartment (passenger side).
  • Fuel filler neck: mud accumulates there and rots the hose, leading to fuel leaks and rust in the tank.
  • Rear wheel arches, sills, and floors (perforating corrosion).
  • Cold start operation on carburetor models (the engine must hold idle without stalling or racing).
  • Boost pressure on the G40 (a gauge is essential during the test drive to check the supercharger's health).
  • Play in the steering and wear of front suspension bushings (wishbones).

Alternatives

Verdict

The Volkswagen Polo II (86C) is an excellent entry point into the world of Youngtimers. If you are looking for a reliable vintage daily driver, absolutely prioritize a facelifted version (2F, 1990-1994) equipped with an injection engine (1.0i or 1.3i): they start in all weather and consume little. Carburetor versions have undeniable charm but will require tuning skills or a conversion to a Weber carburetor. The legendary G40 is a pure collector's item, but demands a rigorous maintenance budget for its supercharger. The number one buying criterion should not be mileage, but the complete absence of corrosion, especially around the battery tray.
