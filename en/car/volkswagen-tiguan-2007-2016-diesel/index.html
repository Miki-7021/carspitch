Volkswagen Tiguan

I (5N) · 2007 – 2016

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

All Volkswagen models

Summary

The first-generation Volkswagen Tiguan (Type 5N) marked the brand's successful entry into the highly competitive compact SUV segment. Based on the PQ46 platform (a hybrid of the Golf V and Passat B6 platforms), it stands out for its rigorous road handling, meticulous finish, and highly efficient 4MOTION all-wheel drive (Haldex).

Evolution: A major facelift (Phase 2) occurred in mid-2011, modernizing the front end, lighting, and updating the powertrains and onboard electronics.

Positioning: Premium-mainstream compact SUV. Although this sheet focuses on Diesel engines (which represent the overwhelming majority of sales in Europe), the Tiguan ushered in the 'all-turbo' era at VW, completely abandoning naturally aspirated engines in favor of TSI and TDI.

Strengths 6

  • Safe road handling, similar to a sedan
  • Interior finish quality and material aging
  • Efficiency of the 4MOTION all-wheel drive (Haldex)
  • DSG gearboxes (DQ250 6-speed and DQ500 7-speed) reliable if serviced
  • Excellent resale value on the used car market
  • Reliability of the bare 2.0 TDI engine block (capable of exceeding 300,000 km)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of TSI gasoline engines (chains, oil) before 2013
  • Consequences of the 'Dieselgate' update on 2.0 TDI EA189 (EGR/DPF)
  • Trunk volume (470 liters) a bit small for the category
  • Firm suspension, especially with 18 or 19-inch wheels (R-Line trim)
  • High maintenance cost within the Volkswagen network

Check before buying

  • DSG gearbox maintenance history: mandatory oil change every 60,000 km (filter + oil).
  • Haldex coupler (4MOTION) maintenance history: oil change every 3 years or 60,000 km.
  • On 2.0 TDI EA189: ask if the emissions update has been done and if the EGR valve has been replaced since.
  • Panoramic sunroof: check for water ingress (clogged drainage channels).
  • Rear rolling noises: very common tire feathering wear on this chassis.

Official recalls

  • 2016 (Dieselgate): Massive recall (EA189) for engine control unit update to remove the defeat device software.
  • 2017 (Safety): Recall concerning Takata airbags (risk of metallic fragment projection during deployment).
  • 2014 (Electrical): Replacement of the exterior lighting fuse which could melt and cause loss of lighting.
  • 2011 (Fuel): On 2.0 TDI, risk of high-pressure injection line cracking due to vibrations.
  • 2017 (Braking): Grounding issue on the ABS/ESP unit that could lead to braking system failure in an emergency.

What sets it apart 7

Safety

  • Maximum Safety for All Occupants — The Tiguan I achieved excellent Euro NCAP safety test results, effectively protecting both adults and children thanks to its robust design.

Braking

Comfort

Performance

  • Efficient 4MOTION All-Wheel Drive — The 4MOTION (Haldex) system ensures optimal grip and enhanced traction on all surfaces, for a serene and controlled drive, even in challenging conditions.

Economy

  • Excellent Resale Value — The Volkswagen Tiguan I maintains a strong market value in the used car segment, representing a wise investment and easy resale thanks to its popularity and reputation.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 3008 I (more minivan-like, no 4x4 except HYbrid4), Ford Kuga I and II (excellent chassis), BMW X1 E84 (more premium, RWD/xDrive).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda CR-V III and IV (more spacious, very reliable), Toyota RAV4 III and IV (reliability benchmark, hybrid on the IV).
  • North America: Honda CR-V, Toyota RAV4, Ford Escape (Kuga equivalent).

Verdict

The Volkswagen Tiguan I is an SUV packed with driving qualities, but its reliability record is mixed. If you are looking for a Diesel: absolutely prioritize a Phase 2 model (after 2011). The 2.0 TDI EA189 (140/170 hp) is robust, but requires a clear history regarding the EGR valve and DPF, especially if it has undergone the Dieselgate update. The Holy Grail remains the 2015-2016 models equipped with the 2.0 TDI EA288 (150 hp), which are significantly more reliable in terms of emissions control. Avoid gasoline models from before 2013, whose timing chain and oil consumption problems can ruin your budget. A Tiguan 2.0 TDI 140/150 hp with a manual or DSG gearbox (if serviced) remains an excellent used purchase, provided the price is negotiated based on the maintenance history.
