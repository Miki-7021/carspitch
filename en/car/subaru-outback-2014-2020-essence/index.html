Summary

The fifth-generation Subaru Outback (chassis code BS) was launched in 2014, continuing the brand's tradition of rugged, raised station wagons. Based on the Legacy platform, it positions itself as a spacious, comfortable family crossover particularly capable off-road thanks to its permanent Symmetrical All-Wheel Drive and low-center-of-gravity Boxer engines.

This generation received a facelift in 2017 (for the 2018 model year), bringing aesthetic updates (bumpers, grille, headlights), interior improvements (materials, infotainment system), and technical optimizations (steering, suspension, EyeSight active safety system). It is renowned for its robustness, safety, and off-road capabilities, but its fuel consumption and the reliability of certain diesel engines can be points to consider. Overall, it is a reliable and durable vehicle, especially with petrol engines.

Strengths 6

  • Highly effective and safe Symmetrical AWD
  • Boxer engines (petrol) renowned for their reliability and low center of gravity
  • Generous cabin space and boot volume
  • Suspension comfort and off-road capabilities (high ground clearance)
  • Highly effective active safety (EyeSight system) on equipped models
  • Overall robustness and durability

Weaknesses 6

  • High fuel consumption for the 3.6R, and the CVT can be thirsty
  • Noise from the Lineartronic (CVT) transmission during strong acceleration
  • Diesel engine (EE20) reliability inferior to petrol engines
  • Interior material quality lags behind some premium competitors
  • Original infotainment system sometimes slow on early model years
  • Dealer network less dense than mainstream brands

Check before buying

  • Complete service history, especially for the CVT (fluid changes) and diesel (DPF/EGR)
  • Engine oil level and consumption on the 2.5i (FB25), especially before the facelift
  • Lineartronic transmission operation (absence of abnormal noises, jerks, excessive slipping)
  • Condition of the DPF and EGR valve on the 2.0D (warning lights on, suspicious smoke)
  • Condition of suspensions and bearings (noises, play)
  • EyeSight system operation (if equipped) and absence of warning lights
  • Presence of rust under the chassis and on the exhaust line, especially in salted regions

Verdict

The fifth-generation Subaru Outback is an excellent choice for those seeking a spacious, safe family vehicle that is particularly at home on challenging or snowy roads. Its symmetrical all-wheel drive is a benchmark.

**For a worry-free purchase, prioritize petrol engines:** the 2.5i (FB25) offers a good balance of versatility/fuel consumption, while the 3.6R (EZ36D) is a connoisseur's choice for its power and bulletproof reliability, despite higher fuel consumption.

**Be more vigilant with the 2.0D (EE20):** it can be appealing for high-mileage motorway drivers, but requires rigorous maintenance and can lead to unexpected costs in case of DPF/EGR issues.

In all cases, a complete service history and regular CVT fluid changes are essential to ensure the vehicle's longevity. The 2017 facelift brought notable improvements, making post-2017 models slightly more desirable.
