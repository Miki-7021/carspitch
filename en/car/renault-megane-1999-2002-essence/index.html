Summary

The Renault Mégane I Phase 2 (appeared in spring 1999) marks a major evolution for the diamond-badged compact car. Aesthetically, it adopts a modernized front end (bonnet, grille, smooth headlights) and new rear lights. But it's under the bonnet and in the cabin that the revolution takes place: massive introduction of multi-valve (16v) petrol engines replacing the old Energy/Cléon blocks, arrival of Renault's first common rail diesel (1.9 dCi in 2000), and significantly improved safety equipment (ABS and 4 airbags as standard). Available in multiple body styles (5-door Hatchback, 4-door Classic, Grandtour Estate, Coupé, Cabriolet and Scénic - although the latter gained independence), it offers an excellent comfort/road holding compromise. Today, it is a relevant economical choice on the used market, particularly with petrol engines, provided certain electrical peripherals and timing belt maintenance are monitored.

Strengths 7

  • Suspension comfort and soft seats
  • Healthy and safe road handling
  • Very successful, lively and economical 16v petrol engines (1.4 and 1.6)
  • Generous standard safety equipment for the time (ABS, 4 airbags)
  • Variety of body styles (especially the very spacious Estate and the stylish Coupé)
  • Derisory used purchase price today
  • Abundant and inexpensive spare parts

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 2.0 IDE engine (direct petrol injection)
  • Sagem ignition coils on 16v petrol engines
  • Interior plastic quality (aging, squeaks)
  • Recurring electrical problems (power windows, central locking)
  • Capricious immobilizer (key desynchronizes)
  • Imperfect soundproofing on the highway (especially with dTi)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (imperative every 5 years/120,000 km).
  • Condition of the rear axle (torsion bars): check for sagging or creaking.
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (windows, ventilation, central locking).
  • Hot and cold start (if difficult on petrol: TDC sensor or coils).
  • On diesels: turbo whistling (dCi) and condition of the auxiliary belt (dTi/dCi).
  • Windshield scuttle sealing (water drains clog and flood the cabin/UCH).

Official recalls

  • - **2000** : Recall concerning a risk of inadvertent deployment of side airbags (ECU reprogramming). - **2001** : Check and possible replacement of the brake booster (Mastervac) on certain series. - **2002** : Recall on dCi engines for a risk of high-pressure fuel circuit leak. - **2002** : Check of front seat belt fixings.

Verdict

The Renault Mégane I Phase 2 is today a 'youngtimer' or a very low-cost 'daily driver'. If focusing on petrol engines (excluding the 2.0 IDE which is absolutely to be avoided), it offers very good mechanical reliability once the early issues are resolved (ignition coils replaced with Beru/Denso, TDC sensor changed). The 1.6 16v is the best choice for its versatility. Diesels, although economical, have not aged as well and are subject to traffic restrictions (Crit'Air). Purchase is recommended provided an example with a clear service history (up-to-date timing belt) is found and the small electrical quirks inherent to Renaults of this era are accepted.
