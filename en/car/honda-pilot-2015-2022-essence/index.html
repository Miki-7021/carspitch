Summary

The third-generation Honda Pilot (YF3) is a large 3-row family SUV (up to 8 seats), designed primarily for the North American market. It breaks away from the boxy design of the previous generation (YF1) to adopt softer and more aerodynamic lines. Based on a platform shared with the Acura MDX and the Honda Odyssey, it stands out for its ride comfort, generous interior space, and robust 3.5L V6 engine from the EarthDreams family. A major facelift occurred in 2019, bringing aesthetic tweaks, a modernized infotainment system (with a physical volume knob), and a lifesaving reprogramming of the 9-speed automatic gearbox. According to data from ADAC and J.D. Power, the Pilot ranks above average in its category in terms of overall reliability, earning the 'Recommended' label from Consumer Reports for almost its entire career.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of the 3.5L V6 engine block (confirmed by ADAC and Consumer Reports)
  • Exceptional interior space and modularity (up to 8 real seats)
  • High-level suspension comfort and soundproofing
  • Highly capable i-VTM4 AWD system on snow
  • High resale value on the North American market

Weaknesses 4

  • Hesitant and jerky ZF 9-speed automatic gearbox (high trims) on 2016-2017 models
  • Slow and bug-prone infotainment system on 2016-2018 models
  • Sensitivity of the direct injection to carbon buildup at high mileage
  • Numerous NHTSA recalls regarding electronics and accessories

Check before buying

  • Automatic transmission fluid change history (especially the ZF 9-speed automatic)
  • Proof of timing belt replacement (interval: 160,000 km / 100,000 miles)
  • Verification of the status of critical recalls (fuel pump, connecting rods, belt)
  • Smooth operation of the infotainment system and backup camera
  • Absence of leaks at the i-VTM4 system (rear axle)

Verdict

The third-generation Honda Pilot is an excellent choice for large families looking for a spacious, comfortable SUV equipped with an extremely robust V6 engine. Data from ADAC and J.D. Power confirm its above-average reliability. Verdict: BUY, but with an important nuance depending on the year and trim. Strongly favor the facelifted models (2019-2022) which correct the early flaws of the ZF 9-speed automatic gearbox and offer a multimedia system that is finally up to par. If your budget points you toward the 2016-2018 models, prefer the LX, EX, or EX-L trims equipped with the 6-speed automatic gearbox, which is much smoother and more reliable than the 9-speed automatic of the early years. Make absolutely sure that the critical recalls (fuel pump, timing belt, connecting rods) have been performed.
