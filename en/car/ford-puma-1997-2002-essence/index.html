Summary

The first-generation Ford Puma is a small front-wheel-drive coupé based on the Ford Fiesta Mk4 platform. Praised by critics upon its release for its exceptional chassis and highly precise gearbox control (IB5), it established itself as one of the most enjoyable coupés to drive in its category. Its 'New Edge' design has aged well, making it a highly sought-after 'youngtimer' today. The flagship engine, the 1.7-liter developed in collaboration with Yamaha, features variable valve timing (VCT) and Nikasil cylinder lining. Although mechanically robust, the Puma Mk1 suffers from a deadly enemy: corrosion, which has decimated a large part of the car fleet.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (agility, precise front axle)
  • Very characterful and willing 1.7 VCT engine
  • Gearbox control (IB5) with short and precise throws
  • Timeless exterior design
  • Reduced mechanical maintenance cost (parts shared with Fiesta Mk4)

Weaknesses 5

  • Endemic and destructive corrosion (fenders, sills, floor pans)
  • Symbolic rear seats and very limited headroom
  • Original braking lacking endurance (rear drums on standard models)
  • Dated interior finish (Fiesta Mk4 dashboard)
  • Light soundproofing on the highway

Check before buying

  • Corrosion (CRITICAL) : Thoroughly inspect the rear wheel arches (often disguised), sills, boot floor, and chassis rails.
  • Timing belt (1.7 VCT) : Demand invoices. The operation is costly as it requires specific Ford/Yamaha tools to set the camshafts.
  • Heater control valve (HCV) : Test the ventilation. If it only blows hot air, the valve is faulty (inexpensive but annoying repair).
  • Rear axle bushings : Wear out over time, causing vague handling or clunking over bumps.
  • Clutch : The hydraulic release bearing can leak with age, requiring gearbox removal.

Official recalls

  • 1998 : Risk of friction wear of the front brake hoses against the bodywork. Installation of a protective sleeve.
  • 2000 : Possible replacement of front seat belt buckles that could engage incorrectly.
  • 2001 : Check of the brake master cylinder on a small series (soft pedal).

Performance

  • Characterful and Eager 1.7 VCT Engine — The 1.7 VCT engine, developed in collaboration with Yamaha, delivers spirited revs and a sporty sound, guaranteeing unparalleled driving pleasure.

Design

  • Timeless and Sought-After Design — With its 'New Edge' styling that has aged remarkably well, the Puma is today a highly appreciated 'youngtimer', combining modern aesthetics with classic charm.

Reliability

  • Robust and Proven Mechanics — Based on a reliable platform, the Puma benefits from generally very solid mechanics, ensuring durability and peace of mind for its owner.

Economy

  • Simplified and Affordable Maintenance — Thanks to many shared parts with the Ford Fiesta Mk4, the Puma's mechanical maintenance is reduced and parts are readily available, optimizing costs.

Braking

  • Effective Braking for its Era — The braking system of the Puma I was noted as strong and reassuring for its time, perfectly suited to its sporty and dynamic character.

Comfort

  • Precise and Enjoyable Gearbox — The IB5 gearbox control is renowned for its short throws and high precision, contributing to a fluid and engaging driving experience.

Alternatives

  • Europe : Opel Tigra (first generation), Renault Mégane Coupé (Mk1), Alfa Romeo GTV (larger but 90s coupé spirit).
  • Japan/Global : Honda CR-X Del Sol, Toyota Celica, Mazda MX-3.

Verdict

The Ford Puma Mk1 is a masterpiece of 90s dynamism. The 1.7 VCT engine is by far the most desirable for its character and performance. However, buying a Puma today should not be based on the mechanics (which are generally very solid), but exclusively on the condition of the bodywork. Rust is the scourge of this model. A healthy example justifies a high price and an immediate purchase for collection, while a rusty model will turn into a financial black hole for bodywork.
