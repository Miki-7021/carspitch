Hyundai Ioniq

I (AE) · 2016 – 2019

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The **first-generation Hyundai Ioniq (code AE)**, launched in 2016, is a landmark model for the Korean manufacturer. It is the first car in the world to have been designed from the outset to offer three electrified powertrains on a single platform: hybrid (HEV), plug-in hybrid (PHEV), and 100% electric (BEV).

The **plug-in hybrid (PHEV)** version, the focus of this sheet, combines a 4-cylinder 1.6 GDI Atkinson cycle engine (Kappa II family) with a more powerful electric motor than the standard hybrid version, powered by an 8.9 kWh lithium-ion polymer battery. It offers a real-world electric range of about 45 to 50 km. The Ioniq stands out for its highly refined aerodynamics (drag coefficient of 0.24), remarkable energy efficiency, and the use of a dual-clutch gearbox (DCT-6) that offers a more natural driving experience than the e-CVT gearboxes of its direct competitors (such as the Toyota Prius). The overall reliability of this model is excellent, backed by a 5-year manufacturer warranty (and 8 years/200,000 km for the high-voltage battery).

Strengths 6

  • Excellent overall reliability (TÜV defect rate at 3 years: 8.5%, very good for an electrified vehicle)
  • Class-leading energy efficiency and aerodynamics
  • Driving pleasure of the dual-clutch gearbox (DCT-6) superior to competing CVT gearboxes
  • Highly effective thermal management of the 8.9 kWh battery (PHEV), limiting degradation
  • Very robust McPherson strut front suspension
  • Very slow brake wear thanks to effective regenerative braking

Weaknesses 5

  • Some reports of jerks from the DCT gearbox at very low speeds (non-blocking but noticeable clutch engagement)
  • Rear headroom limited by the sloping roofline
  • Hard interior plastics that scratch easily
  • Rear visibility hindered by the split tailgate
  • Fire risk on the batteries of the 100% electric (BEV) versions, which led to a major recall

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: adherence to engine oil changes (15,000 km / 1 year) and DCT gearbox oil changes
  • Condition of the 12V battery (starting failures are often linked to this small battery, not the hybrid system)
  • Smooth operation of the DCT-6 gearbox during electric/combustion transitions at low speeds
  • For the BEV version: proof of completion of manufacturer recalls (BMS / Battery)
  • Condition of the brake discs (risk of corrosion due to underutilization linked to regenerative braking)

Alternatives

* **Europe / Global:** Toyota Prius IV (the absolute benchmark in hybrids, but less dynamic e-CVT gearbox), Kia Niro PHEV (technical cousin of the Ioniq, compact SUV format, identical mechanics), Volkswagen Golf GTE (sportier but more complex hybrid system and DSG gearbox, less reliable in the long term). * **North America:** Chevrolet Volt (generation 2, excellent electric range but discontinued), Honda Clarity Plug-in Hybrid. * **100% Electric:** Nissan Leaf (generation 2, but battery lacks liquid cooling), Renault ZOE.

Verdict

The first-generation Hyundai Ioniq (AE) is an **extremely relevant and reliable** used car choice, particularly in its **Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV)** version. Data from ADAC (1.8 breakdowns/1000 vehicles) and TÜV confirm a robust design and very healthy aging. The 1.6 GDI engine is free from the excessive oil consumption flaws known on other blocks, and the battery's thermal management guarantees good longevity. Maintenance is simplified compared to an equivalent diesel, and brake wear is minimal. If you opt for the 100% electric version, it is imperative to verify that the critical recall concerning the high-voltage battery has been carried out. For the PHEV and HEV, it is a worry-free purchase, provided you check the condition of the 12V battery and the smoothness of the DCT gearbox.
