Hyundai Tucson

II (LM) · 2009 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 55/100 — moyen

Summary

The **second-generation Hyundai Tucson (code name LM)**, widely marketed as the **Hyundai ix35** in Europe and other regions, marks a complete break from its predecessor. Adopting the *Fluidic Sculpture* design language, it sheds its rustic 4x4 appearance to become a modern, aerodynamic, and urban compact SUV. Sharing its platform with the Kia Sportage III, it appealed with its rich equipment, spaciousness, and a 5-year manufacturer's warranty. A facelift occurred in late 2013, bringing LED daytime running lights, new interior materials, and mechanical evolutions (replacement of the 2.0 MPI with the 2.0 GDI Nu in some markets). While the model is generally a commercial success, **its mechanical reliability is extremely polarized**: diesel engines are robust, while large-displacement gasoline engines (Theta II family) suffered from major design flaws that led to historic class-action lawsuits, particularly in North America.

Strengths 5

  • Exterior design that ages well (Fluidic Sculpture)
  • Very favorable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Generous interior space and decent trunk volume (465 L)
  • Remarkable reliability of the 2.0 CRDi diesel engine
  • Simple and intuitive control ergonomics

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 and 2.4 gasoline engines (Theta II)
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches)
  • Imperfect sound insulation (road and aerodynamic noise)
  • Vague electric power steering (MDPS) lacking feel
  • Suspension sometimes jittery at low speeds

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history and recalls: Crucial for gasoline engines (check if the engine has been replaced or if KSDS has been installed).
  • Engine noises: Any knocking on a 2.0 or 2.4 gasoline engine should deter the buyer.
  • All-wheel drive: Test the lock function and listen for differential noises.
  • Electronics: Functionality of the rearview camera (lens fogs up or takes on water), parking sensors.
  • Interior: Very frequent peeling of the steering wheel leather and gear knob.

Official recalls

  • - **Theta II Engines (2015 and later)**: Massive recalls (especially in North America, NHTSA) for premature connecting rod bearing wear that can lead to engine stall and fire risk. Replacement of the engine block or installation of the KSDS sensor.
  • - **ABS/HECU Module (2020/2021)**: Risk of short circuit and fire in the engine compartment, even with the ignition off, due to brake fluid leakage into the electronic hydraulic module. Recommendation to park outdoors before repair.
  • - **Brake Light Switch (2013)**: Replacement of the faulty switch preventing brake lights from illuminating, cruise control deactivation, or push-button start.
  • - **Airbag (2014)**: Replacement of the *clock spring* in the steering column, whose breakage illuminates the airbag warning light and deactivates the driver's airbag.
  • - **Transmission Cooling Hose (2012)**: On some automatic transmissions, possible leakage leading to loss of motive power.

Verdict

Purchasing a used Hyundai Tucson II (ix35) requires **extreme vigilance regarding the chosen engine**. If you are targeting a gasoline model, the 2.0 and 2.4 (Theta II) versions are true mechanical time bombs: unless the engine has been replaced new by Hyundai with supporting invoices, **they are to be absolutely avoided**. In Europe, the small 1.6 GDI is an acceptable compromise for urban use, although it requires rigorous monitoring for carbon buildup. Paradoxically for a buyer looking for gasoline, it is the diesel versions (especially the excellent 2.0 CRDi) that offer the best reliability and longevity for this generation. Buy a gasoline model with full awareness, and prioritize post-facelift models (2014-2015).
