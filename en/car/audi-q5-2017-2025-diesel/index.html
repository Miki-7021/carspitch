Summary

The second-generation Audi Q5 (internal code FY) is based on the modular MLB Evo platform, shared with the A4 and Q7. Lighter (up to -90 kg) and stiffer than its predecessor, it has established itself as one of the benchmark premium family SUVs. The late 2020 facelift brought an OLED light signature, the abandonment of the MMI rotary controller in favor of an all-touch interface, and the generalization of 12V or 48V mild-hybrid (MHEV) technology. Although demand for gasoline and plug-in hybrid (TFSI e) has exploded, the Q5 remains intrinsically linked to its diesel engines (TDI), which are particularly well-suited to its size and for high-mileage drivers. Manufacturing quality is at the top of the category, but the complexity of the emissions control systems (AdBlue) and onboard electronics requires rigorous monitoring.

Strengths 5

  • Top-tier finish and assembly quality
  • Excellent sound insulation, especially with acoustic glazing
  • Remarkable efficiency of the 2.0 TDI 190/204 hp (40 TDI)
  • Efficient and economical Quattro ultra system (disconnectable)
  • Suspension comfort (especially with the adaptive air suspension option)

Weaknesses 5

  • Chronic fragility of SCR (AdBlue) emissions control systems on TDIs
  • Annoying response time (lag) of the Tiptronic gearbox on V6 TDIs
  • High weight, particularly on TFSI e (PHEV) versions
  • Very high maintenance and spare parts cost within the network
  • Recurring bugs in the MMI multimedia system (black screens, CarPlay disconnections)

Check before buying

  • History of AdBlue system refills and repairs (TDI).
  • Absence of coolant leaks (water pump/thermostat on TDI and TFSI).
  • Smooth operation of the S tronic gearbox (no jerks at low speed).
  • Absence of parasitic noises around the panoramic sunroof (frequent creaking).
  • Condition of front suspension bushings (premature wear due to vehicle weight).

Official recalls

  • November 2021 (Gateway Module): Risk of liquid infiltration (if spilled on the rear seat) into the gateway control module, leading to power loss. Installation of a protective cover.
  • March 2024 (Starter-Generator): On MHEV models, risk of overheating and fire of the starter-generator. Replacement of the part.
  • May 2022 (Rear Axle): Possible misalignment of the rear axle which could lead to uneven tire wear and loss of control (2019-2021 models).
  • July 2019 (Trims): Wheel arch trims may detach while driving. Reinforced fastening.
  • December 2018 (Braking): Possible defect in the brake master cylinder requiring stronger pedal pressure.

Verdict

The Audi Q5 (FY) is a sophisticated premium SUV, sovereign on the highway and remarkably well-finished. If you opt for a TDI, the 40 TDI (190 or 204 hp) is the most rational choice: it offers a perfect balance between performance and fuel consumption. However, buying a diesel is only justified if you drive more than 20,000 km/year, mainly on roads/highways, to avoid DPF clogging and AdBlue failures. The V6 TDI (50 TDI / SQ5) is an auditory and mechanical delight, but its maintenance is expensive and its gearbox sometimes sluggish. For low-mileage drivers or urban dwellers, you should definitely turn to the 45 TFSI or the plug-in hybrid versions (TFSI e), although the latter are heavy. Overall, mechanical reliability is very good, but the electronics and emissions control systems require a comfortable maintenance budget.
