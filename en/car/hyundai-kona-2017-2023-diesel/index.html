Summary

The first-generation Hyundai Kona (OS code) is an urban SUV (B-segment) with a bold design that marked a stylistic break for the Korean brand. Launched in late 2017, it received a significant facelift in late 2020 (phase 2), softening its front end and modernizing its interior. The Kona stands out with an exceptional multi-energy offering: gasoline, diesel, hybrid (HEV), and 100% electric (BEV). Diesel Focus: Although the market shunned diesel in this segment, the Kona offered the excellent 1.6 CRDi engine (U3 / Smartstream family), available in 115 hp and 136 hp, the latter often coupled with the DCT-7 dual-clutch gearbox and all-wheel drive (HTRAC), a rarity in its category. The diesel was removed from the European catalog in late 2020 during the facelift, replaced by 48V mild hybridization in some markets before disappearing entirely in favor of the HEV. Overall, the Kona OS is a very reliable vehicle, supported by the manufacturer's 5-year warranty, although penalized by a modest trunk and rigid interior plastics.

Strengths 6

  • Excellent overall reliability, backed by a 5-year unlimited mileage warranty
  • Very fuel-efficient 1.6 CRDi engine on roads and highways (often under 5.0 L/100 km)
  • Physical ergonomics preserved (real buttons for climate control)
  • Very rich standard equipment (driving aids, infotainment)
  • Availability of all-wheel drive (HTRAC) on the 136 hp diesel
  • Plethora of engine options (from diesel to electric)

Weaknesses 6

  • Very firm suspension, especially with 18-inch wheels
  • Disappointing trunk volume for the category (361 liters, 332 L in 4x4 diesel)
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches) before the facelift
  • DCT-7 gearbox sometimes hesitant at low speeds and causing jerks
  • Fickle AdBlue system on the 1.6 CRDi (common problem with modern diesels)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (road noise)

Check before buying

  • On the 1.6 CRDi: Engine light or 'Antipollution fault' message (sign of an AdBlue/DPF problem).
  • On the 1.6 CRDi: DCT-7 gearbox behavior (juddering on startup = worn clutches).
  • On the Electric: Traction battery replacement certificate (recall campaign).
  • On all models: Condition of interior plastics and exterior bumpers (very exposed).
  • On all models: Touchscreen operation (some cases of frozen screens requiring a reset or update).

Official recalls

  • March 2021 (Electric): Risk of internal short circuit in high-voltage battery cells (LG Chem). Battery pack replacement on models produced between 2018 and March 2020.
  • December 2020 (Electric / Hybrid): Electronic braking system (IEB) fault. Software update to prevent loss of braking assistance.
  • May 2021 (2.0 MPI - North America): Incorrect heat treatment of piston rings leading to engine damage. Engine replacement if necessary.
  • November 2018 (All models): Possible leak at the high-pressure fuel pump. Replacement of the hose or pump.

Verdict

The first-generation Hyundai Kona is an excellent choice on the used car market. Regarding the 1.6 CRDi diesel specifically: it is a robust engine (chain timing) and extremely frugal, ideal for high-mileage drivers. However, like any modern diesel, it is vulnerable to clogging (DPF, EGR) and AdBlue system failures if used predominantly in the city. If your use is urban or suburban, you should definitely opt for the excellent hybrid version (1.6 GDi HEV) or the electric one (checking that the battery recall has been performed). The DCT-7 gearbox requires smooth driving in the city to preserve its clutches. Prioritize a post-2019 model to benefit from transmission software updates.
