Peugeot 3008

I (0U) · 2009 – 2016

AVOID Reliability 35/100 — faible

Summary

The first-generation Peugeot 3008 (internal code T84 / 0U) marked a major turning point for the lion brand. At the crossroads of a compact MPV, an SUV, and a sedan, it achieved immense commercial success thanks to its original interior (i-Cockpit ahead of its time with head-up display and 'aviation-style' console), excellent road holding, and comfort. It underwent a facelift (Phase 2) in late 2013, modernizing its front end and lights.

**However, in terms of petrol engines (which are the subject of this report), the reliability record is historically poor.** The 3008 I was equipped with the notoriously problematic 'Prince' engines (1.6 VTi and 1.6 THP) co-developed with BMW, as well as the first 1.2 PureTech units at the end of its production run. These blocks accumulated major design flaws (timing chain, oil consumption, submerged belt). While the chassis is brilliant, buying a petrol 3008 I is an uphill battle to find a healthy example.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (typical Peugeot comfort/dynamism compromise)
  • Original and appealing interior presentation (center console, i-Cockpit)
  • Generous boot volume (432 liters) and modularity (flat floor, 'hobby' two-part tailgate)
  • Excellent sound insulation on petrol versions
  • Remarkable reliability of 2.0 HDi engines (for those who accept diesel)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of petrol engines (VTi, THP, PureTech)
  • BMP6 automated manual gearbox (often paired with e-HDi or HYbrid4) slow, jerky, and unreliable
  • Premature tire wear (especially front and 18-inch)
  • Capricious electronics (obsolete WipNav GPS, head-up display bugs)
  • Poor rear visibility (thick pillars)

Check before buying

  • CLEAR service history and invoices for timing system replacement (chain on VTi/THP, belt on PureTech).
  • Engine oil level: a low level during inspection is a huge 'red flag' on Peugeot petrol engines.
  • Noise on cold start: any metallic clatter on a 1.6 THP/VTi indicates an urgent need for timing system overhaul.
  • Operation of the electric parking brake (frequent seizing of rear calipers).
  • Condition of the retractable GPS screen mechanism and the head-up display blade (fragile mechanisms).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2020 (PureTech)**: Massive recall (code JZR) for inspection and replacement of the timing belt, vacuum pump, and oil strainer on 1.2 PureTech engines produced between 2013 and 2017.
  • - **February 2011**: Recall for a risk of chafing of the power steering pump's electrical harness.
  • - **October 2012**: Engine management software update on 1.6 THP engines to prevent malfunctions of the additional electric water pump.
  • - **December 2015**: Replacement of the fuel heater on certain diesel models (risk of short circuit).
  • - **January 2016**: Inspection and possible replacement of the front suspension wishbone fixing bolts.

Verdict

If you are looking for a first-generation Peugeot 3008 with a **PETROL** engine, the verdict is clear: it's a minefield. The 1.6 VTi and 1.6 THP engines (up to 2014) are among the least reliable of their decade (timing, oil, carbon buildup). The 1.2 PureTech, which arrived late in the model's life, suffers from the notoriously problematic submerged belt issue.

**Buying advice:** If you absolutely must buy this model with a petrol engine, look for a **1.6 THP 165 (Phase 2, 2015-2016)** with an impeccable service history, or a 1.2 PureTech whose belt has just been replaced with the new model and the oil strainer cleaned. Ironically, the 3008 I is an excellent vehicle... provided you buy it with a diesel engine (2.0 HDi 150), for which it was brilliantly designed.
