Kia Sportage

IV (QL) · 2015 – 2021

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The **fourth-generation Kia Sportage (Type QL)**, a technical cousin of the Hyundai Tucson III, marked a stylistic break with its bold front end (raised headlights in the style of the Porsche Macan) and a significant upgrade to its interior. Launched in late 2015, it received a **facelift in 2018**, introducing new powertrains to meet Euro 6d-Temp standards, notably replacing the aging 1.7 CRDi with a new 1.6 CRDi (Smartstream) and adding 48V mild-hybridization to the 2.0 CRDi. Backed by the 7-year manufacturer's warranty (which still covers late-production models), it is a highly sought-after compact SUV in the used car market. Its overall reliability is excellent, particularly for European diesel engines, although some dual-clutch transmissions (DCT7) require vigilance.

Strengths 6

  • 7-year manufacturer's warranty (reassuring for used cars)
  • Overall reliability of diesel engines (especially 1.7 and 2.0 CRDi)
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume (except MHEV versions where trunk is reduced)
  • Physical ergonomics preserved (real buttons for climate control)
  • Sound and safe road handling
  • Very competitive price/equipment ratio

Weaknesses 6

  • DCT7 dual-clutch gearbox sometimes harsh and prone to wear (dry clutches)
  • Temperamental AdBlue system on the 1.6 CRDi (Smartstream)
  • Firm suspension, especially with 19-inch wheels
  • Fuel consumption of gasoline engines (1.6 GDi and T-GDi) higher than average
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind noise)
  • Catastrophic Theta II engines (North American market)

Check before buying

  • DCT7 gearbox behavior: test hill starts and very low-speed driving (no excessive juddering or slipping should be felt).
  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) history: check if the vehicle has not only been driven in the city (risk of clogging, oil level rising due to dilution).
  • AdBlue system (1.6 CRDi): check for absence of engine light or 'Antipollution fault' message.
  • Steering rack: listen for any clunking noises when turning the wheel at a standstill (steering bushing to be replaced).
  • Air conditioning: check its effectiveness (cases of leaks on the condenser exposed to gravel).

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Peugeot 3008 II (better chassis, but temperamental BlueHDi diesels), Volkswagen Tiguan II (more expensive, reliable TDIs but DSG to watch), Hyundai Tucson III (its technical twin), Nissan Qashqai II (very economical 1.5 dCi). - **Japan / Asia:** Toyota RAV4 (unbeatable hybrid, but more expensive), Honda CR-V, Mazda CX-5 (2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel to avoid, prefer gasoline). - **North America:** Toyota RAV4, Honda CR-V, Subaru Forester (all much more reliable than the Sportage with gasoline engines in this market).

Verdict

The Kia Sportage IV (QL) is an excellent used car choice for high-mileage drivers looking for a reliable and well-equipped diesel SUV. **The top choice is the 1.7 CRDi 115 hp with a manual gearbox** (before 2018) for its all-around robustness, or the **2.0 CRDi** for those who tow or seek a true automatic transmission (torque converter). The newer 1.6 CRDi is more modern but its AdBlue system requires vigilance. It is essential to **test the DCT7 gearbox** before purchase, as its dry clutches age poorly in urban use. Finally, for North American buyers, the 2.4 and 2.0 Turbo gasoline engines are to be avoided due to major defects in the Theta II block.
