Toyota C-HR

I (AX10) · 2016 – 2023

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

Summary

The first-generation Toyota C-HR (Coupé High-Rider) (AX10) marked a major stylistic departure for the Japanese manufacturer. Based on the excellent TNGA-C platform (shared with the Corolla and Prius 4), it offers dynamic road handling and a high level of comfort. Primarily designed for Europe, where it was predominantly sold in its hybrid version, it received a facelift in late 2019 (Model Year 2020), bringing a new 2.0L 184 hp hybrid engine, aesthetic tweaks (standard LED headlights), and a multimedia system finally compatible with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. It is an extremely reliable model, although its polarizing design sacrifices rear passenger space and visibility.

Weaknesses 5

  • Poor rear passenger space (feeling of claustrophobia due to small windows).
  • Limited trunk volume (377 liters on 1.8H, 358 liters on 2.0H due to relocated 12V battery).
  • Very poor rear and 3/4 rear visibility (reversing camera essential).
  • Pre-2020 infotainment system slow, dated, and without CarPlay/Android Auto.
  • Highway sound insulation (wind and road noise) could be improved.

Check before buying

  • 12V battery condition: very prone to discharge if the vehicle is driven infrequently (classic Toyota hybrid issue).
  • Windshield: recognized fragility on 2016-2018 models (spontaneous cracks).
  • Hybrid battery filter: check if it is not obstructed by dust or pet hair.
  • Maintenance history: essential to benefit from the extended warranty on the high-voltage battery (annual hybrid health check).
  • Interior noises: some rattles possible in the dashboard on degraded roads.

Official recalls

  • - **September 2018 (Electrical Harness)**: Risk of short-circuit and fire related to the hybrid system wiring harness coming into contact with a casing. Concerns models produced between May 2016 and May 2018.
  • - **November 2020 (Fuel Pump)**: Replacement of the low-pressure fuel pump (Denso supplier) whose impeller may deform and stall the engine. Concerns a wide range of Toyota models produced between 2017 and 2019.
  • - **November 2017 (Parking Brake)**: ECU software update to prevent the electric parking brake (EPB) from disengaging or engaging correctly.
  • - **April 2021 (PCS Radar)**: Recalibration of the pre-collision system radar sensor which might not correctly detect obstacles.

What sets it apart 14

reliability

  • Exceptional Toyota hybrid system reliability, backed by an extended warranty. — The Hybrid Synergy Drive (HSD) system is renowned for its robustness and durability, offering unparalleled peace of mind. The Toyota Relax warranty can extend up to 10 years or 160,000 km if serviced within the network.

economy

  • Ultra-low fuel consumption and very high resale value. — Enjoy remarkable fuel efficiency, especially in urban areas where consumption is often below 5.0 L/100 km. Its strong demand in the used car market ensures excellent resale value.

safety

  • High-level passive safety, awarded by Euro NCAP. — The C-HR achieved a maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with high scores for adult (86%) and child (77%) protection, ensuring optimal safety for all occupants.

braking

  • Powerful, consistent, and reassuring braking performance. — Benefit from good and consistent braking performance, with competitive stopping distances, often around 38-40 meters from 100 km/h, and a firm pedal feel that inspires confidence.

road_handling

  • Dynamic, agile, and engaging road behavior. — Thanks to its TNGA chassis, the C-HR offers exceptional road handling, with surprising agility and dynamism for a crossover, good body control, and precise, responsive steering.

comfort

  • Excellent comfort/dynamism compromise thanks to the TNGA platform. — The TNGA-C platform ensures dynamic road behavior while providing a high level of comfort, making every journey enjoyable, whether in the city or on longer trips.

Design

  • Bold and timeless design that stands out. — Its avant-garde and distinctive style marked a major stylistic breakthrough for Toyota and continues to age well, ensuring a unique and modern presence on the road.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Kia Niro (very good hybrid competitor, more spacious), Renault Captur E-Tech (hybrid, more practical), Honda HR-V e:HEV, Peugeot 2008. - **North America**: Subaru Crosstrek, Honda HR-V, Mazda CX-30. - **Asia / Japan**: Honda Vezel (HR-V), Nissan Kicks e-Power.

Verdict

The first-generation Toyota C-HR is a highly recommended purchase on the used car market. If you accept its family compromises (dark rear seats, small trunk), it will reward you with bulletproof reliability and negligible running costs. **Prioritize hybrid versions**. The 1.8H is perfect for city and daily commutes. If your budget allows, aim for a facelifted model (post-2020) with the 2.0H 184 hp engine: it corrects the multimedia flaws of the first phase while offering a transformed driving experience, without sacrificing reliability.
