Kia Sportage

II (KM) · 2004 – 2010

NEGOTIATE Reliability 70/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Kia Sportage (codename KM) marks a complete break from its predecessor. Gone is the rustic ladder frame; it adopts a monocoque structure (shared with the Hyundai Tucson JM), transforming it into a modern, comfortable compact SUV suitable for road use. Offered in front-wheel drive (2WD) or non-permanent all-wheel drive (4WD), it greatly contributed to establishing Kia's reputation in Europe thanks to its excellent price/equipment ratio. It underwent two minor facelifts (late 2007 and late 2008) improving interior finish, steering, and introducing more powerful diesel engines. Although demand focuses on diesel (CRDi), it remains an aging vehicle whose reliability will heavily depend on maintenance history.

Strengths 5

  • Generous interior space and modularity (flat-folding rear seats, opening rear window)
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Sound and safe road handling (for its time)
  • Overall reliability of gasoline engines
  • Towing capabilities and effective 4WD system on snow/dirt roads

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption (all engines, including diesel)
  • Interior plastic quality (easy to scratch) and peeling steering wheel leather
  • Imperfect sound insulation (especially on the 112 hp CRDi)
  • Problematic dual-mass flywheel and DPF on 140/150 hp CRDi
  • Trunk a bit small for the category (332 liters under parcel shelf)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (crucial on CRDi and V6 engines).
  • Clutch and flywheel on 140/150 hp CRDi (juddering on startup, knocking at idle).
  • DPF or engine warning light on the dashboard (140/150 hp CRDi).
  • Suspension noises (anti-roll bar bushes, ball joints) very common at the front and rear.
  • Driveshaft operation on 4WD versions (center bearing prone to wear, causing vibrations under the floor).
  • Air conditioning operation (frequent condenser leaks).

Official recalls

  • **Brake light switch (April 2013)** : Massive recall (2007-2010 models) to replace the faulty brake pedal switch (inoperative brake lights, inability to deactivate cruise control, ESP light on). - **ABS/ESP Module (Various campaigns)** : Risk of short circuit and fire in the ABS hydraulic module (HECU) due to moisture infiltration. Installation of a protective relay or module replacement. - **Airbags (2005)** : Reprogramming of the airbag control unit on the very first models to prevent inadvertent deployment.

Verdict

The Kia Sportage II (KM) is a very affordable used SUV, ideal for tight budgets seeking space and a raised driving position. If you're targeting **diesel**, the choice is difficult: the 112 hp is more reliable (no DPF, no dual-mass flywheel) but noisy and sluggish; the 140/150 hp are much more pleasant but expose to hefty bills (flywheel, DPF, turbo) if the vehicle has only been driven in the city. Opt for a gasoline model (2.0 16V) if you drive little, or a 140 hp CRDi with a clear maintenance history (recent timing belt and clutch) for high-mileage drivers. Inspection of the underbody (4WD versions) and running gear is mandatory before purchase.
