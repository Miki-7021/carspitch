Citroën C5

I (DC) (2001–2008) · 2001–2008

NEGOTIATE Reliability 70/100 — bon

Summary

The first-generation Citroën C5 (chassis code DC for the sedan, DE for the wagon) was launched in 2001, succeeding the Xantia. It marked a turning point for Citroën by abandoning the X-sounding name for a simpler designation. Positioned in the family sedan segment, it stands out for its exceptional comfort, inherited from Citroën tradition, thanks to its standard Hydractive III hydropneumatic suspension.

Available as a sedan and a wagon (called C5 Tourer), it emphasizes spaciousness, smooth driving, and careful sound insulation. A facelift occurred in 2004, bringing aesthetic modifications (front fascia, rear lights) and technical evolutions, notably the arrival of new diesel engines (1.6 HDi 110, 2.0 HDi 138) and gasoline engines.

Despite its intrinsic qualities of comfort and space, the C5 I sometimes suffered from a mixed reliability image, mainly due to electronic issues and certain weaknesses in specific engines, particularly the 1.6 HDi. However, the gasoline versions and older 2.0 HDi diesels generally prove robust if well-maintained. It is a car that offers excellent value for money on the used market, provided you choose the right engine and check key points.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional suspension comfort (Hydractive III)
  • Generous spaciousness and large trunk (especially in the wagon)
  • Careful sound insulation
  • Excellent value for money on the used market
  • Very robust 2.0 HDi 90/110 diesel engines
  • Often comprehensive equipment

Weaknesses 6

  • Sometimes capricious electronic reliability (COM2000, BSI, sensors)
  • 1.6 HDi 110 (DV6) engine prone to turbo and injector problems
  • 2.0 HPi (gasoline) engine to avoid
  • Interior finish inferior to German competitors
  • Maintenance cost of Hydractive III suspension if poorly maintained
  • Fragile dual-mass flywheel on some diesels

Check before buying

  • **Service History:** Invoices, service book, oil change frequency (especially for diesels).
  • **Hydractive III Suspension:** Check for leaks (orange LDS fluid), proper functioning of modes (comfort/sport), vehicle raising/lowering, condition of spheres (no knocking).
  • **Electronics:** Test all equipment (air conditioning, windows, radio, GPS), check dashboard warning lights, COM2000 operation (turn signals, wipers, horn).
  • **Engine (diesel specific):** Absence of excessive smoke (blue = turbo, black = injection/DPF), abnormal noises (flywheel, injectors), cold start. For the 1.6 HDi, check turbo history.
  • **Gearbox:** Smooth gear changes (manual), absence of jerks (automatic).
  • **Braking:** Wear of discs and pads, effectiveness of electric parking brake (if present).
  • **Bodywork and Chassis:** Signs of impact, corrosion, tire condition.

Alternatives

The Citroën C5 I operates in a very competitive segment. Here are some alternatives by market:

* **Europe:** * **Peugeot 406/407:** Shares many engines (HDi) and platforms, but with a more conventional suspension (407). The 406 is known for its robustness. * **Renault Laguna II:** Direct competitor, also focused on comfort, but with more erratic electronic reliability in the early years. * **Ford Mondeo II/III:** A benchmark in road handling, good spaciousness, powerful TDCi diesel engines. * **Volkswagen Passat B5.5/B6:** Image of superior perceived quality, renowned TDI engines, but less plush comfort. * **Opel Vectra C:** Solid and spacious alternative, but with less character. * **Japan/Asia:** * **Toyota Avensis I/II:** Reputation for exemplary reliability, but less comfort and personality. * **Honda Accord VII:** Excellent reliability, powerful gasoline engines, dynamic handling. * **Mazda 6 I:** Dynamic design, good road holding, solid reliability. * **North America:** The C5 I was not marketed in North America. Alternatives would be mid-size sedans like the Ford Fusion, Honda Accord, Toyota Camry, Nissan Altima. * **Emerging Markets:** Often the same European alternatives or specific local models, but the C5 I was less present in these markets than other more basic models.

Verdict

The first-generation Citroën C5 is a car worth considering on the used market, especially for those who prioritize absolute comfort and space at an affordable price. Its main asset, the Hydractive III suspension, is also its Achilles' heel if maintenance has not been followed.

**Buying Advice:**

* **Buy without hesitation:** Versions equipped with the **2.0 HDi 90 hp (DW10TD)** or **110 hp (DW10ATED)** engines, especially if the 110 hp is without DPF or if the DPF has been properly maintained. These engines are safe bets for robustness. The 1.8i, 2.0i, and 3.0 V6 gasoline engines are also good choices if one accepts their fuel consumption and regular maintenance. * **Negotiate strongly and with caution:** Versions equipped with the **1.6 HDi 110 hp (DV6TED4)**. An impeccable service history is imperative, with very frequent oil changes and proof of turbo-related corrective actions. A thorough inspection by a specialist is essential. The 2.2 HDi 136/173 hp are also to be negotiated due to their complexity (bi-turbo, DPF) and potentially higher maintenance costs. * **Avoid:** The **2.0 HPi (EW10D)** engine due to its fouling problems and first-generation direct injection reliability issues.

In all cases, prioritize a model with a clear history, complete service invoices, and a clean technical inspection report. A well-maintained C5 I is an excellent road car for a modest budget.
