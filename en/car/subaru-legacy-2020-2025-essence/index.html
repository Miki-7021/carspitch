Summary

The seventh-generation Subaru Legacy (BT code) is based on the Subaru Global Platform (SGP). Designed primarily for the North American market (it was withdrawn from the European and Japanese markets in favor of SUVs like the Outback), it stands out for its significantly improved ride comfort, enhanced soundproofing, and large 11.6-inch vertical touchscreen. True to the brand's DNA, it offers symmetrical full-time all-wheel drive (Symmetrical AWD) and the EyeSight preventive safety system as standard. A facelift was introduced for the 2023 model year, bringing a redesigned front end and technological updates. It is recognized as one of the safest and most reliable midsize sedans in its segment, although its sales suffer from the general disaffection for sedans.

Strengths 5

  • Symmetrical all-wheel drive (AWD) as standard, formidable in winter conditions
  • Above-average overall reliability (recommended by Consumer Reports since 2021)
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing worthy of the higher segment
  • Excellent all-around visibility (thin pillars)
  • Highly effective standard EyeSight safety system

Weaknesses 5

  • 11.6-inch infotainment system sometimes slow and prone to bugs (especially 2020-2021 models)
  • CVT gearbox that heavily smooths out acceleration sensations
  • Fuel consumption slightly higher than FWD competitors
  • Very conservative exterior design
  • Limited availability (withdrawn from many international markets)

Check before buying

  • Recall history (very important on 2020-2021 models: fuel pump, CVT gearbox TCU)
  • Smooth operation of the central touchscreen (test Apple CarPlay / Android Auto and climate controls)
  • Absence of CVT gearbox jerks at low speeds or when shifting between forward and reverse
  • Engine oil level (check for lack of overconsumption on the 2.5L)
  • Tire condition: on a Subaru AWD, all 4 tires must have an identical wear level to avoid damaging the center differential.

Verdict

The 7th generation Subaru Legacy (BT) is an extremely rational and safe choice for North American buyers looking for a midsize sedan. Its standard all-wheel drive makes it a winter queen, and its mechanical reliability is very solid, surpassing the category average according to Consumer Reports and J.D. Power. Subaru's old demons (head gaskets) are ancient history with the FB25D and FA24F engines. However, the first year of production (2020) suffered numerous early recalls (fuel pump, CVT gearbox programming, infotainment).

Buying advice: Favor models from 2021 or 2022 onwards, where early defects were corrected at the factory. The naturally aspirated 2.5L engine is the most sensible choice for 90% of drivers, offering excellent durability. Ensure that all recalls (especially those concerning the gearbox TCU) have been performed by the previous owner.
