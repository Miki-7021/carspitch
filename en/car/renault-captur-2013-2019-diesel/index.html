Summary

Renault Captur I (J87)

A pioneer and undisputed best-seller in the urban SUV segment in Europe, the first-generation Renault Captur is based on the Alliance's B platform (shared with the Clio IV). Launched in 2013, it appealed with its two-tone design, clever modularity (sliding bench seat, 'Easy Life' drawer glove box), and ride comfort.

The facelift (Phase 2) introduced in spring 2017 is crucial: it corrected the model's main flaw at launch, namely a very plastic and unappealing interior finish, by adopting soft-touch materials and new assemblies.

Diesel Focus: In this generation, the 1.5 dCi (K9K) engine reached full maturity. Freed from the serious injection issues (metal filings) of the 2000s, it proves to be a real workhorse, extremely reliable if used according to its purpose (road/highway driving). Vigilance should be exercised regarding the emission control peripherals in case of exclusively urban use and the EDC dual-clutch automatic gearbox.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable reliability of the 1.5 dCi engine in this generation
  • Extremely low real-world diesel consumption (often under 5L/100km)
  • Excellent modularity (rear bench sliding by 16 cm)
  • Very reasonable maintenance and insurance costs
  • Suspension comfort and smooth driving
  • Significantly improved interior finish quality after the 2017 facelift

Weaknesses 5

  • Hard interior plastics, very prone to scratches (Phase 1 before 2017)
  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.2 TCe 120 petrol engine
  • EDC automatic gearbox sometimes causes jerks and clutch wear in urban driving
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind noise)
  • R-Link 1 multimedia system slow and prone to bugs (MediaNav more basic but more reliable)

Check before buying

  • 1.5 dCi service history: the timing belt must have been replaced on time.
  • EDC gearbox behavior: no juddering on hill starts or during low-speed maneuvers should be tolerated.
  • Front axle: clunking noises over speed bumps indicating wear of stabilizer bar links or bushings (frequent around 80,000 km).
  • Air conditioning: check its proper functioning (condenser exposed to gravel and frequent gas leaks).
  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) on dCi: check for absence of engine warning light if the vehicle has been driven extensively in the city.

Official recalls

Major Recalls Identified (Europe)

  • April 2015 (Braking): Risk of wear to the front brake hoses due to rubbing against the wheel arches. Concerns models produced until November 2014. Installation of a protective sleeve or replacement.
  • July 2015 (Fuel Gauge): Dashboard reprogramming due to an erroneous fuel level indication (2013-2014 models).
  • May 2016 (Steering/Hubs): Risk of cracks on the front wheel hubs that could affect steering. Concerns a small series produced between September and November 2015.
  • 2019 (dCi Emissions Control): Recall campaign (often during revisions) for engine management software update to optimize NOx emissions on 1.5 dCi engines.

What sets it apart 7

Reliability

  • Proven Reliability of the 1.5 dCi Diesel Engine — The 1.5 dCi engine of this generation is renowned for its exceptional robustness and longevity, offering peace of mind on the road.

Economy

  • Remarkably Low Fuel Consumption — Enjoy first-class energy efficiency with the diesel engine, enabling long and economical journeys.

Safety

  • Exemplary Passive Safety — The Captur I ensures optimal protection for all occupants, as evidenced by its excellent score in Euro NCAP tests.

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from short stopping distances and a reassuring pedal feel, ensuring perfect vehicle control in all circumstances.

  • Stable and Comfortable Road Handling — With predictable road holding and light steering, the Captur offers a pleasant and serene driving experience, both in the city and on the open road.

Practicality

  • Ingenious Interior Modularity — The 16 cm sliding rear bench seat and clever storage solutions offer remarkable flexibility to adapt to all your transport needs.

Comfort

Alternatives

Direct Competitors

  • Europe: Peugeot 2008 I (1.6 e-HDi / BlueHDi very fuel-efficient but AdBlue issues on BlueHDi), Nissan Juke I (divisive design, less spacious, shares the 1.5 dCi), Opel Mokka (heavier, noisy 1.6 CDTI diesel), Ford EcoSport.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda HR-V (more spacious, very reliable), Mazda CX-3 (superior finish, 1.5 Skyactiv-D diesel prone to some clogging issues).
  • Emerging Markets (vs Kaptur B0): Hyundai Creta, Nissan Kicks, Jeep Renegade.

Verdict

The Renault Captur I is an excellent used car choice if you target the right engines. For diesel, it's a big 'YES'. The 1.5 dCi, whether 90 hp or 110 hp, has reached an exceptional level of reliability in this generation. It's indestructible, economical, and perfectly suited to the vehicle.

Buying Advice: Prioritize a Phase 2 model (after April 2017) with the 1.5 dCi 110 hp (6-speed manual gearbox) for total versatility and a decent interior finish. If you opt for a dCi 90 EDC, ensure the automatic gearbox has been thoroughly tested when cold and in traffic jams. Absolutely avoid the 1.2 TCe 120 petrol versions.
