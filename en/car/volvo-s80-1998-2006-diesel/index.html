Volvo S80

I (P2) · 1998 – 2006

NEGOTIATE Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The **first-generation Volvo S80 (P2)** marked a major turning point for the Swedish manufacturer. Replacing the S90 (900 series), it abandoned rear-wheel drive and cubic design to adopt the new P2 platform (front-wheel drive or all-wheel drive) and softened lines designed by Peter Horbury. As the brand's flagship, it distinguished itself with its exceptional level of passive safety (inauguration of WHIPS anti-whiplash seats and IC inflatable curtains) and a supreme ride comfort.

A facelift occurred in mid-2003, bringing minor aesthetic touches (grille, taillights, chrome-ringed gauges) but, more importantly, **crucial electronic improvements** (more reliable multiplexed network) and new engines. Although the focus here is on diesels, the S80 offered a wide range of 5 and 6-cylinder engines. The diesel versions, particularly the in-house D5, are now the most sought after for their exceptional longevity.

**Overall generation rating: 7.5/10** (Penalized by electronic issues in early model years and the fragility of automatic gearboxes on 6-cylinder models, but saved by the indestructibility of its 5-cylinder blocks).

Strengths 5

  • Unrivaled seat comfort (among the best in the automotive industry)
  • Exceptional reliability of 5-cylinder diesel engines (D5 and TDI)
  • Paint quality and corrosion resistance (galvanized steel)
  • Impressive high-end audio system (HU-801 / HU-850 with Dolby Pro Logic)
  • Passive safety still relevant (robust chassis, SIPS, WHIPS)

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic turning radius (like a truck, due to the wide transverse engine)
  • Heavy and fragile front axle (wishbone bushings, ball joints, shock absorber mounts wear out quickly)
  • Undersized GM 4-speed automatic gearbox on 6-cylinder models (T6 and 2.9)
  • Capricious electronics on 1998-2002 models (DIM instrument cluster, CEM module, ETM throttle body)
  • High cost of genuine Volvo spare parts

Check before buying

  • History of the timing belt AND accessory belt (especially on D5).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior: gear changes must be smooth, without jerks or slipping when hot. Check the oil color (should be red/pink, not black).
  • Full functionality of the instrument cluster (DIM): check that all LCD displays and gauges work after a few minutes of warming up.
  • Noises from the front axle over speed bumps (clunking = worn drop links, wishbones, or strut mounts).
  • Power steering fluid leaks at the steering rack.
  • On diesels: absence of excessive black smoke during acceleration (EGR/injectors) and no metallic knocking at idle (dual-mass flywheel).

Official recalls

  • - **November 1999**: Replacement of front suspension ball joints (premature wear potentially leading to loss of control). - **2004**: Risk of overheating and fire of the cooling fan control module (1999-2001 models). - **2006 (Warranty extension / Silent recall)**: Software update and conditional replacement of the Magneti Marelli throttle body (ETM) on 1999-2002 gasoline engines. - **Various**: Minor recalls concerning seat belt anchorages and the climate control module (CCM) software.

Verdict

The Volvo S80 I is a formidable road car that offers an unbeatable price/performance/comfort ratio on the used car market. **If you are looking for a diesel, the D5 (163 hp) is a masterpiece of reliability**, capable of exceeding 500,000 km with rigorous maintenance. The original Audi 2.5 TDI is also indestructible but less refined.

**Buying advice:** Prioritize a facelifted model (post-2003) with a D5 (163 hp) or 2.4/2.5T gasoline engine. Absolutely avoid T6 versions due to their disastrous GM automatic gearbox, and be very vigilant about the service history of Aisin automatic gearboxes (demand invoices for gearbox fluid changes). A well-maintained model is a highly recommended purchase.
