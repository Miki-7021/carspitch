Summary

The second-generation Audi A5, known by the internal code B9 (or F5 for the platform), was launched in 2016, succeeding the first-generation B8. It shares its MLB Evo platform with the A4 B9, offering a lighter and more rigid architecture. Available as a coupe, Sportback (5-door), and convertible, the A5 B9 positions itself as a more stylish and sporty alternative to the A4, targeting customers seeking elegance and performance.

A major facelift was introduced in late 2019 (2020 models), bringing aesthetic updates (headlights, grille, bumpers), a more modern MMI Touch infotainment system, and the integration of mild-hybrid (MHEV) technology on most powertrains to improve efficiency.

Overall, the A5 B9 is perceived as a well-built, comfortable, and technologically advanced car, with a high level of interior finish quality. Its reliability is generally good, but certain powertrains and technologies (notably MHEV) require particular attention.

Strengths 6

  • Very high level of interior finish and material quality.
  • Excellent ride comfort, even with large wheels.
  • Elegant and timeless design (coupe, Sportback, convertible).
  • Advanced onboard technology (Virtual Cockpit, MMI Touch post-facelift).
  • Wide choice of powerful and efficient powertrains.
  • Balanced and safe road handling (efficient Quattro).

Weaknesses 6

  • High maintenance and parts cost, especially for S/RS versions.
  • Long-term reliability of MHEV (mild-hybrid) systems still uncertain.
  • Valve carbon buildup on direct-injection TFSI engines (especially pre-facelift).
  • Fragile water pump on 2.0 TFSI.
  • Limited rear space in the coupe and convertible.
  • AdBlue system sometimes temperamental on diesels.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices: Essential for all powertrains, especially TFSI and TDI.
  • TFSI engines (petrol): Check for abnormal noises on startup (timing chain), water pump condition (leaks), and if decarbonization has been performed.
  • TDI engines (diesel): Check for proper AdBlue system operation (warning lights, error messages), DPF condition (no excessive smoke), and regeneration history.
  • MHEV system (post-facelift): Ensure no warning lights related to the 48V battery or starter-generator are illuminated. Test the Start&Stop.
  • S tronic (DSG) gearbox: Check for smooth gear changes, absence of jerks, and if the gearbox oil change has been performed according to recommendations (every 60,000 km).
  • Electronics and Infotainment: Test all functions (Virtual Cockpit, MMI, GPS, connectivity) and ensure software updates.
  • Tire and brake wear: Especially on S/RS versions, wear can be rapid and replacements costly.
  • Bodywork and chassis: Check for signs of impact, panel alignment, and wheel condition.

Official recalls

Official Recalls (non-exhaustive, common examples)

  • 2017-2018 (various Audi models, including A5 B9) : Potential issue with engine control unit software that could lead to NOx emissions exceeding standards (primarily concerns diesels, but updates may have affected petrol engines). Subject : Software update.
  • 2018 (certain A5 B9 models) : Risk of fuel leakage at the fuel filter. Subject : Replacement of the fuel filter or its seal.
  • 2019 (certain A5 B9 models) : Issue with the airbag system control module that could prevent correct deployment in the event of a collision. Subject : Replacement of the control module.
  • 2020-2021 (MHEV post-facelift models) : Risk of overheating or short-circuiting of the 48V battery in the mild-hybrid system. Subject : Inspection and replacement of the 48V battery or its wiring.
  • 2021 (certain A5 B9 models) : Issue with the eCall emergency call system software, which could prevent correct transmission of location data. Subject : Software update.

*It is imperative to check with an Audi dealership, using the vehicle's chassis number (VIN), whether all applicable recalls have been performed.*

Verdict

The Audi A5 B9 is an excellent choice for anyone looking for a premium, elegant, and comfortable car, with impeccable build quality.

For a worry-free purchase, prioritize:

  • The 2.0 TDI and 3.0 TDI diesel engines (pre-facelift) for high-mileage drivers; their reliability is proven, provided the AdBlue system and DPF are monitored.
  • The 3.0 TFSI (S5) and 2.9 TFSI (RS5) petrol engines for performance enthusiasts, provided you accept high maintenance costs and corresponding fuel consumption.

Be more vigilant with:

  • The 2.0 TFSI pre-facelift (Gen3): Valve carbon buildup and the water pump are known weak points. A history of decarbonization and water pump replacement is a plus.
  • The MHEV (mild-hybrid) post-facelift versions: Although more efficient, 48V technology is still young and can lead to costly failures. Ensure all recalls have been performed and the system operates without fault.

Tips:

  • A complete and clear service history is non-negotiable. Prefer a vehicle serviced at an Audi dealership or by a VAG specialist.
  • A pre-purchase inspection by a professional is highly recommended, especially for TFSI and MHEV versions.
  • Remember that the cost of parts and labor is that of a premium car. Anticipate a significant maintenance budget.

In summary, the Audi A5 B9 is a desirable car. With the right precautions and by choosing the right powertrain, it can offer many years of enjoyment.
