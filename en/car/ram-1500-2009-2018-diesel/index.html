Ram 1500

4th gen (DS) · 2009 – 2018

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The 4th generation of the Ram 1500 (code name DS) marks a major turning point in the history of American pickup trucks. Initially launched under the Dodge brand in 2009, it simply became "Ram 1500" in 2010 following the split of the Chrysler group brands. It radically distinguishes itself from its competitors (F-150, Silverado) by abandoning rear leaf springs in favor of coil springs (and an optional air suspension), offering unmatched ride comfort in the category.

In 2013, a facelift brought the highly renowned 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (TorqueFlite 8) and the Pentastar V6. **In 2014, Ram struck a major blow by introducing the 3.0L V6 EcoDiesel** (supplied by the Italian VM Motori), becoming the first modern light-duty pickup to offer a diesel engine in the United States. While this engine appealed with its torque and record fuel consumption, it unfortunately proved to be a nightmare in terms of reliability, accumulating major recalls (EGR, injection pump, crankshaft sensor). The model continued to be produced beyond 2018 under the name "Ram 1500 Classic".

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional ride comfort (rear coil springs)
  • Responsive and reliable 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (from 2013)
  • Very intuitive Uconnect infotainment system (8.4 inches)
  • Timeless "mini-truck" look (Big Rig styling)
  • Spacious and well-finished cabin on high-end trims (Laramie, Longhorn)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 3.0L EcoDiesel engine (exorbitant repair costs)
  • Very temperamental air suspension (Active-Level) in cold weather (leaks, dead compressor)
  • Lower payload than the competition (trade-off for the coil springs)
  • Very high fuel consumption of the older engines (3.7 V6, 4.7 V8) and pre-2013 4/5/6-speed gearboxes
  • Susceptibility to corrosion on the rear wheel arches and rocker panels

Check before buying

  • Complete recall history (CRUCIAL on the EcoDiesel, see NHTSA list)
  • Operation of the air suspension: raise and lower the vehicle in all positions without error messages
  • Ticking noise on cold start on the 5.7 Hemi (broken exhaust studs or worn lifters)
  • Smell of coolant or drop in level on the EcoDiesel (EGR leak)
  • Condition of the tailgate (frequent recalls for unexpected opening)

Alternatives

- **Ford F-150 (2009-2014 / 2015-2020):** The leader. Offers better payload capacities, an aluminum body (from 2015) impervious to rust, and highly performant EcoBoost V6 engines (but requiring rigorous maintenance of the timing chains). - **Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500:** Very reliable V8 engines (5.3L and 6.2L) (despite some issues with AFM cylinder deactivation). The 3.0L Duramax diesel (which appeared later) is infinitely more reliable than Ram's EcoDiesel. - **Toyota Tundra (2007-2021):** The absolute benchmark for reliability (indestructible 5.7L V8). In return: dated technology, gargantuan fuel consumption, and lower comfort than the Ram.

Verdict

The 4th generation Ram 1500 is an extremely appealing pickup, offering the best comfort in its category and a killer look. **However, the 3.0L EcoDiesel engine is to be avoided at all costs**, unless the vehicle has already undergone the replacement of the HPFP pump, the EGR cooler, and the reluctor wheel, and you have a substantial repair budget. Breakdowns of this VM Motori engine are catastrophic and frequent.

**The best choice:** Opt without hesitation for the **5.7L V8 Hemi coupled with the 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (post-2013)**. Although the exhaust manifold studs must be monitored (the famous "Hemi tick"), it is a robust, high-performance package with excellent resale value. Also avoid the air suspension if you live in a cold region.
