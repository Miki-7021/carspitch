Summary

The first-generation Renault Modus underwent a major facelift in 2008 (Phase 2, code JP0). This facelift not only modernized its front and rear, but also introduced the Grand Modus variant (+16 cm wheelbase and overhang). This extended version corrects the main flaw of Phase 1: a tiny boot. Based on the excellent Clio III platform, the Modus Phase 2 is an extremely practical urban mini-MPV, featuring a sliding rear bench seat and very sound road handling. In terms of reliability, Phase 2 reached maturity, correcting most of the early defects (electronics, diesel injection) of Phase 1. With petrol engines, it shines with its robustness, avoiding problematic engines that appeared later in the Renault range.

Strengths 6

  • Exemplary modularity (sliding and folding rear bench seat)
  • Boot volume on the Grand Modus version (up to 410 liters)
  • Excellent reliability of petrol engines (D4F and D4Ft)
  • Suspension comfort and decent sound insulation for the category
  • Peripheral visibility (large glass surfaces)
  • Very affordable maintenance and insurance costs

Weaknesses 5

  • Boot of the "short" Modus version still too small
  • Outdated, slow, and unreliable automatic gearbox (BVA4 / DP0)
  • Difficult mechanical accessibility (especially for changing headlight bulbs, although slightly improved on Phase 2)
  • Body roll in corners (typical of small MPVs)
  • Hard interior plastics that can scratch easily

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (all Modus engines have a belt, not a chain).
  • Operation of all electric windows (a chronic issue with Renault at that time).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior on the 1.6 16v (jerks when shifting gears = worn hydraulic block).
  • Absence of engine light related to clogging (EGR/DPF) on diesel versions tested in the city.
  • Condition of the stalks (indicators, wipers) which may have loose connections.

Official recalls

  • Steering (2009): Recall on certain models manufactured in 2008 for a risk of loss of power steering assistance or locking (reprogramming or replacement of the column).
  • Rear bench seat (2008): On early Grand Modus models, verification of the rear bench seat slide locking system.
  • Front seats (2008): Inspection of the front seat frame welds on a small series.

What sets it apart 7

safety

  • High-level Safety — The Renault Modus Phase 2 achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, ensuring optimal protection for adult and child occupants.

braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — Equipped with ABS and EBD as standard on most versions, the Modus offers reliable and stable braking performance, ensuring precise and safe stops.

handling

  • Healthy and Comfortable Road Handling — Based on the excellent Clio III platform, the Modus provides stable and predictable road holding, combining urban agility with on-road comfort.

reliability

  • Exemplary Petrol Engine Reliability — The petrol engines (especially the 1.2 TCe 100 hp and 1.2 16v 75 hp) are renowned for their robustness and longevity, offering daily peace of mind.

practicality

  • Optimized Modularity and Space — With its sliding and folding rear bench seat, and a generous boot volume in the Grand Modus version (up to 410 liters), it adapts to all your needs.

comfort

  • Refined Suspension Comfort and Soundproofing — Enjoy significant driving comfort thanks to well-calibrated suspensions and decent soundproofing for its category, making journeys pleasant.

economy

  • Very Affordable Running Costs — The Renault Modus Phase 2 petrol stands out for its particularly low maintenance and insurance costs, making it an economical and smart choice.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Citroën C3 Picasso (direct competitor, very spacious), Opel Meriva A/B, Honda Jazz (benchmark for reliability and modularity), 1st generation Nissan Note (Modus' technical cousin), Peugeot 1007 (sliding doors but heavy and less reliable).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Fit/Jazz, Toyota Ractis.
  • Americas: Segment non-existent at the time.

Verdict

The Renault Modus Phase 2 (and particularly the Grand Modus) is an excellent used car purchase, often undervalued due to its divisive design. With a petrol engine, it is an extremely reliable vehicle. The 1.2 TCe 100 hp (D4Ft) engine is the gem of the range: it offers the pleasure of a larger engine while maintaining flawless reliability (not to be confused with the 1.2 TCe H5F of subsequent generations). The 1.2 16v 75 hp is perfect for the city but will lack power on the motorway. Avoid the 1.6 16v versions equipped with the 4-speed automatic gearbox, a source of costly breakdowns. Diesels are decent but only make sense if you drive a lot outside urban areas.
