Land Rover Range Rover Velar

I facelift (L560) · 2021 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 66/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Range Rover Velar (L560) received a major technical and interior facelift for the 2021 model year. While the exterior changes little (lighting signature, bumpers), the real revolution is under the hood and in the cabin. This facelift introduces the **Pivi Pro** infotainment system (much faster and more reliable than the old Touch Pro Duo) and a complete overhaul of the engines with the massive arrival of hybridization. The star of this update is the **P400e (PHEV)** plug-in hybrid version, accompanied by new 48V mild-hybrid (MHEV) inline 6-cylinder engines, replacing the old V6s. Positioned between the Evoque and the Range Rover Sport, the Velar remains one of the most elegant SUVs on the market, but its high weight penalizes the efficiency of its hybrid engines once the battery is empty.

Strengths 6

  • Timeless design and sloping roofline that remains spectacular
  • Pivi Pro multimedia system is finally responsive, modern, and ergonomic
  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with the air suspension)
  • Very high-level cabin soundproofing
  • Real off-road capabilities (Terrain Response 2) despite its luxury SUV appearance
  • Highly attractive taxation for the P400e (PHEV) version for businesses

Weaknesses 6

  • Excessive weight (often > 2 tonnes), penalizing agility and fuel consumption
  • Disappointing real-world electric range of the P400e in winter (often < 35 km)
  • Overall electronic reliability is still temperamental (sensors, driving aids)
  • Rear seat space and trunk volume (reduced on the PHEV) are average for its size
  • Maintenance costs and spare parts prices in the Land Rover network
  • Rapid depreciation on the used market

Check before buying

  • Complete software update history (crucial for the hybrid system and Pivi Pro)
  • Smooth operation of the air suspension (raising/lowering without excessive compressor noise)
  • Flush door handles: check that they all deploy and retract smoothly
  • Body panel alignment and rattling noises in the cabin on rough roads
  • On PHEV versions: test the thermal/electric transition while driving (should not cause violent jerks)
  • Oil level on diesel engines (risk of dilution)

Verdict

The facelifted Range Rover Velar (2021+) corrects the biggest flaw of the first phase thanks to the excellent Pivi Pro multimedia system. The **P400e** plug-in hybrid version is attractive on paper and essential to escape ecological penalties in Europe, but it suffers from its pachydermic weight which limits its real-world electric range and burdens fuel consumption when the battery is empty. The 6-cylinder engines (P400, D300) are mechanically more noble and better suited to the vehicle's size. Although the mechanical reliability of the Ingenium engines has improved since 2019, the Velar remains prone to electronic temperaments (48V network, sensors, software bugs) and is the subject of numerous recalls. A purchase driven by passion that imperatively requires an **extended manufacturer warranty** to drive with peace of mind.
