Toyota Auris

I (E150) · 2006 – 2012

BUY Reliability 86/100 — excellent

Summary

The **first-generation Toyota Auris (E150)** had the difficult task of succeeding the legendary Corolla in the European market (although it retained the Corolla name in other markets like Australia). Designed on the MC platform, it stands out with a tall architecture offering excellent interior space and a flat rear floor. While its exterior design and road handling were often judged as placid, it shines with a very high level of overall reliability, particularly in gasoline and hybrid powertrains. The **2010 facelift** corrected the model's main flaw (low-end interior plastics) and introduced the **HSD (Hybrid)** version, adopting the proven mechanics of the Prius 3. It is a rational, economical, and extremely reassuring used car choice, provided certain diesel engines and the M-MT automated manual gearbox are avoided.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional reliability of gasoline (except 1.33) and hybrid engines
  • Generous interior space and completely flat rear floor
  • Reduced maintenance cost (especially in HSD: no clutch, no belt, very durable brakes)
  • Ergonomics of controls (raised gear lever)
  • Correct sound insulation on gasoline versions

Weaknesses 5

  • Very disappointing interior plastic quality before the 2010 facelift (easy to scratch)
  • Slow, jerky, and unreliable M-MT (MultiMode) automated manual gearbox
  • Clumsy road handling, electric power steering lacking feel
  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 and 2.2 D-4D diesel engines
  • Reduced boot volume on the Hybrid version (battery under the floor)

Check before buying

  • On 1.33 VVT-i: Demand rigorous monitoring and question the seller about oil top-ups between oil changes.
  • On all versions: Condition of the steering column (knocking during low-speed turns).
  • On manual gearbox versions: Whistling of gearbox bearings (especially on 1.4 and 1.6).
  • On HSD: Demand a Toyota 'Hybrid Health Check' certificate less than one year old to guarantee the battery.
  • Verify that all major recalls (accelerator pedal, airbags) have been carried out at a dealership.

Official recalls

  • **Accelerator Pedal (2010)**: The famous 'PedalGate'. Risk of the accelerator pedal getting stuck in the depressed position. Installation of a reinforcement shim. - **Takata Airbags (2015-2018)**: Replacement of the passenger and/or driver airbag inflator due to a risk of abnormal explosion. - **Power Window Switch (2012 & 2015)**: Risk of short circuit and fire initiation in the driver's side control panel. Lubrication or replacement. - **Steering Column (2012)**: Replacement of the intermediate steering shaft which could exhibit excessive play. - **Hybrid Inverter (2014)**: Software update of the inverter ECU on HSD models to prevent transistor overheating.

Alternatives

- **Europe** : Honda Civic VIII (excellent gasoline reliability, polarizing design), Volkswagen Golf VI (superior finish, but TSI/TDI engines from that era very problematic), Ford Focus II/III (excellent chassis), Mazda 3 (very reliable in gasoline). - **Japan / Asia** : Nissan Tiida, Honda Fit/Jazz, Subaru Impreza Hatchback. - **North America** : Model not sold under this name (Toyota Matrix or Corolla sedan as direct alternatives).

Verdict

The Toyota Auris I is the archetype of a rational purchase. If you are looking for a gasoline compact, it is one of the most reliable cars of its generation. The 1.4 and 1.6 VVT-i engines are benchmarks for longevity, capable of exceeding 300,000 km with basic maintenance. The Hybrid (HSD) version, which appeared late in its career, is the absolute best buy for urban and suburban use. However, absolutely avoid the 2.0 and 2.2 D-4D diesel versions, as well as the M-MT automated manual gearbox, which ruin the model's excellent reputation. Opt for a facelifted model (after 2010) to benefit from a significantly improved interior finish.
