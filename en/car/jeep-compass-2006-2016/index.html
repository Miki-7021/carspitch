Summary

The first-generation Jeep Compass (code name MK49) marks Jeep's entry into the highly competitive compact SUV (crossover) segment. Based on the GS platform co-developed by Chrysler and Mitsubishi (shared with the Dodge Caliber and Jeep Patriot), it had a difficult start due to a controversial design and a very basic interior finish.

The 2011 Turning Point (Facelift): Jeep made a major facelift in 2011, transforming the front fascia to make it look like the Grand Cherokee, significantly improving the quality of interior plastics, and revising the suspension settings. In 2014, a 6-speed automatic transmission replaced the much-criticized CVT transmission on certain gasoline engines.

Overall Reliability Rating: Mediocre on the early phases (2006-2010) due to electrical problems (TIPM), fragile running gear, and a temperamental CVT transmission. Reliability becomes acceptable from 2011 onwards, although the model remains below the Japanese standards of the same era.

Strengths 5

  • Very successful look from 2011 onwards (mini Grand Cherokee)
  • Decent off-road capabilities (especially with the Freedom Drive II package in North America)
  • Satisfactory roominess and modularity for its size
  • High-performance and torquey 2.2 CRD engine (Mercedes)
  • Often very attractive used prices (significant depreciation)

Weaknesses 6

  • Unworthy interior finish and soundproofing before 2011
  • Unpleasant (moped effect) and fragile Jatco CVT transmission
  • Hit-or-miss electrical reliability (TIPM module)
  • Premature wear of front axle components (ball joints, control arms)
  • High fuel consumption of gasoline engines
  • Disappointing trunk volume (328 liters) for the category

Check before buying

  • TIPM module: Erratic electrical behaviors (horn sounds on its own, wipers activate, engine stalls). Very expensive replacement.
  • Front axle: Listen for clunking noises on rough roads or speed bumps (sign of worn ball joints or control arm bushings).
  • CVT transmission: Demand fluid change invoices (every 60,000 km max). Run away if the transmission whines or jerks.
  • Water leaks: Check for moisture under the floor mats and in the headliner (clogged sunroof drains or defective roof rack seals).
  • FAP and EGR (Diesels): Check for the absence of a check engine light and jerking during acceleration, signs of clogging.

Official recalls

- 2016 (Passive safety): Recall to replace the Occupant Restraint Controller (ORC) module which could short-circuit and prevent deployment in the event of an accident.,- 2013 (Airbags): Reprogramming of the airbag control module following inadvertent deployments or non-deployments.,- 2012 (Fuel): Replacement of the fuel tank rollover valve which could leak in the event of an accident.,- 2008 (Steering): Replacement of an improperly secured power steering hose that could rub and leak.,- 2007 (Electronics): Update of the ABS module and TIPM to correct power losses or engine stalls.

What sets it apart 7

Design

  • Iconic and Modern SUV Styling — The Jeep Compass, especially after its 2011 facelift, boasts a bold and refined design, inspired by the Grand Cherokee, giving it a distinctive presence on the road.

Performance

  • Authentic Off-Road Capabilities — Inheriting Jeep's DNA, the Compass is designed to take you beyond the beaten path, offering off-road aptitudes that expand your adventure horizons.
  • 2.2 CRD Diesel Engine: Power and Smoothness — The Mercedes-Benz-sourced 2.2 CRD engine stands out for its high torque and responsiveness, offering dynamic and enjoyable driving, ideal for long journeys.

Practicality

  • Spacious and Functional Interior — The Compass's cabin is designed for the comfort of all occupants and ease of transport, with generous space and modularity that adapts to your needs.

Economy

  • A Smart Choice in the Used Market — Acquire a unique SUV with multiple advantages at a very attractive price on the second-hand market, making the Compass a wise and economical choice.

Comfort

  • Refined Driving Experience — Facelifted versions benefit from optimized suspension tuning and, for some gasoline engines after 2014, a 6-speed automatic transmission, for a smoother and more serene drive.

Reliability

  • Proven Reliability of Key Powertrains — Gasoline engines with manual transmission or the 6-speed automatic transmission (post-2014), as well as the 2.2 CRD engine, are recognized for their robustness and longevity.

Alternatives

- Europe: Volkswagen Tiguan (better finished, more frugal TDI engines), Nissan Qashqai (segment benchmark, more reliable in gasoline), Ford Kuga (better road handling)., - North America: Honda CR-V (untouchable reliability, more spacious), Toyota RAV4 (excellent resale value), Subaru Forester (best all-wheel drive)., - Asia / Emerging: Hyundai Tucson / ix35, Kia Sportage (better quality/price/equipment ratio).

Verdict

The first-generation Jeep Compass is a vehicle to approach with great caution. Phase 1 models (2006-2010) accumulate low-end plastics, mediocre soundproofing, and reliability issues (running gear, TIPM electronics, clogged VW diesels).

Verdict: If you fall for its look, imperatively target a facelifted model (post-2011). In Europe, the 2.2 CRD (Mercedes origin) with a manual transmission is the most coherent choice, provided the injectors have been made reliable. In gasoline, run away from the CVT transmission and look for the rare versions equipped with the 6-speed automatic transmission (from 2014 onwards).
