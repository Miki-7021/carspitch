Summary

The fourth-generation Nissan X-Trail (code name T33), also sold as the Nissan Rogue in North America (since 2020/2021), marks a spectacular move upmarket for the Japanese manufacturer. Based on the Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi Alliance's CMF-C/D platform, it stands out with a highly refined interior, the option of 7 seats, and the introduction of e-Power technology in Europe. Unlike traditional hybrids, the e-Power system uses the combustion engine (a 1.5L 3-cylinder with variable compression) solely as a generator to power the battery and the electric motors, which alone drive the wheels. Although very accomplished in terms of drivability, this model raises questions regarding the long-term reliability of its highly complex variable compression combustion engine (VC-Turbo), particularly in its non-hybrid versions sold outside Europe.

Strengths 5

  • Significantly improved interior finish (soft-touch materials, semi-aniline leather on high trims)
  • Drivability of the e-Power system (sensation of driving an electric vehicle)
  • Efficiency of the e-4ORCE 4-wheel drive system (instant responsiveness of the electric motors)
  • Rear passenger space (row 2) and doors opening to nearly 90 degrees
  • Very refined soundproofing (double glazing on certain trims)

Weaknesses 5

  • Major uncertainty regarding the mechanical reliability of the 1.5 VC-Turbo block (connecting rod assembly)
  • Disappointing highway fuel consumption for the e-Power system (the combustion engine revs high to power the battery)
  • Third row of seats (7-seater) purely symbolic and reserved for very young children
  • Reduced trunk volume on e-Power 4WD and 7-seater versions
  • High weight (nearly 2 tons for the e-4ORCE) which impacts agility

Check before buying

  • Metallic noises or knocking from the lower engine on the 1.5 VC-Turbo (warning sign of connecting rod bearing failure)
  • Clear oil change history (crucial for the variable compression system)
  • Smooth operation of the combustion/electric transition on e-Power models
  • Tire wear (the immediate torque of the electric motors and the weight wear out tires quickly on the e-4ORCE)
  • Body panel alignment (some assembly disparities in early production)

Verdict

The Nissan X-Trail T33 is an extremely accomplished family SUV in terms of comfort, technology, and perceived quality. However, the choice of engine is absolutely crucial. **We strongly advise against purchasing the direct-drive 1.5 VC-Turbo versions (163 hp MHEV in Europe, or 201 hp in North America)** due to the complexity of the variable compression and the ongoing US federal investigation regarding catastrophic engine failures (connecting rod assembly). On the other hand, **the e-Power versions (204 and 213 hp) are highly recommended**: since the combustion engine only serves as a generator at a stabilized speed, it is spared from destructive mechanical stress. Therefore, exclusively favor the e-Power for a worry-free purchase.
