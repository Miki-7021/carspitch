Summary

The 5th generation Nissan Altima (L33) is a family sedan (D-segment / Midsize) primarily designed for the North American market, the Middle East, and parts of Asia. Recognized for its very spacious interior, ride comfort (notably thanks to the NASA-inspired *Zero Gravity* seats), and excellent highway fuel efficiency, it was a best-seller. However, its reputation was heavily tarnished by the disastrous reliability of its continuously variable transmission (Jatco CVT), which is the major black mark of this generation. A facelift occurred in 2016, bringing a refreshed design (V-Motion grille) and some suspension and steering adjustments.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional comfort of the front seats (Zero Gravity technology)
  • Very low fuel consumption on the highway (2.5L QR25DE)
  • Spacious and well-soundproofed interior
  • High-performing and very pleasant V6 engine (3.5L)
  • Safe and predictable road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of the Jatco CVT gearbox (JF016E/JF017E)
  • Oil dilution by gasoline on early 2.5L engines (2013-2015)
  • Recurring hood latch problems (multiple recalls)
  • Dated and slow infotainment system
  • Quality of some interior plastics below the competition (Camry/Accord)

Check before buying

  • Complete and documented history of CVT gearbox oil changes (crucial)
  • CVT behavior during the test drive: absence of excessive slipping, judder, or whining
  • Engine oil level and smell on the 2.5L (checking for gasoline smell)
  • Correct operation of the passenger detection system (ODS Airbag)
  • Condition of the hood lock and latch (corrosion, binding)

Official recalls

The L33 generation has been the subject of numerous recalls, mainly in North America (NHTSA):

  • Hood latch (2014, 2015, 2016, 2020): Risk of unexpected hood opening while driving due to corrosion or binding of the secondary latch (affects 2013-2018 models).
  • Airbag / ODS sensors (2013, 2014, 2015, 2016): Occupant Classification System (ODS) defect that could prevent the passenger airbag from deploying in a crash (2013-2017 models).
  • Rear door cable (2017, 2018): Improper cable routing that could cause the door to open when the window is lowered (2015-2017 models).
  • Steering and Suspension (2011, 2012): Transverse link and steering rack bolts improperly tightened at the factory (2011-2013 models).
  • Brakes (2015): Front brake caliper bolts improperly tightened (2015 models).
  • Backup camera (2019): Backup camera display issue (2018-2019 models).

Alternatives

The Altima competes in a highly competitive segment: * North America / Middle East: Toyota Camry (XV50 - absolute benchmark for reliability), Honda Accord (9th generation - excellent dynamics), Mazda 6 (GJ - design and driving), Ford Fusion, Hyundai Sonata. * Asia / Russia: Toyota Camry, Kia Optima, Skoda Superb.

Verdict

The Nissan Altima L33 is a sedan full of paradoxes. On one hand, it offers top-flight comfort, excellent roominess, and inherently solid engines (QR25DE and VQ35DE). On the other, it is completely dragged down by its Jatco CVT transmission, whose reliability is rated below average by Consumer Reports and the ADAC. CVT failures are frequent, often occur around 160,000 km, and cost between $3500 and $5000 to repair.

Buying verdict: Avoid 2013-2015 models (which combine CVT problems and oil dilution). For 2016-2018 models, purchase is only conceivable if the CVT gearbox has already been recently replaced with a supporting invoice, or if the vehicle has very low mileage with a flawless maintenance history. When in doubt, favor a Toyota Camry or a Honda Accord from the same era.
