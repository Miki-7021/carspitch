Summary

The third-generation Volkswagen Touareg (internal code CR7), launched in 2018, marks a significant evolution for the brand's high-end SUV. Based on the Volkswagen Group's MLB Evo platform (shared with the Audi Q7, Porsche Cayenne, Bentley Bentayga, and Lamborghini Urus), it positions itself as a serious competitor to German premium SUVs. Larger, more technological, and more luxurious than its predecessors, it emphasizes comfort, connectivity, and driving aids. The Touareg III received a facelift in 2023, bringing aesthetic changes (notably the IQ.Light HD Matrix headlights) and technological updates (software updates, Innovision Cockpit improvements). Overall, it is a well-built and refined vehicle, but its technological complexity and sophisticated powertrains can lead to high maintenance costs. General reliability is good, but there are points of concern, particularly regarding the anti-pollution systems of diesel engines and the complexity of hybrid versions.

Strengths 7

  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with air suspension)
  • High-end finish quality and interior materials
  • Advanced onboard technology (responsive and customizable Innovision Cockpit)
  • Generous cabin space and boot volume
  • Towing and off-road capabilities (for a luxury SUV)
  • Powerful and refined engines
  • Excellent sound insulation

Weaknesses 6

  • High purchase price and rapid depreciation on early models
  • High maintenance and spare parts costs, especially for complex versions
  • Technological complexity that can lead to electronic bugs (especially on early model years)
  • High fuel consumption for petrol and V8 TDI versions in real-world use
  • Significant weight impacting agility and wear of consumables (tires, brakes)
  • Reliability of AdBlue/EGR/DPF systems on diesels

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (particularly for diesels and hybrids, ideally at a Volkswagen dealership)
  • Functioning of the air suspension (height, abnormal noises, air leaks, reaction time)
  • Condition and functioning of the Innovision Cockpit (screens, responsiveness, absence of dead pixels or software bugs)
  • Absence of illuminated engine, AdBlue, or braking fault warning lights
  • Coolant level and color (for V6 TSI, sign of water pump leak)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (rapid wear due to weight and power)
  • Check if all manufacturer recalls have been performed (request service book or check at dealership)
  • For PHEVs: battery health and charging system operation.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Touareg III is an excellent SUV, offering comfort and cutting-edge technology. Its general reliability is good, but its technical complexity, particularly on diesel and plug-in hybrid versions, requires rigorous maintenance and can lead to high repair costs in case of problems. The V6 TSI petrol versions are a good compromise for those seeking power and refinement without the constraints of modern diesels, although they are not without points of concern (water pump). Plug-in hybrid versions are interesting for tax advantages and electric urban driving, but their complexity and the potential cost of battery replacement should be considered.

Buying advice: Prioritize models with a complete and clear service history, ideally at a Volkswagen dealership. Verify that all manufacturer recalls have been performed. An extended warranty can be a wise investment. For predominantly road use, the V6 TDI remains relevant despite its anti-pollution systems. For more versatile use or if you fear diesel problems, the V6 TSI is an excellent choice. The eHybrid is worth considering if you have the ability to charge regularly and your daily use is compatible with electric range. Given the recurring recalls and potential costs, price negotiation is highly recommended.
