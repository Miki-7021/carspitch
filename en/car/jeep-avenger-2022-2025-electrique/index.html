Jeep Avenger

Summary

The **Jeep Avenger (Generation I)** marks a historic turning point for the American manufacturer, becoming its very first 100% electric model. Voted “Car of the Year 2023” in Europe, this B-segment urban SUV (4.08 m) is based on the Stellantis group's e-CMP2 platform (shared with the Peugeot e-2008, Fiat 600e, and Alfa Romeo Junior). Manufactured in Tychy, Poland, it stands out with a very successful cubic design, integrating Jeep's stylistic DNA (7-slot grille, trapezoidal wheel arches) while offering ideal compactness for the city. Although initially conceived as a purely electric vehicle (BEV) for Western Europe, Jeep quickly introduced internal combustion (1.2 PureTech) and mild-hybrid (e-Hybrid) powertrains to meet demand in certain markets (Italy, Spain, then France). The electric version, the focus of this report, benefits from the new Stellantis powertrain (156 hp Emotors M3 motor and 54 kWh NMC battery), offering excellent efficiency and correcting the flaws of the group's previous generation of electric motors.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable efficiency of the electric version (real-world consumption often under 15 kWh/100 km in mixed use)
  • Very successful exterior design, charismatic and true to the Jeep spirit
  • Ultra-compact size (4.08 m) perfect for urban parking
  • Physical ergonomics preserved (real buttons for air conditioning)
  • Well-calibrated suspension comfort, good compromise between dynamism/flexibility
  • New e-Hybrid engine finally abandons the problematic wet belt

Weaknesses 6

  • Very restricted rear passenger space (limited legroom for adults)
  • Ubiquitous hard interior plastics, prone to scratches
  • Average boot volume (355 liters on the electric version)
  • Infotainment system sometimes slow and prone to connection bugs (CarPlay/Android Auto)
  • Presence of the old 1.2 PureTech with wet belt on the entry-level manual version
  • DC fast charging speed limited to 100 kW (average for the category)

Check before buying

  • Electric: Test locking and unlocking of the charging cable on a public charging station (cases of stuck cables reported).
  • Electric: Check the system software version (early 2023 versions suffered from intermittent screen restarts).
  • Electric: Ensure the heat pump (standard on most trims) operates correctly without abnormal metallic noise.
  • 1.2 Manual ICE: Demand strict annual service history. Check belt condition via the oil filler cap.
  • General: Body panel alignment and interior trim noises on degraded roads (hard plastics).

Verdict

The Jeep Avenger is an excellent proposition in the urban electric SUV market. The **100% electric (156 hp)** version is by far the most recommendable: it benefits from the new Stellantis powertrain, which proves to be very efficient, smooth, and, so far, free of major mechanical defects. The only drawbacks concern early software bugs, often corrected by updates. If you are looking for an internal combustion alternative, absolutely prioritize the **1.2 e-Hybrid 100 hp** version (automatic gearbox), which uses a much more reliable chain timing system. **AVOID the 1.2 Turbo 100 hp gasoline version with a manual gearbox**, which retains the problematic wet belt of the Stellantis group. In summary: a love-at-first-sight purchase for its design, validated by reason if you choose the electric or mild-hybrid.
