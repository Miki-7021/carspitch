Summary

The 14th generation Ford F-150 (codename P702) is a profound evolution of the previous model. It retains the military-grade aluminum body but introduces an all-new electronic architecture (SYNC 4), OTA update capabilities, and electrified powertrains (PowerBoost hybrid and 100% electric Lightning). Note on the Diesel: The 3.0L Power Stroke V6, introduced on the previous generation, only survived for the 2021 model year on this 14th generation before being removed from the catalog due to low sales and the arrival of the hybrid V6. According to J.D. Power data (VDS 2023), the F-150 ranks average for the segment (~185 PP100), lagging behind the Ram 1500 and the Chevrolet Silverado/GMC Sierra. ADAC also notes a below-average start to its career due to wiring and early production issues on the hybrid versions.

Strengths 5

  • Rust-proof aluminum body (excellent for cold/coastal regions)
  • 5.0L Coyote V8 engine identified as the most reliable option (ADAC)
  • Revolutionary Pro Power Onboard system (electric generator) on hybrid versions
  • Class-leading towing and payload capacities
  • Interior ergonomics and highly responsive SYNC 4 system

Weaknesses 5

  • Below-average initial reliability (ADAC) due to electronic and hybrid issues
  • J.D. Power ranking lagging behind the Ram 1500 and GM competitors
  • 3.0L Diesel engine discontinued prematurely (long-term availability of specific parts uncertain)
  • 10-speed automatic gearbox (10R80) sometimes rough and hesitant
  • Very high number of manufacturer recalls (NHTSA) on the first years of production (2021-2022)

Check before buying

  • Behavior of the 10R80 gearbox (jerking when shifting into reverse or from 3rd to 4th)
  • On the 3.0L Diesel: fuel filter replacement history and absence of metal shavings in the system
  • On the EcoBoosts: listen carefully to the engine during a cold start (rattling noise = failed cam phasers)
  • On models with the Max Tow package: check the rear axle hub bolt (major recall for risk of breakage)
  • Operation of the center screen and digital dashboard (frequent bugs requiring OTA or module updates)

Official recalls

The 14th generation has been the subject of numerous recalls (NHTSA). Here are the most critical:

  • 2.7L / 3.0L EcoBoost Engine (August 2024): Risk of intake valve breakage leading to catastrophic engine failure (2021-2022 models).
  • 3.5L GTDI Engine (Dec 2024 / March 2025): Misaligned engine oil pan plug that can cause a rapid oil leak and engine seizure.
  • Transmission / Axle (August 2025): On models equipped with the *Trailer Tow Max Duty* package (9.75-inch axle), the rear hub bolt can break, resulting in a loss of traction or a risk of a wheel detaching while driving.
  • Gearbox (June 2026 / March 2025): The parking pawl can engage while driving. Machining defects in the transmission valve body causing loss of drive.
  • Braking (Dec 2025 / July 2025): Malfunction of the electronic brake boost (EBB) module increasing braking distance.
  • Electronics / Wiring (June 2025): Engine wiring harness rubbing against a coolant hose, which can disconnect the PCM module and stall the engine while driving.
  • Steering (Jan 2025 / May 2025): Incorrect assembly of the steering rack or detachment of the intermediate shaft, resulting in a loss of control.
  • Trailer Brake Controller (Feb 2026): Software failure preventing trailer braking.

Alternatives

  • North America: Ram 1500 (better ride comfort, more luxurious interior, 3.0L EcoDiesel engine available until 2023), Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500 (their 3.0L Duramax diesel engine is vastly superior and more reliable than Ford's Power Stroke), Toyota Tundra (new hybrid generation, higher perceived reliability).
  • Europe (Import): Ram 1500 (very popular as an LPG import), Volkswagen Amarok V6 TDI (for diesel use more suited to the European market, incidentally based on the Ford Ranger chassis).

Verdict

The 14th generation Ford F-150 remains the undisputed king of sales, but its launch was marred by an avalanche of recalls and electronic issues, confirmed by ADAC and J.D. Power. Regarding the 3.0L Power Stroke Diesel engine: it is a powertrain to avoid or to buy with full knowledge of the facts. Discontinued after only one year on this generation, it suffers from the fragility of its CP4 injection pump and does not offer the reliability of General Motors' 3.0L Duramax. If you are looking for fuel economy, the PowerBoost (Hybrid) is Ford's intended alternative, although it requires a strict verification of its electrical history. The sensible choice and absolute reliability remains the 5.0L Coyote V8, which stands the test of time with a robustness that turbocharged and electrified powertrains still struggle to match.
