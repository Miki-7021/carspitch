Summary

The **Renault Grand Modus** (generation I, phase 2, project code JP0) was introduced in early 2008 with the restyling of the classic Modus. Designed to correct its predecessor's main flaw (a boot that was too small), it was extended by 16 cm to reach 4.03 m. Based on the excellent Clio III and Nissan Note platform, it offers very sound road handling, remarkable suspension comfort, and exemplary modularity thanks to its sliding rear bench seat. Although it was overshadowed by the trend for small SUVs (like the Captur which would replace it), the Grand Modus remains an extremely rational, economical, and reliable used car choice, particularly in its post-2010 versions.

Strengths 6

  • Spaciousness and boot volume (up to 410 liters) relative to its size (4.03 m)
  • Sliding, folding, and split-folding rear bench seat (very practical)
  • Overall engine reliability (the years 2008-2012 correspond to the maturity of Renault engines)
  • Safe road handling and suspension comfort (Clio III platform)
  • Maintenance, insurance costs, and spare parts availability
  • Raised driving position appreciated in the city

Weaknesses 5

  • Replacement of front headlight bulbs is still complex (although improved compared to Modus phase 1, it often requires very thin hands or bumper removal)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (easy scratches, peeling steering wheel and gear knob)
  • 4-speed automatic gearbox (on 1.6 16v) completely outdated and fuel-thirsty
  • Sensitivity to crosswinds due to body height

Check before buying

  • **Windshield scuttle:** Water drains get clogged with leaves. Water overflows and can drown the wiper motor or seep into the cabin. Clean preventively.
  • **Electric power steering:** Check for hard spots, noises, or warning lights. A faulty steering column is very expensive to replace.
  • **Running gear:** Wear of anti-roll bar bushes, steering ball joints, and shock absorber top mounts (clunking over bumps).
  • **Timing belt history:** On all engines (petrol and diesel), it's a belt. Demand the replacement invoice (generally 5 to 6 years).
  • **Air conditioning:** Check its proper functioning (the condenser is exposed to gravel at the front).

Official recalls

  • - **February 2008:** Reprogramming of the electric power steering control unit (risk of loss of assistance).
  • - **September 2009:** Inspection and possible replacement of front seat belts (locking defect).
  • - **November 2011:** On 1.2 (petrol) engine, risk of premature wear of valve collets which can lead to engine failure (concerns a small series produced in 2010).

What sets it apart 12

Practicality

  • Optimized Modularity and Space — Despite its compact size of 4.03 m, the Grand Modus offers remarkable spaciousness and a generous boot volume of up to 410 litres. Its sliding, folding, and split rear bench allows you to adapt the space to all your needs.

Reliability

  • Proven Mechanical Reliability — This generation of the Grand Modus (2008-2012) benefits from very solid mechanical reliability, particularly thanks to the maturity of the 1.5 dCi diesel engines, renowned for their robustness and longevity.

Economy

  • Controlled Running Costs — Enjoy an economical vehicle to run. The 1.5 dCi diesel engines boast very low fuel consumption, often less than 5L/100km, and maintenance and insurance costs are particularly attractive.

Comfort

  • Driving Comfort and Visibility — Thanks to its platform derived from the Clio III, the Grand Modus offers remarkable suspension comfort. Its raised driving position ensures excellent visibility, making city driving particularly pleasant and safe.

Safety

  • Serene Road Handling — Based on the excellent Clio III platform, the Grand Modus guarantees very sound and safe road handling. It offers stable and predictable road holding for confident driving.

Performance

  • Versatile Diesel Engines — The 1.5 dCi diesel engines, especially the 85 and 90 hp versions, offer an excellent compromise between performance and fuel efficiency. They provide enough torque for versatile driving, whether in the city or on the open road.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Opel Meriva A/B (suicide doors on B), Nissan Note I (technical cousin, same platform and dCi engines), Citroën C3 Picasso (more cubic design, very spacious), Honda Jazz (unbeatable petrol reliability, magical modularity), Skoda Roomster. - **Other markets:** The B-segment mini-MPV segment was primarily European. In Japan, equivalents like the Toyota Ractis or Honda Fit (Jazz) could be found.

Verdict

The Renault Grand Modus is an excellent used car choice for small budgets looking for an ultra-practical vehicle, compact on the outside but spacious on the inside. Contrary to its reputation sometimes tarnished by the catastrophic beginnings of dCi engines in the early 2000s, this generation (2008-2012) benefits from **very solid mechanical reliability**.

**For Diesel:** The **1.5 dCi 85 hp** (without DPF) or **90 hp** (with DPF) is the best buy. It offers enough torque to move a loaded vehicle while consuming less than 5L/100km. Avoid diesels if you only drive in the city (risk of EGR/DPF clogging). **For Petrol:** The **1.2 TCe 100 hp** is the best alternative, offering superior driving pleasure to the sluggish 1.2 16v 75 hp.

The purchase is highly recommended provided you check the timing belt history and the absence of water ingress in the windshield scuttle area.
