Opel Meriva

B (2010–2017) · 2010–2017

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The Opel Meriva B compact MPV, produced from 2010 to 2017, is the second generation of the model. It is primarily distinguished by its 'FlexDoors' opposing door system, a unique feature in its category, facilitating access to the cabin. Based on the Opel Corsa D and Astra J platform, it offers a modular interior and good cargo volume. Positioned as a practical and versatile family vehicle, it was praised for its comfort and maneuverability. However, its reliability has been mixed, with recurring weaknesses on certain engines and electronic issues. The Meriva B benefited from a slight facelift in late 2013, notably introducing new, more modern and efficient 1.6 CDTI diesel engines.

Strengths 5

  • Cabin modularity and unique FlexDoors system
  • Suspension comfort and good sound insulation (especially 1.6 CDTI)
  • Good rear passenger space
  • Maneuverability in the city despite its size
  • Pleasant and relatively efficient 1.6 CDTI and 1.4 Turbo petrol engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Mixed reliability on certain engines (1.3 CDTI, 1.7 CDTI, 1.4 petrol chain)
  • Recurring electronic problems (sensors, modules, multimedia system)
  • Limited rear visibility
  • Sometimes high fuel consumption on 1.4 Turbo petrol engines in dynamic driving
  • Aging of some interior plastics

Check before buying

  • Abnormal engine noises (timing chain on 1.4 petrol, knocking on 1.7 CDTI)
  • DPF and EGR valve operation (warning lights on, loss of power)
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel and clutch on diesels
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (windows, air conditioning, multimedia)
  • Absence of oil or coolant leaks
  • Complete service history and invoices for major repairs

Verdict

The Meriva B is a compact MPV that appeals with its opposing door concept and modularity. However, its reliability is a point to consider carefully. The 1.7 CDTI diesel engines are to be avoided due to their recurring problems and lack of refinement. The 1.3 CDTI can be suitable for mixed use, provided rigorous maintenance and regular journeys. The **1.6 CDTI (from 2014 onwards)** is by far the best diesel choice, offering a good compromise between performance, fuel consumption, and reliability. On the petrol side, the 1.4 Turbo engines are pleasant but the timing chain remains a weak point. A clear service history and a meticulous inspection are crucial. If you find a well-maintained model, ideally a 1.6 CDTI or a 1.4 Turbo with the timing chain already replaced, the Meriva B can be a practical and comfortable family companion. Otherwise, negotiation is in order.
