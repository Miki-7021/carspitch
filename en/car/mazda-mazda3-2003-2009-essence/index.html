Mazda Mazda3

I (BK) · 2003 – 2009

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The first-generation Mazda3 (internal code BK) succeeded the 323, marking a true stylistic and dynamic revolution for the Hiroshima-based manufacturer. Based on the excellent C1 platform (developed jointly with Ford and Volvo, and shared with the Focus II and S40), it offers first-class road handling, precise steering, and driving pleasure superior to the category average.

While the gasoline engines (MZR family) are 100% Mazda-designed and boast exceptional reliability (chain timing), the model's overall record is heavily marred by an **extreme susceptibility to corrosion**, particularly on models produced before the 2006 facelift. The diesel engines, borrowed from PSA/Ford (1.6) or developed by Mazda (2.0), present a much darker reliability record. The mid-life facelift (2006) brought slight cosmetic improvements, better sound insulation, and a (slightly) revised anti-corrosion treatment, although the rust problem was never completely eradicated on this generation.

Weaknesses 5

  • CORROSION: the worst enemy of this model (rear fenders, underbody, doors)
  • Poor sound insulation (road and wind noise very present on the highway)
  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 diesel (PSA/Ford origin) if poorly maintained
  • Quality of some interior plastics (easy to scratch)
  • Gasoline engine consumption a bit high compared to the competition of the time

Check before buying

  • Rear wheel arches and sills: look for the slightest rust blister (often concealed during sales).
  • Underbody and subframes: check for perforating corrosion on a lift.
  • Air conditioning: the AC compressor is a known weakness (check that it engages and produces cold air).
  • Gasoline engines: check oil level (2.0 and 2.3 can consume oil at high mileage).
  • 1.6 diesel engine: lift the engine cover and look for traces of black tar around the injectors.
  • Rear suspension: wear of trailing arm bushings (clunking noises over bumps).

Verdict

The first-generation Mazda3 is a car with two faces. If you opt for a gasoline engine (1.6 or 2.0 MZR), you are buying one of the most reliable and enjoyable compact cars of its generation. However, diesel versions are to be avoided due to potentially ruinous maintenance costs. **However, the purchase decision should not be based on the engine, but on the bodywork.** Corrosion is an absolute scourge on the BK generation. A perfectly maintained gasoline model with rust on the rear fenders or underbody will not pass the technical inspection in the long run. Prioritize a post-2006 (facelifted) model, originally from the south of France (or regions without de-icing salt), and inspect it thoroughly.
