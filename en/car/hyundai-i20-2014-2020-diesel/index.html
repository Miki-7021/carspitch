Summary

The second-generation Hyundai i20 (code GB for Europe, IB for India under the name 'Elite i20') marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer in the B-segment. Designed at the European R&D center in Rüsselsheim, it stands out with record-breaking interior space for its category, significantly improved finish, and a more mature 'Fluidic Sculpture 2.0' design.

The 2018 facelift brought technological updates (touchscreen compatible with CarPlay/Android Auto), the 7DCT dual-clutch gearbox, but most notably marked the discontinuation of diesel engines (CRDi) in Europe due to WLTP standards.

Overall, it's an extremely rational, reassuring car, originally covered by a 5-year unlimited mileage warranty, which often ensured good follow-up within the official network during its first years of life.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent overall reliability, very few immobilizing breakdowns
  • Diesel engines (1.4 CRDi) with very durable timing chains
  • Rear passenger space and boot volume (326 liters)
  • Simple and intuitive control ergonomics
  • 5-year manufacturer's warranty which encouraged good initial maintenance

Weaknesses 5

  • Refinement of the 1.1 CRDi (vibrations and sound of the 3-cylinder diesel)
  • Hard interior plastics prone to scratches
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (road noise)
  • Electric power steering (MDPS) lacking feel and a center point
  • DPF very sensitive to clogging if the vehicle is only used in the city

Check before buying

  • Service history: crucial for diesels (oil quality for DPF/Turbo).
  • Clutch behavior: test a cold hill start (frequent juddering on 1.4 CRDi and 1.2 MPI).
  • Steering: turn from lock to lock when stationary. A 'clunk' indicates wear of the rubber coupling star in the steering column (a €5 part but costly labor).
  • Paint: check for clear coat peeling, especially on non-metallic colors (Polar White, Red).
  • Electronics: test the operation of the touchscreen, rearview camera, and power windows.

What sets it apart 7

Reliability

  • Robust and Durable Diesel Engine — The 1.4 CRDi engine, renowned for its exceptional reliability and chain distribution, ensures remarkable longevity and consistent performance, ideal for high-mileage drivers.
  • Exemplary Initial Maintenance — Thanks to its 5-year manufacturer's warranty, this vehicle benefited from rigorous monitoring and quality maintenance within the official network during its first years, ensuring peace of mind.

Practicality

  • Spacious Interior and Generous Boot Volume — Enjoy record interior space for its category, offering optimal comfort for rear passengers and a boot volume of 326 liters, perfect for daily needs and weekend trips.
  • Intuitive Ergonomics — The controls are simple and well-arranged, offering immediate familiarity and pleasant daily use, for a stress-free driving experience.

Safety

  • Proven Safety — The Hyundai i20 II achieved excellent safety test results, effectively protecting its occupants thanks to a robust design and high-performance safety equipment.
  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from a competent and adequate braking system for its class, offering short stopping distances and sufficient power for serene daily driving.

Comfort

  • Safe and Comfortable Handling — The i20 II offers predictable and comfortable handling, prioritizing ease of driving and on-board well-being, with balanced body control for all your journeys.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 208 I (1.6 BlueHDi), Renault Clio IV (1.5 dCi), Volkswagen Polo V/VI (1.4 TDI / 1.6 TDI), Ford Fiesta (1.5 TDCi), Toyota Yaris (Hybrid, urban diesel alternative).
  • India / Asia: Maruti Suzuki Baleno (1.3 DDiS), Tata Altroz, Honda Jazz.
  • Emerging markets: Kia Rio (technical cousin sharing the same engines and chassis).

Verdict

The Hyundai i20 (GB) is an extremely rational and reassuring used car choice. If your search focuses on diesel, the 1.4 CRDi 90 hp is by far the best choice: it's a robust, torquey engine with a reliable timing chain. It's perfect for high-mileage drivers. The 1.1 CRDi should be avoided due to its lack of refinement (vibrations).

However, be careful: only buy a diesel i20 if you regularly drive on roads or highways. In strictly urban use, the DPF and EGR valve will inevitably clog. If you drive less than 15,000 km/year or do a lot of city driving, opt for the indestructible 1.2 MPI petrol or the more modern 1.0 T-GDi.
