Citroën C4 Picasso

I · 2006 – 2013

AVOID Reliability 45/100 — faible

All Citroën models

Summary

The first-generation Citroën C4 Picasso (available in a short 5-seater version and a 7-seater Grand C4 Picasso) marked its era with its 'Visiospace' concept, offering an exceptional glass area, a panoramic windshield, and class-leading roominess. Its ride comfort is worthy of the brand's reputation. However, its career was heavily marred by major reliability problems, particularly on the gasoline engines resulting from the PSA/BMW partnership (Prince VTi and THP engines), the infamous 1.6 HDi 16v, the capricious BMP6 automated gearbox, and the rear air suspension on high-end trims. A mild facelift took place in late 2010 (LED daytime running lights, new chevrons).

Overall reliability rating: Poor (dragged down by the 1.6 THP/VTi and 1.6 HDi 110 engines).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional roominess and modularity (3 true independent rear seats)
  • Brightness and visibility (Visiospace panoramic windshield)
  • Suspension comfort (especially with the air suspension when it works)
  • Generous trunk volume
  • Careful soundproofing for its time

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.6 THP and VTi gasoline engines (Prince engines)
  • Reliability of the 1.6 HDi 110 (frequent turbo failures)
  • Drivability and reliability of the BMP6 automated gearbox (jerks, slowness, breakdowns)
  • Very fragile rear air suspension (Exclusive trim)
  • Aging of interior plastics (peeling, detachment of the dual glovebox)
  • High weight penalizing driving dynamics and fuel consumption

Check before buying

  • Clear timing chain history and oil consumption on the 1.6 VTi and THP.
  • Absence of injector leaks and turbo whistling on the 1.6 HDi 110.
  • Behavior of the BMP6 gearbox: it must not shudder on startup or give violent jerks.
  • Vehicle stance when stationary (Exclusive trim): if the rear is sagging, the air springs or the compressor are dead.
  • Operation of onboard electronics (electric parking brake, air conditioning, screens).

Verdict

Purchasing a first-generation Citroën C4 Picasso with a gasoline engine is strongly discouraged if you are targeting the 1.6 VTi or 1.6 THP engines (Prince engines), whose design is marred by major flaws (timing chain, oil consumption, turbo). If you absolutely must drive a gasoline car, look for the rare versions equipped with the older 1.8 16V or 2.0 16V blocks, which are much more robust despite higher fuel consumption. Generally speaking, this vehicle can only be considered with peace of mind with the 2.0 HDi diesel engine (136 or 150 hp) paired with a manual gearbox or a true automatic gearbox (BVA), avoiding the BMP6 automated gearbox and the rear air suspension.
