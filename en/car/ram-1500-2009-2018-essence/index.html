Summary

The **4th generation Ram 1500 (DS)** marked a major turning point in the full-size pickup segment. Initially launched under the Dodge brand in 2009 before Ram became an independent brand in 2010, it stood out by abandoning rear leaf springs in favor of a **coil spring suspension** (and later optional air suspension), offering unmatched ride comfort in the category.

It received a major facelift in **2013**, introducing the Pentastar V6, the excellent 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (TorqueFlite 8), a significantly higher quality interior, and the Uconnect multimedia system. Although replaced by the 5th generation (DT) in 2019, this model continued to be produced under the name *Ram 1500 Classic* due to its immense popularity and value for money. It is an extremely versatile pickup, but one that requires special attention regarding certain engines.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional ride comfort (rear coil spring suspension)
  • Excellent 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (TorqueFlite 8) (from 2013)
  • Interior finish quality and Uconnect system (on phase 2 models)
  • Performance and drivability of the 5.7L HEMI V8
  • Innovative storage spaces (optional RamBox)

Weaknesses 5

  • Costly camshaft/lifter issues on the HEMI V8
  • Disastrous reliability of the 3.0L EcoDiesel engine
  • Air suspension (optional) very fragile in cold weather
  • Payload slightly lower than leaf-sprung competitors
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (rear fenders, cab corners)

Check before buying

  • **The 'HEMI Tick':** Start the V8 cold. A ticking sound that disappears when warm often indicates broken exhaust studs. A persistent ticking indicates a camshaft/lifter issue (very costly repair).
  • **Air suspension (Active-Level):** Check that it raises and lowers without error. Moisture freezes in the system in winter, burning out the compressor or bursting the air bags.
  • **Corrosion:** Inspect the rear wheel arches, rocker panels, cab corners, and tailgate, particularly on models imported from cold regions.
  • **Electronics (TIPM):** On 2009-2012 models, the Totally Integrated Power Module (TIPM) can cause random failures (fuel pump, wipers, headlights).
  • **Gearbox:** The older 5- and 6-speed gearboxes (RFE) are robust but slow. The 8-speed ZF (rotary dial on the dashboard) is superior but requires an oil change around 100,000 km despite the manufacturer's claims.

Alternatives

- **Ford F-150 (2009-2014 / 2015-2020)**: The market leader. Aluminum body (from 2015), high-performance EcoBoost engines but requiring rigorous maintenance (timing chains). - **Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500**: Very reliable 5.3L V8s, but known issues with the cylinder deactivation system (AFM) and the 6- and 8-speed automatic gearboxes. - **Toyota Tundra (2007-2021)**: The undisputed king of reliability with its 5.7L V8 (3UR-FE). Gargantuan fuel consumption, dated interior, but indestructible. - **Nissan Titan**: Less common alternative, robust 5.6L V8, but significant depreciation and a less developed maintenance network in Europe.

Verdict

The 4th generation Ram 1500 is an excellent pickup, particularly appreciated for its ride comfort and refined interior (post-2013). **The ideal choice is a 2014-2018 model equipped with the 5.7L HEMI V8 and the 8-speed ZF gearbox**, provided it is purchased with full knowledge of the 'HEMI tick' (plan a budget for the exhaust studs or meticulously inspect the lifters). The 3.6L Pentastar V6 is a very rational and reliable alternative for light use. On the other hand, **avoid the 3.0L EcoDiesel** unless the recall history is crystal clear and you like risk, and avoid the air suspension if you live in a cold region.
