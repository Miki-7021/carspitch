ACHETER Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The fourth-generation Citroën C3 (2024+) marks a radical shift for the French brand. Abandoning the traditional rounded hatchback shape, it adopts a squared-off, crossover-like silhouette. Built on the cost-effective 'Smart Car' platform (a heavily modified derivative of the Stellantis CMP architecture), it is designed to aggressively undercut competitors in pricing.

Crucially for petrol buyers, this generation introduces the heavily revised 1.2 PureTech engine (Generation 3), which finally replaces the problematic oil-bathed timing belt with a robust **timing chain**. This resolves the biggest reliability dark cloud that hung over previous Stellantis models. While the electric ë-C3 grabs headlines, the petrol and mild-hybrid (MHEV) variants remain the volume sellers, offering excellent comfort (Advanced Comfort suspension) and unbeatable value, positioning it as a direct rival to the Dacia Sandero.

**Global Note:** 80/100 (Projected). The mechanical upgrades are highly promising, though long-term data is pending.

Strengths 5

  • Introduction of the timing chain on the 1.2 PureTech (finally solving the wet-belt issue)
  • Highly competitive pricing (undercutting most B-segment rivals)
  • Advanced Comfort suspension provides best-in-class ride quality
  • Spacious interior and good headroom due to the crossover silhouette
  • Robust LFP battery technology for the electric version

Weaknesses 5

  • Hard, scratchy plastics throughout the interior (cost-cutting is evident)
  • Basic 'Smartphone Station' instead of a real screen on entry-level 'You' trims
  • Lack of physical climate control buttons on higher trims
  • Early production software bugs (infotainment and ADAS)
  • No high-output engine options for highway cruisers

Check before buying

  • Infotainment and digital cluster software versions (ensure the latest patch is installed to prevent freezing)
  • Panel gaps and interior trim fitment (early production units from the Trnava plant may have minor assembly inconsistencies)
  • Smoothness of the e-DCS6 gearbox on MHEV models during low-speed transitions (electric to petrol)
  • Operation of the ADAS (Advanced Driver Assistance Systems) which can be overly intrusive or glitchy

Verdict

The 4th generation Citroën C3 is a highly recommended purchase (**ACHETER**), primarily because Stellantis has finally addressed the elephant in the room: the 1.2 PureTech engine. By switching to a timing chain, the new petrol variants offer peace of mind that was entirely absent in the previous generation.

If you are buying the petrol version, the standard 1.2 PureTech 100 MT6 is the safest and most cost-effective bet. The Hybrid 100 is excellent for city dwellers wanting an automatic, though its complex dual-clutch system requires a leap of faith regarding long-term reliability. As with any first-year production model, expect minor software gremlins, but mechanically, this C3 is built on much sounder foundations than its predecessor.
