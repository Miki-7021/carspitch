Summary

The second-generation Subaru XV (code GT), also marketed as the Crosstrek in North America, marks a technical turning point for the brand by inaugurating the new SGP (*Subaru Global Platform*). This architecture gives it a 70% increase in rigidity, better road handling, and class-leading passive safety. True to Subaru's DNA, it retains its permanent *Symmetrical AWD*, flat Boxer engines, and a generous ground clearance of 22 cm, making it one of the few compact SUVs truly capable off-road. In Europe, anti-pollution standards pushed Subaru to gradually replace 100% internal combustion engines with a mild-hybrid version named e-Boxer. It is a niche vehicle, extremely robust, designed for harsh climates and mountain dwellers, but penalized by a mandatory CVT gearbox and fuel consumption above the segment average.

Strengths 6

  • Formidably effective Symmetrical AWD
  • Exceptional ground clearance (22 cm) worthy of a true off-roader
  • Standard active safety (highly capable EyeSight system)
  • SGP chassis offering an excellent comfort/handling compromise
  • Overall mechanical reliability well above the European average
  • Excellent peripheral visibility (thin pillars, large glass areas)

Weaknesses 5

  • Mandatory CVT gearbox (engine revving effect under hard acceleration)
  • Anemic 114 hp 1.6i engine unsuited to the vehicle
  • Very average trunk volume (385L), even critical on the e-Boxer (340L)
  • High fuel consumption on the highway
  • Sparse dealer network in Europe

Check before buying

  • CVT gearbox maintenance history (demand an oil change invoice if > 60,000 km)
  • Windshield condition: the glass is thin and very sensitive to impacts (high replacement cost due to EyeSight camera recalibration)
  • Rear wheel bearings: frequent premature wear (growling noise from 80 km/h)
  • Starlink touch screen operation (check for bubbles or dead zones)
  • Underbody condition (vehicle often used off-road or in the mountains)

Official recalls

  • [2021-06-16] Brake light switch (Code SRT270 / 201907): The brake light switch may be defective. Risk of brake lights not illuminating or inability to start the vehicle (injury/collision). Affects XV, Impreza, Forester.
  • [2019-11] PCV Valve: The aluminum positive crankcase ventilation (PCV) valve may break. Debris can fall into the engine, leading to a loss of power or engine failure.
  • [2019-10] Ignition coils / ECM: Reprogramming of the engine control module (ECM) and possible replacement of ignition coils that could degrade, causing misfires or stalling while driving.
  • [2021-04] Rear stabilizer bar bolts: The rear stabilizer bar mounting bolts may loosen and fall out, altering road handling.

Verdict

The Subaru XV II (GT) is a sensible choice for drivers living in mountainous, snowy, or rural regions. It is not an urban parade SUV, but a true all-weather mobility tool. Its mechanical reliability is excellent, with the FB engines having reached maturity. However, you must absolutely avoid the 1.6i, which is too weak and thirsty, and focus on the 2.0i (or the 2.5i as an import). The European e-Boxer version is a tax compromise that unfortunately reduces the trunk without transforming the vehicle into an economy champion. A highly recommended purchase if you have use for its 4x4 capabilities, provided you scrupulously respect the CVT gearbox maintenance.
