Summary

The first-generation Hyundai i10 (codename PA) succeeded the Atos/Santro. Primarily designed for Europe and India (where it was assembled), this A-segment city car stood out for its surprising spaciousness, generous standard equipment for its time, and standard 5-door body.

**Evolution:** A major facelift (Phase 2) occurred in late 2010 / early 2011, adopting the brand's *Fluidic Sculpture* design, improving perceived quality, and updating the engines (the 1.2 went from 78 to 85 hp, and the 1.0 3-cylinder appeared in some markets).

**Expert's overall rating:** It's an excellent small urban car, very mechanically reliable, although penalized by basic interior materials and some peripheral weaknesses (clutch, headlight adjustment).

Strengths 5

  • Excellent overall mechanical reliability (petrol engines)
  • Remarkable spaciousness for its size (3.56 m)
  • 5 doors as standard (practical compared to the C1/107/Aygo triplets)
  • Very versatile and willing 1.2 Kappa engine
  • Very low maintenance and insurance costs

Weaknesses 6

  • Sticky gear shift (reverse and 1st gear difficult to engage)
  • Clutch pedal becoming stiff (cable or release bearing wear)
  • Light sound insulation on the highway
  • Hard interior plastics, very prone to scratches
  • Firm suspension, bumpy comfort on degraded roads
  • Limited boot volume (225 liters)

Check before buying

  • **Clutch:** Test the pedal stiffness. A seized cable or worn release bearing are very common (often around 60,000 - 80,000 km).
  • **Gearbox:** Check that reverse and first gear engage without grinding (weak synchros or linkage adjustment needed).
  • **Power Steering (EPS):** Listen for clunking noises in the steering column during rapid turns at a standstill (worn steering column star bushing).
  • **Lighting:** Headlight height adjustment motors frequently fail.
  • **Braking:** Possible seizing of rear brake calipers (if equipped with discs) or front guide pins. Check handbrake travel.

Verdict

The first-generation Hyundai i10 is an extremely rational and recommendable used car choice. It surpasses many of its European rivals of the era in terms of spaciousness and mechanical reliability (especially compared to purely European engines).

**Expert's advice:** Avoid the 1.1 CRDi diesel, a source of costly problems and unnecessary in this segment. Opt for a Phase 2 model (after 2011) equipped with the **1.2 Kappa 85 hp engine**: it offers surprising versatility, allowing you to leave cities without worry, while benefiting from a trouble-free timing chain. Be sure to check the clutch pedal's smoothness and gear shifting during the test drive.
