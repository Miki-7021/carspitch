Citroën C1

I (PM/PN) · 2005 – 2014

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The first-generation Citroën C1 (factory code PM for the 3-door, PN for the 5-door) is the result of the "B-Zero" industrial project jointly led by the PSA group (Peugeot-Citroën) and Toyota. Produced in the TPCA factory in Kolín, Czech Republic, it shares 90% of its parts with the Peugeot 107 and Toyota Aygo. Designed as an ultra-compact (3.43 m) and economical city car, it stands out for its agility and negligible running costs. The model underwent two facelifts (Phase 2 in late 2008, Phase 3 in early 2012) which mainly modernized the front end and improved sound insulation. Overall, it is an extremely reliable vehicle, supported by the excellent reputation of the Toyota-sourced 3-cylinder gasoline engine.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall reliability of the gasoline engine (Toyota origin)
  • Among the lowest maintenance and insurance costs on the market
  • Very low fuel consumption (often under 5L/100km in real-world driving)
  • Ideal compactness for urban parking
  • Timing chain on the gasoline engine (no belt to replace)

Weaknesses 5

  • Frequent water ingress in the trunk (rear light seals, vents, or tailgate seal)
  • Very light sound insulation (wind and road noise on the highway)
  • Slow Sensodrive/BMP automated manual gearbox, causing jerks and wearing out the clutch
  • Basic interior finish (hard plastics, exposed sheet metal)
  • Tiny trunk volume (139 liters)

Check before buying

  • Lift the trunk mat and check for water or rust in the spare wheel well.
  • Test the clutch slip point (if it's very high, replacement is imminent).
  • Check the condition of the rear exhaust muffler (very exposed to corrosion).
  • Ensure that gears (especially reverse and 1st) engage without grinding.
  • Check the seals of the door gaskets (especially on 5-door versions).

Economy

  • Ultra-Economical Running Costs — The Citroën C1 stands out with some of the lowest maintenance and insurance costs on the market, complemented by remarkably low fuel consumption, often below 5L/100km.

Practicality

  • Unrivaled Urban Agility — Its compactness and light, direct steering make it the ideal city car for effortlessly navigating and parking in the densest urban environments.

Safety

  • Optimal City Safety — The Citroën C1 offers solid protection for its occupants, as evidenced by its excellent Euro NCAP test results, ensuring serene daily driving.

Braking

  • Efficient and Safe Braking — Benefit from a powerful braking system suited to its size, with braking distances within its category average, enhanced by the presence of ABS for increased safety.

Handling

  • Agile and Predictable Road Behavior — Appreciate agile road holding and excellent maneuverability, especially in the city. Its comfort-oriented suspension ensures stable and predictable driving, suited to its primary use.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Peugeot 107 / Toyota Aygo (technical clones), Renault Twingo II (more spacious), Volkswagen Up! / Seat Mii / Skoda Citigo (better finished), Fiat Panda (more versatile). - **Asia / Japan**: Daihatsu Mira, Suzuki Alto. - **North America**: Not marketed (Smart Fortwo or Chevrolet Spark as size alternatives).

Verdict

The first-generation Citroën C1 is a benchmark choice for a budget used car purchase. Its 1.0i gasoline engine, designed by Toyota, is a model of robustness if basic maintenance is respected. It is absolutely essential to prioritize gasoline models with a manual gearbox, ideally post-2009 (Phase 2) to benefit from the reinforced clutch and more reliable water pump. Avoid the automated manual gearbox (Sensodrive) which degrades driving pleasure and reliability, as well as the 1.4 HDi diesel engine, whose presence under the hood of a micro-city car is an economic and mechanical nonsense. Before purchase, a meticulous inspection of the trunk for water ingress is imperative.
