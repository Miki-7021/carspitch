Nissan Qashqai

I (J10) · 2006 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Nissan models

Summary

The first-generation Nissan Qashqai (codename J10) is a historic model that literally created and popularized the compact crossover (C-SUV) segment in Europe. Designed to offer the driving position of an SUV with the road handling and running costs of a compact sedan, it achieved phenomenal commercial success. A major facelift (Phase 2) occurred in spring 2010, bringing a modernized front end, improved sound insulation, and recalibrated suspensions. It was also available in a stretched 7-seater version (Qashqai+2, code JJ10) from 2008 to 2013. While its concept was innovative, its overall reliability was marred by early issues, particularly with the widely distributed Renault-sourced 1.5 dCi diesel engine.

Strengths 5

  • Pioneer of the segment, design that ages rather well (especially Phase 2)
  • Safe road handling, close to a sedan
  • Reliability of gasoline engines and the 2.0 dCi
  • Qashqai+2 version very practical for families (7 seats or large trunk)
  • Available with efficient All-Mode 4x4 all-wheel drive

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of early 1.5 dCi (connecting rod bearings, turbo)
  • Quality of interior plastics and assembly (rattles, peeling steering wheel)
  • Chronic fragility of rear shock absorbers (especially before 2010)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on Phase 1 models (before 2010)
  • Average rear passenger space and trunk volume on the 5-seater version

Check before buying

  • Clear service history (vital for 1.5 dCi: check if connecting rod bearings have been changed)
  • Clunking noises from the rear axle (silentblocks, leaking shock absorbers)
  • Idling tremors or clutch slip (worn dual-mass flywheel on diesels)
  • Air conditioning operation (fragile compressor) and power windows
  • Condition of the particulate filter (DPF) on diesels: warning light on, loss of power, black smoke

Official recalls

  • September 2012: Risk of steering wheel detachment due to hub weakness (models produced between Feb. and May 2012).
  • December 2011: Risk of fuel leak at the particulate filter connection on 1.5 dCi engines.
  • November 2009: Power steering housing sealing defect potentially leading to loss of assistance.
  • May 2007: Replacement of front suspension ball joints (risk of dislodgement on very early models).

What sets it apart 6

Safety

  • Top-tier Safety — The Nissan Qashqai achieved the maximum rating in Euro NCAP tests, ensuring optimal protection for all occupants.

Handling

  • Dynamic Road Handling — Enjoy exceptional road handling for a crossover, offering remarkable agility, excellent body control, and precise steering.

Braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — The Qashqai provides reliable and progressive braking performance, ensuring adequate stopping power for its class.

Reliability

  • Proven Mechanical Reliability — The 1.6 dCi (130 hp) and 2.0 dCi (150 hp) diesel engines are renowned for their robustness and reliability, featuring chain distribution for enhanced peace of mind.

Performance

  • Versatility and Performance — The 2.0 dCi engine offers generous performance, ideal for long journeys or towing, while the 1.6 dCi combines power with controlled consumption.

Practicality

  • Segment Pioneer and Practicality — The Qashqai defined the crossover segment, offering a raised driving position and a highly practical +2 version for large families or increased boot volume.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Volkswagen Tiguan I (higher trim but capricious TDI), Peugeot 3008 I (dynamic handling, beware of the 1.6 HDi), Ford Kuga I, Kia Sportage III.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda CR-V III (more spacious, very reliable), Toyota RAV4 III, Subaru Forester.
  • North America: The Qashqai J10 was not sold there. The direct alternative from Nissan was the Rogue (S35), based on the same platform but with a different body.

Verdict

The Nissan Qashqai I is a pivotal vehicle in automotive history, but buying it used requires great caution, especially if you are targeting a diesel. The 1.5 dCi should be approached with extreme suspicion, especially models before 2010, unless the engine has been replaced or the connecting rod bearings preventively changed. For diesel driving, absolutely prioritize the 1.6 dCi 130 (appeared late 2011) or the indestructible 2.0 dCi 150. If you drive less than 15,000 km per year, the gasoline versions (1.6 and 2.0) are excellent choices, very reliable and economical to maintain, although rare on the European market.
