Porsche Cayenne

III (9YA) · 2018 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 76/100 — bon

Summary

The third-generation Porsche Cayenne (Type 9YA) marks a historic turning point for the brand: **it is the first generation to completely bypass diesel engines**. Following the Dieselgate scandal, Porsche officially abandoned diesel in 2018. To meet the demand of high-mileage drivers, the manufacturer has heavily invested in plug-in hybrid versions (E-Hybrid), which offer massive torque and tax advantages, *de facto* replacing diesel in the lineup. Based on the MLB Evo platform (shared with the Audi Q8 and Bentley Bentayga), the 9YA received a major facelift in 2023 (revised chassis, return of the V8 on the Cayenne S, new interior). Although diesel is absent from this generation, we will cover the TDI blocks of the previous generation (958) for the context of buyers specifically targeting this fuel, while detailing the petrol and PHEV engines of the 9YA.

Strengths 5

  • Unmatched driving dynamics in the large SUV category
  • Exceptional finish and assembly quality
  • Return of the V8 on the S version (since 2023)
  • Electric range of post-2023 E-Hybrid versions (25.9 kWh battery)
  • Overall mechanical reliability significantly improved compared to the 955/958 generations (ADAC rate of 2.8)

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of diesel engines on this generation (penalizing for very high-mileage drivers/towing)
  • Recurring electronic and software bugs (screens, ADAS)
  • Excessive weight of E-Hybrid versions (accelerated wear of tires and brakes)
  • Cost of options and maintenance in the Porsche network
  • Air suspension still prone to costly leaks over time

Check before buying

  • Complete history of software updates (very numerous recall campaigns)
  • Condition of the air suspension (check if the vehicle sags after prolonged parking)
  • Wear of tires and brake discs (especially on hybrid versions weighing over 2.3 tons)
  • Smooth operation of the Tiptronic S gearbox (possible jerks if the software is not up to date)
  • For hybrids: request a high-voltage capacity test (SoH - State of Health)

What sets it apart 12

Safety

  • Certified Cutting-Edge Safety — The Porsche Cayenne III achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 95% for adult protection and 80% for child protection, ensuring absolute peace of mind.

Braking

  • Exceptional Braking Performance — Equipped with a powerful and enduring braking system, the Cayenne III offers remarkable performance, with a stopping distance of just 48.5 meters (159 feet) from 70 to 0 mph for the Cayenne S, an achievement for an SUV in this category.

Handling

  • Unrivaled Driving Dynamics — The Cayenne III redefines the agility of large SUVs. Its precise steering, controlled body roll, and advanced systems like PDCC and PASM offer sporty and secure road holding, even at high speeds.

Reliability

  • Optimized Mechanical Reliability — Benefiting from a robust design, the Cayenne III shows significantly improved overall mechanical reliability, with an exceptionally low breakdown rate of 2.8 breakdowns per 1000 vehicles according to ADAC 2024.

Comfort

  • Premium Finish and Assembly Quality — The interior of the Cayenne III is a luxurious sanctuary, characterized by exceptional finish and assembly quality, using noble materials for the most refined travel experience.

Economy

  • The Smart Alternative to Diesel: The E-Hybrid — For high-mileage drivers, the Cayenne E-Hybrid advantageously replaces diesel by offering massive torque, impressive performance, and significant tax benefits, with increased electric range thanks to its 25.9 kWh battery (post-2023).

Alternatives

* **Europe**: BMW X5 (which still offers excellent, very reliable 30d/40d diesels), Mercedes-Benz GLE (including the 350 de diesel-hybrid), Audi Q8 (shares the same platform, available as 50 TDI). * **North America**: BMW X5, Range Rover Sport, Genesis GV80. * **Emerging markets**: Toyota Land Cruiser (for pure robustness), Lexus RX.

Verdict

The Porsche Cayenne III (9YA) is undoubtedly the most dynamic luxury SUV in its segment. **However, if you absolutely want a diesel engine, you must turn to the previous generation (958) or to the competition (BMW X5 30d, Mercedes GLE 350d).** Porsche made the radical choice to replace diesel with plug-in hybrids (E-Hybrid). While this solution is tax-advantageous and formidable in performance, it requires daily charging to be relevant and suffers from electronic complexity that is reflected in breakdown statistics (software bugs, frequent recalls). Mechanically, the petrol blocks (V6 EA839 and V8 EA825) are very robust. It is a vehicle to buy with an approved warranty (Porsche Approved) due to the exorbitant cost of electronic and air suspension repairs.
