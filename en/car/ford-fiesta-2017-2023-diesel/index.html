Summary

The seventh-generation Ford Fiesta (often referred to as Mk8 in the UK and by purists) marks the culmination of the famous blue oval city car, whose production definitively ceased in July 2023 to make way for electrification (Puma EV). Based on an evolution of Ford's global B platform, it corrects the main flaws of the previous generation: the interior is transformed with better quality materials and the integration of the SYNC 3 multimedia system, while sound insulation takes a leap forward. Road handling remains the absolute benchmark in its category, combining dynamism and comfort. A restyling (facelift) occurred at the end of 2021, modernizing the front fascia (logo in the grille) and introducing Matrix LED headlights. Although the market has massively shifted towards petrol, the Fiesta Mk8 offered an excellent 1.5 TDCi diesel engine, particularly frugal and powerful, ideal for high-mileage drivers.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis: the best comfort/dynamism compromise in its class
  • Extremely economical, torquey, and reliable 1.5 TDCi engine on the road
  • Responsive and ergonomic SYNC 3 infotainment system (Apple CarPlay / Android Auto)
  • Significantly improved sound insulation compared to the previous generation (Mk7)
  • Fantastic ST version (200 hp) and very accomplished mHEV versions (post-2020)

Weaknesses 4

  • Rear passenger space and boot volume (292 liters) inferior to competitors (Clio V, Polo)
  • Disastrous reliability of the wet belt on 1.0 and 1.1 petrol engines from before 2020
  • Quality of some plastics in the lower part of the cabin
  • DPF and EGR valve very sensitive to clogging on the 1.5 TDCi in case of urban use

Check before buying

  • On the 1.5 TDCi: Journey history (avoid ex-urban fleet vehicles), DPF status (engine light), absence of jerks during acceleration (EGR).
  • On the 1.5 TDCi 120 hp: Listen carefully to the idle in neutral to detect a metallic clatter (sign of a worn dual-mass flywheel).
  • On 1.0 and 1.1 petrol (pre-2020): Demand maintenance invoices proving exclusive use of Ford WSS-M2C948-B oil. If in doubt, inspect the oil strainer.
  • Electronics: Test all touchscreen functions, the rearview camera, and driving aids (lane keeping).

Official recalls

  • September 2017 (Steering Column): Risk of crack on the steering column which could lead to loss of control. Concerns the very first models produced in 2017.
  • May 2019 (Wiring Harness): Risk of short circuit and fire due to friction of the wiring harness near the battery.
  • August 2020 (Brake Pedal): On some versions, the brake pedal plate may detach due to a defective weld.
  • 2021 (mHEV System): Recall on mild hybrid versions for a risk of 48V battery overheating (software update or module replacement).

Verdict

The Ford Fiesta Mk8 is a brilliant city car, particularly engaging to drive. If you are targeting the 1.5 TDCi diesel engine, it's an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers. This engine is robust, willing, and incredibly economical. However, it absolutely must be driven on roads or highways to avoid fatal clogging of its DPF and EGR valve in urban use.

*Note on petrol:* If you change your mind to a petrol engine, absolutely avoid the 1.0 EcoBoost and 1.1 Ti-VCT from before 2020 (wet belt issues), and prioritize the 1.0 EcoBoost mHEV (2020+) versions equipped with a timing chain, which are infinitely more reliable.
