Volvo XC60

II (UZ) · 2017 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Volvo XC60 (code name UZ), based on the modular SPA (Scalable Product Architecture) platform, is the Swedish brand's global best-seller. Positioned in the highly competitive premium family SUV (D-SUV) segment, it stands out for its refined Scandinavian design, high-level comfort, and advanced safety arsenal. Under the hood, Volvo made the radical choice to offer only 2.0-liter 4-cylinder engines (VEA - Volvo Engine Architecture family), available in petrol, diesel, mild-hybrid (MHEV), and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions. A facelift occurred in mid-2021, introducing subtle aesthetic touches, the generalization of mild-hybridization (abandonment of classic 100% thermal engines), and the transition to the Android Automotive infotainment system. Overall, it is a very accomplished vehicle, but its technological complexity (especially on PHEV versions) requires rigorous monitoring.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with the optional air suspension).
  • Premium interior finish, noble materials (open-pore wood, Orrefors crystal).
  • Seats among the most ergonomic and comfortable in the automotive market.
  • Benchmark active and passive safety (City Safety, very well-calibrated Pilot Assist).
  • Bowers & Wilkins audio system (option) with breathtaking acoustic quality.
  • Electric range of post-2022 PHEV versions (often > 60 km real-world).

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the ERAD (rear electric motor) on plug-in hybrids before 2022.
  • Aging Sensus infotainment system and Android Automotive very buggy at its launch.
  • Very high weight (up to 2.2 tons in PHEV), impacting agility and tire wear.
  • Standard suspension a bit firm at low speeds with 20-inch wheels or larger.
  • Very high cost of maintenance and spare parts within the Volvo network.

Check before buying

  • On PHEVs (2017-2021): Listen carefully to the rear axle in 100% electric mode. A whistling or groaning sound indicates premature ERAD wear.
  • Air suspension (option): Check that the vehicle does not sag after prolonged parking (air spring leak or tired compressor).
  • Panoramic sunroof: Check for water ingress at the A-pillars and floor (drains often clogged).
  • Electronics: Test all functions of the central screen, 360° cameras, and ensure software updates have been performed.
  • D5 Diesel engines: Check the replacement history of the PowerPulse hose.

Official recalls

  • - **March 2020 (Emergency Braking - AEB):** The automatic emergency braking system (City Safety) may not detect certain obstacles due to a software bug. Update required.
  • - **July 2020 (Seatbelts):** The steel cable securing the front seatbelts may wear prematurely and fail. Replacement with a rigid attachment.
  • - **August 2020 (Wipers):** The fastening nuts for the front wiper arms may loosen.
  • - **July 2021 (Fuel Pump):** A 15A fuse for the low-pressure fuel pump may blow, causing the engine to stop. Replacement with a 20A fuse.
  • - **October 2023 (Steering):** On some 2019-2020 models, a loose screw in the steering gear housing can lead to loss of control.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Audi Q5 (clinical finish, robust TDIs), BMW X3 (better dynamism, excellent petrol/diesel engines), Mercedes-Benz GLC (comfort, PHEV with long range). - **North America:** Lexus NX (unbeatable reliability, efficient hybrid), Genesis GV70 (price/equipment ratio, bold design), Acura RDX. - **Asia:** Lexus NX, BMW X3 (locally produced in China), Cadillac XT5.

Verdict

The second-generation Volvo XC60 is a highly desirable premium SUV, offering a calming and safe alternative to its German competitors. If you are targeting a petrol engine, the T4/T5 versions or the recent B4/B5 mild-hybrids are excellent choices, combining smoothness and solid mechanical reliability. The plug-in hybrid versions (T6/T8) are very appealing on paper, but models produced between 2017 and 2021 require great caution due to costly ERAD (rear electric motor) failures. If your budget allows, prioritize a post-2022 PHEV model (18.8 kWh battery): reliability has been corrected, and the electric range becomes truly relevant. A clear service history within the Volvo network is absolutely imperative for this highly technological vehicle.
