Summary

The third-generation Renault Kangoo (internal code KFK for the MPV version, FFK for the utility vehicle) marks a true step upmarket compared to its predecessor. Based on the Alliance's CMF-C/D platform, it shares its underpinnings with the Nissan Townstar and the Mercedes Citan/T-Class. It stands out with a greatly improved interior finish, road handling worthy of a compact sedan, and practical innovations like the 'Open Sesame' system (removal of the right-side B-pillar on the utility version). In the MPV and van market, it benefits from the reliability issues of its direct competitors (Stellantis) to establish itself as a sensible choice, particularly with its well-developed thermal powertrains.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent overall reliability of thermal engines (highly developed 1.5 dCi and 1.3 TCe)
  • Significantly improved road handling and comfort (CMF platform)
  • Cargo volume and practical features (Open Sesame, integrated roof bars)
  • Responsive and smooth EDC7 automatic gearbox
  • Improved interior finish quality compared to the Kangoo II

Weaknesses 5

  • EasyLink multimedia system sometimes slow and prone to bugs
  • Prices significantly higher compared to the previous generation
  • Range of the E-Tech electric version a bit limited (285 km WLTP, often < 200 km in real-world driving)
  • High-speed sound insulation (wind noise due to cubic shape)
  • Sensitivity to crosswinds

Check before buying

  • History of software updates (multimedia and injection ECUs)
  • Functioning of the AdBlue system on Blue dCi versions (absence of 'Antipollution fault' warning light)
  • Condition of underbody and shock absorbers (vehicles often carrying heavy loads)
  • Alignment of the sliding side door (sometimes difficult to close or noisy on bad roads)
  • Wear of front tires (front axle heavily stressed)

Alternatives

  • Europe: Volkswagen Caddy IV (very good finish, robust 2.0 TDI), Ford Tourneo Connect (Caddy clone), Citroën Berlingo / Peugeot Rifter / Opel Combo (caution: 1.5 BlueHDi and 1.2 PureTech engines are very problematic, the Kangoo is a much better thermal choice).

Verdict

The Renault Kangoo III is currently the best choice in its category for thermal powertrains. Faced with the serious reliability problems encountered by its Stellantis competitors (PureTech and BlueHDi), the Kangoo shines with the maturity of its engines. The 1.5 Blue dCi is a master choice for professionals and families who drive a lot: it is extremely reliable, torquey, and frugal. One simply needs to ensure it is not used exclusively in the city to preserve its DPF and AdBlue system. The 1.3 TCe is an excellent alternative for those who drive less. A highly recommended purchase.
