Summary

The eighth-generation Nissan Sentra (B18), also known as the Sylphy in Asian markets, marks a spectacular break from the previous generation (B17). Gone are the anonymous design and subpar dynamic performance: the B18 adopts lines inspired by the large Maxima sedan, a significantly more rewarding interior with very comfortable "Zero Gravity" seats, and a new platform incorporating a multi-link independent rear suspension.

Positioned in the highly competitive compact sedan segment (C-segment), it does not aim for the sportiness of a Honda Civic or the hybrid efficiency of a Toyota Corolla (in North America), but establishes itself as a rational, comfortable offering with an unbeatable price-to-equipment ratio. Not sold in Western Europe (where Nissan favors SUVs and EVs), it is a best-seller in North America and China. Reliability, a black spot of the previous generation due to the CVT transmission, has made a significant leap forward, earning it recommendations from organizations like Consumer Reports and J.D. Power.

Strengths 6

  • Overall reliability significantly improved (ranked above average by J.D. Power and Consumer Reports)
  • CVT transmission finally made reliable compared to the 2010-2018 years
  • Ride comfort and front seat quality (Zero Gravity technology)
  • Excellent price/equipment/roominess ratio (ideal first purchase or student)
  • Very successful exterior design, statutory for the category
  • Simple onboard electronics, not very prone to bugs

Weaknesses 5

  • 2.0L engine lacking verve when overtaking on the highway
  • Soundproofing could be improved at high speeds (road and wind noise)
  • No hybrid engine available in North America (reserved for Asia)
  • Past reputation of Nissan CVT gearboxes which can still deter some used car buyers
  • Numerous minor assembly recalls (NHTSA) on the first years of production

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: demand the CVT gearbox fluid change invoices if the vehicle is approaching or exceeding 60,000 km.
  • Front axle: check for play or abnormal noises (known recall on tie rods that can bend).
  • Cabin: check for moisture under the front carpets (recall concerning a windshield cowl sealing defect on 2022 models).
  • Electronics: test the proper functioning of the reversing camera (wiring harness sometimes faulty, subject to a recall).

What sets it apart 11

Reliability

  • Improved overall reliability over previous generation — J.D. Power and Consumer Reports rank this generation above average, so buyers face fewer unexpected repair risks than with the previous Sentra.
  • Reinforced CVT transmission reliability — The CVT has been significantly improved versus 2010-2018 models and includes a dedicated cooler, reducing the transmission concerns that affected earlier cars.
  • Simple, bug-resistant onboard electronics — The embedded electronics are simple and rarely prone to bugs, so owners spend less time dealing with glitches and more time using the features they need.
  • Recommended by independent quality organizations — Consumer Reports and J.D. Power recommend the car, giving used-car buyers independent confirmation that this generation meets above-average quality standards.

Comfort

  • Comfortable Zero Gravity front seats — The front seats use Zero Gravity technology, which supports the body over long drives and makes daily commuting and highway trips noticeably more comfortable.
  • Smooth ride from independent rear suspension — The new platform includes a multi-link independent rear suspension, which absorbs road imperfections better and improves comfort for passengers on uneven surfaces.

Economy

  • Strong value for price and equipment — The Sentra offers an excellent price-to-equipment and roominess ratio, making it a rational choice for first-time buyers or students seeking a well-equipped compact sedan.
  • Proven 1.6L engine with low running costs — The HR16DE engine has been used for years, uses indirect injection to avoid valve carbon buildup, and keeps maintenance costs very low for urban and suburban driving.
  • Efficient e-POWER urban hybrid option — On Asian markets, the e-POWER version delivers excellent urban fuel consumption and fully electric-feeling driving, with the petrol engine acting only as a generator.

Performance

  • Robust 2.0L engine with controlled consumption — The MR20DD is a simple, turbo-free engine with smooth power delivery and controlled fuel consumption, suited to calm high-mileage drivers on city and highway routes.

Design

  • Statutory exterior design for the segment — The exterior styling draws inspiration from the larger Maxima, giving the Sentra a more upscale and stately appearance than many rivals in the compact sedan class.

Alternatives

  • North America: Toyota Corolla (absolute benchmark in reliability and available as a hybrid), Honda Civic (more dynamic, superior finish, but significantly more expensive), Hyundai Elantra (bold design, excellent warranty), Mazda 3 (premium finish, classic torque converter automatic gearbox).
  • Asia / China: Volkswagen Lavida, Toyota Levin/Corolla, Honda Crider.

Verdict

The Nissan Sentra B18 is a highly recommendable choice, which deserves a BUY verdict. Nissan has learned from past mistakes: the naturally aspirated 2.0L mechanics are robust and the CVT transmission, once the brand's Achilles heel, has been made considerably more reliable. Although the list of NHTSA recalls may seem long, these are mostly minor assembly defects covered by the network, and not major powertrain design flaws. It is a rational sedan, extremely comfortable, economical to run, and offers a price/quality ratio often superior to its direct rivals (Civic, Corolla) on the used market. The only condition to guarantee its longevity is to perform a CVT gearbox oil change every 60,000 km.
