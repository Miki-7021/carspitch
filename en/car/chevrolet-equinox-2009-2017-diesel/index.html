Summary

The second-generation Chevrolet Equinox (2010-2017, produced from 2009) is a highly popular mid-size SUV in North America. Based on the GM Theta platform, it stands out for its generous interior space and ride comfort. Expert note regarding the targeted diesel engine: The diesel engine (1.6L Turbo-Diesel LH7) was never offered on Generation II. It was introduced in 2018 on Generation III. To answer your request, this sheet exhaustively covers the Gen II engines, while including a specific analysis of the Gen III Diesel for context. According to Consumer Reports and J.D. Power, the Gen II Equinox displays above-average reliability for its category, supported by an excellent 6-speed Hydra-Matic automatic gearbox and a vast after-sales network (particularly in Canada).

Strengths 5

  • Above-average overall reliability (Consumer Reports / J.D. Power IQS 2022-2023)
  • 6-speed Hydra-Matic automatic gearbox (6T45/6T70) considered excellent and very durable
  • Rear seat space (very practical Multi-Flex sliding bench seat)
  • Very robust V6 engines (3.0L and 3.6L)
  • Extensive after-sales network and high popularity (especially in Canada)

Weaknesses 5

  • Absence of a diesel engine on this generation (Gen II)
  • Critical oil overconsumption on the 2.4L Ecotec (2010-2013 years)
  • Quality of certain interior plastics (prone to scratching)
  • Limited rear visibility (thick C-pillars)
  • Corrosion of the wiper linkages (subject to recalls)

Check before buying

  • Engine oil level on the dipstick (crucial on the 2.4L Ecotec)
  • Metallic rattling noise on cold start (sign of timing chain wear)
  • Smooth operation of the front wipers (check if the NHTSA recall has been performed)
  • Automatic gearbox oil change history (recommended every 80,000 km despite manufacturer recommendations)

Official recalls

According to the NHTSA (United States), 9 major recalls have been issued for this generation: * [2022-03-17] & [2016-08-04] (Visibility / Wipers): Corrosion of the wiper module ball joints which can lead to a failure of one or both wipers (2013-2015 Models). * [2016-07-05] (Electrical system / Ignition): The electronic lock lever may allow the ignition key to be removed without the transmission being in PARK (2016 Models). * [2016-06-21] (Equipment / Labels): Certification labels containing incorrect information on rim/tire size and cold tire pressure (2016 Models). * [2015-10-16] (Side airbags): Potentially incorrect deployment of the front seat-mounted side airbags (2015 Models). * [2014-07-23] (Power seats): Problem with the height adjustment bolt of the front power seats (2010-2012 Models). * [2011-10-19] (Tires / TPMS): Non-compliance of the tire pressure monitoring system (2012 Models). * [2010-12-15] (Seat belts): The driver/passenger seat belt buckle anchor can fracture (2011 Models). * [2009-12-23] (Visibility / Defrost): Software non-compliance of the windshield defrost/defog system (2010 Models).

Verdict

The Gen II Chevrolet Equinox is an overall solid SUV, validated by excellent ratings from Consumer Reports and J.D. Power for its mature versions. If you absolutely want a Diesel, you will have to turn to Generation III (from 2018) with the 1.6L LH7. For Generation II, the choice of engine dictates reliability: the V6 versions (3.0L and especially 3.6L) are extremely robust and recommended. If you opt for the 2.4L 4-cylinder, strictly avoid the 2010 to 2013 years due to severe oil consumption problems, and favor the 2014-2017 models which justify the 'very reliable' rating awarded by testing organizations.
