Peugeot 307

I phase 2 (3A/C) · 2005 – 2008

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The **Peugeot 307 Phase 2** (launched in June 2005) marks the mid-career facelift of the Lion-badged compact car. Aesthetically, it adopts the large gaping grille typical of the Peugeot era of the 2000s and new lights. Technically, it abandons the capricious VAN/CAN electronic architecture of Phase 1 for a **Full CAN** network, significantly improving electronic reliability, although not everything is perfect.

Mechanically, this period coincides with the widespread adoption of **1.6 HDi (DV6)** diesel engines developed with Ford, replacing the older 2.0 HDi 90/110. While road handling, spaciousness, and comfort remain absolute benchmarks in the category, the mechanical reliability of the diesels of this generation (particularly the 1.6 HDi 110) requires extreme vigilance on the used market. The model was available as a sedan (3/5 doors), station wagon (SW/Estate), and coupé-cabriolet (CC).

Strengths 5

  • Imperial road handling (exceptional comfort/road holding compromise)
  • Spaciousness and brightness (especially in SW version with panoramic roof)
  • Significantly improved electronic reliability compared to Phase 1 (Full CAN network)
  • Remarkable fuel efficiency of 1.6 HDi engines
  • Decent finish quality for the era and good material resistance

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 HDi 110 if maintenance has not been meticulous (turbo failure)
  • Very fragile dual-mass flywheel on 110 and 136 hp diesels
  • Rapid wear of front tires (incisive but heavy front axle)
  • Catchy and imprecise manual gearbox control
  • Turn signal/headlight stalk (COM2000) still prone to weaknesses

Check before buying

  • On 1.6 HDi: Presence of black tar or exhaust smell around the injectors (leak = danger for the turbo).
  • On 110/136 hp diesels: Metallic knocking noise at idle or vibrations in the clutch pedal (flywheel faulty).
  • Oil change history on diesels: demand invoices proving an annual oil change or every 15,000 km with the recommended oil (5W30 Ineo).
  • Air conditioning operation (condenser exposed to gravel due to the large grille).
  • On SW version: Operation of the panoramic roof blind (motor or rails often broken).

Official recalls

  • - **January 2006**: Risk of ABS/ESP unit malfunction (water infiltration). Concerns models produced until end of 2005.
  • - **November 2007**: On 1.6 HDi and 2.0 HDi engines, defective vacuum pump check valve for brake assistance, leading to a hard brake pedal. Valve replacement.
  • - **February 2008**: Software update for the BSI (Intelligent Service Unit) to correct exterior lighting bugs.
  • - **May 2008**: Starter wiring harness potentially rubbing against the EGR valve on the 2.0 HDi 136, risk of short circuit.

Verdict

The Peugeot 307 Phase 2 is an excellent compact car dynamically and for families, but it is undermined by the notorious reputation of its flagship engine: the 1.6 HDi 110. If you are looking for a diesel, absolutely prioritize the **1.6 HDi 90** (simpler and more robust) or the **2.0 HDi 136** for high-mileage drivers, provided the clutch has been replaced. Avoid 1.6 HDi 110 models without an impeccable service history and dry injector seals. Paradoxically, the petrol versions (especially the 1.6 16v 110hp) are remarkably reliable and today represent the best used car purchases for this model.
