Summary

The second-generation Mitsubishi ASX, launched in 2022, marks a turning point for the model in Europe. It is actually a **rebadged Renault Captur II**, a result of the Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi alliance. This strategy allows Mitsubishi to maintain a presence in the highly competitive urban SUV segment in Europe, without developing a new model from scratch. The ASX II therefore adopts all of the Captur II's platforms, powertrains, and technologies, with minimal aesthetic modifications (logos, slightly redesigned grille). It is positioned as a versatile compact SUV, focused on practicality and technology. Its reliability is intrinsically linked to that of the Renault Captur II. Overall, it is a modern and well-equipped vehicle, but it does not offer a diesel engine, unlike the first generation ASX.

Strengths 6

  • Modern and customizable design (inherited from Captur II)
  • Spaciousness and modularity (sliding rear bench)
  • Good technological and safety features
  • Wide choice of powerful and efficient petrol and hybrid powertrains
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Mitsubishi manufacturer warranty (5 years / 100,000 km in Europe)

Weaknesses 5

  • Absence of diesel powertrain (can be a deterrent for some buyers)
  • Potentially higher price than the equivalent Captur (Mitsubishi positioning)
  • Limited rear visibility (SUV coupe design)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (lower part)
  • Multimedia system sometimes slow or unintuitive (depending on software versions)

Check before buying

  • Complete and regular service history (especially for turbo petrol engines)
  • Hybrid system operation (petrol/electric switching, charging for PHEV)
  • Condition of the 12V battery (source of many small electronic problems)
  • Tire and brake wear (particularly on heavier PHEV versions)
  • Software updates performed (for multimedia and engine/hybrid management)
  • Absence of abnormal noises from the timing chain (1.3 TCe, although made more reliable)

Verdict

The second-generation Mitsubishi ASX is a relevant choice for those looking for a modern, well-equipped, and versatile urban SUV, with the added benefit of the Mitsubishi warranty. However, it is crucial to understand that it is a rebadged Renault Captur II. Its reliability is therefore directly linked to that of its French counterpart.

**If you are looking for a diesel:** This model is not for you. You will have to turn to the first generation of the ASX or to competitors still offering this powertrain (increasingly rare in this segment).

**For petrol and hybrid powertrains:** * **1.0 MPI-T (91 hp):** Ideal for primarily urban or suburban use, with a controlled budget. Correct reliability if maintenance is rigorous. * **1.3 Mild Hybrid (140 hp):** The best compromise for versatility, offering good performance and reasonable consumption. It is the most balanced powertrain for mixed use. * **1.6 E-Tech Hybrid (143 hp):** Excellent choice for city and mixed journeys, with very low consumption in urban cycles. A bit more complex, but generally reliable. * **1.6 E-Tech PHEV (160 hp):** Interesting if you have the possibility to recharge regularly and you make many short trips. Allows daily electric driving. More expensive to buy and heavier.

**Buying advice:** Prioritize the 1.3 Mild Hybrid or 1.6 E-Tech Hybrid versions for their versatility and efficiency. Always check the service history and ensure that manufacturer recalls have been carried out. The Mitsubishi ASX II is a good vehicle, but one should not expect a "Japanese reliability" distinct from that of Renault for this generation.
