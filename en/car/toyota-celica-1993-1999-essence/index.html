Toyota Celica

T200 (6e gen) · 1993 – 1999

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The 6th generation Toyota Celica (T200 series) marked a stylistic break with the abandonment of pop-up headlights in favor of four distinctive round headlights. Based on a platform shared with the Corona/Carina, it was available as a coupé (notchback), liftback (hatchback), and convertible. Renowned for its legendary robustness typical of 90s Toyota models, it offers an ideal compromise between sporty looks and everyday reliability. While the base versions are docile front-wheel-drive cars, the formidable all-wheel-drive GT-Four (ST205) served as the homologation basis for the World Rally Championship (WRC). A minor facelift occurred in late 1995/early 1996 (front bumper, taillights, minor mechanical improvements).

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall mechanical reliability (S and A series engines)
  • Timeless and distinctive design that ages very well
  • Healthy and predictable road handling
  • Low driving position and typical 90s sports car ergonomics
  • Historical rally pedigree (GT-Four)
  • Availability of common wear parts (shared with other Toyota models)

Weaknesses 6

  • Exorbitant cost of overhauling the 'Super Strut Suspension' (SSS) front axle
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (though better than the previous generation)
  • 1.8 and 2.2 engines lacking sporty character
  • Hard interior plastics that tend to creak with age
  • Front-wheel-drive architecture (except GT-Four) disappointing for rear-wheel-drive purists
  • GT-Four (ST205) specific parts becoming rare and expensive

Check before buying

  • Corrosion: rear wheel arches, sills, trunk floor, windshield cowl.
  • Front axle: clunking over potholes (sign of wear on Super Strut arms on equipped models).
  • Gearbox: crunching when shifting into 2nd and 3rd gear (worn synchros).
  • Engine: oil traces around the distributor (3S-GE/3S-FE) and valve cover.
  • Steering: power steering fluid leaks at the rack or pump.
  • Maintenance history: particularly crucial for the GT-Four (differential/gearbox/engine oil changes).

What sets it apart 14

Reliability

  • Legendary Toyota Reliability — The Celica T200 is renowned for its exceptional mechanical robustness, a legacy of Toyota's 90s quality standards. Its petrol engines are designed for remarkable longevity, easily exceeding 300,000 km with regular maintenance.

Comfort

  • Timeless and Distinctive Design — With its four iconic round headlights and fluid lines, the Celica T200 boasts a style that transcends decades without aging. Its sporty and elegant appearance makes it a coupé that still stands out on the road.

Performance

  • Sporty and Precise Handling — Praised for its balanced chassis and independent four-wheel suspension, the Celica T200 offers excellent road holding. Its precise steering and maneuverability make it an engaging car, capable of tackling corners with agility.
  • Rally Heritage and Dynamic Performance — With its World Rally Championship pedigree from the GT-Four, the Celica T200 offers petrol engines providing a sporty character and dynamic performance, especially with the 3S-GE engine and the powerful 3S-GTE.

Safety

  • Efficient and Stable Braking — Equipped with ventilated disc brakes at the front and solid discs at the rear, the Celica T200 ensures powerful braking performance and good stability for its era. ABS, often standard or optional, enhances safety during deceleration.
  • Reassuring Active Safety — The combination of excellent road holding, a balanced chassis, and an efficient braking system provides the Celica T200 with reassuring active safety, allowing the driver to control the vehicle in all circumstances.

Practicality

  • Simplified Maintenance and Parts Availability — Thanks to the platform shared with other Toyota models, common wear parts for the Celica T200 are generally available and at reasonable costs, simplifying its daily maintenance.

Verdict

The Toyota Celica T200 is the archetype of a 90s coupé: stylish, incredibly reliable, and easy to live with. For daily use or a first collector's car (youngtimer), the 1.8 ST and 2.2 GT versions are safe choices, capable of exceeding 300,000 km with basic maintenance. For more thrills, the 2.0 GT (3S-GE) offers an excellent compromise, provided you check the condition of its suspension (avoid the Super Strut option if on a tight budget). Finally, the GT-Four is a true collector's item with strong appreciation potential, but it demands a maintenance budget worthy of a rally car. The number one enemy remains rust: prioritize a bodywork-sound example, as the mechanics are easily repairable.
