Summary

The second-generation Jaguar XF (X260) marks a major technical turning point for the British sedan. Based on the iQ-Al (D7a) modular aluminum platform, it sheds up to 190 kg compared to its predecessor (X250), significantly improving its agility and efficiency. Aesthetically, it evolves smoothly under the pen of Ian Callum, retaining a feline allure while modernizing its interior with the introduction of the InControl Touch Pro infotainment system.

Under the hood, this generation sees the definitive transition from the old Ford-sourced blocks to the Ingenium modular engine family designed in-house by Jaguar Land Rover (JLR). Although the chassis and the 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox are benchmarks in the category, the overall reliability of the XF X260 has been marred by teething problems on the Ingenium engines (especially in diesel, but gasoline is not spared) and recurring electronic bugs. On the used market, the gasoline versions (the core of this analysis) offer better drivability and superior reliability to the diesels, provided they are rigorously maintained.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional aluminum chassis: agility, steering precision, and suspension comfort
  • 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (smooth and very reliable)
  • Timeless and elegant exterior design
  • Very meticulous cabin soundproofing
  • Absence of structural corrosion thanks to aluminum

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the early Ingenium diesel engines (timing chain)
  • InControl Touch Pro infotainment system slow and buggy before the 2018 updates
  • Quality of some interior plastics lagging behind Audi or Mercedes
  • Sparse dealer network and very high cost of spare parts
  • Steep depreciation on the used market

Check before buying

  • Complete recall history (especially Takata Airbags and fuel injection rail on gasoline models)
  • Coolant level and color on gasoline V6s (chronic leaks)
  • Operation of the central screen (test the GPS, Bluetooth, and rearview camera to detect screen freezes)
  • Noise on cold start (clunking = loose timing chain, gasoline as well as diesel)
  • Condition of the underbody and rocker panels (NHTSA recall for left sill panel crack)

Official recalls

Safety Recalls (Takata Airbags)

  • 2016 to 2026 (Multiple NHTSA & Rappel Conso recalls): Mandatory replacement of frontal airbags (driver and passenger). The propellant gas can degrade over time and with humidity, risking the projection of deadly metal fragments upon deployment.

Engine & Fuel Recalls (Gasoline)

  • February 2018 (NHTSA 18V090000): On 2018 models equipped with the 2.0L gasoline engine, the fuel injection rail end caps may leak, leading to a fire risk.
  • September 2024 (Rappel Conso): Possible oil leak at the turbocharger oil feed pipe (recent models, but concerns the Ingenium architecture), which can create deposits on the road or a fire risk.
  • May 2023 (Rappel Conso): On 2.0L Ingenium gasoline engines (notably PHEV), the coolant pump can be damaged, causing rapid overheating and a fire risk.
  • November 2016 (NHTSA 16V796000): Possible leak on the fuel hoses of 2.0L GTDi engines (older Ford blocks fitted until 2015).

Electrical & Structural Recalls

  • October 2017 (NHTSA 17V678000): The digital instrument cluster (gauges) may blank out intermittently, depriving the driver of vital information.
  • February 2017 (NHTSA 17V084000): Possible crack in the left inner sill panel, compromising protection in the event of a side impact.
  • February 2018 (NHTSA 18V112000): Incorrect calibration of the restraint system (SRS) on Sportbrake (estate) versions, which may affect airbag deployment.
  • September 2022 (Rappel Conso): Manufacturing defect in the seat belt pretensioners (tubes damaged during assembly).

What sets it apart 6

Safety

  • Benchmark Passive Safety — The Jaguar XF II offers optimal protection for all occupants, confirmed by exceptional results in crash tests.

braking

  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — Enjoy excellent braking performance, with precise pedal feel and strong stopping power, inspiring confidence in all situations.

handling

  • Exceptional Dynamics and Agility — Thanks to its advanced aluminum chassis, the XF II offers a perfect balance between ride comfort and dynamic agility, with precise steering and exemplary roll control.

Design

  • Timeless and Distinctive Elegance — Its exterior design, a work by Ian Callum, maintains a feline and elegant allure, ensuring a road presence that is both modern and timeless.

comfort

  • Superior Acoustic and Ride Comfort — The cabin benefits from very careful sound insulation, complemented by an aluminum chassis that ensures remarkable suspension comfort on all surfaces.

Performance

  • Refined Petrol Engines and Responsive ZF Gearbox — Ingenium petrol engines offer smoothness and quietness, while the Supercharged V6 delivers high-flying performance. All enhanced by the smoothness and responsiveness of the 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox.

Alternatives

Europe

  • BMW 5 Series (G30): The segment benchmark. Extremely reliable B48/B58 gasoline engines, superior infotainment, but a more conservative design.
  • Mercedes-Benz E-Class (W213): Imperial comfort, cutting-edge technology, but 4-cylinder gasoline engines can sometimes be rough.
  • Audi A6 (C8): Untouchable interior finish, efficient Quattro transmission, but less dynamic road handling than the Jaguar.

North America & Asia

  • Lexus GS / ES: Legendary reliability, efficient hybrid powertrains, but less sporty handling and dated infotainment.
  • Genesis G80: Excellent price/equipment ratio, long-term warranty, but brand image still building and heavy weight.
  • Cadillac CTS: Excellent chassis (Alpha platform), high-performance gasoline engines, but uneven interior finish.

Verdict

The Jaguar XF (X260) is a sedan with character aimed at drivers looking for a dynamic and elegant alternative to German classicism. In gasoline form, it is a highly recommendable choice, provided you remain vigilant. The 2.0t Ingenium is a good daily companion, as long as the recalls (fuel injection rail, turbo) have been carried out and oil changes are done early. The V6 Supercharged offers exceptional drivability, but requires a substantial maintenance budget, particularly to make its cooling system reliable.

Buying advice: Avoid pre-2019 Ingenium diesels (major timing chain risks). Favor a post-2018 250 hp 2.0t gasoline model (more reliable infotainment), with a crystal-clear maintenance history and proof that the Takata recalls have been performed.
