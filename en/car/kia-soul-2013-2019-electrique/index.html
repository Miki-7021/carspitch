Summary

The second-generation Kia Soul, known by the internal code PS, was launched in 2013 and continued its career until 2019. It represents a stylistic evolution of the original model, retaining its distinctive cubic silhouette while refining its lines for a more modern and less caricatural look. Positioned as a compact urban crossover, it stands out with its bold design and surprising interior space for its size.

This generation marked a major turning point for the model with the introduction of a 100% electric version, the Soul EV, from 2014. This variant was one of Kia's first mass-market electric offerings, providing a respectable range for its time and the driving pleasure typical of electric vehicles. The Soul II benefited from a slight facelift in 2017, bringing minor aesthetic touches and technological improvements, notably a larger capacity battery for the Soul EV in some markets. Overall, the Soul II is appreciated for its originality, comfort, and versatility, with solid general reliability, particularly for its naturally aspirated and electric powertrains.

Strengths 6

  • Original and distinctive design
  • Surprising interior space and trunk volume for its size
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially EV)
  • Long manufacturer's warranty (7 years/150,000 km) transferable
  • General reliability of powertrains (particularly MPI and EV)
  • Electric version very pleasant to drive and economical to use

Weaknesses 5

  • Fuel consumption of GDI and MPI gasoline engines can be high
  • Range of the first-generation Soul EV (27 kWh) limited for long journeys
  • Quality of some interior plastics could be improved
  • Steering sometimes considered too light or lacking feel
  • Rear visibility limited by the design

Check before buying

  • Soul EV: Battery State of Health (SoH), history of fast/slow charges, operation of the heating/air conditioning system.
  • GDI/T-GDI Engines: Presence of abnormal noises on cold start (timing chain), decarbonization history.
  • Diesel Engines: DPF operation (no warning light on), EGR valve condition, absence of excessive smoke.
  • General: Tire wear (particularly on EV due to torque), brake condition (caliper seizing on EV), operation of infotainment and electrical equipment.
  • Complete service history and invoices.

Official recalls

Known Official Recalls for the Kia Soul II (PS)

  • 2014-12-16 (Europe/North America): Subject: Risk of power steering loss. Description: The steering rack pinion plug may detach, leading to a loss of power steering. Affected population: Certain vehicles produced between July 2013 and September 2014.
  • 2015-01-20 (Soul EV - Europe/North America): Subject: Parking brake issue. Description: The 'P' (parking) position locking mechanism may not engage correctly, allowing the vehicle to roll even in 'P' position. Affected population: Certain Soul EV models produced between July 2014 and November 2014.
  • 2015-02-05 (North America): Subject: Stuck accelerator pedal. Description: The accelerator pedal stopper may detach, allowing the pedal to get stuck under the floor mat. Affected population: Certain vehicles produced between July 2013 and January 2015.
  • 2019-03-08 (North America - 2.0 MPI Engine): Subject: Risk of engine fire. Description: Premature wear of the connecting rod bearings in the 2.0L Nu MPI engine, which can lead to engine seizure and a risk of fire. Affected population: Certain vehicles equipped with the 2.0 MPI engine produced between 2014 and 2019. (This recall is crucial for 2.0 MPI versions).

*Note: Dates and affected populations may vary slightly by market. It is always recommended to check with a Kia dealership using the vehicle's VIN.*

Verdict

The Kia Soul II (PS) is a vehicle that stands out from the ordinary, offering a refreshing alternative to more conventional crossovers. Its reliability is generally good, especially with the naturally aspirated engines (2.0 MPI) and the electric version (Soul EV).

For the Soul EV: It's an excellent choice for urban and suburban use. Its driving pleasure, quietness, and low maintenance costs make it a very relevant option on the used car market. The range of the 27 kWh versions is sufficient for daily use, while the 30 kWh versions offer a bit more peace of mind. It is imperative to check the battery's State of Health (SoH) and the specific EV maintenance history.

For gasoline engines: The 2.0 MPI is the most reliable and simplest to maintain, ideal for those who prioritize robustness. The 1.6 GDI is a good compromise but requires particular attention to carbon buildup. The 1.6 T-GDI offers more performance but is more complex and potentially more expensive to maintain long-term.

For Diesel (1.6 CRDi): This is a very reliable and economical powertrain for high-mileage drivers, provided it is not used exclusively in the city to preserve the DPF.

In summary: The Kia Soul II is a recommended purchase, particularly the Soul EV for its current relevance. Negotiate the price if the maintenance history is incomplete or if the EV battery's SoH is low. Avoid 2.0 MPI models not affected by the engine recall or without proof of repair if the recall was performed.
