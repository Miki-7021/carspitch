BMW X5

F15 · 2013 – 2018

NEGOTIATE Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The **third-generation BMW X5 (F15)** marks a gentle evolution compared to its predecessor (E70). Produced in Spartanburg (USA), it retains the same wheelbase but significantly improves ride comfort, sound insulation, and interior finish quality. It also introduces modern driving aids and the iDrive NBT infotainment system. Although heavy (often over 2.1 tons), it remains one of the most dynamic SUVs in its class. In terms of reliability, the F15 generation is generally more robust than the E70, notably thanks to the excellent standard ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox. However, diesel engines (which represent the vast majority of sales in Europe) have been marred by massive recall campaigns concerning the EGR valve cooler. The choice of engine and rigorous maintenance (especially the xDrive transfer case) are crucial.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional driving pleasure from the inline 6-cylinder diesel (30d), torquey and economical
  • Responsive and exemplarily reliable ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox (Steptronic)
  • Significantly improved interior finish quality compared to the E70
  • Very intuitive and responsive iDrive infotainment system
  • Dynamic road handling for a vehicle of this size and weight
  • Excellent sound insulation at highway speeds

Weaknesses 6

  • Major and recurrent EGR cooler issue on all diesels (fire risk)
  • Fragile rear air suspension (optional or standard depending on trim) (porous air springs around 100,000 km)
  • Extreme sensitivity of the xDrive transfer case to tire wear differences
  • Very high maintenance and repair costs (BMW parts and labor)
  • Imposing size and significant weight (rapid wear of brakes and suspension arm bushings)
  • Variable reliability of V8 gasoline engines (50i)

Check before buying

  • **EGR recall history:** Request BMW certification proving the replacement of the EGR cooler (diesels).
  • **Rear suspension:** Leave the vehicle parked and check if it sags at the rear (sign of air spring leak). Listen if the compressor runs too often.
  • **Transfer case (xDrive):** During the test drive, turn the steering wheel fully at low speed and accelerate gently. Any jerking or shuddering indicates a worn transfer case or degraded oil.
  • **Tires:** Check for the star marking (*) on the tires and even wear between front and rear.
  • **Water ingress:** Check for moisture under the front floor mats and in the trunk (panoramic sunroof drains often clogged).
  • **Front suspension arms:** Dull thuds over speed bumps indicating worn thrust arm bushings (common given the weight).

Verdict

The BMW X5 F15 is an excellent luxury SUV, particularly brilliant in its **xDrive30d** version. This engine offers the best balance between performance, consumption, and overall reliability. However, buying a used diesel X5 requires absolute vigilance regarding the **EGR cooler recall**, which must imperatively have been performed (and sometimes redone) at a dealership. Avoid models without a clear service history, as the xDrive transfer case and air suspension do not forgive negligence. V8 versions (50i) are to be avoided unless you have a very comfortable maintenance budget. In summary: look for a first-owner 30d, with recent star-marked tires, new air springs, and a complete service record.
