Mercedes SLK

R170 · 1996 – 2004

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The first-generation Mercedes-Benz SLK (code name R170) revolutionized the roadster segment upon its release in 1996 thanks to its electro-hydraulic retractable hardtop ('Vario-Roof'). Based on the shortened platform of the C-Class (W202), it offers an ideal compromise between the comfort of a coupe and the pleasure of a convertible. Facelifted (Phase 2) in February 2000, it benefited from aesthetic improvements, the introduction of ESP as standard, a 6-speed manual gearbox, and the arrival of the V6 engine (SLK 320). Although its road handling is more 'Grand Touring' oriented than purely sporty (uncommunicative recirculating ball steering), its overall mechanical reliability is excellent. The main enemies of the R170 today are corrosion and the aging of the roof's hydraulic seals.

Strengths 6

  • Vario-Roof offering exceptional versatility (coupe in winter, convertible in summer)
  • Excellent overall mechanical reliability (very robust M111 and M112 engines)
  • 5G-Tronic (722.6) automatic gearbox almost indestructible if serviced
  • Timeless design that ages well
  • Reasonable maintenance costs (outside the manufacturer network and specific AMG parts)
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing with the roof closed

Weaknesses 6

  • Very frequent corrosion (a disease of Mercedes from this era): wheel arches, front fenders, trunk lid
  • 'Soft-touch' interior paint on the center console and plastics that peels and becomes sticky
  • Vague recirculating ball steering, lacking precision and sporty feel
  • Notchy and unpleasant manual gearbox
  • Aging roof hydraulic system (inevitable strut leaks with age)
  • Central locking/roof pump (PSE) located in the trunk, very vulnerable to water ingress

Check before buying

  • **Roof hydraulics:** Inspect the headliner around the central rearview mirror (front locking cylinder leak) and the hydraulic fluid level in the trunk.
  • **Corrosion:** Carefully inspect the wheel arches, the bottom of the doors, the area around the trunk lock, and the jack pads.
  • **Engine wiring harness (M111):** Unplug the electromagnet connector at the front of the engine and check for the absence of oil. Also check the ECU plug.
  • **K40 relay:** If the vehicle stalls when hot or refuses to start (dashboard lights on but no starter), the solder joints of the K40 module (fuse box) are probably cracked.
  • **Water ingress:** Check for moisture in the bottom of the trunk (right side). If the drains are clogged, water drowns the PSE pump, disabling the central locking and the roof.
  • **Automatic gearbox:** Demand invoices for the automatic gearbox oil change (every 60,000 km). Check for the absence of leaks at the gearbox electrical connector.

Verdict

The Mercedes SLK R170 is an excellent choice for entering the world of classic roadsters (youngtimers). Its mechanics are of rare robustness, particularly the M111 (Kompressor) and M112 (V6) blocks. The ideal choice is an SLK 230 Kompressor Phase 2 (for the compromise) or an SLK 320 (for the nobility of the V6), obligatorily with an automatic gearbox. **However, the purchase must be conditional on a rigorous inspection of corrosion and the roof's hydraulic system.** A healthy example, even with high mileage, will offer you years of pleasure provided you fix the few known defects (M111 pigtail cable, K40 relay, automatic gearbox oil change).
