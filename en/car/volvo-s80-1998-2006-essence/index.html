Summary

The first-generation Volvo S80 (P2) marked a major turning point for the Swedish manufacturer. Replacing the S90 (900 series), it abandoned rear-wheel drive and cubic lines to introduce the P2 platform (FWD/AWD) and the "broad shoulders" design by Peter Horbury. Designed as a stately executive car, it stands out for its supreme comfort, legendary orthopedic seats, and an exceptional level of passive and active safety for its time (inauguration of the WHIPS system and IC inflatable curtains).

However, its launch was marred by early electronic problems (nascent multiplexing) and questionable technical choices on high-end versions (GM gearbox on 6-cylinder models). The 2003 facelift (MY2004) corrected most of these flaws, improved electronic reliability, and subtly modernized the aesthetics. It's an excellent "youngtimer" or long-distance cruiser, provided you choose the right engine.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional ride comfort (a true rolling lounge)
  • Seats among the best in the automotive industry (ergonomics)
  • Mechanical reliability of 5-cylinder engines (petrol and diesel)
  • Quality of the audio system (Premium Sound / Dynaudio with Dolby Pro Logic)
  • Excellent corrosion resistance
  • Passive and active safety still relevant (SIPS, WHIPS)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic turning radius (worthy of an aircraft carrier)
  • Disastrous reliability of the GM automatic gearbox on T6 versions
  • Annoying electronic problems on 1999-2002 model years (ETM, ABS, DIM)
  • Rapid wear of front axle components (significant weight)
  • Significant exterior bulk for only adequate rear passenger space
  • High cost of genuine Volvo spare parts

Check before buying

  • Glove test (PCV) on petrol engines: a clogged breather can cause camshaft oil seals to fail.
  • Automatic gearbox behavior when hot: no jerks should be felt when shifting from N to D or between 2nd and 3rd gear.
  • Functionality of all dashboard displays (DIM) and automatic climate control (ECC).
  • Dull thuds or clunks from the front axle when going over speed bumps (strut mounts, control arms).
  • Condition of the timing belt (to be done every 160,000 km or 10 years on petrol, 120,000 km on early diesels).
  • Alarm module: the internal battery leaks onto the circuit board with age ('Alarm service required' message).

Verdict

The Volvo S80 I is a fantastic executive car that suffers from a mixed reputation due to its early production years. The verdict is clear: absolutely prioritize a facelifted model (post-2003) equipped with a 5-cylinder engine (2.4, 2.5T, or D5). These versions offer remarkable reliability, imperial comfort, and reasonable maintenance costs if you avoid the official network for routine operations.

Conversely, avoid T6 versions unless you have irrefutable proof that the gearbox has been reinforced or recently replaced, and be very vigilant about the electronic history of 1999-2002 models.
