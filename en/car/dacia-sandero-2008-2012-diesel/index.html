Summary

The first-generation Dacia Sandero (codename B90) marked a major turning point in the European automotive market by popularizing the 'low-cost' versatile city car concept. Based on the B0 platform (derived from the Renault Clio II), it offers record spaciousness for its segment and robust durability thanks to proven technical solutions amortized by the Renault group. While the interior finish is basic and soundproofing is light, it shines with its extremely low running costs. The 'Stepway' adventurer version (introduced in 2009) greatly contributed to its success. With the diesel engine (1.5 dCi), it proves to be an excellent economical long-distance runner, although vigilance is required for the first generations of injection systems.

Strengths 6

  • Unbeatable price/spaciousness ratio
  • Extremely low maintenance and insurance costs
  • Raised ground clearance (ideal for poor roads, especially the Stepway)
  • Excellent overall reliability (basic electronics, few immobilizing breakdowns)
  • Very low fuel consumption for 1.5 dCi engines
  • Generous boot volume (320 liters)

Weaknesses 6

  • Almost non-existent soundproofing on the highway (especially in dCi)
  • Interior plastic quality (easy scratches, rattles)
  • Basic driving position (steering wheel not adjustable for reach)
  • Risk of metal filings on dCi 70 and 85 (Delphi pump)
  • Rudimentary safety equipment (ESP often absent or optional)
  • Significant body roll in corners

Check before buying

  • Fuel filter on dCi 70/85: open it to check for metallic flakes (filings).
  • Timing belt history (to be done every 4 years or 60,000 km on petrol, 5 years or 120,000 km on dCi).
  • EGR valve on diesel models: check for absence of jerks during acceleration or black smoke.
  • Condition of steering and suspension ball joints (premature wear reported at technical inspection).
  • Corrosion of the exhaust line and underbody on models driven in mountainous areas.
  • Operation of ventilation and air conditioning (fragile heating resistor).

Official recalls

  • May 2011: Recall concerning a risk of steering rod end breakage (models produced late 2010).
  • November 2009: Inspection and potential replacement of front seat belts (poor buckle locking).
  • 2010 (Unofficial / Technical Note): Reprogramming of the injection computer on some 1.5 dCi to avoid cold knocking.

Verdict

The Dacia Sandero I is an excellent rational purchase. With the 1.5 dCi diesel engine, it transforms into a true workhorse capable of racking up kilometers for a ridiculously low cost. However, the choice of year is crucial. The dCi 70 and 85 versions (Euro 4, 2008-2010) should be approached with caution due to the fragility of the Delphi injection pump; demand a clear service history. Strongly favor the dCi 75 and 90 versions (Euro 5, from late 2010 onwards) which are free of this problem, provided your usage includes road driving to preserve the DPF. If you only drive in the city, avoid diesel and opt for the very robust 1.4 MPI or the 1.2 16v.
