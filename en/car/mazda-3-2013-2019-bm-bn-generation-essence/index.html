Mazda 3

· 2013 – 2019 (BM/BN generation)

ACHETER Reliability 75/100 — bon

Summary

The third-generation Mazda 3, internally designated as the **BM series (2013–2016)** and the facelifted **BN series (2016–2019)**, marked a significant leap forward for the brand. It fully integrated Mazda's 'Kodo: Soul of Motion' design language and the complete suite of 'Skyactiv' technologies (chassis, engines, and transmissions). Available as a 5-door hatchback and a 4-door sedan (Fastback), it is widely praised for its engaging driving dynamics, premium interior feel, and the outstanding reliability of its naturally aspirated petrol engines. However, it is also known for subpar sound insulation in early models and severe reliability issues with its larger diesel engine.

Strengths 6

  • Class-leading driving dynamics and precise steering
  • Exceptional reliability of Skyactiv-G petrol engines
  • Excellent manual gearbox (short, precise throws)
  • High-quality interior materials and ergonomic layout
  • Attractive and timeless exterior design
  • Real-world fuel efficiency of petrol engines

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability record of the 2.2 Skyactiv-D engine
  • Infotainment screen prone to 'Ghost Touch' (delamination)
  • Thin paint that chips easily (especially Soul Red)
  • High road and wind noise on pre-facelift (BM) models
  • Cramped rear legroom compared to class rivals (e.g., VW Golf)
  • Poor rearward visibility due to thick C-pillars

Reliability by engine

EngineReliabilityKnown Issues
**1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 Skyactiv-G**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐Exceptionally reliable. High compression ratio naturally aspirated engines. No major systemic mechanical flaws. Minor reports of ignition coil wear at high mileage.
**1.5 Skyactiv-D**⭐⭐⭐Better than the 2.2D, but still suffers from typical modern diesel issues if used in the city: DPF clogging, EGR valve soot buildup.
**2.2 Skyactiv-D**⭐**High Risk.** Prone to premature camshaft wear (especially pre-2015), oil dilution leading to rising oil levels, carbon buildup in the intake manifold, exhaust pressure sensor failure, and vacuum pump issues.

Check before buying

  • **Infotainment Screen:** Check for 'Ghost Touch' (screen acting on its own) or spiderweb-like cracks under the glass. This requires a screen replacement.
  • **Power Folding Mirrors:** Test both mirrors. The internal plastic gears are prone to stripping, causing the mirror to fail to fold or unfold.
  • **Service History (Diesels):** For the 2.2D, meticulous service history is mandatory. Check the oil dipstick; if the oil level is above the 'X' mark, it indicates severe oil dilution with diesel fuel.
  • **Paint Condition:** Inspect the front bumper and hood for excessive stone chips, a known weakness of Mazda's eco-friendly paint process.
  • **Cold Start (2.2D):** Listen for rattling chains or rough idling, which could indicate camshaft wear or carbon buildup.

Alternatives

* **Volkswagen Golf VII:** Better space packaging, quieter cabin, wider range of engines, but less engaging to drive and less reliable petrol engines. * **Honda Civic (9th/10th Gen):** Excellent reliability, huge trunk space, but polarizing design and less premium interior feel. * **Toyota Auris / Corolla:** The benchmark for hybrid reliability and low running costs, but features a very dull driving experience compared to the Mazda. * **Ford Focus (Mk3/Mk4):** Matches the Mazda 3 for driving dynamics, but suffers from interior quality issues and the problematic 1.0 EcoBoost engine.

Verdict

The Mazda 3 (BM/BN) is a tale of two cars. If you buy a **Skyactiv-G petrol model**, you are getting one of the most reliable, stylish, and best-driving compact cars of its decade. It is a highly recommended purchase. However, if you are looking at the **2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel**, it is a vehicle to avoid entirely due to a litany of expensive and catastrophic engine failures. Opt for a post-2016 facelift (BN) petrol model to get the best combination of reliability, improved sound insulation, and modern tech.
