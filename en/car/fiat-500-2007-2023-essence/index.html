Summary

The Fiat 500 (Generation 312) is an icon of modern automotive design. Launched in 2007, this neo-retro city car saved Fiat thanks to its timeless design and immense commercial success. Based on the Fiat Panda II platform, it received a major facelift in 2015 (Phase 2) improving sound insulation, the infotainment system (Uconnect), and the light signature. Although its overall design is old, it remains a benchmark in urban environments. Its reliability depends drastically on the engine and gearbox chosen. The internal combustion versions coexisted with the new electric 500 (Gen 332) until their gradual withdrawal.

Strengths 6

  • Timeless design and enduring appeal
  • Legendary reliability of the 1.2 FIRE 69 hp engine
  • Very low maintenance and insurance costs
  • Exceptional maneuverability in the city (City mode for steering)
  • Availability and price of spare parts
  • Very low depreciation (excellent resale value on the used market)

Weaknesses 7

  • Catastrophic Dualogic automated manual gearbox (slow, jerky, actuator failures)
  • Imperfect driving position (steering wheel not adjustable for reach)
  • Rear tailgate wiring harness that breaks over time
  • Very fragile 'Blue&Me' Bluetooth/USB module (before 2015) (causes the odometer to flash)
  • Rapid wear of front suspension components (wishbones, strut mounts, silentblocks)
  • Interior door handles whose hinge breaks easily
  • Symbolic trunk volume and rear seats

Check before buying

  • Tailgate wiring harness: Check that the rear wiper, defroster, and license plate light are working. The trunk opening should not be temperamental.
  • Odometer: If it flashes, it's a sign of a fault on the CAN network, usually the Blue&Me module (expensive to replace) or a crashed Uconnect system.
  • Gearbox: Avoid Dualogic automated manual gearboxes. On manual gearboxes, listen for a whistling clutch release bearing when in neutral.
  • Front suspension: Listen for 'clonks' over speed bumps (sign of worn strut mounts or wishbones).
  • Door handles: Gently pull on the chrome interior handles to ensure they are not cracked at the base.
  • Daytime Running Lights (DRL): Bulbs burn out very frequently, and the plastic of the lights tends to melt slightly on Phase 1 models.

Official recalls

  • 2008 (Brakes): Risk of leakage on front brake hoses (models produced until March 2008).
  • 2014 (Seatbelts): Replacement of front seatbelt pretensioners.
  • 2016 (Electrical): Possible rubbing of the electrical harness behind the dashboard, potentially causing a short circuit.
  • 2018 (Transmission - Abarth): Replacement of the driveshaft on certain Abarth 595/695 versions.
  • 2021 (Steering): Risk of loss of power steering assistance due to a tightening defect of a column component.

Verdict

The Fiat 500 is an 'emotional purchase' that can prove very rational if you choose the right version. The verdict is clear: absolutely prioritize the 1.2 FIRE 69 hp engine or the recent 1.0 FireFly MHEV, always with a manual gearbox. In this configuration, the 500 is one of the most reliable and economical city cars on the market. However, you must avoid the Dualogic automated manual gearbox, avoid the 0.9 TwinAir engine (too temperamental and expensive to maintain), and banish the 1.3 MultiJet diesel if you drive in the city. Minor finish defects (handles, trunk wiring harness) are real but inexpensive to repair compared to mechanical failures in other competing models.
