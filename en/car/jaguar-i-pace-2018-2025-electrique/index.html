Jaguar I-PACE

I (X590) · 2018 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

Summary

The Jaguar I-PACE (X590) is the British brand's first 100% electric SUV. Launched in 2018, it was critically acclaimed for its spectacular design (penned by Ian Callum), sports car-like handling, and luxurious interior, winning a historic treble at the 2019 *World Car Awards*. However, its career has been heavily marred by major reliability issues regarding its high-voltage battery (supplied by LG Chem), which is prone to fire risks, leading to massive and repeated recall campaigns.

**Major evolutions:** - **2020 (MY21):** Crucial technical update. Replacement of the slow infotainment system (Touch Pro Duo) with the much more responsive **Pivi Pro** system. Introduction of an 11 kW three-phase onboard charger (compared to the previous 7 kW single-phase) reducing home charging time. - **2023:** Slight cosmetic facelift (closed grille, new wheels).

*Note: The I-PACE is based on a dedicated platform and is exclusively electric. There is no combustion or hybrid version.*

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional handling (50/50 weight distribution, low center of gravity)
  • Timeless and spectacular exterior design
  • Top-flight interior finish quality and soundproofing
  • EV400 performance (0 to 100 km/h in 4.8 s)
  • Generous onboard space thanks to the long wheelbase

Weaknesses 6

  • Risk of high-voltage battery fire (LG Chem cells) leading to endless recalls
  • Obsolete DC fast charging speed (100-104 kW max), making long trips tedious
  • High electrical consumption (often > 24 kWh/100 km on the highway)
  • Recurring failures of the coolant heater (HVCH)
  • Very slow and buggy multimedia system on 2018-2019 models
  • Very high weight (over 2.2 tons) leading to rapid wear of tires and brakes

Check before buying

  • Exact status of high-voltage battery recalls (H441, H484, etc.): is the vehicle limited to 85% charge?
  • Operation of cabin heating and defrosting (HVCH module failure is very common)
  • Condition of tires (asymmetrical or premature wear due to weight and torque)
  • Health of the 12V battery (source of many electronic bugs if weak)
  • Fluidity of touch screens (especially on pre-2021 models)
  • Suspension noises (bushings, wishbones) stressed by the vehicle's weight

Verdict

The Jaguar I-PACE is a textbook case of the electric transition: a fantastic car to drive, but ruined by problematic battery technology. The fire risks associated with the LG Chem cells forced Jaguar to impose software updates that often limit the battery capacity to 85% while awaiting the replacement of defective modules. Furthermore, its DC fast charging speed (100 kW) is now obsolete for a vehicle of this price.

**Buying advice:** **NEGOTIATE WITH EXTREME CAUTION**. Absolutely avoid 2018-2019 models (slow infotainment, 7 kW AC charging, high mileage on at-risk batteries). If you fall for its design and chassis, aim for a post-2021 model (Pivi Pro, 11 kW AC charging) and **demand written proof** from the Jaguar dealer certifying that the high-voltage battery has been checked, repaired (modules replaced if necessary), and is not subject to any software charging restrictions. Also verify that the heater (HVCH) works perfectly.
