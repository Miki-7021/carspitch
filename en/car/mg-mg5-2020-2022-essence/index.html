MG MG5

BUY Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The **MG5** nameplate designates two radically different vehicles depending on the market, which requires an important clarification.

In **Europe**, the MG5 is known as the first 100% electric station wagon on the market (based on the Roewe Ei5). However, in **global markets** (Middle East, Asia, Oceania, Latin America/Mexico), the first-generation MG5 (2020-2022) is a **compact gasoline-powered sedan** (based on the Roewe i5).

Positioned as an ultra-competitive alternative to established benchmarks (Toyota Corolla, Hyundai Elantra), the gasoline MG5 shines with its unbeatable price/roominess/equipment ratio. Mechanically, it relies on SAIC-sourced engines (1.5L naturally aspirated and 1.5L Turbo) of classic but proven design. While the interior finish and soundproofing betray its aggressive pricing strategy, it proves to be a pragmatic and generally reliable choice for tight budgets.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional price/equipment ratio
  • Generous rear roominess (sedan and station wagon)
  • Long manufacturer warranty (often 6 or 7 years depending on the market)
  • Overall mechanical reliability of the naturally aspirated 1.5L block
  • Rewarding exterior design for the category

Weaknesses 5

  • Very slow and noisy CVT gearbox during hard acceleration (1.5 NA)
  • Slow multimedia system, unintuitive interface, and frequent bugs
  • Soundproofing could be improved above 100 km/h (road and wind noise)
  • Quality of the hard plastics omnipresent in the cabin
  • Sluggish road handling, steering lacking feedback

Check before buying

  • Full operation of the central touchscreen and Apple CarPlay/Android Auto connectivity.
  • Gearbox behavior during a test drive: look out for jerking on the DCT (Turbo) or whining on the CVT (NA).
  • Tire wear: the original tires (often from budget Chinese brands) wear out quickly and offer precarious grip in the rain.
  • Body panel alignment and paint quality (sensitive to micro-scratches).

Verdict

The **MG5 (Generation I, 2020-2022)** is the archetype of a pragmatic purchase. In its gasoline sedan version (sold outside Europe), it offers no automotive thrills but delivers exactly what is expected of it: moving a family from point A to point B at a negligible cost, with abundant equipment.

The **naturally aspirated 1.5L** engine is the sensible choice: its older design makes it very reliable, although its performance is limited by a lazy CVT gearbox. The **1.5L Turbo** offers better drivability, but its dual-clutch gearbox requires more vigilance in the long term. The main drawbacks of this model lie in the onboard electronics (temperamental infotainment) and soundproofing. It is a vehicle to **BUY** if you prioritize the price/volume ratio and the manufacturer warranty, but to **NEGOTIATE** firmly on the used market due to depreciation that is often faster than its Japanese or Korean competitors.
