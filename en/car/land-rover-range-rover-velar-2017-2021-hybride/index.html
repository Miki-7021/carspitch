Land Rover Range Rover Velar

I (L560) · 2017 – 2021

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Land Rover Range Rover Velar (L560) slots between the Evoque and the Range Rover Sport. Based on the iQ-Al platform (shared with the Jaguar F-Pace), it stands out with its avant-garde design, flush door handles, and uncluttered cabin introducing the *Touch Pro Duo* dual-screen system.

**Hybrid Focus:** To meet emissions standards, Land Rover introduced mild-hybrid (MHEV 48V) powertrains in late 2020 on its new inline 6-cylinder engines (Ingenium), as well as a plug-in hybrid (PHEV) version named **P400e**. The latter combines a 2.0L 4-cylinder petrol engine with an electric motor for a combined output of 404 hp. While the Velar shines with its comfort and style, its overall reliability is heavily penalized by recurring electronic bugs and mechanical frailties on certain combustion engines, typical of the JLR (Jaguar Land Rover) group during this period. An extended warranty is highly recommended.

Strengths 5

  • Spectacular and timeless exterior design
  • Interior finish quality and luxurious materials
  • Performance and taxation of the P400e PHEV version
  • Off-road capabilities (Terrain Response) preserved
  • Soundproofing and ride comfort (especially with air suspension)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous electronic and software reliability (black screens, various bugs)
  • Reliability of diesel engines (DPF, chain, crankshaft on V6)
  • Very high weight penalizing driving dynamics and fuel consumption
  • Average rear seat space for its size
  • Prohibitive maintenance and repair costs out of warranty

Check before buying

  • **Flush door handles:** The electric motors frequently fail (the handle no longer extends or retracts).
  • **Infotainment system:** Test both *Touch Pro Duo* screens. Sluggishness, random reboots, and black screens are commonplace.
  • **Air suspension:** Check that the vehicle raises and lowers without suspicious noise from the compressor. Look for leaks on the air springs.
  • **Hybrid System (P400e):** Demand a state of health (SOH) diagnostic of the battery and verify that the transition from electric to combustion is done without violent jerks.
  • **Maintenance history:** Steer clear of any model (especially diesel) that has followed the manufacturer's interval of 30,000 km / 2 years. Annual oil changes are mandatory.

Verdict

The Range Rover Velar is a 'heart over head' purchase. Its design is one of the most successful of its decade. The **P400e** plug-in hybrid version (and the 6-cylinder MHEVs) introduced at the end of the first phase corrects the course on performance and emissions, but does not erase Jaguar Land Rover's heavy legacy regarding electronic reliability. Infotainment bugs, door handle failures, and the quirks of hybrid modules are frequent.

**Buying advice:** If you opt for a P400e or an MHEV, demand a model with a crystal-clear history in the official dealer network, all software updates performed, and **imperatively subscribe to an extended mechanical breakdown warranty**. Absolutely avoid the 2.0L diesel engines (D180/D240) if you drive in the city.
