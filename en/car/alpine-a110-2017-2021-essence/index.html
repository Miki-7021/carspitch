Alpine A110

II · 2017 – 2021

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

All Alpine models

Summary

The second-generation Alpine A110 (often called A110 II or AS110) marks the spectacular rebirth of the legendary French brand. Designed around a 100% aluminum chassis and body, it stands out for its featherweight (around 1,100 kg) and its mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive architecture. Positioned as a lighter and more playful alternative to the Porsche 718 Cayman, it prioritizes driving sensations, agility, and suspension comfort over the pure horsepower race. Powered exclusively by the 4-cylinder 1.8 TCe (shared with the Renault Mégane IV RS) mated to a 7-speed EDC dual-clutch gearbox, it was available in Première Édition, Pure, and Légende versions, then in an S version (292 hp) from 2019 with a more radical chassis setup. Overall, it is a very well-born sports car with reassuring mechanical reliability, although a few teething problems (electronics, finish) marred the early model years.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (agility, lightness, road feel)
  • Unbeatable performance-to-fuel consumption ratio
  • Remarkable suspension comfort (Pure/Légende versions)
  • Lower maintenance and running costs than its direct competitors (Porsche, Lotus)
  • Very satisfactory overall reliability of the M5P engine block

Weaknesses 5

  • Dated, slow, and bug-prone infotainment system (Bosch)
  • Quality of certain interior plastics (Renault-sourced stalks)
  • Almost non-existent storage spaces (no glovebox, tiny trunks)
  • EDC gearbox sometimes considered less responsive than a Porsche PDK in extreme driving
  • Fragile windshield (sensitive to chassis stress and stone chips)

Check before buying

  • Usage history: look for signs of intensive track use (condition of brake discs, tire wear, impacts on the front end).
  • Windshield: check for micro-cracks, a very common issue on the A110 due to the rigidity of the aluminum chassis.
  • EDC gearbox: ensure there is no jerking when pulling away and check if fluid changes have been carried out in case of track use.
  • Underbody: the flat aluminum underbody can be damaged by speed bumps or track excursions.
  • Rattles and squeaks: the cabin tends to develop interior noises (creaking plastics, trim rattles) over time.

Verdict

The Alpine A110 (2017-2021) is a future collector's item and a masterful dynamic success. In terms of reliability, it comes out with flying colors: the 1.8 TCe engine is robust and the aluminum structure ages well. The main annoyances concern the onboard electronics (temperamental multimedia), some interior rattles, and the fragility of the windshield. When buying used, history is the number one criterion: a car that has seen a lot of track use will require special attention to the EDC gearbox (frequent fluid changes) and the running gear. If the maintenance record is crystal clear, it is a highly recommended passion purchase.
