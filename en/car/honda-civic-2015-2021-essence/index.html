Honda Civic

X (FC/FK) · 2015 – 2021

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The 10th generation Honda Civic (FC codes for the 4-door sedan, FK for the 5-door hatchback and coupe) marks a major turning point for the Japanese manufacturer. Designed on an entirely new global platform, it abandons the 'spaceship' design of the 8th and 9th European generations for a lower, longer, and more aggressive (fastback) silhouette. It stands out with an exceptional chassis, offering a first-rate comfort/dynamism compromise, and record-breaking interior space in the compact category. This is also the generation that democratized gasoline turbocharging at Honda with the VTEC Turbo engine family. A light facelift occurred in late 2019, improving interior ergonomics (return of physical buttons for multimedia and climate control) and correcting early defects. **Expert's overall rating: An excellent compact, penalized by some design flaws (air conditioning, 1.0L belt) but whose dynamic and practical qualities remain at the top.**

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional chassis: high-level comfort/handling compromise
  • Giant rear passenger space and trunk volume (478L on the 5-door)
  • Brilliant 1.5 VTEC Turbo engine (powerful and very fuel-efficient)
  • Low driving position and ergonomic steering wheel
  • Very rich standard equipment (Honda Sensing included from entry-level)
  • Absolute reliability of naturally aspirated engines (outside Europe)

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of the 1.0 VTEC Turbo (wet belt) unworthy of Honda's reputation
  • Endemic air conditioning problems (condenser/compressor)
  • Slow, dated, and unintuitive infotainment system (pre-facelift)
  • Imperfect sound insulation (road noise on the highway)
  • Imposing size for a compact (4.52 m long)
  • Slightly small fuel tank (46 liters)

Check before buying

  • STRICT service history on the 1.0 VTEC Turbo (specific oil, adherence to deadlines).
  • Air conditioning operation (must blow very cold quickly).
  • Oil level on the 1.5 VTEC Turbo (check if the level is above max, a sign of gasoline dilution).
  • Condition of front tires (the incisive front axle can wear out tires quickly depending on driving style).
  • Operation of driving aids (Honda Sensing radars sensitive to front impacts).
  • 2nd gear engagement on the Type R (when cold and at high revs).

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Volkswagen Golf VII/VIII (segment benchmark), Toyota Corolla (for hybrid and reliability), Mazda 3 (premium finish, naturally aspirated engines), Ford Focus (equivalent dynamism), Peugeot 308 II (same wet belt issue on the PureTech). - **North America:** Toyota Corolla, Mazda 3, Hyundai Elantra, Kia Forte, Subaru Impreza. - **Asia / Emerging Markets:** Toyota Corolla Altis, Nissan Sylphy, Mazda 3.

Verdict

The 10th generation Honda Civic is an engineer's car: brilliant on the road, spacious, and equipped with excellent 4-cylinder engines. **The ideal choice in Europe is the 1.5 VTEC Turbo**, preferably after 2018 to avoid early issues (oil dilution), or the very frugal 1.6 i-DTEC for high-mileage drivers. **The 1.0 VTEC Turbo is to be avoided** unless it has a clear service history and a recently changed belt, as it suffers from the same ailments as Stellantis' PureTech or Ford's EcoBoost. Outside Europe, the 2.0L and 1.8L naturally aspirated versions are monuments of reliability that justify a purchase with eyes closed. In all cases, imperatively check the proper functioning of the air conditioning before purchase.
