Toyota Hilux

VIII (AN120/AN130) · 2015 – 2020

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The eighth-generation Toyota Hilux (AN120/AN130 series), launched in 2015, perpetuates the model's reputation for indestructibility. Resting on a reinforced ladder frame chassis, it significantly improves ride comfort and technological equipment compared to the previous generation. Although the European market is almost exclusively dominated by diesel engines (2.4 and 2.8 D-4D), the Hilux is a global vehicle whose petrol engines (2.0, 2.7 and 4.0 V6) are extremely popular in the Middle East, Asia, South America, and Australia for their foolproof reliability. These petrol blocks, although thirsty, offer absolute peace of mind and exceptional tolerance to poor quality fuels and extreme conditions.

**Overall generation rating: 8.5/10** (penalized only by DPF/FAP issues on the diesels and the fuel consumption of the petrols).

Strengths 5

  • Monumental reliability of the petrol engines (2.7 and 4.0 V6)
  • Off-road capabilities and robustness of the ladder frame chassis
  • Exceptional resale value in all markets
  • Tolerance to extreme conditions (heat, dust, poor quality fuel)
  • Payload capacity (up to 1 ton) and towing capacity (up to 3.5 tons)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very high fuel consumption on petrol versions
  • Petrol engines impossible to find on the European market (import mandatory)
  • Recurring DPF/FAP issues on diesels (2015-2019) in urban use
  • Firm comfort when unladen (rear leaf suspension designed for load)
  • Dated and slow infotainment system

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: petrol engines are robust but require regular oil changes to preserve the timing chain.
  • Underbody: check for impacts, excessive rust, or damage related to intensive off-road use.
  • For diesels (if considered): check the DPF/FAP regeneration history and whether the Toyota software update has been performed.
  • Suspension and transmission: check for leaks at the axles, transfer case, and the condition of the driveshaft universal joints.
  • Airbox: check for dust downstream of the filter (known sealing issue in some dusty markets).

Practicality

  • Master of Load and Towing — Designed for work, the Hilux offers an impressive payload capacity of up to 1 tonne and a towing capacity of up to 3.5 tonnes, making it ideal for all your professional or leisure missions.

Safety

  • Optimal Safety with 5 Euro NCAP Stars — The Toyota Hilux VIII achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 89% for adult protection and 82% for child protection, ensuring a high level of safety.

Braking

  • Solid and Controlled Braking — Equipped as standard with ABS, Electronic Brakeforce Distribution (EBD), and Brake Assist (BA), the Hilux ensures effective and secure stops, even when loaded, for increased confidence on the road.

Handling

  • Refined and Stable Road Handling — Benefiting from significant improvements, the Hilux VIII offers a more stable and predictable drive. Its robust chassis ensures excellent road holding, suitable for versatile use on and off the beaten track.

Economy

  • Exceptional Resale Value — The Toyota Hilux is globally recognized for its ability to maintain exceptional resale value, ensuring an excellent return on investment and constant demand in the used car market.

Verdict

The 8th generation Toyota Hilux is a highly recommended purchase, particularly in its petrol versions (2.7L and 4.0L V6) which offer absolute mechanical reliability, free from modern emission control constraints (DPF/FAP, AdBlue) that affect diesels. If you live in a region where these models are available (or if you are considering an import), the only real deterrent will be the fuel budget. For European buyers restricted to diesel, favor models from late 2018/2019 onwards, where the DPF/FAP clogging issues were largely resolved by Toyota via the addition of a manual regeneration button and software updates.
