Kia Niro

I (DE) · 2016 – 2022

BUY Reliability 84/100 — bon

Summary

The **first-generation Kia Niro (DE)** marks a turning point for the Korean manufacturer: it is its first model based on a platform exclusively designed for electrified powertrains (shared with the Hyundai Ioniq). Positioned between the Stonic and the Sportage, this front-wheel-drive (2WD) compact crossover was first launched as a simple hybrid (HEV) in 2016, followed by the plug-in hybrid (PHEV) in 2017, and then by the 100% electric version (e-Niro) in 2018.

A **facelift (phase 2) occurred in mid-2019**, bringing double-arrow-shaped LED daytime running lights, higher quality interior materials (soft-touch plastics), a standard electric parking brake, and especially a new 10.25-inch infotainment screen. The Niro shines with its rationality: it offers the efficiency of a Toyota Prius in a highly popular SUV body, while retaining a dual-clutch gearbox (DCT6) that avoids the "revving" effect typical of competing e-CVT gearboxes. It is a pragmatic, overall extremely reliable choice, backed by the famous 7-year manufacturer's warranty.

Strengths 6

  • Very well-controlled real-world consumption on the HEV (often under 5L/100km in mixed use)
  • Pleasant dual-clutch gearbox (DCT6) compared to Toyota's CVT gearboxes
  • 7-year or 150,000 km manufacturer's warranty (covering the high-voltage battery)
  • Excellent interior ergonomics (physical climate controls retained)
  • Generous rear passenger space for its size (4.36 m)
  • Formidable efficiency of the 100% electric version (e-Niro)

Weaknesses 6

  • Fairly firm suspension comfort, especially with 18-inch wheels
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • Noisy combustion engine during heavy acceleration (hills, overtakes)
  • Average boot volume on the HEV (436 L) and very reduced on the PHEV (324 L)
  • Disappointing interior plastic quality on Phase 1 (before 2019)
  • e-Niro's fast charging speed (DC) limited to 77 kW (obsolete today)

Check before buying

  • DCT6 gearbox behavior (HEV/PHEV): no juddering or hesitation should be felt during hill starts or at very low speeds.
  • Coolant level: check for leaks at the exhaust heat exchanger (EHR) on hybrid versions.
  • Electric motor noise (e-Niro): listen carefully at low speeds during deceleration (regenerative braking). A continuous clicking noise indicates a faulty reducer bearing.
  • Condition of rear brakes: as regenerative braking is heavily used, rear discs and pads tend to oxidize or seize due to lack of mechanical friction.
  • Maintenance history: the 7-year warranty is conditional on strict adherence to the Kia maintenance schedule (very low tolerance for exceeding intervals).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2018 (HEV/PHEV):** Risk of overheating of the Power Relay Assembly (PRA) located under the rear seat. Replacement of the entire relay assembly.
  • - **June 2019 (HEV/PHEV):** Potential fluid leak at the hydraulic clutch actuator, which could lead to a short circuit and fire risk. Replacement of the actuator.
  • - **December 2020 (e-Niro):** Possible coolant leak inside the Electric Power Control Unit (EPCU), which could cause a loss of motive power.
  • - **2022 (HEV/PHEV):** Minor recall concerning the LCD instrument cluster which could turn off unexpectedly (software update).

Verdict

The Kia Niro I (DE) is an **excellent used car choice**, particularly in its simple hybrid (HEV) version, which offers the best compromise of price, performance, and reliability. It is aimed at rational buyers seeking the running costs of a Toyota without experiencing the "revving" effect of the e-CVT gearbox.

**Buying advice:** Strongly prioritize a **Phase 2 (post-2019)** model to benefit from a significantly more upscale interior and a modern multimedia system. Ensure that maintenance has been carried out on time within the Kia network to retain the benefit of the 7-year warranty. If you opt for the e-Niro 64 kWh, listen carefully during the test drive for any suspicious noise from the reducer.
