Summary

The first-generation Audi A5 (internal code B8 or 8T for the coupe/convertible, 8TA for the Sportback) was launched in 2007, marking Audi's return to the mid-size coupe segment. Based on the MLB (Modular Longitudinal Matrix) platform shared with the A4 B8, it stands out with an elegant and sporty design, penned by Walter de Silva. Initially available as a coupe, the range expanded with the convertible in 2009 and the Sportback (a five-door coupe sedan) also in 2009.

A major facelift took place in 2011 (2012 models), bringing aesthetic modifications (headlights, taillights, grille), interior improvements (MMI, steering wheel), and mechanical updates, notably the introduction of more efficient TFSI engines and the evolution of the TDIs.

The A5 B8 is renowned for its build quality, comfort on long journeys, and balanced road handling, especially with quattro all-wheel drive. General reliability is good, but some engines, particularly the early versions of the TFSI and certain TDIs, have experienced specific problems that require particular attention at purchase and rigorous maintenance. It is a vehicle that ages well aesthetically and mechanically if well maintained.

Strengths 6

  • Timeless and elegant design (coupe, convertible, Sportback)
  • Quality of finish and interior materials
  • Ride comfort and sound insulation (especially on long journeys)
  • Balanced road handling, excellent with quattro
  • Wide choice of powerful and efficient engines (especially diesel)
  • Good resale value for well-maintained models

Weaknesses 6

  • High maintenance and parts cost (Audi specific)
  • Reliability of certain gasoline engines (1.8/2.0 TFSI pre-facelift, S5 V8) with costly problems
  • Excessive oil consumption on some 1.8/2.0 TFSI pre-facelift
  • Potential problems on 2.0 TDI (injectors, EGR, DPF)
  • Limited rear seat space on Coupe/Convertible
  • MMI system sometimes dated on early versions

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (essential for at-risk engines).
  • Oil consumption on 1.8/2.0 TFSI (ask for oil top-up invoices).
  • Timing chain noises on cold start (TFSI, FSI, TDI V6/V8).
  • DPF and EGR valve operation on diesels (warning lights, regenerations).
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel on diesels (noises, clutch vibrations).
  • MMI operation (screen, buttons, GPS, Bluetooth).
  • Condition of suspensions and suspension arms (noises, play).
  • Presence of leaks (water pump, crankcase, turbo).
  • Check if manufacturer recalls have been performed (especially Dieselgate for TDIs).

Official recalls

Official Manufacturer Recalls (Examples)

  • 2016-2018 (Dieselgate): Engine control unit software update for diesel vehicles equipped with the EA189 engine (2.0 TDI, 3.0 TDI) to comply with emissions standards. Affected population: Wide range of Audi models, including the A5 B8. Check if the recall has been performed.
  • 2016 (Takata Airbags): Replacement of passenger and/or driver frontal airbag gas generators, potentially defective and at risk of projecting metallic fragments. Affected population: Vehicles produced between 2007 and 2011 (specific dates vary by batch).
  • 2012 (Fuel Pump): Risk of fuel leakage at the high-pressure pump. Affected population: Certain vehicles produced between 2011 and 2012.
  • 2011 (ABS/ESP): Potential problem with the ABS/ESP control module that could lead to a loss of braking assistance. Affected population: Certain vehicles produced between 2008 and 2011.

*It is imperative to check with an Audi dealership, using the chassis number (VIN), if all applicable recalls for the vehicle have been performed.*

Verdict

The Audi A5 B8 is an excellent choice for anyone looking for an elegant, comfortable, and well-finished coupe, convertible, or Sportback. However, engine choice is crucial for reliability and maintenance budget.

For diesels (our focus): * The 2.0 TDI (EA189) are economical and powerful, but pre-facelift versions (before 2011) with Siemens/Continental injectors should be closely monitored. Post-facelift versions are more reliable. An impeccable service history and verification of the Dieselgate recall are imperative. Ideal for high-mileage drivers. * The 2.7 TDI and 3.0 TDI V6 offer superior driving pleasure. The 2.7 TDI is often considered a good reliability/performance compromise. The 3.0 TDI is powerful but the timing chain can be a weak point (rare but very costly). These engines are perfect for long journeys and highway driving.

For gasoline engines: * AVOID 1.8/2.0 TFSI (EA888 Gen 2) produced before 2012, unless proof of an engine overhaul (pistons/rings) is provided. Oil consumption and the chain tensioner are ticking time bombs. * The 1.8/2.0 TFSI (EA888 Gen 3) post-2012 are much more reliable and an excellent choice. * The S5 4.2 FSI V8 and RS5 4.2 FSI V8 are enthusiast cars with incredible performance, but the timing chain is a major and very costly risk. Reserved for very comfortable budgets and connoisseurs. * The S5 3.0 TFSI V6 is an excellent alternative, more reliable and very powerful.

General advice: Prioritize a facelift model (from late 2011) for both gasoline and diesel engines, as many improvements have been made. A complete and transparent service history is non-negotiable. The A5 B8 remains a very pleasant car to live with, but the purchase must be thoughtful and informed.
