Summary

The **Renault Alaskan (Generation I, D23)** marks Renault's entry into the highly competitive one-ton payload pickup segment. Developed within the Renault-Nissan Alliance, it is technically a clone of the **Nissan Navara NP300 (D23)**, from which it borrows the ladder chassis, powertrains, and interior, while sporting a front end specific to Renault's identity. It also shares its platform with the Mercedes X-Class.

Its main asset lies in its multi-link rear suspension with coil springs (on Double Cab versions), offering significantly superior ride comfort compared to the traditional leaf springs of competitors, without sacrificing its towing capacity (3.5 tons). Withdrawn from the European market in late 2020/early 2021 due to ecological penalties and disappointing sales, it continues its career in South America (produced in Argentina). Overall, it is a robust vehicle, benefiting from the proven reliability of Alliance utility vehicles.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional suspension comfort for a pickup (multi-link rear axle on Double Cab)
  • 3.5-ton towing capacity and one-ton payload
  • Overall reliability of the 2.3 dCi (M9T) engine is very reassuring
  • Off-road capabilities (low range, optional rear differential lock)
  • Successful and distinctive front-end design

Weaknesses 5

  • Interior plastic quality (hard and scratch-prone, inherited from the Navara)
  • Dated and slow infotainment system
  • Imposing size (5.40 m) making urban maneuvers complex
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speed
  • Renault network sometimes unaccustomed to maintaining true ladder-frame 4x4s

Check before buying

  • **Chassis and underbody:** Inspect for rust, impacts, or deformations related to intensive off-road use.
  • **Rear suspension:** Check for sagging coil springs if the vehicle has regularly carried heavy loads (springs fatigue faster than leaf springs).
  • **4x4 transmission:** Test the engagement of 4H and 4LO modes. Shifting should occur without grinding or error lights.
  • **Anti-pollution system (2.3 dCi):** Check AdBlue refill history and absence of engine warning light (sensitive NOx sensors or AdBlue injector).
  • **Towing history:** A vehicle that has towed 3.5t daily will require particular attention to the clutch (manual gearbox) or gearbox oil (automatic gearbox).

Official recalls

  • **November 2017 (Steering):** Risk of detachment of the lower steering column joint. Concerns models manufactured until 2017.
  • **May 2018 (Child safety):** The child safety lock on the rear doors may unexpectedly deactivate.
  • **September 2020 (Suspension):** Possible replacement of rear suspension link arm mounting bolts that could loosen or break (common recall with the Nissan Navara).

Verdict

The Renault Alaskan is an excellent choice for those looking for a pickup combining true utility capabilities with above-average ride comfort. By choosing to clone the Nissan Navara, Renault ensured an extremely solid technical base. The 2.3 dCi engine (of Renault origin) is a reliable workhorse, especially in the 160 hp version. The 190 hp version is more pleasant for daily use but requires rigorous maintenance of its two turbos. It is a **recommended** purchase, provided you prioritize a model with a clear service history that has not been abused off-road or constantly overloaded.
