Summary

The Subaru Ascent (SK generation) is the largest SUV ever produced by the Japanese brand, designed specifically for the North American market to succeed the Tribeca. This 3-row family SUV (offering 7 or 8 seats) stands out for its generous roominess, its standard Symmetrical AWD, and the introduction of the 2.4L turbocharged flat-4 engine (FA24F). While the early model years (2019-2020) suffered from numerous teething problems (oil consumption, electronic and structural recalls), build quality and reliability have seen a clear improvement from 2021 onwards. It positions itself as a robust and safe alternative to the segment leaders, although its initial reliability falls short of some Japanese competitors.

Strengths 5

  • Permanent and very reliable Symmetrical AWD
  • Generous interior space (up to 8 real seats)
  • Excellent all-around visibility and active safety (standard EyeSight)
  • High towing capacity for the category (2,268 kg / 5000 lbs)
  • Safe and comfortable road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Hit-or-miss reliability on 2019 and 2020 model years (numerous early recalls)
  • Oil consumption on early models (FA24F)
  • Relatively high fuel consumption in the city
  • CVT gearbox drivability in dynamic driving
  • Lack of a hybrid alternative compared to the competition

Check before buying

  • Engine oil level (history of top-ups on 2019-2020 models)
  • Timing belt replacement (to be done at 100,000 km, interference engine)
  • Complete recall history (more than 10 major recalls on early model years)
  • CVT gearbox behavior (absence of jerks at low speed, TCU update performed)
  • Tire condition on versions equipped with 20-inch wheels (premature wear or bead damage)

Official recalls

The Ascent has been the subject of numerous recalls, mainly in its early years of production (NHTSA / Rappel Conso):

  • [2026-07-07] Labels (NHTSA 26V436000): Error on the Gross Axle Weight Rating (GAWR) label on 2019-2026 models.
  • [2023-09-21 & 2019-07-10] Driveshaft (NHTSA 23V647000 / 19V519000): Possible loosening of the driveshaft center support bolts, risking disconnection.
  • [2023-06-01] Suspension (NHTSA 23V392000): Improperly tightened front lower suspension arm ball joint nut (2023 models).
  • [2023-04-07] Tires (NHTSA 23V258000): Possible tire bead damage during mounting on 20-inch wheels (2023 models).
  • [2022-12-08] Electrical (NHTSA 22V907000): Improper fastening of the PTC heater ground bolt (risk of melting/fire, 2019-2022 models).
  • [2021-12-09] Transmission (NHTSA 21V955000): TCU programming error allowing the clutch to engage before the drive chain (2019-2020 models).
  • [2021-12-09] Steering (NHTSA 21V954000): Improper fit of the tie rod ends due to an incorrect tapered hole (2020 models).
  • [2021-07-29 & 2020-04-16] Fuel System (NHTSA 21V587000 / 20V218000): Failure of the low-pressure fuel pump in the tank.
  • [2019-12-02] Engine / PCV (NHTSA 19V856000): Possible separation of the aluminum PCV valve, debris from which can enter the engine.
  • [2019-12-02] Transmission (NHTSA 19V855000): Inaccurate transmission hydraulic pressure sensor.
  • [2018-08-02] Structure (NHTSA 18V508000): Missing spot welds on the B-pillar, compromising structural rigidity (very early 2019 models).

Alternatives

  • North America: Honda Pilot (benchmark for reliability), Toyota Highlander (available as a hybrid, very reliable), Mazda CX-9 (better driving dynamics, superior finish), Kia Telluride / Hyundai Palisade (excellent price/equipment ratio).
  • Europe: Since the Subaru Ascent is not imported, similarly sized alternatives are the Toyota Highlander, the Ford Explorer PHEV, or the Hyundai Santa Fe.

Verdict

The Subaru Ascent is an extremely capable, safe, and practical family SUV. However, its reliability record is split in two. The 2019 and 2020 models suffered from serious teething problems (oil consumption, transmission issues, cascading structural and electronic recalls). From 2021 onwards, Subaru corrected the course, offering a vehicle whose reliability matches the segment average.

Buying advice: Avoid the 2019-2020 model years if possible, or demand a crystal-clear maintenance history proving that *all* recalls (especially TCU, fuel pump, and PCV valve) have been performed. Favor a 2021 or later model. Do not forget to budget for the timing belt replacement at 100,000 km.
