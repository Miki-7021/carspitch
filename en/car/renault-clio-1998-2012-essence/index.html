Renault Clio

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The Renault Clio II is a versatile city car that marked a major turning point for Renault in terms of comfort, safety, and road handling. Marketed from 1998 to 2012 (until 2016 in some emerging markets under the names Clio Campus, Storia, or Symbol/Thalia), it underwent several evolutions: **Phase 1 (1998-2001)** with rounded lines, **Phase 2 (2001-2003)** with a redesigned front end, a higher quality interior, and the introduction of multiplexing, **Phase 3 (2003-2006)** with minor touch-ups, and finally the **Campus/Storia (2006-2012)** versions which coexisted with the Clio III.

On the used car market, the Clio II is a polarizing choice: **its petrol engines (our focus here) are generally very robust and economical**, while its early diesel engines (1.5 dCi) were plagued by catastrophic breakdowns. It is an ideal car for new drivers or as a second vehicle, provided you choose the right engine and monitor the electronic quirks (UCH) typical of the 2000s.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional reliability of small petrol engines (1.2 8v and 16v)
  • Healthy, safe, and comfortable road handling (especially from Phase 2 onwards)
  • Negligible maintenance and insurance costs (excluding RS and V6 versions)
  • Massive availability of spare parts (new and salvaged)
  • Generally good corrosion resistance (except rear wings on Phase 1)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of early 1.5 dCi engines (Delphi pump)
  • Capricious electronics on Phases 2 and 3 (UCH unit, phantom warning lights)
  • Aging interior plastics (peeling steering wheel, sticky plastics)
  • Polycarbonate front headlight lenses that tarnish and become opaque (failure at technical inspection)
  • Rear light connectors that melt (grounding issue)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (especially on 16v petrol engines, very intolerant to breakage).
  • Functioning of all electrical equipment: wipers, electric windows, central locking (symptoms of a faulty UCH unit).
  • Airbag warning light and 'Serv' light illuminated on the dashboard (often the connectors under the seats).
  • Condition of the front hood: check that the locking mechanism is not seized (risk of opening while driving).
  • On petrol versions: unstable idle (clogged throttle body or worn TDC sensor).

Official recalls

  • - **April 2007 (Major Safety)**: Recall to check and lubricate the engine hood locking system. Risk of unintentional opening while driving (concerns models produced between 1998 and 2006).
  • - **February 2002**: Risk of braking assistance failure (master cylinder) on certain models produced in 2001.
  • - **May 2002**: Replacement of the accelerator cable which could fray and jam (1998-2000 models).
  • - **December 2004**: Reprogramming of the injection computer on 1.5 dCi models to prevent turbo destruction.

Safety

  • Reassuring Passive Safety — The Renault Clio II offers solid protection for its occupants, confirmed by a 4-star rating in Euro NCAP tests.

Braking

  • Efficient and High-Performance Braking — Its braking system is high-performing for its class, with competitive stopping distances, often between 39 and 42 meters from 100 km/h in optimal conditions.

Handling

  • Agile and Comfortable Road Handling — The Clio II stands out for its excellent road handling, combining appreciable comfort with agile and engaging road holding, for daily driving pleasure.

Economy

  • Very Economical Running Costs — The petrol engines of the Clio II benefit from particularly low maintenance and insurance costs, making it a very economical choice to run.

Practicality

  • Wide Availability of Spare Parts — The massive availability of spare parts, both new and used, guarantees easy and affordable long-term maintenance.

Comfort

  • Appreciated Ride Comfort and Versatility — It offers appreciated ride comfort and versatility that makes it ideal for both city and longer journeys, especially from Phase 2 onwards.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Peugeot 206 (its main rival, more dynamic handling but inferior finish), Volkswagen Polo III/IV (better finished but less reliable petrol engines), Ford Fiesta Mk4/Mk5, Toyota Yaris I (superior reliability but smaller boot). - **Americas / Emerging Markets**: Fiat Palio, Chevrolet Corsa, Volkswagen Gol. - **Asia / Middle East**: Hyundai Accent, Toyota Vios.

Verdict

The Renault Clio II is an excellent used car **provided you choose a petrol engine**. The 1.2 (60 hp and 75 hp) units are true workhorses, indestructible and perfect for daily use at minimal cost. The 1.4 and 1.6 16v offer more versatility but require particular attention to ignition and timing. On the other hand, **1.5 dCi diesel versions before 2006 are absolutely to be avoided** due to the risk of destruction of the injection system (metal filings). Opt for a Phase 2 or 3 (after 2001) petrol model to benefit from a better level of safety (ABS as standard, better crash tests) while accepting a few minor electronic quirks.
