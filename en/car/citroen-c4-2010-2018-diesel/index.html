Citroën C4

II (B7) · 2010 – 2018

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The second-generation Citroën C4 (codename B7) marked a conservative turn compared to the audacity of the first iteration (abandoning the fixed-hub steering wheel and the coupé version). Based on the PF2 platform (shared with the Peugeot 308 I), it focused entirely on ride comfort, spaciousness, and a generous boot volume (408 liters). Facelifted in early 2015 (new 3D headlights, 7-inch touchscreen, integration of BlueHDi and PureTech engines), it had a discreet but solid career. In the used car market, diesel versions (HDi, e-HDi, BlueHDi) are ubiquitous and particularly sought after by high-mileage drivers, although they show major reliability disparities depending on the emissions control generations.

Strengths 6

  • Royal suspension comfort, typical of Citroën
  • Very refined sound insulation (especially on facelifted versions)
  • Excellent boot volume for the category (408 liters)
  • Remarkable reliability of the 2.0 HDi 150 and 1.6 HDi 92 engines
  • Very low real-world fuel consumption across all diesel engines
  • Significantly improved finish quality compared to the C4 I (soft-touch materials)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the AdBlue system on BlueHDi engines
  • Petrol engines (VTi, THP, PureTech) all affected by major design flaws
  • Slow BMP6/ETG6 automated manual gearbox, causing jerks and premature clutch wear
  • Clumsy road handling, focused solely on comfort (lack of dynamism)
  • Slow, buggy, and dated ergonomics of the multimedia system (SMEG)
  • Average rear passenger space considering the exterior dimensions

Check before buying

  • **AdBlue System (BlueHDi)**: Presence of white traces under the tank (at the rear), history of UREA warning lights, check if the tank has already been replaced.
  • **Injectors (e-HDi)**: Cold start, absence of knocking, stable idle.
  • **Timing Belt (PureTech)**: Condition of the belt via the oil cap (cracks, fraying). Demand annual service invoices.
  • **Gearbox**: On BMP6/ETG6, check for absence of juddering when starting in first gear. On 5-speed manual gearbox, check that reverse gear does not crunch.
  • **Electronics**: Proper functioning of the touchscreen, GPS, Bluetooth, and automatic air conditioning (problems with air blower or mixing flaps).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2020 (PureTech)**: Massive recall (code JZR) for inspection and replacement of the timing belt and vacuum pump on 1.2 PureTech engines produced between 2013 and 2017. - **February 2016 (Electrical)**: Risk of overheating and fire related to the starter motor wiring harness rubbing against the EGR valve. Models produced between May and September 2013. - **October 2015 (Bodywork)**: Replacement of door locks that may open while driving (production from September 2010 to June 2015). - **January 2014 (2.0 HDi)**: Risk of fuel leak on the injector return rail. Models produced until December 2012. - **December 2012 (Braking)**: Non-conformity of the ABS/ESP unit requiring reprogramming or replacement (production early 2012).

Alternatives

Verdict

The Citroën C4 II is an excellent compact car for high-mileage drivers seeking comfort at a low price, **provided you choose the right diesel engine**. The **2.0 HDi 150** is the top choice for its robustness, followed by the modest but indestructible **1.6 HDi 92**. The 1.6 e-HDi 115 is an acceptable compromise if the injectors have been replaced. However, **BlueHDi versions should be heavily negotiated or avoided** if the AdBlue tank is original, at the risk of a €1000 to €1500 bill. On the petrol side, steer clear of VTi, THP, and PureTech engines that haven't been meticulously maintained. Finally, opt for a manual gearbox or the true EAT6 automatic gearbox, avoiding the dreadful BMP6/ETG6 automated manual gearbox.
