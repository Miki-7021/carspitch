Summary

The Porsche 911 type 992 represents the 8th generation of the Stuttgart icon. Launched at the end of 2018 (2019 model year), it marks a subtle stylistic evolution but a technical revolution under the bodywork. All versions now adopt the "wide" body (previously reserved for 4-wheel drive or Turbo models). It introduces a new 8-speed PDK gearbox, a largely digitized interior (PCM 6.0) while retaining the central analog tachometer (until the facelift). In 2024, phase 2 (992.2) makes its appearance, marking a historic turning point with the introduction of the "T-Hybrid" hybrid system on the GTS version and the transition to a 100% digital dashboard. The 992 remains the absolute benchmark for everyday sports cars, combining supercar performance with very high overall reliability.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional versatility (usable daily)
  • Finish and assembly quality significantly improved compared to the 991
  • 8-speed PDK gearbox of formidable speed and intelligence
  • Imperial road handling (much more incisive front axle)
  • Strong value retention on the used market (especially GT3, T and GTS)

Weaknesses 5

  • Exhaust sound muffled by particulate filters (FAP/OPF) in Europe
  • Price of options still exorbitant
  • Increased size (imposing width on small roads)
  • Replacement cost of the Lithium-ion battery (often > €2000)
  • Some parasitic noises (rattles) in the cabin on degraded roads (door panels)

Check before buying

  • Condition and history of the battery (essential to use a trickle charger if the car is driven infrequently).
  • Operation of the Front Axle Lift system: check for leaks or abnormal noises.
  • Presence of error messages related to the dynamic engine mounts (PADM).
  • Coolant seepage around the water pump (known issue of modern Flat-6s, although reduced on the 992).
  • For models equipped with center lock nuts (GTS, Turbo, GT3): verify strict adherence to the tightening torque (600 Nm) and the absence of pending recalls.

Official recalls

  • October 2024 (Center lock nuts): Risk of fracture of the central wheel fixing nuts on certain models (911, 718, Panamera). Mainly concerns recent GT3, Turbo and GTS. Immobilization requested until inspection.
  • March 2024 (Glazing): Poor adhesion of the windshield and rear window glue, which could detach in the event of an accident. Concerns models produced between 2020 and 2024.
  • May 2023 (Airbags): Incorrect deployment of airbags due to a software or wiring issue on a small batch.
  • February 2021 (Suspension): The trailing arms of the rear or front axle may not have been tightened to the specified torque. Risk of loss of control.
  • 2021 (Braking): Software update of the electromechanical brake booster (risk of loss of braking assistance).

  • Legendary Road Holding — A global benchmark, its chassis offers surgical precision, phenomenal grip, and perfect balance, inspiring absolute confidence and actively contributing to your safety.

  • Supercar Performance — With powerful engines and an incredibly fast 8-speed PDK gearbox, the 992 delivers ballistic acceleration and an exhilarating driving experience, worthy of the most exclusive supercars.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Audi R8 (naturally aspirated V10, end of production), Mercedes-AMG GT (more muscle car oriented), Aston Martin Vantage, McLaren Artura (hybrid).
  • Japan/Asia: Lexus LC500 (naturally aspirated V8, more GT approach), Nissan GT-R (aging but high-performing).
  • North America: Chevrolet Corvette C8 Stingray / Z06 (unbeatable price/performance ratio, mid-engine rear).

Verdict

The Porsche 911 (992) borders on perfection in the sports car category. Its mechanical reliability is excellent, with the 3.0L and 4.0L blocks being benchmarks of robustness if well maintained. The only real black marks concern the onboard electronics (especially on the 2019-2020 model years) and the prohibitive cost of certain specific wear parts (Lithium battery, PADM mounts, PCCB brakes). Purchase is highly recommended, but favor a model benefiting from the *Porsche Approved* warranty to protect yourself against hefty electronic or peripheral bills. The Carrera S or GTS (992.1) version represents the ideal balance point.
