Summary

The Fiat Grande Punto (Type 199), designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro, marked a major stylistic and dimensional break for Fiat in the B-segment upon its launch in 2005. Based on the SCCS platform co-developed with General Motors (shared with the Opel Corsa D), it offers excellent interior space and safe road handling. However, this generation (before the 'Punto Evo' facelift in late 2009) suffered from numerous early defects, particularly in terms of electronics and interior finish. The diesel engines (MultiJet), although powerful and economical, require rigorous monitoring to avoid costly breakdowns. Overall note: Economical model to buy, but with very heterogeneous reliability.

Strengths 5

  • Exterior design by Giugiaro that has aged very well
  • Generous interior space for the category (B-segment)
  • Sound and safe road handling
  • Very affordable spare parts cost
  • Extremely reliable and economical to maintain FIRE petrol engines (1.2 and 1.4)

Weaknesses 6

  • Very high breakdown rate according to ADAC (6.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year) and TÜV (23.5% defects at 3 years)
  • Disastrous electronics reliability (power windows, central locking, various sensors)
  • 1.3 MultiJet engine very sensitive to clogging (EGR, DPF) and timing chain failures
  • Fragile 'City' electric power steering (steering angle sensor or faulty electric motor, costly repair)
  • Notorious structural rust (sills, wheel arches) on examples exceeding 100,000 km
  • M32 gearbox (on 1.9 MTJ and 1.4 T-Jet) with prematurely wearing bearings

Check before buying

  • Power steering: Turn the steering wheel from lock to lock while stationary with 'City' mode activated. No hard points, red warning light, or cut-out should appear.
  • Gearbox (M32): On 1.9 diesel and 1.4 T-Jet versions, listen carefully for any whistling in 5th and 6th gear, and check if the lever moves during acceleration.
  • 1.3 MultiJet engine: Listen to the engine when cold. A metallic rattling sound indicates a stretched timing chain (imminent risk of failure). Check oil change history.
  • Corrosion: Thoroughly inspect the sills, wheel arches, and floor, particularly on models that have been driven in cold/salty regions.
  • Electronics: Absolutely test all electrical functions (windows, ventilation, central locking, lights) which are frequent sources of breakdowns.

Verdict

The Fiat Grande Punto from the 2005-2009 period is a vehicle to approach with extreme caution, particularly with diesel engines. ADAC and TÜV statistics are unequivocal: it is among the least reliable vehicles in its category, plagued by capricious electronics, structural corrosion on high-mileage models, and fragile running gear.

If you are looking for a diesel: The 1.3 MultiJet is to be AVOIDED unless you have an impeccable service history (oil changes every 15,000 km maximum) and proof of recent timing chain replacement. The 1.9 MultiJet is mechanically more robust, but beware of the M32 gearbox and flywheel.

Paradoxically, the Grande Punto becomes a very smart purchase if you opt for a 1.2 or 1.4 8v (FIRE) petrol version. These engines are indestructible, parts cost next to nothing, and the design remains appealing. In all cases, a thorough inspection of the power steering (City) and bodywork is mandatory before purchase.
