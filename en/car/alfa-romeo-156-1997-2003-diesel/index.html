Alfa Romeo 156

I (932) · 1997 – 2003

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Alfa Romeo models

Summary

The Alfa Romeo 156 (Type 932) is a pivotal model that saved the brand at the end of the 90s. Voted 1998 Car of the Year, it stands out with Walter de Silva's masterful design (hidden rear door handles, prominent grille) and a chassis of formidable agility (double wishbone front suspension). Historically, it is the very first production car in the world to be equipped with a common rail diesel engine (Common Rail), dubbed JTD (Unijet). While the diesel engines proved to be monsters of robustness, the 156 suffers from a reputation tarnished by the fragility of its Twin Spark petrol engines, uneven interior finish on the early phases, and running gear that wears out prematurely. Phase 1 received an interior update in 2002, before the major exterior facelift (Giugiaro) at the end of 2003.

Strengths 5

  • Timeless design by Walter de Silva, magnificent lines
  • Exceptional chassis: incisive front axle, ultra-direct steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock)
  • Extremely robust JTD diesel engines (the blocks themselves are bulletproof)
  • Sound of the 5-cylinder 2.4 JTD, unique for a diesel
  • Legendary V6 Busso (for petrol versions)

Weaknesses 6

  • Flimsy running gear: front upper and lower wishbones need changing very (too) regularly (squeaking/clunking noises)
  • Catastrophic turning radius (worse than a commercial vehicle)
  • Perforating corrosion on phase 1 models (floors, side skirts, wheel arches)
  • Very fragile Twin Spark petrol engines (timing belt, oil)
  • Capricious electronics (airbag warning light permanently on due to connectors under the seats)
  • Quality of interior plastics on pre-2002 models (sticky, fragile)

Check before buying

  • Front suspension: Listen carefully for squeaks (like an old bed frame) or clunks over speed bumps. Sign of dead wishbones (very frequent).
  • Corrosion: Imperative to inspect the floors (lift the carpets if possible), the sills under the side skirts, and the spare wheel well.
  • Engine temperature (JTD): While driving, the needle should stabilize at 90°C. If it stays at 70°C, the thermostat is dead (classic failure, cheap but crucial part).
  • Timing belt history: On TS and V6, demand invoices less than 3 years / 60,000 km old. On JTDs, it's 5 years / 100,000 km.
  • Dashboard warning lights: The red airbag light is a classic (often just a loose connection under the passenger seat, but a reason for MOT failure).

Official recalls

  • 2000: Recall concerning the engine hood locking system (risk of opening while driving). Replacement of the latch.
  • 2001: Front brake hoses could rub against the rim or suspension, leading to a brake fluid leak.
  • 2002: Risk of a leak on the power steering rack.
  • 2004: (On the latest models) Rear seat belt anchorage.

What sets it apart 7

Design

  • Timeless and Award-Winning Italian Design — Voted Car of the Year 1998, the Alfa Romeo 156 captivates with its magnificent line by Walter de Silva, a masterpiece of elegance that transcends eras.

reliability

  • Legendary Robustness of JTD Diesel Engines — The JTD diesel engines, pioneers of Common Rail technology, are renowned for their exceptional robustness and generous torque, offering dynamic and reliable driving over long distances.

innovation

  • Pioneer of Common Rail Technology — The Alfa Romeo 156 was the very first production car in the world to introduce the Common Rail diesel engine (JTD), a major innovation that revolutionized the automotive industry.

handling

  • Exceptional Road Holding — Its exceptional chassis, ultra-direct steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock), and remarkable agility offer an engaging and precise driving experience, among the best in its category.

braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — Equipped with disc brakes on all four wheels and ABS as standard, the Alfa Romeo 156 offers good braking performance, with a firm pedal feel and optimal control.

driving_pleasure

  • Captivating Sound of the 5-Cylinder JTD — For enthusiasts, the 2.4 JTD engine offers a unique and melodious sound for a diesel, reminiscent of noble engines and adding to the driving pleasure.

safety

  • Well-Designed Active and Passive Safety — Beyond its excellent road handling and powerful braking, the Alfa Romeo 156 incorporates safety features to protect its occupants.

Alternatives

  • Europe: BMW 3 Series (E46) (benchmark in rear-wheel drive, high-performance 320d diesels but swirl flap issues), Audi A4 (B5/B6) (superior finish, robust TDIs but equally complex front axles), Peugeot 406 (excellent chassis, very reliable 2.0 HDi engines).
  • Japan / Asia: Lexus IS 200 (legendary reliability, rear-wheel drive, but no diesel), Honda Accord (bulletproof VTEC engines).
  • North America: The Alfa 156 was not officially imported to the USA/Canada.

Verdict

The Alfa Romeo 156 is a passion purchase, even in diesel. Paradoxically, it is the JTD engines (1.9 and 2.4) that offer the best overall reliability on this model, as the Twin Spark petrol blocks are too capricious. The 1.9 JTD is a real tractor capable of exceeding 400,000 km without opening the engine, while the 2.4 JTD offers rare driving pleasure for a diesel. However, the mechanical reliability of the diesel must not overshadow the chronic fragility of the running gear (wishbones) and the threat of rust on phase 1 models. Buy with full knowledge of the facts: favor a model with a sound body, even if it means having to rebuild the suspension. Avoid Selespeed gearboxes.
