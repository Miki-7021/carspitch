Mercedes GLC

W254 · 2022 – Présent (Génération X254 pour le SUV, C254 pour le Coupé)

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Mercedes-Benz GLC (X254) is based on the MRA2 platform, shared with the C-Class (W206). Launched in late 2022 (2023 model year), it marks a gentle stylistic evolution but a technological and mechanical revolution. **All engines are now electrified**, ranging from 48V mild-hybrid (MHEV) to plug-in hybrid (PHEV) equipped with a huge 31.2 kWh battery offering over 100 km of electric range.

Although demand for gasoline and PHEV is increasing, **diesel remains a major pillar** for this model, particularly in Europe for high-mileage drivers. The OM 654 M (M for Modified) block has been reworked (displacement increased to 1993 cc, increased injection pressure) and coupled with a second-generation integrated starter-generator (ISG) housed in the 9G-TRONIC gearbox. The GLC X254 positions itself as one of the most comfortable and technologically advanced premium family SUVs on the market, although its long-term reliability is still being evaluated due to its youth and high electronic complexity.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptionally fuel-efficient and powerful OM 654 M diesel engine
  • Electric range of PHEV versions (often > 100 real km)
  • Suspension comfort (especially with the AIRMATIC air suspension option)
  • Onboard technology (MBUX 2.0, Digital Light headlights)
  • Optional rear-wheel steering offering formidable maneuverability
  • PHEV battery integration without a 'step' in the trunk (unlike the W253)

Weaknesses 5

  • Significantly increased weight (PHEV versions easily exceed 2.3 tons)
  • Prohibitive purchase price and option costs
  • Steering wheel ergonomics with imprecise capacitive (haptic) buttons
  • Reduced trunk volume on PHEV versions (470L vs 620L for thermal models)
  • Early electronic bugs (MBUX, driving aids)

Check before buying

  • Software update history (MBUX and engine/hybrid management ECUs)
  • Smooth operation of the 9G-TRONIC gearbox (no jerks when shifting D/R or at low speed)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (very rapid wear on heavy PHEV versions)
  • Body panel alignment and interior trim noises (some complaints on early 2023 models)
  • On diesels: absence of AdBlue warning light and trip history (avoid vehicles used 100% in the city)

Verdict

The Mercedes GLC (X254) is a concentrate of technology that dominates its segment in terms of comfort and energy efficiency. **For high-mileage drivers, the 220 d or 300 d diesel (OM 654 M) is the absolute preferred choice.** It offers reassuring mechanical reliability, negligible consumption for its size, and high-level driving pleasure thanks to mild hybridization.

PHEV versions (including the unique 300 de diesel) are impressive on paper, but their technical complexity and massive overweight encourage caution for a long-term purchase (out of warranty). If you buy a 2023 model, ensure that all recalls (especially electrical and fuel pump) have been carried out and that the MBUX system is up to date to avoid early production electronic inconveniences.
