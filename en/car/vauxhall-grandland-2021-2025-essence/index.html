Summary

The Vauxhall Grandland I Facelift (known as the Opel Grandland in continental Europe) loses its "X" suffix during its mid-life facelift in 2021. Based on the Stellantis EMP2 platform (shared with the Peugeot 3008 II), it adopts the brand's new visual identity with the "Vauxhall Vizor" grille and modernizes its interior with the "Pure Panel" (dual digital screen). It is a comfortable and rational compact SUV, but its overall reliability is heavily burdened by the PSA (Stellantis) internal combustion engines, notably the 1.2 petrol (wet belt) and the 1.5 diesel (camshaft chain). The late introduction of the 1.2 MHEV with a timing chain brings a breath of fresh air at the end of its career.

Strengths 5

  • Highly successful modernized exterior design (Vauxhall Vizor)
  • Good level of suspension comfort and soundproofing
  • Improved interior ergonomics with the Pure Panel
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume (on ICE versions)
  • Top-flight performances on the 300 hp PHEV version (Hybrid4)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.2 petrol 130 hp (wet belt)
  • Very precarious reliability of the 1.5 diesel (7 mm camshaft chain)
  • Reduced trunk volume on plug-in hybrid versions (PHEV)
  • Infotainment system sometimes slow despite the update
  • Rapid depreciation on the used market due to the reputation of Stellantis engines

Check before buying

  • STRICTLY up-to-date service book (zero tolerance for delayed oil changes on the 1.2 Turbo).
  • Visual condition of the timing belt through the oil filler hole (1.2 Turbo 130).
  • Recall history (very numerous on this model, affecting steering, suspension, and fire risks).
  • Invoice for the replacement of the camshaft chain kit (1.5 Diesel).
  • Operation of the power steering assistance (critical recall in progress).

Official recalls

The Vauxhall/Opel Grandland has been the subject of numerous major safety recalls (official Rappel Conso data):

  • [2026-06-15] Power steering: Possible loss of steering assistance due to a failure of the dual-pinion electric power steering. Risk of accident.
  • [2026-02-23] & [2025-01-10] Front suspension: The fixing screws on the ball joint support (front lower suspension arm) can break. Risk of loss of control.
  • [2026-01-28] & [2025-01-31] Fuel leak (High pressure): Possible leak in the high-pressure pipe or at the high-pressure pump connection. Risk of fire.
  • [2025-09-19] High-Voltage Battery (PHEV): Defective high-voltage battery that can cause overheating. Risk of vehicle fire.
  • [2025-07-25] Camshaft chain (1.5 Diesel): Due to oil carbonization, the strength of the 7 mm camshaft chain can be reduced, leading to its breakage. Risk of engine failure and accident.
  • [2025-06-19] Fuel line: The low-pressure line can rub against the ignition coil screw, become damaged, and leak. Risk of fire.
  • [2025-03-07] Seat wiring harness: Incorrect routing of the wiring harness under the front seats that can cause a short circuit. Risk of fire.
  • [2023-11-27] AdBlue system (Diesel): Software error preventing the engine warning light (MIL) from illuminating if the urea (AdBlue) injector stops working. Environmental non-compliance.

Verdict

The facelifted Vauxhall Grandland is an SUV full of objective qualities (comfort, design, roominess), but it is undermined by the congenital flaws of the Stellantis engines of this era. Buying a 1.2 Turbo 130 hp (wet belt) or a 1.5 Diesel 130 hp (7 mm chain) is akin to a mechanical lottery, unless the definitive fixes have already been applied and invoiced. The PHEV versions are more recommendable but are targeted by a critical recall on the battery. The only truly serene choice is the recent 1.2 Turbo MHEV 136 hp, which finally benefits from a reliable timing chain.
