Summary

The third-generation Ford Galaxy (chassis code CDQ), launched in 2015, is a large family MPV that shares its platform with the S-Max and Mondeo. It positions itself as the most spacious and practical option in the Ford range, offering up to seven real seats and a generous boot volume. Designed for comfort on long journeys, it stands out with pleasant road handling and modern technological equipment for its time.

Although the MPV segment has declined in favor of SUVs, the Galaxy III has managed to retain a loyal customer base thanks to its intrinsic qualities. It received a minor update in 2019/2020, introducing new EcoBlue engines and the hybrid option, as well as minor technological improvements. Its overall reliability is considered good, with a few specific points of attention regarding the powertrains.

Strengths 6

  • Generous interior space and exemplary modularity (7 real seats)
  • Quality suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Safe and pleasant road behavior for an MPV
  • Robust and efficient 2.0 TDCi/EcoBlue diesel engines
  • Exceptional reliability of the 2.5 Duratec hybrid version
  • Good value for money on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption of EcoBoost gasoline versions (especially the 2.0L)
  • Reliability of early 1.5 EcoBoost models (pre-2018) to monitor
  • Potential AdBlue system problems on EcoBlue engines
  • Bulkiness in the city and parking difficulty
  • Somewhat austere design for some

Check before buying

  • Complete maintenance history, especially for 1.5 EcoBoost (check recalls/interventions on the cooling system).
  • Condition of the DPF and EGR valve on diesels, especially if predominantly urban use.
  • Functioning of the AdBlue system (warning lights, error messages) on EcoBlue models.
  • Wear of the dual-mass flywheel and clutch on manual diesel versions.
  • General condition of the interior and seat mechanisms (intensive family use).
  • Functioning of air conditioning and electronic equipment (touchscreen, driving aids).

Official recalls

Official Recalls (Examples, non-exhaustive list to be checked according to specific VIN)

  • 2016-2017 (various recalls): Potential problems related to the engine control module (PCM) that could affect engine operation or battery charging. (Affected population: certain vehicles produced between 2014 and 2017, including Galaxy/S-Max).
  • 2017 (Recall 17S09): Risk of fuel leakage at the fuel filter on certain diesel vehicles. (Affected population: certain vehicles produced between 2014 and 2017, including Galaxy/S-Max).
  • 2018 (Recall 18S07): Potential problem with the seat position sensor wiring, which could affect airbag deployment. (Affected population: certain vehicles produced between 2014 and 2018, including Galaxy/S-Max).
  • 2019 (Recall 19S24): Risk of engine overheating on 1.5 EcoBoost due to a coolant circulation problem. (Affected population: certain vehicles equipped with the 1.5 EcoBoost engine, including Galaxy/S-Max, Mondeo, Kuga, Focus, produced between 2014 and 2019. This is the major recall concerning the 1.5 EcoBoost).
  • 2020 (Recall 20S34): Potential problem with the braking system (brake hose) that could lead to a loss of effectiveness. (Affected population: certain vehicles produced between 2019 and 2020, including Galaxy/S-Max).
  • 2021 (Recall 21S26): Transmission control module software problem that could lead to unexpected engagement of the parking brake. (Affected population: certain hybrid vehicles produced between 2020 and 2021, including Galaxy/S-Max Hybrid).

Note: It is imperative to check the recall history for a specific vehicle via its Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) with a Ford dealership.

Verdict

The Ford Galaxy III is an excellent choice for large families looking for a spacious, comfortable, and pleasant-to-drive MPV.

  • For versatile family use and high-mileage drivers, the 2.0 TDCi (pre-2019) or 2.0 EcoBlue (post-2019) versions are the most recommended. The 2.0 TDCi is a very safe choice, while the EcoBlue offers more modernity but requires vigilance regarding the AdBlue system.
  • For predominantly urban and suburban use, the 2.5 Duratec Hybrid version is the most sensible choice, offering very low consumption and almost irreproachable mechanical reliability.
  • The 1.5 EcoBoost gasoline engines should be considered with caution for models prior to 2018. If you opt for this engine, ensure that the fixes have been applied or prioritize a post-2018 model. The 2.0 EcoBoost is powerful but fuel-thirsty.

Overall, the Galaxy III is a reliable vehicle if maintenance is followed and if the specific weaknesses of certain engines are taken into account. It is a credible and often more affordable alternative to its direct competitors.
