Volkswagen Polo

II (86C) · 1981 – 1994

AVOID Reliability 55/100 — moyen

Summary

The Volkswagen Polo II (86C generation) is an iconic city car from the 80s and early 90s. Available in "Coach" (small shooting brake), "Coupé" and "Classic" (sedan with boot, rare) body styles, it underwent a major facelift in 1990 (often called 86C 2F) modernizing its front end and interior. Although renowned for its overall robustness with petrol engines, the Polo's foray into the diesel world was more challenging. Designed to offer minimal fuel consumption during oil crises, the diesel engines (1.3 D then 1.4 D) are mechanical curiosities. The 1.3 D (MN code) is notoriously fragile, being derived from an aluminum petrol block that poorly withstood diesel compression ratios. The 1.4 D (1W) corrected some of these defects, but these versions remain today slow, noisy vehicles, intended for an informed audience or atypical collectors.

Strengths 5

  • Extremely low fuel consumption on diesel versions (often under 5L/100km)
  • General mechanical simplicity (absence of complex electronics)
  • Very generous boot volume on the Coach version
  • Common wear parts still very easy to find and inexpensive
  • Ideal size for city driving and parking

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.3 D (MN) engine from before 1990
  • Anemic diesel performance (0 to 100 km/h in over 20 seconds)
  • Non-existent soundproofing: diesels are deafening at high speeds
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (especially models before the 1990 facelift)
  • Braking from another era (non-assisted on early versions)

Check before buying

  • Overheating history and coolant condition (crucial on diesels to prevent head gasket failure)
  • Corrosion: battery tray (known issue, water then drips onto the fuse box), wheel arches, sills, fuel filler neck
  • Cold start on diesels: if the engine stumbles or smokes excessively, glow plugs or loss of compression
  • Engine mounts: heavily stressed by diesel engine vibrations
  • Condition of the exhaust line (frequent rust)

Alternatives

In Europe, the Polo 86C Diesel faced competitors often better motorized in diesel: - **Peugeot 205 1.7 D**: Infinitely more robust, more powerful XUD engine and a more comfortable car. - **Renault Super 5 1.6 D**: Reliable, very economical F8M engine, but also sensitive to head gasket issues if mistreated. - **Ford Fiesta Mk2 / Mk3 1.6 D**: Rough but indestructible engine. - **Opel Corsa A 1.5 D**: Isuzu-sourced engine, very reliable and powerful for its category.

Verdict

Buying a Volkswagen Polo II (86C) with a diesel engine today is either a challenge or a passion for the atypical. The 1.3 D engine is to be avoided due to its hazardous design (aluminum block) which almost inevitably condemns it to cylinder head problems. The 1.4 D (post-1990) is more recommendable, but remains an agricultural, noisy, and extremely slow engine. If you are looking for a Polo 86C for regular use or as a youngtimer, you should definitely turn to the petrol engines (1.0 or 1.3), which are models of reliability and driving pleasure. The diesel is only of interest to the collector seeking an absolute rarity or for ultra-local use at a negligible running cost, provided you find an example not eaten away by rust.
