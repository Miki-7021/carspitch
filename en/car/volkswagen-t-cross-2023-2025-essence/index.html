BUY Reliability 89/100 — excellent

All Volkswagen models

Summary

The first-generation Volkswagen T-Cross (C11) received a mid-life refresh (facelift) at the end of 2023, with deliveries starting in 2024. Based on the proven MQB-A0 platform (shared with the Polo), this urban SUV corrects the main flaw of phase 1: the perceived quality of its interior. The dashboard finally adopts soft-touch materials, the infotainment is updated (floating screen), and the exterior receives new light signatures, including optional Matrix LED IQ.Light headlights. Mechanically, Volkswagen foregoes hybridization (even mild) and diesel, focusing on optimized thermal gasoline engines (EA211 evo2 generation). It is a mature vehicle, very spacious thanks to its sliding rear bench seat, and its reliability is now solidly established.

Expert's overall rating: 8.5/10

Weaknesses 4

  • Total absence of hybridization (MHEV), penalizing fuel consumption in the city compared to competitors.
  • Rear door panel plastics still hard and prone to scratches.
  • DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox sometimes causes jerks at very low speeds.
  • Prices have become elitist for the urban small SUV category.

Check before buying

  • DSG7 gearbox behavior during a test drive: check for the absence of metallic noises or juddering during 1st-2nd gear changes.
  • Infotainment system (MIB3): ensure that the latest OTA (Over-The-Air) updates have been installed to avoid CarPlay/Android Auto connection bugs.
  • Body panel alignment and interior trim noises on degraded roads (although the facelift improved assembly).

Official recalls

The restyled T-Cross (2023+) benefits from the maturity of phase 1. Recalls are very rare at this stage:

  • February 2024 (Camera Software): Software update on some 2023/2024 models concerning a temporary black screen of the rearview camera when engaging reverse gear.
  • *Note: The early issues of phase 1 (seat belts, handbrake) have been completely eradicated at the factory on this restyled version.*

Verdict

The restyled Volkswagen T-Cross (2023-2025) is a highly recommendable purchase. By correcting its main flaw (the basic interior finish of phase 1) and relying on TSI evo2 engines whose reliability is now excellent, it stands out as a safe bet. The absence of hybridization is its only real weakness compared to the competition, but this, in turn, guarantees a proven and less complex mechanical system.

Buying advice: The 1.0 TSI 115 hp represents the perfect balance. If you drive a lot in the city, prefer the manual gearbox to avoid wear on the dry-clutch DSG7, or opt for the DSG7 by adopting a smooth driving style in traffic jams.
