Summary

The first-generation Hyundai Kona (OS code) is an urban SUV (B-segment) with a bold design that marked a turning point for the Korean brand. Initially launched with internal combustion engines, it particularly distinguished itself with its Kona Electric version (launched in 2018), which democratized access to a long electric range thanks to excellent energy efficiency. A facelift occurred in late 2020, softening the front fascia and modernizing the infotainment. Although rear passenger space and trunk volume are behind the competition, the Kona shines with its technological features, ergonomics, and 5-year manufacturer's warranty. The electric version achieved immense commercial success, though it was marred by a major global recall concerning its high-voltage batteries.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional energy efficiency of the electric version (Kona EV)
  • Real-world range of the 64 kWh version (often > 400 km)
  • Retained physical ergonomics (real buttons for climate control)
  • 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer's warranty (8 years/160,000 km for the HV battery)
  • Very rich standard equipment from intermediate trims
  • Overall reliability of the hybrid version (HEV)

Weaknesses 6

  • Very average rear passenger space for the category
  • Limited trunk volume (332 liters on the electric version)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (hard and scratch-prone)
  • Traction on the front axle could be improved (especially on the 64 kWh EV with original Nexen tires)
  • Heavy history regarding the high-voltage battery recall (EV)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)

Check before buying

  • Battery history (Kona EV): Demand the battery pack replacement certificate or proof of the BMS update (official recall).
  • Reducer noise (Kona EV): During a test drive, listen carefully at low speeds (deceleration/regeneration). A clicking or 'wheel of fortune' noise indicates a reducer bearing or motor failure.
  • 12V Battery (Kona EV & HEV): Tends to discharge quickly if the car remains stationary or if the Bluelink system over-solicits it.
  • DCT7 Gearbox (1.6 T-GDI): Check for any shuddering or hesitation during hill starts or at very low speeds.
  • Oil consumption (2.0 MPI): For imported or North American models, check the engine recall history (piston rings).
  • Paint: White and blue paint colors tend to flake prematurely on the front bumpers.

Official recalls

- High-Voltage Battery (2020-2021): Major global recall (campaign 01D042 / 11D040) for risk of short-circuit and fire on LG Chem cells. Concerns Kona EVs produced between 2018 and March 2020. Complete pack replacement or software update depending on diagnosis.,- IEB Braking (2020): Software update for the Integrated Electronic Brake system following a risk of loss of braking assistance (Kona EV and HEV).,- 2.0 MPI Engine (2021 - North Amer.): Recall for defective piston rings that can scratch cylinders, cause excessive oil consumption, and engine shutdown.,- Seatbelts (2022): Risk of incorrect pretensioner deployment in case of impact (ICE Konas).

What sets it apart 12

Safety

  • Recognized Top-Tier Safety — The Hyundai Kona Electric achieved a maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with high scores for adult (87%) and child (85%) protection, and was named an IIHS 'Top Safety Pick' from 2018 to 2022, ensuring peace of mind on the road.
  • Reliable and Reassuring Braking — Experience a competent and consistent braking system, offering a reassuring pedal feel and effective stopping power, typical for its segment, for optimal control in all conditions.

Performance

  • Agile and Engaging Road Handling — The Kona Electric offers agile and pleasant road handling, with excellent body control and responsive steering, ensuring a stable and confident drive, ideal for both city and winding roads.

Economy

  • Exceptional Electric Range and Record Efficiency — With its 64 kWh version, the Kona Electric delivers a real-world range often exceeding 400 km and boasts some of the best energy efficiency on the market, significantly reducing your fuel costs and charging stops.

Reliability

  • Extended Manufacturer Warranty for Lasting Serenity — Benefit from a 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer warranty and an 8-year/160,000 km warranty for the high-voltage battery, demonstrating Hyundai's confidence in the durability of its electric vehicle.

Comfort

  • Generous Equipment and Intuitive Ergonomics — From intermediate trims, the Kona Electric offers very rich standard equipment and retains appreciated physical ergonomics with real buttons for climate control, providing daily comfort and ease of use.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot e-2008 (less range), Kia e-Niro (technical cousin, more spacious), Renault Captur E-Tech (for the hybrid), MG ZS EV (budget alternative).
  • North America: Chevrolet Bolt EV (similar battery recall history), Kia Niro EV, Subaru Crosstrek (for the ICE AWD).
  • Asia / Japan: Honda Vezel / HR-V, Toyota C-HR, Nissan Kicks e-Power.

Verdict

The first-generation Hyundai Kona is an excellent choice, particularly in its Hybrid (HEV) and Electric (EV) versions. The Kona Electric 64 kWh remains, even years after its release, one of the most efficient electric vehicles on the market. However, buying a used electric model (2018-2020) requires absolute vigilance: it is imperative to verify that the high-voltage battery recall campaign has been carried out by Hyundai. If the battery has been replaced, you benefit from a new pack on a used car, which makes it a golden opportunity. Also, listen for transmission noises (reducer). The internal combustion versions are generally reliable, with the exception of the sometimes capricious DCT7 gearbox and the North American 2.0 MPI, which should be avoided without a clear history.
