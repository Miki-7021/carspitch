Hyundai BAYON

· 2021 – présent

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The Hyundai Bayon is an urban crossover (B-segment) launched in 2021, based on the technical platform of the i20 city car (BC3 generation). Designed specifically for the European market, it is positioned below the Kona in the Korean manufacturer's range. It stands out with a divisive and bold design, generous rear spaciousness for its size (4.18 m), and a very decent trunk volume (411 liters). Its major asset in the used car market is its fully transferable 5-year manufacturer's warranty (unlimited mileage), offering excellent peace of mind to second-hand buyers.

Strengths 5

  • Very reassuring 5-year manufacturer's warranty (unlimited mileage)
  • Surprising rear spaciousness for its size
  • Generous trunk volume (411 liters, except for 48V version where the battery reduces underfloor space)
  • Overall reliability of powertrains (proven across the Hyundai/Kia range)
  • Rich and technological standard equipment (digital gauges, large touchscreen)

Weaknesses 5

  • Interior plastic quality (ubiquity of hard plastics prone to scratches)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • Slightly firm damping at low speeds
  • 7DCT gearbox sometimes hesitant during very low-speed maneuvers
  • 1.2 MPI 84 hp engine too weak for versatile use

Check before buying

  • Service book: must be meticulously up-to-date within the network or according to manufacturer's recommendations to maintain the 5-year warranty.
  • 7DCT gearbox behavior: test the vehicle when cold and in traffic to detect any potential juddering or jerks on startup.
  • Multimedia system: check for absence of bugs, unexpected restarts, and proper functioning of Apple CarPlay / Android Auto.
  • Condition of wheels and bumpers: urban-oriented vehicle, often subject to minor scrapes.
  • iMT system operation (on 48V versions): ensure that the 'coasting' mode activates correctly when lifting off the accelerator.

Alternatives

- **Kia Stonic** : Its direct technical cousin. More classic design, slightly different interior, but identical dynamic and mechanical performance. - **Renault Captur II** : Segment benchmark. Superior interior finish, more cushioned comfort, and availability of full hybrid powertrains (E-Tech). - **Ford Puma** : The choice for dynamism. Much more agile chassis, powerful EcoBoost engines, and a clever trunk with the 'MegaBox'. - **Toyota Yaris Cross** : The urban alternative. Unbeatable fuel consumption thanks to its HSD hybrid system, but reduced rear spaciousness and sound insulation.

Verdict

The Hyundai Bayon is an extremely rational and reassuring choice in the used urban SUV market. While it may not turn heads with luxurious materials or sporty performance, it shines with its overall reliability, optimized interior space, and its 5-year warranty which still covers the majority of models available second-hand. The 1.0 T-GDi 100 hp engine coupled with the manual gearbox (or iMT) represents the best compromise. The 7DCT dual-clutch gearbox is pleasant for daily use but requires careful checking of its behavior during a test drive, especially if the vehicle has been driven extensively in the city.
