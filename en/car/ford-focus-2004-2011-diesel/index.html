Ford Focus

II (DA3) · 2004 – 2011

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

All Ford models

Summary

The second-generation Ford Focus (DA3), launched in late 2004 and facelifted in early 2008 (adopting the *Kinetic Design*), is based on the excellent C1 platform developed jointly with Mazda (Mazda 3) and Volvo (S40/V50/C30). It established itself as the benchmark in its category for road handling, offering an exceptional comfort/handling compromise thanks to its *Control Blade* multi-link rear suspension.

Mechanically, this generation saw the massive integration of diesel engines from the PSA/Ford partnership (1.6 and 2.0 TDCi), alongside the 100% Ford 1.8 TDCi block. While the dynamic qualities and interior space are undeniable, overall reliability varies greatly depending on the engine chosen, with the 1.6 TDCi 109 hp alone accounting for a large proportion of the serious failures of this model. The petrol versions, of Ford, Mazda, or Volvo origin (for the ST/RS), prove to be significantly more robust.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional chassis: precise steering and comfort/handling compromise
  • Interior space and boot volume (396 litres) among the best of its generation
  • Reliability of petrol engines (1.6 100 hp and 2.0 145 hp leading the way)
  • Enjoyment and robustness of the 2.0 TDCi 136 hp
  • Availability of very economical original Flexifuel (E85) versions
  • Cost of spare parts and ease of maintenance (excluding 1.6 TDCi)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 TDCi 109 hp (turbo, injectors, DPF)
  • Endemic problem of instrument cluster (dashboard) solders on all versions
  • Imperfect sound insulation (road and aerodynamic noise on the motorway)
  • Average interior plastic quality and assembly before the 2008 facelift
  • Fragile dual-mass flywheels on diesels (1.8 and 2.0 TDCi)
  • Electro-hydraulic power steering pump (EHPAS) prone to leaks or failures

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history (1.6 TDCi): Demand invoices proving regular oil changes and check for the absence of black tar around the injectors.
  • Instrument cluster: Lightly tap the top of the dashboard cowl with the engine running; if warning lights illuminate or the engine cuts out, the solders are faulty.
  • Flywheel (Diesels): Listen for metallic noises at idle and check for abnormal vibrations in the clutch pedal at mid-travel.
  • Water ingress: Lift the boot carpet (leaky vent seals or hinges) and check the spark plug wells on petrol engines.
  • Power steering: Check for the absence of groaning noises when steering at a standstill and inspect the fluid level (reservoir often hidden under the right headlight).

Official recalls

  • April 2008: Risk of corrosion of the cooling fan control module (models produced between 2004 and 2006 in cold/salty regions), potentially leading to a short circuit or even a fire.
  • January 2009: Non-conformity of the non-return valve of the brake assist vacuum pump on 2.0 TDCi engines (production 2005-2007).
  • February 2010: Risk of fuel return pipe leak on 1.8 TDCi engines.
  • May 2012: Rear wheel hub fastening problem (non-compliant screws) on a small series produced in 2011.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Volkswagen Golf V / VI (higher trim, TDI with pump-injector then Common Rail), Peugeot 308 I (shares the same 1.6/2.0 HDi diesel engines), Renault Mégane II / III (comfortable, dCi made reliable on the III), Honda Civic VIII (bold design, ultra-reliable 2.2 i-CTDi engine).
  • Asia / Japan: Mazda 3 (BK/BL) (shares the same platform and petrol engines, similar handling), Toyota Corolla/Auris (superior reliability, hybrid available on Auris).
  • North America: *Note: The Focus DA3 was not sold there.* Local alternatives: Honda Civic, Toyota Corolla, Mazda 3.
  • Emerging markets: Chevrolet Cruze, Hyundai Elantra.

Verdict

The Ford Focus II is a car with two faces. Featuring a brilliant chassis and excellent interior space, it makes a very smart purchase in its petrol version (especially the 1.6 100 hp blocks or Flexifuel versions) or with the robust 2.0 TDCi diesel. However, the very common 1.6 TDCi 109 hp is a real source of trouble that has ruined the model's reputation; it is to be avoided unless it has been scrupulously made reliable. Whatever the model, budget for the inevitable repair of the instrument cluster solders. This is a vehicle to NEGOTIATE with caution, exclusively targeting the good engine options.
