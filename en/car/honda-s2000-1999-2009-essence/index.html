Honda S2000

AP1/AP2 · 1999 – 2009

ACHETER Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The **Honda S2000 (AP1/AP2)** is a legendary front-mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive roadster introduced to celebrate Honda's 50th anniversary. Renowned for its 50/50 weight distribution, minimalist driver-focused cabin, and one of the best manual transmissions ever made, it remains a benchmark for analog sports cars.

It is divided into two main iterations: * **AP1 (1999–2003):** The rawest version, featuring the 9,000 RPM F20C engine. Known for its aggressive VTEC crossover and a lively rear end prone to 'snap oversteer' at the limit. * **AP2 (2004–2009):** A comprehensive facelift and mechanical update. It introduced revised suspension geometry (reducing snap oversteer), cosmetic updates, and a stronger transmission with carbon synchronizers. In North America and Japan, it received the 2.2L F22C1 engine (8,000 RPM redline) for better mid-range torque. Europe retained the 2.0L F20C throughout the entire production run (often dubbed 'AP1.5' by enthusiasts for 2004+ EU models).

*Note: The S2000 was exclusively offered with naturally aspirated 4-cylinder petrol engines and a 6-speed manual gearbox. No automatic, diesel, hybrid, or EV variants were ever produced.*

Strengths 5

  • Bulletproof engine internals (forged pistons, fiber-reinforced cylinder liners) when properly maintained
  • One of the best 6-speed manual transmissions in automotive history
  • Timeless exterior design that ages exceptionally well
  • Incredible chassis dynamics and 50/50 weight distribution
  • Extremely low depreciation; values are steadily appreciating

Weaknesses 5

  • Snap oversteer on early AP1 models can catch inexperienced drivers off guard
  • Lack of low-end torque makes city driving feel sluggish
  • High cabin noise levels (wind and road noise) on highway journeys
  • Soft top is prone to tearing at specific stress points (latches and B-pillar area)
  • Cramped interior for taller drivers (over 6'2" / 1.88m)

Check before buying

  • Timing Chain Tensioner (TCT): Listen for a ticking/rattling noise at warm idle.
  • Oil Level: F20C engines can consume up to 1L per 1,000 miles under heavy VTEC use. Starvation destroys the engine.
  • Alignment Bolts: Check if caster/camber bolts are seized in the compliance bushings.
  • Soft Top Condition: Inspect for patches or tears behind the side windows and check latch tension.
  • Rear Axle Nuts: Listen for a clicking noise from the rear wheels when accelerating/decelerating (requires re-torquing to a higher spec).
  • Clutch Master Cylinder: Look for fluid leaking down the clutch pedal inside the footwell.

What sets it apart 12

Performance

  • Legendary VTEC Engine with Exceptional Output — The heart of the S2000, the F20C (or F22C1) engine, is an atmospheric engineering marvel. It holds the world record for specific output for a production engine, delivering impressive power and an exhilarating redline, guaranteeing unique driving sensations.
  • Manual Gearbox with Unparalleled Precision — The 6-speed manual gearbox is universally praised as one of the best in automotive history. Its short, precise shifts provide pure driving pleasure and a perfect connection between driver and machine.

  • Balanced and Responsive Sports Chassis — Thanks to its ultra-rigid 'X-bone' chassis and near-perfect weight distribution, the S2000 offers exceptional dynamic behavior. Its precise steering and legendary agility guarantee an unforgettable sporty driving experience.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Porsche Boxster (986/987) for more refinement and flat-6 sound; BMW Z4 (E85) for a torquier inline-6 experience. - **Japan/Asia:** Mazda MX-5 Miata (NB/NC) for a lighter, less powerful but equally fun alternative; Nissan 350Z for a heavier, V6-powered GT feel. - **North America:** Chevrolet Corvette (C5) for V8 power and similar pricing on the used market; Pontiac Solstice GXP / Saturn Sky Redline.

Verdict

The Honda S2000 is a masterpiece of automotive engineering and a guaranteed future classic. Its reliability is outstanding for a high-performance sports car, provided it has not been abused or starved of oil.

**Which one to choose?** If you want the purest, most frantic race-car experience, seek out an **AP1**, but budget for an AP2 valve retainer upgrade and a new TCT. If you prefer a more refined, predictable, and slightly torquier daily driver, the **AP2 (F22C1)** is the superior choice.

Avoid heavily modified examples or cars with a history of oil starvation. A well-documented maintenance history is mandatory.
