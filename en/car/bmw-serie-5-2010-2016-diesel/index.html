BMW Série 5

· 2010 – 2016 (F10/F11, LCI à partir de 2013)

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The sixth-generation BMW 5 Series (F10/F11) marks a return to a more consensual and elegant design after the controversial E60 era (Bangle design). It shares its platform with the 7 Series (F01), which gives it a road behavior more oriented towards imperial comfort than pure sportiness. Featuring exceptional finish quality and introducing the fantastic ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox, it established itself as the benchmark for executive sedans. However, its mechanical reliability is very heterogeneous: while some engines are true benchmarks for longevity, others (especially the first 4-cylinder diesels and the V8 petrol) were marred by serious design flaws requiring absolute vigilance when buying used.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional ride comfort and sound insulation worthy of the 7 Series
  • Responsive, smooth, and reliable ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox (ZF8HP)
  • Interior finish quality and material aging
  • Pleasure and fuel efficiency of the 6-cylinder diesel engines (N57)
  • Ergonomics of the iDrive system (especially post-2013 with the NBT system)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the V8 petrol (550i) and pre-2014 4-cylinder diesel (N47)
  • Imposing size and high weight (less agile than the E60 generation)
  • Very fragile rear air suspension on the wagon (Touring F11)
  • High maintenance cost at official BMW dealerships
  • Electric power steering lacking feel (especially before the facelift)

**N47**2.0 L4 Diesel518d, 520d, 525d (2010-2014)**Critical.** Major problem of timing chain stretch and breakage (located at the rear of the engine). EGR valve clogging.
**B47**2.0 L4 Diesel518d, 520d (2014-2017)**Very good.** Redesigned engine, chain problem resolved. Some cases of EGR cooler leaks (subject to recalls).
**N57**3.0 L6 Diesel525d, 530d, 535d, M550d**Good.** Robust engine but prone to EGR cooler leaks (fire risk, massive recall). Intake manifold flap (Swirl flaps) clogging. Timing chain stronger than the N47 but to be monitored after 200,000 km.
**N53**3.0 L6 Petrol523i, 528i, 530i (2010-2011)**Average.** Recurring problems with piezoelectric injectors, high-pressure fuel pump (HPFP), ignition coils, and NOx sensors.
**N20**2.0 L4 Petrol520i, 528i (2011-2017)**Average.** Fragility of the timing chain and oil pump drive on models produced before March 2015 (risk of engine failure).
**N55**3.0 L6 Petrol535i, ActiveHybrid 5**Good.** Generally reliable. Weak points: electric water pump (often fails around 100-120k km), oil leaks (valve cover gasket, oil filter housing).
**N63**4.4 V8 Petrol550i**Catastrophic.** Premature wear of valve stem seals (massive oil consumption), faulty injectors, chain wear, rapid battery discharge. To be avoided.
**S63**4.4 V8 PetrolM5**Average.** Derived from the N63 but slightly better designed. Rod bearing wear to be monitored, significant oil consumption, fragile coils.

Check before buying

  • **Timing chain history** : Imperative on N47 (diesel) and N20 (pre-2015 petrol) engines. Listen for any 'cricket' or metallic rubbing noise when cold.
  • **Rear suspension (F11 Touring)** : Check that the rear of the vehicle does not sag after prolonged parking (leaking air springs or faulty compressor).
  • **Automatic gearbox oil change** : BMW advertises 'lifetime' oil, but the manufacturer ZF recommends an oil change with filter replacement every 80,000 to 100,000 km. Demand proof of this operation.
  • **EGR cooler (Diesels)** : Check if the vehicle has been subject to the manufacturer's recall concerning the EGR valve cooler (risk of coolant leak and fire).
  • **Oil leaks (6-cylinder petrol)** : Inspect the valve cover gasket and oil filter housing on the N55 engine (535i).
  • **Water ingress** : Check for moisture in the rear carpets (detachment of the sealing film inside the doors).

Verdict

The BMW 5 Series F10/F11 is a masterful executive car that has aged remarkably well. However, it is a vehicle where the choice of engine entirely dictates the ownership experience. Versions equipped with the 6-cylinder diesel (530d) are the perfect compromise between performance, consumption, and reliability, provided that maintenance is rigorous and EGR recalls have been performed. Buyers of 4-cylinder diesels must absolutely target post-2014 models (B47 engine) to avoid the N47's timing chain roulette. Finally, steer clear of the 550i, whose V8 is a financial black hole. A 530d LCI (post-2013) with a clear history and a drained gearbox is a highly recommendable purchase.
