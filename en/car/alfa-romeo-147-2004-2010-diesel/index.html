Summary

The Alfa Romeo 147 Phase 2 (Type 937) was restyled in late 2004 by Giorgetto Giugiaro, abandoning Walter de Silva's original lines for a more aggressive front end, inspired by the 159. A premium compact ahead of its time, it stands out for its exceptional road handling thanks to its double wishbone front axle. While interior finish improved with the restyling, the 147 remains a demanding car in terms of maintenance. On the used car market, diesel engines (JTD and JTDM) are the most common and, paradoxically, often more robust than the Twin Spark petrol units, provided that emission control peripherals and the intake system are monitored. The Q2 version (Torsen limited-slip differential) which appeared in 2006 is particularly sought after for its traction and the robustness of its transmission.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional road handling (incisive front axle)
  • Ultra-direct hydraulic steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock)
  • Timeless design, especially in Phase 2
  • Robustness of the 1.9 JTD engine block (indestructible bottom end)
  • Warm interior presentation and 'cannocchiale' gauges
  • Q2 versions offering sports car-like traction

Weaknesses 7

  • Very fragile running gear (upper/lower wishbones to be changed every 60-80,000 km)
  • Catastrophic turning radius (difficult in town)
  • Capricious electronics (airbag warning lights, CANBUS errors)
  • Risk of engine failure on 150hp JTDM (intake manifold flaps)
  • Overly demanding maintenance for Twin Spark petrol engines (timing belt)
  • Unreliable and slow Selespeed gearbox
  • Average sound insulation on the highway

Check before buying

  • Front suspension noises (squeaks like an 'old bed' = worn upper wishbones; clunks = anti-roll bar or lower wishbones).
  • Timing belt history (imperative: 4 years/100,000 km on JTD, 3 years/60,000 km on Twin Spark).
  • On JTDM 16v: ask if the intake manifold has been replaced or if the 'swirl flaps' have been removed.
  • Airbag warning light on (often due to connectors under the front seats).
  • Thermostat operation (temperature needle should rise to 90°C and stay there. If it stays at 70°C on the road, the thermostat is dead).
  • Condition of the clutch and dual-mass flywheel on diesels (shaking at the biting point, knocking at idle).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2004** : Risk of bonnet opening while driving (bonnet latch replacement). - **December 2005** : Fuel return hose attachment problem on some JTDs (risk of leak). - **May 2007** : Possible defect on the clutch master cylinder (pedal stays at the bottom). - **2008** : Engine management unit update on some JTDMs for DPF regeneration issues.

Verdict

The Alfa Romeo 147 Phase 2 is a compact for enthusiasts. If you're looking for a low-maintenance daily driver, look elsewhere. However, if you love to drive, it offers a rare road feel. **For diesel**, opt for the 1.9 JTDm 8v (120 hp) for maximum reliability at a lower cost. If you choose the 1.9 JTDM 16v (150 hp), **imperatively** ensure that the intake manifold flap issue has been resolved, otherwise, engine failure is a risk. Q2 or Ducati Corse versions are future collectors. Plan for a regular budget for the running gear (wishbones) and avoid Selespeed gearboxes.
