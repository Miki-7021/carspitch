Summary

The Citroën C5 X, launched in 2022, marks the return of the chevron brand to the executive car segment, but with a decidedly modern and transversal approach. Positioned as a crossover blending sedan, wagon, and SUV codes, it aims to offer the best of each world: the comfort of a sedan, the volume of a wagon, and the slightly elevated driving position of an SUV. Based on the Stellantis group's EMP2 V3 platform, it emphasizes comfort, a Citroën signature, with Advanced Comfort suspension and Advanced Comfort seats. This first generation has not yet undergone a major facelift during its short production period. Overall, the C5 X is a bold and comfortable vehicle, but its reliability, particularly for plug-in hybrid powertrains, is still being evaluated in the long term.

Weaknesses 6

  • Limited rear visibility
  • Infotainment system improvable on early versions
  • Fuel consumption of PureTech 130/180 petrol versions can be high on the highway
  • High price of plug-in hybrid versions
  • Lack of diesel engines or 4x4 versions
  • Long-term reliability of PHEVs yet to be proven

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and adherence to manufacturer recommendations (especially for PureTech 1.2L)
  • Condition of the timing belt (PureTech 1.2L) and absence of residues in the oil
  • Operation of the hybrid system (PHEV): charging, electric range, thermal/electric transitions
  • Absence of engine warning lights or dashboard faults
  • Tire and brake wear (more stressed on PHEVs due to weight)
  • Condition of the bodywork and rims (large rims more exposed to impacts)

Verdict

The Citroën C5 X is a vehicle distinguished by its exceptional comfort and bold design. For predominantly urban and suburban use with regular charging possibilities, the **Hybrid 180 or 225** versions are to be preferred. They offer superior driving pleasure, appreciable quietness, and reduced running costs if electric range is maximized. However, the lack of long-term reliability data for complex hybrid systems and batteries calls for caution. It is crucial to ensure that all software updates and manufacturer recalls have been carried out.

The **PureTech 180** versions are a good compromise for those who cannot charge regularly, offering good versatility. The **PureTech 130** is sufficient for calmer use but requires increased vigilance regarding its timing belt maintenance.

**Buying advice**: Prioritize recent models (late 2023-2024) to benefit from the latest software optimizations and potential hardware fixes. A clear service history is non-negotiable, especially for PureTech versions. For PHEVs, check the battery's condition and the previous owner's charging habits. The C5 X is an excellent choice for anyone seeking comfort above all, provided they are aware of the specificities of each engine.
