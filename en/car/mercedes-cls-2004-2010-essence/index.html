Mercedes CLS

C219 (2004–2010) · 2004–2010

NEGOTIATE Reliability 70/100 — bon

Summary

The Mercedes CLS (C219) is a car that marked its era. Launched in 2004, it created a new segment, that of the "four-door coupé," combining the elegance of a coupé with the (relative) practicality of a sedan. Based on the E-Class (W211) platform, it stands out with its sloping roofline and bold design.

The C219 benefited from a slight facelift in 2008, bringing minor aesthetic touches (bumpers, taillights, grille) and some mechanical evolutions, notably the introduction of the 3.5 CGI direct-injection V6 engine.

Overall, the CLS C219 offers a luxurious and comfortable driving experience, with secure road holding. Its reliability is in the upper average for a Mercedes of this generation, but certain engines and electronic components require particular attention. It is a car that ages well aesthetically, but its maintenance can be costly.

Strengths 6

  • Timeless and elegant design
  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with Airmatic)
  • High-quality interior finish and premium materials
  • Very reliable and powerful V8 engines (M113)
  • Excellent sound insulation
  • Good road holding and active safety

Weaknesses 7

  • Reliability of M272/M273 engines (balance shaft sprocket) before 2007
  • High maintenance and repair costs (parts, labor)
  • Reliability of the M156 engine (head bolts) on AMG 63 models
  • Potential problems with Airmatic air suspension (compressor, sensors, air springs)
  • Complex electronics (COMAND, various sensors) that can lead to costly failures
  • Oil cooler leaks on OM642 diesels
  • Limited rear visibility

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for major repairs)
  • Proof of balance shaft problem resolution on M272/M273 (before 2007)
  • Condition of the Airmatic suspension (height, noises, compressor operation)
  • Absence of oil leaks (engine, gearbox, differential) and coolant
  • Functionality of all electronic equipment (COMAND, electric seats, sensors)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (costly to replace)
  • Absence of abnormal exhaust smoke (especially on diesel)
  • Thorough road test to detect abnormal noises, gearbox jerks, or vibrations

Verdict

The Mercedes CLS C219 is a car with undeniable charm, which successfully created its own segment. Its design is still current and its comfort remains first-rate. However, purchasing a CLS C219 requires particular vigilance, especially concerning the engines.

**For gasoline engines:** * **M113 (V8 5.0L)**: This is the safest choice in terms of reliability. If you find a CLS 500 with this engine (before 2006), it's an excellent option for longevity and pleasure. **Verdict: BUY** * **M272/M273 (V6 3.0/3.5L, V8 5.5L)**: Models produced **before late 2006/early 2007** are to be **STRONGLY NEGOTIATED** or **AVOIDED** if the balance shaft problem has not been resolved and proven by invoice. **Post-2007** models are much more reliable and can be **BOUGHT** with a good history. * **M156 (AMG 63)**: Reserved for informed and wealthy enthusiasts. The head bolt problem is major. If unresolved, it's a categorical **AVOID**. If resolved, it's a **NEGOTIATE** as maintenance remains exorbitant.

**For diesel engines (OM642):** * The V6 diesel is a good engine, but oil cooler leaks are a known weak point. A model with a rigorous maintenance history and a repair of this problem is a **BUY**. Otherwise, **NEGOTIATE**.

In all cases, a complete service history and a thorough inspection by a Mercedes specialist are essential. The cost of parts and labor is high, so a substantial budget must be planned for maintenance and potential repairs. A well-maintained CLS is a pleasure, a neglected CLS can become a financial black hole.
