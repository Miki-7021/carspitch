Summary

The Suzuki Baleno II marks the return of this nameplate as a particularly spacious and rational versatile city car (B-segment). Based on the lightweight *Heartect* platform, it stands out for its featherlight weight (often under a ton), which greatly benefits efficiency and dynamism.

Diesel Focus (1.3 DDiS): Although the European career of the Baleno (2016-2019) was exclusively petrol and mild-hybrid, the model achieved immense success in India (Maruti Suzuki) and other emerging markets where it was offered with the famous 1.3 DDiS diesel engine (of Fiat origin). This block was withdrawn from the catalog around 2020 following the entry into force of BS6 anti-pollution standards in India.

The Baleno benefited from a facelift in 2019, then a major redesign in 2022 (mainly for Asian and African markets), improving perceived quality and onboard technology. It is also sold under the names Toyota Glanza or Toyota Starlet depending on the markets.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional fuel consumption of the 1.3 DDiS (often below 4.5 L/100 km real-world)
  • Record rear passenger space for the category (B-segment)
  • Generous boot volume (355 liters)
  • Overall reliability of engines (petrol and diesel)
  • Featherlight weight promoting agility and economy of consumables (brakes, tires)

Weaknesses 5

  • Poor sound insulation (the 1.3 DDiS engine is particularly noisy and vibrating in this lightweight cabin)
  • Interior plastic quality (hard, prone to scratches)
  • Very thin and fragile paint (prone to stone chips)
  • Sometimes choppy damping at low speeds
  • Feeling of lightness at high speed (sensitivity to crosswinds)

Check before buying

  • On the 1.3 DDiS: Listen to the engine when cold. A persistent metallic noise indicates a loose timing chain.
  • On the 1.3 DDiS: Check the history of warning lights (DPF/EGR) and ensure the vehicle hasn't only been driven in the city.
  • Steering: Turn from lock to lock while stationary and drive on cobblestones to detect play or knocking in the steering column.
  • Bodywork: Inspect the hood and front bumper for paint chips, and check for premature corrosion on the underbody (depending on the region).
  • Suspension: Check the condition of the front suspension bushings, which can wear prematurely on degraded roads.

What sets it apart 12

economy

  • Exceptional Fuel Economy from the 1.3 DDiS Diesel — The Baleno II's 1.3 DDiS diesel engine is incredibly frugal, often achieving real-world consumption below 4.5 L/100 km. Perfect for high-mileage drivers, it turns every journey into a significant saving.
  • Controlled Running Costs — The Baleno II's lightweight design not only benefits fuel consumption but also extends the life of consumables like brakes and tires, thereby reducing your long-term maintenance expenses.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Skoda Fabia (for space), Honda Jazz (for reliability and space), Renault Clio, Peugeot 208.

Verdict

The Suzuki Baleno II is an eminently pragmatic choice. If you're aiming for the 1.3 DDiS (diesel) engine, you're opting for a true workhorse, capable of racking up miles at a ridiculously low cost thanks to the legendary robustness of the Fiat-derived block. However, this engine lacks refinement (noise, vibrations) in such a light and poorly insulated car. It is best reserved for high-mileage drivers in countries where it is still relevant. For mixed or urban use, the petrol engines (1.2 Dualjet leading the way) are much more recommendable, offering superior operating silence and almost perfect reliability.
