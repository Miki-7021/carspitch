Vauxhall Crossland

I (P17) · 2017 – 2020

Summary

The **Vauxhall Crossland X** (sold under the Opel brand in continental Europe) is a B-segment urban crossover. Launched in 2017 to replace the Meriva minivan, it is the result of the first technical partnership between General Motors (then owner of Opel/Vauxhall) and the PSA group, based on the PF1 platform (shared with the Peugeot 2008 I and the Citroën C3 Aircross). Although it offers excellent roominess and appreciable modularity (sliding rear bench), its reliability is heavily marred by the adoption of PSA engines from that era, notably the 1.5 diesels (camshaft chain problems) and the 1.2 petrols (wet belt).

**Overall reliability rating: Poor.**

Strengths 5

  • Generous roominess for its size (4.21 m)
  • Sliding rear bench (depending on trim level)
  • Very decent trunk volume (410 to 520 liters)
  • Very low fuel consumption from diesel engines
  • Comprehensive technological equipment for its time (HUD, LED headlights)

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of the petrol engines (PureTech/EB) and the 1.5 diesel (DV5)
  • Firm damping and room for improvement in comfort at low speeds
  • Slow and dated infotainment system
  • Average soundproofing on the highway
  • Sluggish road handling compared to the Peugeot 2008

Check before buying

  • **Maintenance history:** Demand invoices proving strict annual oil changes.
  • **Engine noise (1.5 Diesel):** A rattling noise on the timing side indicates wear of the camshaft chain (imminent risk of breakage).
  • **AdBlue system (Diesels):** Check for the absence of an illuminated UREA or engine warning light. Ask if the tank has already been replaced.
  • **Timing belt (Petrols):** Open the oil cap and visually inspect the back of the belt (cracks, swelling).
  • **High-pressure pump:** Check for the absence of fuel smell or seepage (subject to a recall).

Verdict

The Vauxhall/Opel Crossland X is a practical family vehicle, but its reliability record is weighed down by its PSA-sourced engines. If you absolutely want a **diesel**, favor the 2017-2018 models equipped with the older **1.6 Turbo D**, which is generally more robust despite some quirks with the AdBlue system. The newer **1.5 Turbo D** is to be **avoided** unless the seller can prove, with an invoice, that the camshaft chain kit has been replaced with the reinforced 8 mm version. The petrol versions are also to be avoided due to the wet belt scandal (PureTech).
