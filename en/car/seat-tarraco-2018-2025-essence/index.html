Summary

The Seat Tarraco, launched in 2018, is the Spanish brand's large family SUV, positioned above the Ateca and Arona. Based on the Volkswagen Group's modular MQB A2 platform, it shares its technical underpinnings with the Skoda Kodiaq and Volkswagen Tiguan Allspace. Offered in 5 or 7-seat configurations, the Tarraco stands out with a more dynamic and sporty design, true to Seat's identity, while offering generous interior space and a good level of comfort. It was designed to be a versatile vehicle, capable of meeting family needs while maintaining a certain driving pleasure. No major facelift has been carried out since its launch, but software updates and engine evolutions have occurred. Overall, the Tarraco is a well-conceived SUV, offering an excellent performance/price ratio within the VAG group.

  • Infotainment sometimes prone to software bugs (early models)
  • DSG gearbox sometimes hesitant at low speeds
  • Limited rear visibility (reversing camera almost essential)
  • Fuel consumption of 4Drive petrol versions can be high
  • Lack of non-rechargeable hybrid (HEV) powertrains

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for DSG and AdBlue)
  • DSG gearbox operation (smooth gear changes, no jerks)
  • AdBlue system condition (warning lights, level, operation)
  • Infotainment and driving assistance system operation
  • Tire and brake wear (heavy vehicle)
  • Condition of suspensions and ball joints (bushings)
  • For PHEVs: actual battery range, charging history

Verdict

The Seat Tarraco is an excellent choice for families looking for a large, versatile, spacious, and well-equipped SUV, without sacrificing dynamism. Its proven VAG group platform provides a solid foundation in terms of reliability.

* **For balanced family use (city/road):** The **1.5 TSI ACT 150 hp** is an excellent compromise. It is sufficiently powerful and relatively economical. Prioritize recent models or those that have benefited from software updates for the ACT. * **For high-mileage drivers or highway journeys:** The **2.0 TDI 150 hp** or **190 hp** remains relevant, provided the AdBlue system is closely monitored and road use is prioritized for the DPF. An impeccable service history is crucial. * **For performance enthusiasts and 4Drive:** The **2.0 TSI 190 hp or 245 hp** is a very enjoyable choice, but expect higher fuel consumption. Engine reliability is good, but DSG gearbox maintenance is non-negotiable. * **For predominantly urban use with frequent charging:** The **1.4 eHybrid** can be interesting for its low electric running costs, but its complexity and weight should be considered. Long-term reliability is yet to be confirmed.

Overall, the Tarraco is a recommended purchase, especially if you find a well-maintained example. Do not hesitate to negotiate if the service history is incomplete or if recalls have not been carried out.
