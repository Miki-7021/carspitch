Summary

The third-generation Nissan X-Trail (T32) marked a turning point for the model, abandoning its image as a rugged off-roader to fully embrace the family SUV segment. Based on the Renault-Nissan Alliance's CMF-C/D platform, it shared many elements with the Nissan Qashqai (J11) and Renault Kadjar. Launched in 2013, it was sold as the Rogue in North America. It stood out for its generous spaciousness, often offering a 7-seat configuration, and appreciable ride comfort. A significant facelift occurred in 2017, bringing aesthetic changes (bumpers, more pronounced V-Motion grille, lights) and technological improvements (driver assistance systems, multimedia interface). Overall, the X-Trail T32 is a versatile SUV, generally reliable, but with some specific points of attention for certain powertrains and the CVT transmission.

Strengths 6

  • Generous spaciousness, often available with 7 seats
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially after facelift)
  • Reliable and economical diesel engines (R9M, M9R)
  • Very robust naturally aspirated petrol engines (MR20DD, QR25DE)
  • Good family versatility
  • Comprehensive equipment on high trim levels

Weaknesses 6

  • CVT (Xtronic) transmission: can be noisy, lacks responsiveness, reliability dependent on maintenance
  • 1.6 DIG-T (MR16DDT) engine: risk of oil consumption on some units
  • Modest performance of naturally aspirated petrol engines (MR20DD) in Europe
  • Perceived quality of some interior plastics before facelift
  • Multimedia system sometimes slow and unintuitive (before facelift)
  • Paint sensitivity to scratches

Check before buying

  • **Complete maintenance history**, especially for the CVT transmission (specific fluid changes)
  • **CVT operation**: absence of abnormal noises, slipping, jerks
  • **Engine oil level** on the 1.6 DIG-T (MR16DDT) and absence of blue smoke from the exhaust
  • **Timing chain noises** (rattling on cold start) on the 1.6 DIG-T
  • **Condition of DPF and EGR valve** on diesels (usage history, warning lights)
  • **Electronics operation**: air conditioning, GPS, driver assistance systems, sensors
  • **Suspension condition**: strut top noises, shock absorbers
  • **Brake wear** (pads and discs)

Verdict

The Nissan X-Trail T32 is an excellent choice for families looking for a spacious, comfortable, and versatile SUV, often available with 7 seats. Its general reliability is good, especially with the diesel powertrains (R9M, M9R) and naturally aspirated petrol engines (MR20DD, QR25DE). The main point of concern remains the CVT (Xtronic) transmission: if it has not been properly maintained (regular fluid changes), it can become a source of costly problems. Prioritize models with a clear maintenance history, especially regarding the CVT. The 1.6 DIG-T petrol versions are powerful but require increased monitoring of oil consumption. After the 2017 facelift, the model matured, particularly in terms of infotainment and driver assistance systems. For family and road use, diesels are preferable for their torque and fuel efficiency. For more urban use and smooth driving, naturally aspirated petrol engines are reliable, provided the CVT is thoroughly checked. The X-Trail T32 represents a credible and often more affordable alternative to its direct competitors, provided an informed choice is made regarding the engine and transmission.
