Nissan Leaf

I (ZE0) · 2010 – 2017

All Nissan models

Summary

The first-generation Nissan Leaf (code ZE0 from 2010 to 2012, then AZE0 from 2013 to 2017) is the global pioneer of mass-produced electric vehicles (BEV). Designed from the outset as a 100% electric car, it offers the spaciousness of a compact sedan (C-segment).

**Major evolutions:** - **Phase 1 (ZE0, 2010-2012):** Manufactured in Japan, 24 kWh battery, onboard charger in the trunk, PTC resistance heating (energy-intensive). - **Phase 2 (AZE0, 2013-2017):** Localized production (Sunderland for Europe, Smyrna for North America). Over 100 improvements: charger moved under the hood (trunk space gain), introduction of a heat pump (from Acenta trim), new lighter EM57 motor, and suspension settings adapted to European tastes. - **2015/2016:** Introduction of the 30 kWh battery offering better theoretical range.

**Expert's overall note:** A mechanically indestructible car, but its battery (lacking liquid cooling) undergoes inevitable degradation. A relevant purchase only for short daily trips, provided you master the *LeafSpy* diagnostic tool.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional powertrain reliability (indestructible EM61/EM57 motor and reducer)
  • Near-zero mechanical maintenance cost (no belt, no engine oil change, very little brake wear thanks to regeneration)
  • Remarkable suspension comfort and quiet operation
  • Generous space for 4 adults and decent trunk (especially after 2013)
  • Rich equipment on high trim levels (Tekna: heated seats and steering wheel, 360° cameras)

Weaknesses 5

  • Inevitable battery degradation (passive air cooling), leading to a permanent loss of range
  • Very low real-world range today on used models (often between 60 and 100 km depending on wear)
  • CHAdeMO fast charging standard becoming obsolete in Europe
  • Very energy-intensive heating on Phase 1 (ZE0) models
  • Type 1 charging cable on the vehicle side (requires a Type 1 to Type 2 cable for modern public charging stations)

Check before buying

  • **BATTERY CONDITION (SOH):** Never buy without connecting an OBD2 dongle and using the *LeafSpy Pro* app. The dashboard (bars) is imprecise and can be fraudulently reset.
  • **Heating:** Turn the heating to full. If it doesn't blow hot air after 2 minutes (especially on Phase 1), the PTC module is likely dead.
  • **PDM module (Phase 2):** Check that the car charges correctly on a domestic outlet AND on a CHAdeMO fast charging station.
  • **Running gear:** Clunking noises from the front axle (shock absorber mounts, wishbone bushings) due to the vehicle's weight.
  • **12V Battery:** A weak 12V battery causes a multitude of phantom error messages on the dashboard.

Official recalls

  • - **July 2018 (30 kWh BMS):** Software update for the lithium-ion battery controller to correct an erroneous degradation calculation (30 kWh models only). - **May 2016 (Braking):** Replacement of a relay in the electronic braking system that could freeze in very cold weather, reducing braking assistance. - **April 2014 (Airbag):** Reprogramming of the passenger seat OCS (Occupant Classification System) sensor which might not detect an adult and deactivate the airbag. - **March 2014 (Steering):** Reprogramming of the electric power steering module (risk of loss of assistance).

Economy

  • Minimal Maintenance Costs — Achieve significant daily savings with virtually zero mechanical maintenance costs, thanks to the absence of belts, engine oil changes, and minimal brake wear due to regeneration.

Comfort

  • Comfort and Silent Driving — Experience a serene and relaxing driving experience. The Leaf offers remarkable suspension comfort and absolute silent operation, transforming every journey into a moment of relaxation.

Safety

  • 5-Star Euro NCAP Safety — Travel with complete confidence. The Nissan Leaf achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with scores of 89% for adult protection and 83% for child protection.

Handling

  • Stable and Reassuring Road Holding — Benefit from stable and predictable road holding. Thanks to its low center of gravity, the Leaf offers agile maneuverability in the city and a safe, reassuring drive on all roads.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Renault Zoé (beware of frequent battery rental, very practical 22kW AC charging), BMW i3 (more premium, superior battery thermal management), Volkswagen e-Golf (superior finish, similar range). - **Asia / Japan:** Mitsubishi i-MiEV (smaller, less versatile). - **North America:** Chevrolet Spark EV (very responsive, liquid cooling), Fiat 500e (strictly urban), Ford Focus Electric.

Verdict

The Nissan Leaf I is the perfect choice for a second vehicle dedicated to short daily trips (school, shopping, nearby work), provided you buy it with full knowledge of its characteristics. **The mechanics are indestructible, but the battery is a consumable that degrades.** Avoid Phase 1 models (2010-2012) whose batteries are often worn out and heating is fragile. Prioritize a Phase 2 (AZE0, 2013+) in Acenta or Tekna trim (for the heat pump). **Golden rule:** NEVER buy a Leaf without having measured its SOH (State of Health) with the LeafSpy app. The price must be strictly proportional to the remaining battery capacity.
