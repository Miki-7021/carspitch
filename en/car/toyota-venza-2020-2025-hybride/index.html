Toyota Venza

All Toyota models

Summary

The second-generation Toyota Venza (AV25) marks the return of this nameplate after several years of absence. Based on the TNGA-K modular platform (shared with the RAV4 and Highlander), it is positioned as a premium-oriented mid-size SUV, prioritizing comfort, soundproofing, and design over pure utility volume. In the North American market, it is offered exclusively with a hybrid powertrain and all-wheel drive (e-AWD). In other markets (notably in Japan where it is sold under the name Toyota Harrier), it offers a wider range of powertrains. It is a vehicle praised for its exceptional reliability, very low fuel consumption for its size, and plush interior, although it sacrifices a bit of cargo space compared to its sibling, the RAV4. Its production ends in 2025 to make way for the Toyota Crown Signia.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional mechanical and electronic reliability (components shared with the RAV4)
  • Extremely low fuel consumption for an SUV of this size (~6.0L/100km in hybrid)
  • Premium interior finish, soft-touch materials, and superior soundproofing compared to the RAV4
  • Standard e-AWD all-wheel drive (in North America)
  • Highly effective and standard driving aids (Toyota Safety Sense 2.0/2.5)
  • Innovative electrochromic panoramic roof (Star Gaze) (depending on trim)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disappointing trunk volume for the category (smaller than the RAV4 due to the sloping roofline)
  • Limited headroom in the rear seats
  • Noisy combustion engine during hard acceleration (rubber-band effect of the e-CVT gearbox)
  • No towing capacity officially recommended by Toyota
  • Infotainment system on early models (2020-2022) somewhat dated before the update

Check before buying

  • Corrosion of the high-voltage cable (MGR) powering the rear electric motor (known issue called 'Cablegate' on hybrid RAV4/Venza models in regions using a lot of road salt).
  • Cleanliness of the hybrid battery cooling filter (an obstruction leads to overheating and loss of efficiency).
  • Software update history (especially for the ESC stability control module).
  • Condition of body seals and camera housings (recent recall regarding water leaks).

Official recalls

  • [2024-08-22] Camera housings (various Lexus/Toyota): The camera housings can separate under stress due to improper welding, allowing water entry and camera failure (risk of injury due to lack of visibility).
  • [2024-06-26] Immobilizer (Toyota/Lexus HEV/PHEV): The design of the immobilizer allows the vehicle to unexpectedly enter "Ready On" mode, posing a safety risk.
  • [2025-09-11] Instrument cluster (NHTSA 25V595000): Recall regarding the electrical system of the instrument panel/cluster on several 2023-2025 Toyota/Lexus models, including the Venza.
  • [2022-04-13] ESC module (NHTSA 22V239000): A software issue in the electronic stability control module (Skid Control ECU) may prevent the VSC system from activating when the vehicle is started.

What sets it apart 12

Reliability

  • Proven Hybrid Powertrain Reliability — The 2.5L hybrid system shares proven components with the RAV4, giving buyers a mechanically and electronically robust vehicle with an excellent reliability score of 9.5/10.
  • Robust Naturally Aspirated Engine Option — On some markets the 2.0L atmospheric engine with dual D-4S injection offers mechanical simplicity and no turbo, keeping ownership straightforward and dependable.

Economy

  • Very Low Fuel Consumption — The hybrid powertrain returns around 6.0L/100km, an extremely low figure for a midsize SUV, reducing running costs for urban, highway and family use.
  • Plug-In Hybrid Capability — The Harrier PHEV variant offers around 60 km of electric-only range and 306 ch combined, allowing silent, low-cost daily trips when charged at home.

Handling

  • Standard e-AWD Traction — All-wheel drive is fitted as standard in North America, providing effective traction and added confidence on slippery roads without sacrificing the hybrid's low consumption.
  • Efficient e-AWD System — The electric all-wheel drive system distributes torque effectively while remaining economical, supporting stable handling in varied conditions without a heavy fuel penalty.

Comfort

  • Premium Cabin Materials and Insulation — The interior uses soft-touch materials and superior sound insulation compared with the RAV4, giving occupants a quieter, more refined experience closer to a Lexus.
  • Innovative Electrochromic Panoramic Roof — Depending on trim, the Star Gaze panoramic roof can switch its transparency, letting occupants enjoy daylight while controlling glare and heat inside the cabin.
  • Quiet and Comfortable Ride — Superior sound insulation and a comfort-focused setup make the cabin cossu and calm, rewarding buyers who value a peaceful, refined driving environment.

Safety

  • Comprehensive Driver Assistance Suite — Toyota Safety Sense 2.0/2.5 is fitted as standard and delivers highly effective driving aids, helping the driver avoid collisions and stay in lane.

Performance

  • Smooth Hybrid Operation — The hybrid system delivers smooth, gentle power delivery, making daily driving in town and on the road comfortable and easy to manage for the driver.

Design

  • Refined Design Over Pure Utility — The sloping roofline and premium positioning give the Venza a distinctive, upscale appearance that prioritizes style and comfort over boxy utility.

Alternatives

  • North America: Honda Passport, Subaru Outback, Ford Edge, Hyundai Santa Fe Hybrid, Kia Sorento Hybrid, Lexus NX 350h.
  • Europe (equivalent models): Lexus NX 350h, Toyota RAV4 Hybrid, Honda CR-V e:HEV.
  • Asia: Honda CR-V, Mazda CX-60, Nissan X-Trail e-Power.

Verdict

The Toyota Venza (AV25) is an exceptional choice for buyers looking for a reliable, extremely fuel-efficient mid-size SUV with a finish close to that of a Lexus. Its 2.5L hybrid powertrain is one of the most robust on the current market. Although it sacrifices a bit of practicality (smaller trunk) on the altar of design compared to a RAV4, it compensates with superior acoustic comfort and refinement. It is a highly recommended purchase, with the only real precaution being to check the rear high-voltage cable if the vehicle has been driven in regions with harsh winters.
