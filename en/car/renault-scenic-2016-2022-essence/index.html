Renault Scenic

IV (J95) · 2016 – 2022

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The Renault Scénic IV (codename J95 for the short version, R95 for the Grand Scénic) marks a radical break from its predecessors. To counter the hegemony of SUVs, Renault transformed its compact MPV into a crossover-designed vehicle, characterized by increased ground clearance, a steeply raked windscreen, and, uniquely in the segment, 20-inch wheels as standard on all trim levels (with an atypical tire size of 195/55 R20). While the style gains immensely, rear passenger space and modularity (sliding 2/3-1/3 bench seat instead of individual seats) regress compared to the Scénic III. Mechanically, the Scénic IV's career is split into two: an initial period marred by the infamous 1.2 TCe petrol engine ('Motorgate' scandal), and a second period transformed by the excellent 1.3 TCe developed with Mercedes-Benz. The model's overall rating therefore depends almost exclusively on the year and chosen engine.

Strengths 6

  • Very successful design, dynamic crossover look
  • Remarkable suspension comfort despite 20-inch wheels
  • Pleasure and reliability of the 1.3 TCe petrol engine (from 2018)
  • 'One-Touch' modularity (seat folding from the screen or boot)
  • Sound and safe road handling
  • Generous boot volume (especially on Grand Scénic)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.2 TCe petrol engine (2016-2018)
  • Recurring electronic bugs on the R-Link 2 multimedia system (especially before 2018)
  • Replacement cost of 195/55 R20 tires (rare and expensive at launch, although prices have decreased)
  • Reduced rear passenger space compared to the Scénic III
  • Disappearance of individual rear seats in favor of a 2/3-1/3 bench
  • Aerodynamic wind noise on the highway (windscreen pillars)

Check before buying

  • The service history and exact engine code if purchasing a 2018 petrol model (1.2 TCe / 1.3 TCe transition).
  • Front tire wear (outer edges wear quickly).
  • Proper functioning of the R-Link 2 touchscreen (responsiveness, Bluetooth, GPS).
  • Absence of jerks during gear changes on the EDC automatic gearbox.
  • Condition of the faux leather seats on Intens/Bose trims (frequent cracking on the sides).
  • On recent diesels (1.7 Blue dCi): absence of a warning light related to the AdBlue system.

Alternatives

Verdict

The Renault Scénic IV is a two-faced vehicle. If you're looking for a petrol model, the rule is simple and non-negotiable: **avoid the 1.2 TCe (115/130 hp)** produced until 2018, whose design flaws can lead to engine failure. In contrast, **the 1.3 TCe (115/140/160 hp) is highly recommended**: it is powerful, economical, and very reliable. On the diesel side, the 1.5 dCi and 1.7 Blue dCi units are good companions for high-mileage drivers. Opt for a post-2018 model (Intens or Initiale Paris trim): you will benefit from the 1.3 TCe engine, a more reliable R-Link 2 system with CarPlay/Android Auto, and early fixes for the running gear.
