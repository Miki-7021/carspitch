Summary

Toyota Sequoia III (XK70)

Launched for the 2023 model year, the third generation of the Toyota Sequoia (XK70) marks a radical break from its predecessor (the XK60, which remained in the catalog for 14 years). Based on the new global TNGA-F architecture (ladder frame chassis shared with the Tundra, Land Cruiser 300, and Lexus LX), this large family SUV abandons the legendary but thirsty 5.7-liter V8 in favor of a single, mandatory powertrain: the 3.4L bi-turbo hybrid V6 i-FORCE MAX.

Positioned as the flagship of Toyota SUVs in North America (following the withdrawal of the Land Cruiser 200), the Sequoia XK70 stands out with truck-like torque (790 Nm), a sharply increased towing capacity, and a modernized interior. While overall reliability looks excellent according to early data (J.D. Power ranks it above the category average, surpassing the Ford Expedition and GMC Yukon), the complexity of its new hybrid powertrain requires more rigorous maintenance than the old naturally aspirated V8.

Strengths 5

  • Overall Toyota reliability superior to American competition (Tahoe, Yukon, Expedition)
  • Extremely torquey (790 Nm) and high-performance i-FORCE MAX powertrain
  • Very rigid TNGA-F chassis offering excellent road handling
  • Impressive towing capacity (up to 4,318 kg / 9,520 lbs)
  • Highly responsive Toyota Audio Multimedia infotainment system (14-inch screen)

Weaknesses 4

  • 3rd-row space and trunk volume compromised by the hybrid battery location (non-flat floor)
  • Mechanical complexity (V6 + 2 turbos + hybrid system + 10-speed gearbox) compared to the old 5.7L V8
  • Real-world fuel consumption often higher than EPA figures
  • Lack of mechanical alternatives (no non-hybrid or V8 version available)

Check before buying

  • History of software updates (OTA) for transmission and hybrid system management
  • Gearbox smoothness at low speeds (some jerks reported on early 2023 models)
  • Body panel alignment and wind noise (teething problems on early batches from the San Antonio plant)
  • Premature tire wear due to the very high weight of the vehicle (nearly 2.8 tons)

Official recalls

Official manufacturer recalls

*Note: The Gen III Sequoia enjoys excellent initial reliability, with the majority of fixes being done via software updates (OTA). Here are the recalls documented by Rappel Conso (France/Europe) including recent Toyota/Lexus platforms:*

  • [2024-08-22] Camera housings (TNGA / Lexus / Land Cruiser platforms): The camera housings on certain vehicles may separate under stress due to improper welding, allowing water entry. Risk of image loss and injury (Model codes including FJA310/VJA310 close to the Sequoia).
  • [2024-06-26] Immobilizer device (Toyota/Lexus Hybrids): The design of the accessory immobilizer device is not robust enough. It allows the vehicle to be in "Ready On" mode inappropriately. Risk of unexpected movement.
  • [2024-06-10] Transmission oil leak (Proace City): *[Expert note: This recall concerns Stellantis commercial vehicles rebadged as Toyota (Proace City) and does not apply to the Sequoia, which is based on a 100% Toyota longitudinal architecture].*
  • [NHTSA - USA] V35A-FTS Engine (Information): A massive recall affected 2022-2023 Tundras (non-hybrid) for machining debris causing crankshaft bearing failure. The Sequoia (hybrid only) is not affected by this safety recall, as the electric motor allows braking and steering assistance to be maintained in the event of the V6 stalling. The combustion block remains identical, however.

Alternatives

Competing alternatives

  • North America: Chevrolet Tahoe / Suburban (proven V8s but average electronic reliability), GMC Yukon, Ford Expedition (high-performance EcoBoost V6 but fragile turbos), Jeep Wagoneer.
  • Japan / Asia / Middle East: Nissan Patrol / Armada (very reliable but aging naturally aspirated V8, new generation turbo V6 coming), Toyota Land Cruiser 300 (more off-road oriented, less spacious in the 3rd row).
  • Europe: The Sequoia is not officially imported. Similar-sized alternatives are the BMW X7 or the Mercedes-Benz GLS.

Verdict

Expert verdict

The third-generation Toyota Sequoia (XK70) successfully transitions into the modern era. By imposing the i-FORCE MAX bi-turbo hybrid V6, Toyota offers an SUV with formidable performance and driving pleasure vastly superior to the old generation. Data from J.D. Power and Consumer Reports confirm that it dominates its American rivals (Ford, GM) in terms of initial reliability.

However, this sophistication comes at a price: the battery cuts into the rear cargo space, and the mechanical complexity (turbos + hybridization) will require meticulous long-term maintenance, far from the abuse tolerance of the old 5.7L V8.

Buying advice: Favor 2024 or 2025 model years. The 2023 models (first year of production) suffered some software teething problems and factory finish adjustments. It is an excellent purchase for anyone looking for an ultra-robust family hauler, provided that oil change intervals are strictly respected.
