Summary

The first-generation Volvo V40 (and its S40 sedan variant) marks a historic turning point for the Swedish manufacturer. Developed in partnership with Mitsubishi (sharing the Carisma platform) and assembled at the NedCar plant in the Netherlands, it abandons traditional cubic lines for a more fluid design.

Major Evolution: The model underwent a crucial facelift (Phase 2) starting from the 2001 model year (mid-2000). This update corrected the early flaws of Phase 1 (1995-1999) by profoundly modifying the running gear (wider tracks, new suspension), steering, sound insulation, and interior finish quality.

Positioned as a premium compact wagon, it distinguished itself by its exceptional level of passive safety for the time (the S40 was the first in its category to achieve 4 Euro NCAP stars). In the used car market, gasoline versions (Volvo modular engines) are particularly sought after for their robustness, with the notable exception of the Mitsubishi-sourced 1.8i.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional reliability of Volvo modular gasoline engines (1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.0T).
  • High level of passive safety (SIPS, WHIPS on Phase 2, 4 Euro NCAP stars).
  • Front seat comfort (among the best of the decade).
  • Transformed and very sound road handling on Phase 2 models (2001-2004).
  • Timeless design that ages very well for a 90s wagon.
  • Excellent corrosion resistance on Phase 2 models.

Weaknesses 6

  • 1.8i GDI (Mitsubishi) engine catastrophic in terms of reliability (carbon buildup).
  • Renault-sourced diesel engines (especially dCi) unworthy of Volvo's reliability standard.
  • Clumsy road handling and harsh suspension on Phase 1 (1995-1999).
  • Very large turning radius (tedious city maneuvers).
  • Limited rear passenger space and trunk volume for a wagon (more 'lifestyle' than utilitarian).
  • Average interior plastic quality and assembly on Phase 1 (rattles).

Check before buying

  • Clattering noise at idle on Phase 2 gasoline engines (indicates a faulty VVT pulley, costly repair).
  • PCV system (oil breather) on gasoline engines: check for absence of overpressure in the crankcase.
  • Corrosion on Phase 1: inspect rear wheel arches, sills, and front fenders.
  • Rear axle: wear of trailing arm bushings (causes vague handling and asymmetrical tire wear).
  • Electrics: operation of central locking, power windows, and immobilizer antenna (ignition barrel ring).
  • Headliner: tendency to detach and sag with age and heat.

Official recalls

- 1997: Replacement of the brake booster vacuum pump (risk of loss of assistance).,- 2001: Inspection and possible replacement of front lower suspension arms (risk of corrosion and breakage on certain batches).,- 2003: Replacement of the fuel filler pipe (risk of leakage during refueling).,- 2004: Inspection of front seat belt buckles (risk of non-locking).

What sets it apart 7

reliability

  • Legendary Volvo Petrol Engine Reliability — Benefit from the exceptional robustness and longevity of Volvo's modular petrol engines (1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.0T), engineered to exceed 300,000 km with regular maintenance.

safety

  • Exemplary Passive Safety — Travel with complete peace of mind thanks to a state-of-the-art passive safety level for its era, integrating SIPS and WHIPS systems (on Phase 2) for optimal occupant protection.

comfort

  • Unrivaled Seating Comfort — Discover the legendary comfort of Volvo's front seats, recognized as among the best of their decade, offering perfect support for long journeys.

handling

  • Sound and Predictable Road Handling — Enjoy stable and predictable road handling, especially on Phase 2 models (2001-2004) where the chassis was optimized to offer a serene and reassuring drive, prioritizing comfort and safety.

braking

  • Effective and Predictable Braking — Benefit from an effective and predictable braking system, with ABS widely available, ensuring optimal vehicle control in all circumstances.

Design

  • Timeless Design and Body Durability — Its elegant and timeless design ages remarkably well, complemented by excellent corrosion resistance, especially on Phase 2 models, ensuring preserved aesthetics over the years.

Performance

  • Refined Driving Pleasure (2.0T) — Choose the 2.0T (Low Pressure Turbo) engine for superior driving pleasure, offering generous torque from low RPMs and a smoothness that transforms every journey into an enjoyable experience.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Audi A4 Avant (B5) (superior finish but fragile front axles), BMW 3 Series Touring (E36/E46) (more dynamic, rear-wheel drive), Volkswagen Golf/Bora Variant (more utilitarian), Peugeot 406 Break (benchmark in comfort/chassis).

  • North America: Subaru Outback, Volkswagen Jetta Wagon (1.8T or VR6 engines).

Verdict

The first-generation Volvo V40 is an excellent youngtimer or economical 'daily driver', provided you choose the right version.

The verdict is clear: Avoid the diesel engines (Renault) and the 1.8i GDI (Mitsubishi) which accumulate costly flaws. Focus exclusively on the 100% Volvo gasoline engines (1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.0T).

Imperatively prioritize a Phase 2 (produced after summer 2000, model year 2001+). The chassis is transformed, sound insulation is improved, and rust protection is optimal. The 'Sweet Spot' of the range is undoubtedly the 2.0T (Low Pressure): it offers modern driving pleasure, bulletproof reliability, and reasonable fuel consumption for its time. A well-maintained example will easily exceed 300,000 km.
