Chevrolet Silverado 1500

3rd gen (K2XX) · 2014 – 2018

All Chevrolet models

Summary

The 3rd generation Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (K2XX) marked a major evolution for General Motors pickups, introducing much more refined interiors, better soundproofing, and the EcoTec3 family of gasoline engines (direct injection, variable valve timing, AFM cylinder deactivation).

Important clarification regarding Diesel: Contrary to the request targeting a diesel engine, the Silverado 1500 (K2XX) was never offered with a diesel engine between 2014 and 2018. At that time, GM reserved diesel (6.6L Duramax V8) exclusively for its Heavy Duty models (2500HD / 3500HD). To get a diesel Silverado 1500, you have to look to the 4th generation (T1XX, from 2019/2020) which introduced the excellent 3.0L Duramax inline-6. Buyers absolutely looking for a light-duty diesel pickup from those years had to turn to the Ram 1500 EcoDiesel. This sheet therefore covers the gasoline engines of the 1500, while integrating data for the Duramax V8 of the HD versions to address the context of the request.

Strengths 5

  • Exemplary reliability of the 6.2L V8 engine and the 6-speed automatic gearbox (Hydra-Matic)
  • Excellent ride comfort and cabin soundproofing for the category
  • Maintenance costs average for the segment (according to J.D. Power)
  • Intuitive and responsive MyLink infotainment system
  • Very competitive towing capacities

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of a diesel engine on the 1500 model (reserved for HDs or the next generation)
  • Recurring and costly lifter issues (AFM lifters) on the very popular 5.3L V8
  • Frequent failures of the brake vacuum pump (manufacturer recall)
  • 8-speed automatic gearbox (8L90) prone to vibrations and shudder
  • Very high number of recalls concerning curtain airbags

Check before buying

  • Clacking or ticking noises in the top end of the 5.3L V8 engine (warning sign of AFM lifter failure)
  • Abnormal stiffness of the brake pedal at low speeds (vacuum pump failure)
  • Vibrations or hesitations during gear shifts on models equipped with the 8-speed gearbox
  • Complete recall history (particularly airbags and braking system)
  • On HD versions (6.6L Duramax): presence of metal shavings in the fuel filter (CP4 pump wear)

Official recalls

The Silverado K2XX has been the subject of numerous recalls (19 recorded by the NHTSA). Here are the most critical:

  • Curtain airbags (Multiple recalls: 2016 to 2026 - e.g.: 26V325000, 24V756000, 21V504000): Risk of detachment or rupture of the curtain airbag (RRAB) inflator end cap, which could project fragments into the cabin.
  • Brake assist (20V603000, 19V645000 - Oct 2020 / Sep 2019): Decrease in the vacuum level generated by the mechanical vacuum pump, leading to a loss of brake assist and increasing stopping distances (5.3L and 6.2L engines).
  • Power steering (18V586000, 17V414000 - Sep 2018 / Jun 2017): Temporary loss of electric power steering (EPS) assist, particularly during low-speed maneuvers.
  • ABS / Driveline system (19V761000 - Oct 2019): Software issue that can cause unintended braking of a single wheel, causing a loss of control.
  • Brake pedal (18V578000, 16V069000 - Aug 2018 / Feb 2016): The brake pedal pivot nut can loosen, rendering braking inoperative.
  • Seat belts (21V245000, 18V291000 - Apr 2021 / May 2018): Defective or incorrectly replaced pretensioner cable.

Verdict

The Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (2014-2018) is an overall solid pickup, offering excellent comfort and serious work capabilities. However, its reliability record is tarnished by the best-selling engine in the lineup: the 5.3L V8, whose cylinder deactivation system (AFM) causes costly lifter failures.

If you are looking for a Diesel: This model is not for you. Look to a Silverado 2500HD (6.6L V8), a Ram 1500 EcoDiesel from the same era, or a next-generation Silverado 1500 (2019+ with the 3.0L Duramax, considered very reliable by ADAC). If you buy this model: Absolutely favor the 6.2L V8 paired with the 6-speed gearbox, a combination considered very reliable. If you opt for a 5.3L, demand a flawless maintenance history and consider electronically deactivating the AFM system to preserve the engine.
