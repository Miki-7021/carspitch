Summary

The second-generation Volvo S60 (Y20) marks a major stylistic turning point for the Swedish manufacturer, abandoning cubic lines for a very dynamic four-door coupé profile. Based on the P3 platform (shared with Ford), it stands out for its exceptional ride comfort, some of the best seats on the market, and a cutting-edge safety arsenal (City Safety as standard).

Range evolution: - 2010-2013 (Phase 1): Engines sourced from Ford (4-cylinder petrol), PSA (1.6 diesel), and Volvo (5-cylinder and 6-cylinder) parts bins. - 2013 (Facelift): New front end (one-piece headlights), digital gauges, multimedia improvements. - 2014-2018: Mechanical revolution with the introduction of the 100% Volvo VEA (Drive-E) engine family (4-cylinder 2.0L petrol and diesel) coupled with an excellent Aisin 8-speed automatic gearbox, gradually replacing the old blocks.

*Overall note: An excellent alternative to German premium cars, particularly popular in diesel for high-mileage drivers, but whose reliability depends heavily on the year, engine type (4 or 5 cylinders), and transmission.*

Strengths 6

  • High-level suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Front seats among the most ergonomic in automotive production
  • Passive and active safety (City Safety, BLIS, highly refined adaptive cruise control)
  • Enjoyment and sound of 5-cylinder engines (diesel and petrol)
  • Excellent 8-speed Geartronic automatic gearbox (on post-2014 VEA engines)
  • Paint quality and corrosion resistance

Weaknesses 5

  • Rear passenger space and boot volume (380 liters) unworthy of the category
  • Powershift dual-clutch automatic gearbox (on 1.6D and 1.6/2.0T Ford) unreliable and unpleasant
  • Very large turning radius (especially with 18/19-inch wheels)
  • Aging and unintuitive Sensus infotainment system (many buttons on the center console)
  • High weight penalizing agility compared to a BMW 3 Series

Check before buying

  • Accessory belt replacement history on 5-cylinder diesel engines (D3, D4, D5 2.0L and 2.4L).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior during a cold and hot test drive (jerks, slipping).
  • Soot traces or exhaust smell in the cabin (manifold or EGR cooler leak on VEA diesels).
  • Air conditioning operation (fragile compressor on early model years).
  • Front tire wear (the significant torque of the D4/D5/T5 tends to quickly wear out the front axle on front-wheel-drive versions).

Official recalls

- July 2019 (Intake Manifold / Fire): Major recall on 4-cylinder VEA diesel engines (2014-2019). The plastic intake manifold can melt due to a defective EGR cooler. Cooling system modification and software update.,- November 2020 (Seatbelts): The front seatbelt anchor cable can wear out and break over time. Replacement with a reinforced part.,- December 2011 (Fuel): Risk of a fuel line rubbing under the floor. Installation of a protective cover.,- 2013 (Belt Tensioner): On some D5 engines, a software update to modify the idle speed and reduce vibrations on the accessory belt tensioner.

What sets it apart 14

Safety

  • Benchmark Safety — The Volvo S60 II excels in safety, having achieved 5 stars in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores for adult (89%) and child (83%) protection. It has also been repeatedly recognized by the IIHS as a "Top Safety Pick+". Its advanced safety arsenal, including standard City Safety, ensures unparalleled peace of mind.

Comfort

  • Exceptional Travel Comfort — Designed for long journeys, the S60 II offers high-level suspension comfort and sound insulation. Its front seats are renowned as among the most ergonomic in automotive production, guaranteeing optimal well-being for the driver and passengers.

Performance

  • Robust and Enjoyable Diesel Engines — The 5-cylinder diesel engines (D3, D4, D5) provide generous torque, a distinctive sound, and recognized robustness, ideal for high-mileage drivers. The 4-cylinder VEA versions (post-2016) stand out for their remarkable fuel efficiency and excellent pairing with the 8-speed Geartronic automatic transmission, offering a smooth and efficient drive.

Reliability

  • Reliability and Build Quality — The Volvo S60 II benefits from Swedish build quality, featuring excellent corrosion resistance and durable paintwork. The Geartronic automatic transmissions (Aisin 6 or 8 speeds) are renowned for their design reliability, contributing to the vehicle's longevity.

Handling

  • Safe and Precise Road Handling — The S60 II's road behavior is safe and predictable, prioritizing stability and comfort. Its good road holding, reassuring in all circumstances, is complemented by precise steering and well-controlled body roll, offering a serene driving experience.

Braking

  • Efficient and Controlled Braking — True to Volvo's reputation for safety, the S60 II demonstrates excellent braking performance. Stable and effective, it guarantees short and controlled stopping distances, ensuring optimal active safety.

Economy

  • Cost-Effectiveness for High-Mileage Drivers — Particularly popular with diesel engines, the S60 II is an excellent option for high-mileage drivers thanks to the fuel efficiency of its latest-generation VEA engines and the robustness of its 5-cylinder blocks, offering an excellent balance between performance and long-term running costs.

Alternatives

  • Europe: BMW 3 Series (F30) for dynamism, Audi A4 (B8/B9) for finish, Mercedes C-Class (W204/W205) for comfort, Peugeot 508 I for price/performance ratio.
  • North America: Lexus IS (superior reliability), Acura TLX, Infiniti Q50.
  • Asia: Audi A4L, BMW 3 Series Li (long-wheelbase versions competing with the local S60L).

Verdict

The Volvo S60 II is an extremely endearing sedan, built to cover miles in supreme comfort. For diesel, the choice of engine is crucial.

If you're looking for character and raw robustness, opt for a 5-cylinder (D3, D4, or D5 from before 2014) with a manual or Geartronic 6 gearbox, provided that accessory belt maintenance is impeccable. If you prioritize modernity, fuel consumption, and smoothness, the 4-cylinder VEA (D3, D4 post-2015) coupled with the Geartronic 8 gearbox are excellent, but avoid the 2014-2015 model years (EGR and piston ring issues).

Absolutely to avoid: All versions equipped with the Powershift automatic gearbox (often associated with the 1.6 D2 or 1.6 petrol T3/T4).
