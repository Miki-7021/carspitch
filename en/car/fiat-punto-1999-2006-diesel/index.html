Summary

The **Fiat Punto II (Type 188)**, launched for the brand's centenary in 1999, was a huge commercial success in Europe. It stands out for its remarkable interior space for its segment and extremely low maintenance costs. A major facelift (Phase 2) occurred in 2003, profoundly changing the front end (large headlights) and introducing the revolutionary small 1.3 MultiJet diesel. While its engines (notably the 1.2 FIRE and 1.9 JTD) are generally very robust, the model is heavily penalized by uneven build quality, capricious electronics (the famous 'City' power steering), and a strong propensity for structural corrosion on aging examples.

**Expert's overall rating:** An ultra-economical choice today, provided you avoid neglected and rusty examples. JTD diesels are marathon runners, but the car around them tends to fall apart.

Strengths 5

  • 1.9 JTD (diesel) and 1.2 8V (petrol) engines with exceptional mechanical robustness
  • Extremely low purchase and insurance cost on the used market
  • Extremely cheap and abundant spare parts in scrapyards
  • Excellent interior space and boot volume (297 litres) for the category
  • Very low fuel consumption for the 1.3 MultiJet

Weaknesses 7

  • High ADAC breakdown rate (6.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year), very fragile electronics
  • Alarming TÜV defect rate (23.5% at 3 years), among the worst in the segment
  • Highly faulty 'City' electric power steering (torque sensor/electric motor)
  • Notorious structural rust (sills, wheel arches, floor) on examples > 100,000 km
  • Poor reliability of the 1.3 MultiJet (EGR, DPF, turbo, chain)
  • Very poor sound insulation, especially on the atmospheric 1.9 D
  • Very low What Car? Reliability score (2/5, 35% of breakdowns reported over 12 months)

Check before buying

  • Red steering wheel shaped warning light on the dashboard (imminent or confirmed power steering failure)
  • Thorough inspection of underbody, sills, and rear wheel arches for perforating corrosion
  • On the 1.3 MultiJet: listen for any metallic noise on startup (sign of a loose timing chain)
  • On the 1.9 JTD: check for excessive black smoke on acceleration (clogged EGR or punctured turbo hose)
  • On the 1.2 8V: check for 'mayonnaise' in the oil cap or oil in the expansion tank (head gasket)
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (power windows, central locking, wipers)

Verdict

The Fiat Punto II is a car with two faces. Mechanically, the 1.9 JTD and 1.2 8V FIRE engines are true workhorses capable of exceeding 300,000 km without flinching. However, everything surrounding the engine (electronics, power steering, bodywork) is fragile, as documented by ADAC and TÜV. The 1.3 MultiJet, although appealing on paper for its fuel consumption, should be avoided as a high-mileage used car due to its fragile timing chain and sensitive peripherals (EGR/Turbo). If you're looking for a diesel, absolutely prioritize the much more robust 1.9 JTD (80 or 85 hp). The purchase is only justified today for an extremely tight budget, requiring a clear service history and a rigorous inspection for corrosion.
