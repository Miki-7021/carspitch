Alfa Romeo 156

I (932) · 1997 – 2003

The Alfa Romeo 156 is an engineer's and enthusiast's car. If you are looking for an economical and trouble-free commuter, walk away. However, for driving enthusiasts, it offers rare sensations at this price point. Summary

The Alfa Romeo 156 (Type 932) is a D-segment sedan that marked the renaissance of the cloverleaf brand in the late 90s. Voted Car of the Year 1998, it stands out with its timeless design by Walter de Silva (hidden rear door handles, aggressive grille) and its sharp road handling thanks to a double-wishbone front suspension. This sheet focuses on Phase 1 (1997-2002) and Phase 2 (interior facelift in 2002), before the major exterior facelift by Giugiaro in late 2003. While it offers exceptional driving pleasure, particularly with petrol engines (Twin Spark and the legendary V6 Busso), it is a car that requires meticulous maintenance, otherwise risking costly breakdowns. Build quality is uneven, and the reliability of peripherals (electronics, running gear) demands constant vigilance.

Strengths 5

  • Magnificent exterior and interior design, aging very well
  • Exceptional chassis: ultra-direct steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock) and sharp front axle
  • Petrol engines (Twin Spark and Busso) with a sporty character and intoxicating sound
  • JTD diesel engines among the most reliable and high-performing of their era
  • Love factor and collector potential (especially V6 and GTA)

Weaknesses 6

  • Fragile running gear: very rapid wear of upper and lower suspension wishbones (squeaking/clunking noises)
  • Demanding and costly maintenance for petrol engines (Twin Spark) (timing belt every 60,000 km)
  • Unreliable and costly to repair Selespeed automated gearbox
  • Sensitivity to corrosion on the floor pans (especially Phase 1 from 1997 to 2000)
  • Catastrophic turning radius (especially on V6)
  • Capricious electronics (airbag warning light often on due to connectors under the seats)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history (invoice less than 3 years old for Twin Spark, with phase variator replacement)
  • Noises on the front axle (speed bumps, degraded roads): sign of wishbones to be replaced (worn silent blocks)
  • Condition of the floor pans (lift the carpets if possible) and rocker panels to hunt for rust
  • Thermostat operation: the temperature needle should stabilize at 90°C. If it stays at 70°C, the thermostat is dead
  • Oil level on petrol engines (Twin Spark and JTS can consume up to 1L/1,000 km without being considered defective by Alfa)
  • Airbag warning light and check engine light (often linked to lambda sensors or mass air flow sensor on petrol)
