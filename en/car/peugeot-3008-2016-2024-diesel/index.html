Summary

The second-generation Peugeot 3008 (code name P84) marked a radical turning point for the lion brand. Abandoning the monospace look of its predecessor to adopt the codes of the compact SUV, it met with dazzling commercial success in Europe. Hailed for its bold design, incisive road handling (the famous 'Peugeot road feel') and futuristic cabin (i-Cockpit), it was elected Car of the Year 2017. A mid-career restyling (Phase 2) occurred late 2020, modernizing the front end (borderless grille, LED fang lights) and updating the infotainment. While its dynamic qualities are undeniable, its reliability record is heavily tarnished by major design flaws in its high-volume engines (1.2 PureTech and 1.5 BlueHDi), requiring extreme vigilance when buying used.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional road handling (comfort/dynamism compromise)
  • Spectacular interior presentation (i-Cockpit) and improved finish quality
  • Generous boot volume (520 liters in thermal versions)
  • Excellent soundproofing on high-end versions
  • Mechanical reliability of the 2.0 BlueHDi engine
  • Extremely easy resale (strong demand)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.2 PureTech (belt) and 1.5 BlueHDi (camshaft chain)
  • Chronic and costly problems related to the AdBlue system on all diesels
  • Slow infotainment system and sometimes unintuitive interface (Phase 1)
  • Average rear passenger space for the category (legroom)
  • Reduced boot volume on plug-in hybrid versions (395 liters)
  • i-Cockpit driving position that doesn't suit all body types

Check before buying

  • On 1.5 BlueHDi: Metallic rattling noise at idle (sign of 7mm camshaft chain wear).
  • On all diesels: History of AdBlue/Urea warning lights. Ask if the tank has already been replaced.
  • On 1.2 PureTech: Condition of the timing belt (cracks, fraying) via the oil filler cap.
  • On PHEV: Smooth operation of the e-EAT8 gearbox during thermal/electric transitions.
  • Electronics: Functioning of the touchscreen, piano keys (Toggle Switches) and digital instrument cluster.
  • Running gear: Wear of front tires (the front axle is very incisive but stresses the tires) and rear suspension noises.

Official recalls

  • November 2020 (1.2 PureTech): Massive recall for timing belt inspection and brake ECU update (risk of loss of assistance due to vacuum pump clogging).
  • October 2020 (Diesel): Risk of fire related to a plastic component near the DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) that can melt. Addition of a heat shield.
  • February 2021 (PHEV): Risk of short circuit on traction battery cables.
  • May 2023 (PHEV): Update of high-voltage battery management software to prevent overheating/fire risk.
  • September 2019: Non-compliant tightening of rear axle mounting screws potentially leading to loss of control.

Verdict

The Peugeot 3008 II is a brilliant SUV on the road, but it demands absolute caution when buying used. If you are looking for a diesel, avoid the 1.5 BlueHDi if it hasn't been made reliable (8mm chain upgrade with invoices to prove it). Unhesitatingly prefer the 2.0 BlueHDi (150 or 180 hp): it's the most robust engine in the range, ideal for this heavy SUV, even if you'll need to keep a budget aside for the inevitable AdBlue tank whim. In petrol, the 1.6 PureTech 180 is the choice for peace of mind compared to the catastrophic 1.2 PureTech 130. Only buy a model with a 100% up-to-date Peugeot network service book, a *sine qua non* condition to hope for coverage in case of damage.
