Summary

The first-generation Opel Insignia (Insignia A) marked a major turning point for the brand with the lightning bolt emblem, replacing the aging Vectra. Voted « Car of the Year 2009 », it appealed with its stately design, highway comfort, and onboard technologies (AFL+ headlights, FlexRide suspension). However, its very high weight (often over 1.6 tons unladen) penalizes dynamism, fuel consumption, and wear of consumables. A significant facelift (Phase 2) occurred in late 2013, bringing a redesign of the center console (removing the myriad of buttons), a new infotainment system, and updated engines. Although this report focuses on diesel engines (which are largely predominant in Europe), the Insignia was a global car, sold under the Vauxhall (United Kingdom), Buick (North America, China), and Holden (Oceania) badges, with specific petrol engines.

Strengths 6

  • Ride comfort and highway sound insulation
  • Exterior design that has aged very well
  • AGR ergonomic seats (among the best on the market)
  • Secure road holding and effective FlexRide chassis
  • Generous boot volume (especially in Sports Tourer wagon)
  • Rich technological equipment for its time (AFL+, driving aids)

Weaknesses 6

  • Excessive weight (penalizes performance, fuel consumption, and brake/tire wear)
  • Disastrous reliability of the 2.0 CDTI (A20DTx) due to the oil pump seal
  • Center console ergonomics on Phase 1 (too many buttons)
  • Average rear spaciousness given the exterior size (sloping headroom on the sedan)
  • Poor peripheral visibility (thick pillars)
  • Fragile M32 manual gearbox on smaller engines

Check before buying

  • On 2.0 CDTI (110 to 163hp): Demand the invoice for the replacement of the oil pump strainer seal. If not done, avoid or have it done immediately.
  • On 1.6 CDTI: Listen carefully to the cold engine (driver's side) to detect a chain noise.
  • Manual gearbox (1.4T, 1.6, 1.8, 1.6 CDTI): Test 5th and 6th gears at 90 km/h. If the lever moves during acceleration or if a whistling sound is heard = bearings are faulty.
  • Automatic gearbox (AF40-6): Check that gear changes are smooth both hot and cold. Demand proof of oil change (every 60,000 km).
  • Electronics: Test the operation of the electric tailgate (fragile struts), heated seats (resistors that break), and the touchscreen/GPS.
  • 4x4 versions: Check for the absence of a 'Rear differential service' message and demand the service history of the Haldex system.

Official recalls

  • November 2009: Recall on 2009-2010 models for a risk of detachment of the driver airbag module.
  • April 2010: Reprogramming of the engine control unit (ECU) on 2.0 CDTI models to prevent DPF overheating during regenerations.
  • December 2014: Replacement of tailgate struts on wagon versions (Sports Tourer) produced between 2009 and 2014 (risk of tailgate falling).
  • June 2016: Recall concerning steering tie rods that may loosen (models produced in 2016).
  • October 2018: Software update on 2.0 CDTI models to correct NOx emissions (Dieselgate).

What sets it apart 7

comfort

  • Premium Ride Comfort and Sound Insulation — Enjoy a serene and refined driving experience, with remarkable sound insulation and ride comfort optimized for long motorway journeys. The ergonomic AGR seats, among the best on the market, guarantee perfect support.

safety

  • Maximum Safety with 5 Euro NCAP Stars — The Opel Insignia A achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 89% for adult protection and 83% for child protection, ensuring unparalleled peace of mind.

  • Timeless and Elegant Exterior Design — The Insignia A stands out with its stately and elegant design, which has aged remarkably well, giving it a distinctive and modern presence on the road.

performance|economy

  • Powerful and Refined Diesel Engine — Discover the 170 hp 2.0 CDTI engine, a new-generation powertrain renowned for its quiet operation, dynamic performance, and fuel efficiency, ideal for long journeys.

practicality

  • Exceptional Cargo Space — Whether in sedan or Sports Tourer version, the Opel Insignia A offers a generous boot volume, perfect for families and travel, allowing you to carry everything you need.

technology

  • Advanced Technological Equipment — Benefit from rich technological equipment for its time, including advanced systems like AFL+ headlights and various driving aids, for a modern and secure experience.

Alternatives

In Europe, the Insignia competes with the Volkswagen Passat B7/B8 (more spacious, more reliable TDIs but more expensive), the Peugeot 508 I (excellent chassis, robust 2.0 HDi engines), the Ford Mondeo III/IV (very spacious, good handling) and the Skoda Superb (unbeatable for interior space). In North America (Buick Regal), it competes with the Acura TSX, the Lincoln MKZ or the Volvo S60. In Oceania (Holden), it is positioned against the Ford Falcon or the Toyota Aurion.

Verdict

The Opel Insignia A is an excellent, comfortable, and stately road car, but it hides serious reliability flaws in its best-selling engines. The 2.0 CDTI (110 to 163 hp) is a real lottery due to its oil pump seal: buy *only* if this seal has been recently replaced preventively. The 1.6 CDTI suffers from its fragile timing chain. If you are looking for a reliable diesel, opt for the 2.0 CDTI 170 hp (B20DTH) introduced in 2015, which is mechanically much more robust despite some AdBlue quirks. For petrol, the 1.4T and 1.6T versions are good choices provided the health of the M32 manual gearbox is checked. A car to negotiate firmly based on its service history.
