Jaguar S-Type

· 1999 – 2008

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Jaguar models

Summary

The Jaguar S-Type (code name X200) marks the return of the Coventry firm to the executive car segment (E-segment). Based on the Ford DEW98 platform (shared with the Lincoln LS and Ford Thunderbird), it features a neo-retro design inspired by the legendary 1963 Jaguar Mark 2.

**Model evolution:** * **Phase 1 (1999-2002):** Launched with the 3.0 V6 and 4.0 V8 engines. Interior finish criticized for its lack of prestige (too many Ford switches) and reliability issues (chain tensioners on the V8, temperamental 5-speed automatic gearbox). * **Phase 2 (2002-2004):** Major overhaul. New, much more luxurious dashboard, redesigned running gear (new front suspension), introduction of the excellent ZF 6-speed automatic gearbox, replacement of the 4.0 with the highly reliable 4.2 V8, and launch of the fiery S-Type R version (395 hp supercharged V8) and the small 2.5 V6. * **Phase 3 (2004-2008):** Exterior facelift (aluminum hood, modified grille, sleek taillights) and introduction of the 2.7 V6 twin-turbo diesel engine (developed with PSA) to conquer the European market.

**Expert's overall rating:** A sedan with undeniable charm, whose reliability was transformed from 2002 onwards. Post-2002 models are excellent bargains, provided you monitor for corrosion, the model's true Achilles heel today.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional ride comfort and high-level soundproofing
  • Timeless neo-retro design that ages very well
  • Remarkable reliability of post-2002 petrol engines (3.0 V6 and 4.2 V8)
  • Transformed road handling on phases 2 and 3 (revised running gear)
  • Current price/performance ratio on the used market
  • Excellent ZF 6-speed automatic gearbox (from 2002)

Weaknesses 6

  • Very frequent and hidden perforating corrosion on the rocker panels (under the plastic covers)
  • Disappointing interior finish and plastic quality on Phase 1 (1999-2002)
  • Disastrous reliability of the original 4.0 V8 (chain tensioners)
  • High cost of Jaguar-specific spare parts
  • 2.7 Diesel engine unsuited for short trips (DPF, EGR)
  • Rapid wear of suspension bushings (front and rear axles)

Check before buying

  • **Rocker panel corrosion (CRITICAL):** Remove or pull back the side plastic covers. The frame rails and rocker panels rot from the inside. This is the #1 reason for S-Types being scrapped today.
  • **Automatic gearbox history:** On post-2002 models (ZF 6-speed), verify that an oil change has been done. Test for the absence of jolts when braking (the famous 'Lurch').
  • **Electric Parking Brake (EPB):** From 2002 onwards, check that it engages and disengages without a screeching noise or error message on the dashboard.
  • **Running gear:** Listen for clunking noises on rough roads. The front lower control arm bushings and sway bar links wear out quickly (heavy vehicle).
  • **Cooling system:** Hunt for traces of pink/orange coolant around the thermostat housing and the expansion tank (which tends to micro-crack).
  • **Electrics:** Check the proper operation of all accessories (power windows, dual-zone climate control which can blow hot on one side and cold on the other due to a seized DCCV valve).

Verdict

The Jaguar S-Type is a car with two faces. Phase 1 models (1999-2002) are generally to be avoided: finish unworthy of the badge, fragile 5-speed automatic gearbox, and a 4.0 V8 engine with catastrophic reliability (chain tensioners).

On the other hand, **models produced from mid-2002 onwards (Phase 2 and 3) are excellent sedans**. The interior is sumptuous, the ZF 6-speed gearbox is a delight, and the 3.0 V6 and 4.2 V8 engines are exceptionally robust. The 2.7 Diesel should be reserved for high-mileage drivers, otherwise you risk ruin in emissions control costs (DPF/EGR).

**Expert's advice:** Look for a post-2003 3.0 V6 or 4.2 V8 model. The most crucial pre-purchase inspection is not mechanical, but the bodywork: **meticulously inspect the rocker panels under the plastic covers**. If there is perforating rust, run away; repairs will exceed the vehicle's value.
