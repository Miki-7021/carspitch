Nissan Leaf

II (ZE1) · 2017 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The **second-generation Nissan Leaf (ZE1)**, launched in late 2017, had the challenging task of succeeding the world's pioneering electric vehicle. It corrects the main flaws of the first generation (ZE0) with a much more consensual design, a larger trunk (435 liters), and higher capacity batteries (40 kWh then 62 kWh). It introduces significant technologies such as the **e-Pedal** (one-pedal driving) and the **ProPILOT** semi-autonomous driving system.

However, Nissan made the controversial choice to retain two aging technologies: the **CHAdeMO** fast-charging standard (now superseded by CCS in Europe and North America) and **passive battery cooling** (by air, without a liquid circuit). This latter point leads to the *Rapidgate* phenomenon (throttling of charging speed during successive fast charges to protect cells from overheating).

Overall, it is a mechanically extremely reliable car, ideal for daily use and home charging, but unsuitable for regular long highway trips.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall reliability (very robust EM57 electric motor and power electronics)
  • Suspension comfort and quiet operation
  • Very well calibrated and intuitive e-Pedal system
  • Generous trunk volume for the category (435 L)
  • Attractive prices on the used car market
  • Negligible maintenance cost

Weaknesses 5

  • Passive battery cooling (Rapidgate phenomenon on highway)
  • Obsolete CHAdeMO fast-charging standard (increasingly rare charging stations)
  • Dated and slow NissanConnect infotainment system
  • Imperfect driving position (steering wheel not adjustable for reach)
  • Bulky central tunnel in the rear seats (legacy of shared platform)

Check before buying

  • Battery State of Health (SoH): Demand a test or use the LeafSpy app with an OBD2 dongle before purchase.
  • Front suspension noises: Clunking over speed bumps (fragile strut mounts or stabilizer bar links).
  • Brake disc condition: Check for deep rust or caliper seizure.
  • Age of the 12V battery: A weak 12V battery causes multiple error messages on the dashboard.
  • Front tire wear: The immediate torque (especially on the 62 kWh) tends to “eat” through the rubber quickly.

Safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — The Nissan Leaf II achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores for adult (93%) and child (86%) protection, and was designated an IIHS Top Safety Pick.

Practicality

  • Comfort and Generous Space — Enjoy remarkable suspension comfort and quiet operation, complemented by a generous boot volume of 435 liters, ideal for all your activities.

Performance

Economy

  • Negligible Running Costs — Benefit from extremely low maintenance costs and very attractive prices on the used car market, making the Nissan Leaf II an economical and wise choice for your budget.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Renault Zoe ZE50 (more compact, 22kW AC charger), Volkswagen ID.3 (RWD, CCS charging, better on highway), Hyundai Kona Electric (record efficiency, liquid cooling), Peugeot e-208. - **North America:** Chevrolet Bolt EV (better range, liquid cooling), Tesla Model 3 Standard Range (used), Hyundai Kona Electric. - **Asia / Emerging Markets:** BYD Dolphin, MG4 (RWD, dedicated platform, superior fast charging).

Verdict

The Nissan Leaf II (ZE1) is an excellent transitional electric car, provided you clearly define your needs. If you are looking for a primary vehicle to travel across the country, **AVOID IT**: the disappearing CHAdeMO standard and passive battery cooling will turn your long journeys into a nightmare. However, if you are looking for a spacious, ultra-reliable, comfortable secondary vehicle for daily commutes and you charge at home, it's a golden opportunity on the used car market. **NEGOTIATE** firmly on the price, arguing the obsolescence of the CHAdeMO port.
