Summary

The **KGM Tivoli** (marketed under the name **SsangYong Tivoli** until 2023) is an urban SUV (B-segment) that marked the revival of the Korean brand. Designed to compete with the Renault Captur and Peugeot 2008, it stands out with generous interior space, rich standard equipment, and aggressive pricing.

The model underwent a major evolution in **2019 (Phase 2)** with the introduction of new turbocharged engines (developed with Mahindra), a redesigned dashboard, and improved soundproofing. In **2024**, following the acquisition of SsangYong by the KG Group, the model was renamed KGM Tivoli with a slight exterior facelift.

Overall, it is a pragmatic vehicle. While it suffers from higher-than-average fuel consumption for its category and basic interior plastics on the early phases, it compensates with **very reassuring mechanical reliability**, notably thanks to the use of timing chains on its engines and an excellent Aisin-sourced automatic transmission.

Strengths 5

  • Remarkable interior space for the segment (rear legroom)
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Overall mechanical reliability well above the European average
  • Smooth and very robust Aisin torque converter automatic transmission
  • Long manufacturer warranty (often 5 to 7 years depending on markets, reassuring for recent used models)

Weaknesses 6

  • High fuel consumption, particularly on petrol engines
  • Soundproofing could be improved on Phase 1 models (2015-2019)
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and prone to scratching)
  • Sparse KGM/SsangYong dealer network
  • Rapid depreciation (an advantage when buying, a disadvantage when selling)
  • Slightly firm suspension at low speeds

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: verify that oil changes were done on time (crucial for GDi-T and e-XDi versions).
  • Automatic transmission: ensure there are no jerks when shifting gears (D to R). Demand proof of a fluid change if the vehicle has over 80,000 km.
  • On-board electronics: test the touchscreen, rearview camera, and TPMS sensors (tire pressure valves are often temperamental).
  • Front axle: premature wear of front tires on the 1.5 GDi-T 163 hp version (the front axle sometimes struggles to handle the torque).
  • DPF (Diesel): check for the absence of a check engine light and ask for the previous owner's driving profile (avoid diesels that have only been driven in the city).

Verdict

The KGM (SsangYong) Tivoli is an **extremely rational and smart used choice**. SHUNNED by the general public due to its little-known badge, it suffers from heavy depreciation, making it a bargain on the second-hand market. Mechanically, it is much more reliable than many of its European competitors (especially those equipped with first-generation PureTech or TCE engines).

**Our advice:** Favor a **Phase 2 model (from late 2019 onwards)** equipped with the **1.5 GDi-T 163 hp engine and automatic transmission**. Although thirsty for fuel, it offers superior driving pleasure, a modernized interior, and flawless reliability. The 1.6 e-XDi diesel is also an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers, provided the condition of the DPF is checked.
