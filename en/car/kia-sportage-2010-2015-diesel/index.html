Summary

The third-generation Kia Sportage (Type SL) marked a radical turning point for the Korean brand. Designed by Peter Schreyer, it abandoned the rustic off-roader look for a dynamic and urban compact SUV design, sharing its platform with the Hyundai ix35.

Positioning: It is a compact SUV (C-SUV) with a very aggressive price/equipment ratio, supported at the time by the famous 7-year warranty.

Evolutions: A facelift occurred in early 2014, bringing a new honeycomb grille, LED taillights, higher quality soft-touch interior plastics, revised sound insulation (acoustic windshield), and suspension adjustments to mitigate the firmness of early models.

Expert's overall rating: A pivotal model, generally very reliable with diesel engines (which represented the vast majority of sales in Europe), but whose reputation was tarnished across the Atlantic by the woes of the Theta II gasoline engines. The all-wheel drive (AWD) system also requires particular vigilance.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of diesel engines (1.7 and 2.0 CRDi)
  • Exterior design that has aged very well
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Spaciousness and rear passenger room
  • Smooth and very robust torque converter automatic gearbox (6-speed auto)

Weaknesses 5

  • Chronic fragility of the AWD system (angle drive splines wear out and become smooth)
  • Very firm damping (especially with 18-inch wheels and before the 2014 facelift)
  • Poor rear visibility (very wide C-pillars)
  • Quality of interior plastics on Phase 1 models (prone to scratches)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)

Check before buying

  • AWD operation (on 2.0 CRDi): Perform a firm start on a slippery surface (gravel/grass). If the front wheels spin without the rear engaging, the transfer case is faulty (repair > €2000).
  • Clutch and Flywheel (Manual Gearbox): Shuddering when starting in first gear or knocking at idle (especially on the 1.7 CRDi).
  • Panoramic sunroof: Check for complete opening/closing. The rails get dirty and the motor strains, which can lead to mechanism failure.
  • Onboard electronics: Reversing camera becoming blurry or black screen (water infiltration in the lens), Bluetooth/GPS system bugs.
  • Steering rack: Knocking felt in the steering wheel during low-speed maneuvers (worn steering column star bushing, a €5 part but significant labor).

Official recalls

  • Brake light switch (2013): Replacement of the brake pedal switch which could prevent brake lights from illuminating or block the automatic gearbox lever in 'P'.
  • Seatbelt pretensioners (2014): Risk of incorrect deployment during a collision.
  • ABS / HECU module (2021/2022): Risk of short circuit in the ABS hydraulic module potentially causing an engine compartment fire, even with the ignition off. Replacement of fuses with lower amperage models and module inspection.
  • Theta II Engines (North America primarily): Multiple recalls for inspection, software update (KSDS), or complete engine replacement due to risks of seizing and fire.

Verdict

The Kia Sportage III (SL) is an excellent used car choice if you target diesel engines. The 1.7 CRDi 115 hp is the sensible choice for daily use: economical, reliable (if the clutch has been maintained), and sufficient for its size. The 2.0 CRDi (136 or 184 hp) is a true workhorse, indestructible in terms of the engine block, but it requires a rigorous inspection of the AWD system before purchase.

Expert advice: Prioritize a facelifted model (post-2014) with the 1.7 CRDi or 2.0 CRDi 2WD (front-wheel drive) for maximum peace of mind. Avoid 2.0 and 2.4 gasoline versions (Theta II) imported or sold outside Europe without an impeccable service history or an already replaced engine.
