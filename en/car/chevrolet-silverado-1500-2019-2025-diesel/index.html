Chevrolet Silverado 1500

4th gen (T1XX) · 2019 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The 4th generation Chevrolet Silverado 1500 (codename T1XX) is an essential full-size pickup truck on the North American market. Launched for the 2019 model year, it received a major facelift in 2022 (often referred to as '2022.5') which corrected its main flaw: an interior initially considered too basic compared to the competition (Ram 1500, Ford F-150). This facelift brought new screens, higher quality materials, and the extreme off-road ZR2 version. In terms of reliability, the record is mixed: while the inline 6-cylinder diesel engine (3.0L Duramax) shines with its excellent reliability and fuel efficiency, the traditional V8s (5.3L and 6.2L) are marred by recurring hydraulic lifter problems linked to the cylinder deactivation system (DFM). The overall rating reflects an average between the excellence of the diesel and the shortcomings of the V8s.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional 3.0L Duramax diesel engine (torquey, reliable, and very fuel-efficient)
  • Competitive towing and payload capacities
  • Very modern and ergonomic interior on facelifted versions (2022+)
  • Sound road handling and suspension comfort
  • Impressive variety of configurations and trims (from the Work Truck to the luxurious High Country or the extreme ZR2)

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the V8 engines (5.3L and 6.2L) dragged down by the cylinder deactivation system (DFM/AFM)
  • Very disappointing interior finish and material quality on 2019-2021 models
  • Transmission jerks on the early 10-speed automatic gearboxes (2019-2020)
  • Imposing size making parking difficult in urban environments
  • Cost of replacing the oil pump belt on the LM2 diesel (requires removing the transmission)

Check before buying

  • Strict maintenance history: crucial for the V8s (frequent oil changes to protect the lifters).
  • Ticking noises on startup on the 5.3L and 6.2L V8s (early warning sign of lifter failure).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior: check for the absence of jerks or hesitations when shifting gears cold.
  • On 2019-2020 models: check if the recall regarding the seatbelt pretensioner fire risk has been performed.
  • Condition of the emissions control system on the 3.0L Duramax (DPF regeneration history, absence of check engine light).

Alternatives

* **Ford F-150 (Gen 13 & 14)**: The direct competitor. Offers excellent turbo V6 engines (EcoBoost) and a 5.0L V8 that is more reliable than GM's 5.3L. The 3.0L PowerStroke diesel (discontinued in 2021) is good but less capable than the Duramax. * **Ram 1500 (DT)**: Absolute benchmark for ride comfort (coil/air suspensions) and interior quality. The 5.7L Hemi V8 is robust (despite some exhaust manifold issues). The 3.0L EcoDiesel experienced reliability problems in its early generations. * **Toyota Tundra (Gen 3 - 2022+)**: Japanese alternative (manufactured in the USA). Abandoned the V8 for a twin-turbo V6 (which had major recalls for machining debris in 2022-2023). Historically the king of reliability, but the new generation has yet to prove itself.

Verdict

The 4th generation Chevrolet Silverado 1500 is a pickup truck with two faces. If you opt for the **3.0L Duramax Diesel** engine, it's a big **YES**: this engine is a masterpiece of efficiency, offering V8 torque with the fuel consumption of a compact SUV, while boasting excellent reliability. On the other hand, if you are aiming for the traditional V8s (5.3L or 6.2L), caution is advised. The recurring hydraulic lifter problems linked to the DFM system are a costly sword of Damocles out of warranty. It is imperative to favor the facelifted models (2022 and newer) to benefit from an interior worthy of the name, as the 2019-2021 versions show a glaring technological and qualitative lag compared to the competition.
