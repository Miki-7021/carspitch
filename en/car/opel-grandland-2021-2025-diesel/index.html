Summary

The second-generation Grandland, often called Grandland II or Grandland Facelift (as it is a major redesign of the Grandland X), was launched in 2021. It marks a major stylistic evolution with the adoption of Opel's 'Vizor' grille, aligning its design with the Mokka and Crossland. Based on Stellantis' (formerly PSA) EMP2 platform, it shares its technical underpinnings with models like the Peugeot 3008 and Citroën C5 Aircross. Positioned in the highly competitive compact SUV segment, it aims to offer a compromise between comfort, space, and modern technologies. Overall reliability is average for the segment, with specific points of attention for Stellantis engines.

Strengths 6

  • Modern and distinctive design (Vizor)
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Decent interior space and boot volume
  • Good driving position
  • Efficient engines (especially the 1.5 BlueHDi and PHEVs)
  • Up-to-date technological equipment (depending on trim level)

Weaknesses 5

  • Quality of some interior plastics (inferior to some competitors)
  • Multimedia interface sometimes slow or unintuitive
  • High weight of PHEV versions impacting dynamism
  • Reliability of the AdBlue system on diesel (widespread problem at Stellantis)
  • Vigilance required for the 1.2 PureTech timing belt

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for 1.2 PureTech oil changes and PHEV services)
  • Functioning of the AdBlue system (warning lights, error messages) on the 1.5 BlueHDi
  • Condition of the timing belt on the 1.2 PureTech (request a visual inspection)
  • Real-world range and condition of the traction battery on PHEV versions
  • Absence of abnormal noises from the front axle or steering
  • Functioning of all electronic equipment (screen, driving aids)

Verdict

The Grandland II is a compact SUV that offers a good compromise for families and frequent drivers. Its modern design and comfort are undeniable assets. For a confident purchase, the choice of engine is crucial. The **1.5 BlueHDi 130** is an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers thanks to its fuel efficiency and torque, provided that vigilance is maintained regarding the AdBlue system's maintenance, which remains its major weakness. The **1.2 PureTech 130** is suitable for more versatile use but requires rigorous monitoring of the timing belt. The **PHEV** versions are interesting for those who can charge regularly and maximize electric use, but their technical complexity and the potential cost of battery replacement must be considered. Overall, the Grandland II is a vehicle to **NEGOTIATE** to ensure that known issues have been addressed or to anticipate specific maintenance costs. A clear service history is non-negotiable.
