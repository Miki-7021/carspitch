Summary

The second-generation Volkswagen Tiguan (code name AD1 for the short wheelbase, BW1 for the long-wheelbase Allspace) is based on the famous VAG group's modular MQB platform. A true global best-seller, it has established itself through its spaciousness, rigorous finish, and abundant technological offerings. Restyled in late 2020 (phase 2), it modernized its front end and updated its infotainment system (MIB3), while introducing 'Evo' diesel engines with double AdBlue injection (Twin Dosing) and a plug-in hybrid version. Diesel Focus: In Europe, the 2.0 TDI accounted for the vast majority of sales, particularly popular with high-mileage drivers and families for its generous torque and fuel efficiency on the highway. Although powerful, this EA288 (then EA288 evo) engine requires rigorous monitoring, especially concerning its emissions control system (AdBlue/FAP).

Strengths 5

  • Excellent spaciousness and modularity (sliding rear bench, 7-seater Allspace version)
  • Enjoyment and fuel efficiency of the 2.0 TDI on long journeys
  • Efficiency of the 4Motion all-wheel drive and DSG gearbox (on wet-clutch versions)
  • Build quality and sound insulation (especially in phase 2)
  • Towing capacity (up to 2.5 tons on 2.0 TDI 4Motion)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the AdBlue system (pump/tank) on TDIs
  • Fragile DSG7 (DQ200 dry-clutch) gearbox on small front-wheel-drive petrol engines
  • Very firm damping with 19/20-inch wheels (R-Line trim)
  • Frequent electronic bugs on the MIB3 multimedia system (post-2020)
  • High maintenance cost within the Volkswagen network

Check before buying

  • History of engine/AdBlue warning lights (ask if the tank has already been replaced)
  • Coolant level (frequent water pump leaks on TDI and TSI)
  • DSG gearbox oil change invoices (every 60,000 km max) and Haldex (every 3 years)
  • Traces of moisture under floor mats or in the trunk (clogged panoramic sunroof drains)
  • Smooth operation of the DSG gearbox (no jerking on startup or when shifting between forward/reverse)

Official recalls

  • Ambient lighting (2018): Risk of short circuit and fire related to the panoramic sunroof's LED module.
  • Rear shock absorbers (2019): Welding defect on the shock absorber fork potentially leading to loss of control.
  • Brake pedal (2020): On automatic models, the brake pedal plate may detach due to a faulty weld.
  • Engine cover (2022): The decorative engine cover may detach and touch hot parts (fire risk). Primarily concerns 2.0 TSI and some TDI models.
  • Tow bar device (2018): The locking mechanism of the retractable tow bar may be defective.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 3008 (more dynamic but less powerful diesels), BMW X1 (premium, excellent diesels), Skoda Kodiaq (technical cousin, more spacious), Toyota RAV4 (hybrid benchmark, no diesel).
  • North America: Honda CR-V, Toyota RAV4, Mazda CX-5 (the Tiguan stands out there with its European handling and its 2.0 TSI).
  • Asia / China: Honda Breeze, Toyota Wildlander, Buick Envision.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Tiguan II is a remarkably well-rounded family SUV. In its 2.0 TDI 150 hp version, it offers the best power/consumption compromise for high-mileage drivers. However, buying a used diesel model requires extreme vigilance regarding the condition of the AdBlue system and water pump. Avoid diesel if you primarily drive in the city (guaranteed clogging). For mixed or urban use, the 1.5 TSI 150 hp is a much more sensible and generally more reliable choice, provided you avoid the DSG7 DQ200 gearbox if possible, or ensure it is in perfect condition. Opt for a post-2019 model with a clear service history.
