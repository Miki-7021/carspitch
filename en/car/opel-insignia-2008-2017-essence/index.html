Opel Insignia

A · 2008 – 2017

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Opel Insignia (Insignia A) marked a radical break from the Vectra it replaced. Voted Car of the Year 2009, it impressed with its stately design, significantly improved finish quality, and excellent road manners. However, its main drawback lies in its very high weight (often over 1.6 tonnes unladen), which negatively impacts performance, fuel consumption, and the wear of the running gear. The 2013 facelift (Phase 2) brought a welcome redesign of the center console (removing the overabundance of buttons), an infotainment system update (IntelliLink), and new engines (notably the direct-injection SIDI petrol units). Although European demand at the time was massively oriented towards diesel, the petrol offering is plentiful and deserves particular attention, especially since some units prove very reliable with rigorous maintenance.

Strengths 6

  • Exterior design that has aged very well
  • Ride comfort and highway sound insulation
  • AGR ergonomic seats (among the best on the market)
  • Secure road holding and effective FlexRide chassis
  • Generous boot volume on the estate version (Sports Tourer)
  • Very attractive price/equipment ratio on the used market

Weaknesses 6

  • Excessive weight penalizing dynamics, fuel consumption, and brakes
  • Disappointing rear passenger space given the exterior size (sedan)
  • Center console ergonomics on Phase 1 (too many buttons)
  • Poor peripheral visibility (thick pillars, small rear window)
  • Reliability of the M32 manual gearbox (bearings)
  • Fatal design flaw on the best-selling diesel (2.0 CDTI oil strainer seal)

Check before buying

  • Manual gearbox (M32): listen for any whistling in 5th and 6th gear, and observe if the lever moves during acceleration.
  • SIDI petrol engines: demand invoices proving the use of Dexos 1 Gen 2 oil.
  • Running gear: premature wear of front wishbone bushings, shock absorbers, and brakes due to weight.
  • Electronics: operation of heated seats (heating elements often break), electric tailgate (struts), and touchscreen (Phase 2).
  • 4x4 transmission (Haldex): check the service history of the rear differential oil changes (shuddering or 'Rear axle service' message = danger).
  • Water ingress: check the boot floor and rear lights.

Verdict

The Opel Insignia A is an excellent, comfortable, and stately road car, now available at very attractive prices. However, its enormous weight has repercussions on general wear and tear and fuel consumption. For petrol engines, the choice must be strategic: the 1.4 Turbo is a good economic compromise but requires strict verification of the M32 gearbox. The 2.0 Turbo and V6 versions are enjoyable but very expensive to maintain. The 1.6 SIDI should be avoided if the oil history is not perfect (LSPI risk). Prioritize a Phase 2 model (post-2013) for its modernized interior, with the 1.4 Turbo for practicality, or the 2.0 Turbo for driving pleasure, while keeping a budget aside for running gear maintenance.
