Summary

The second-generation Jeep Compass (code MP) was launched in 2017, replacing both the first-generation Compass and the Patriot. Positioned as a compact SUV, it fits between the Renegade and the Cherokee in the Jeep lineup. It shares its platform (Small Wide 4x4) with the Renegade and the Fiat 500X. Its design, inspired by the Grand Cherokee, gives it a more stately and modern look than its predecessors.

A significant **facelift** was introduced in late 2020 for the European market (2021 model), then gradually for other markets. This restyling brought aesthetic changes (bumpers, headlights, grille) and, more importantly, a major interior redesign, with a new dashboard, a larger infotainment screen (Uconnect 5), and improved materials. Powertrains also evolved, notably with the introduction of the 1.3 GSE T4 engines and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions in Europe, as well as the 2.0 GME T4 in North America.

Overall, the Compass II offers a good compromise between off-road capabilities (especially in Trailhawk versions) and on-road comfort. Its reliability is mixed, highly dependent on powertrains and markets, with recurring weaknesses in certain drivetrains and electronics.

Weaknesses 6

  • Uneven reliability depending on powertrains (notably 2.4 Tigershark and 1.4 MultiAir)
  • Excessive oil consumption on the 2.4 Tigershark
  • Average interior material quality before the facelift
  • 9-speed automatic gearbox (ZF 9HP) sometimes hesitant
  • Uconnect system sometimes slow or buggy (before Uconnect 5)
  • Potentially high parts and maintenance costs

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for belt replacements and oil changes)
  • Engine oil level (particularly on 1.4 MultiAir and 2.4 Tigershark)
  • MultiAir unit operation (stable idle, no misfires, no engine light)
  • Absence of abnormal engine noises (knocking, whistling)
  • Automatic gearbox operation (smooth shifts, no jerks)
  • Condition of DPF and EGR on diesels (no excessive smoke, no warning light)
  • Operation of all electronic equipment (Uconnect, driving aids)
  • Condition of the battery and charging system on PHEV (4xe) versions

Verdict

The Jeep Compass II is an SUV with an attractive design and appreciable off-road capabilities for its category. However, its reliability is a crucial point to consider, as it varies considerably depending on the powertrain and production year.

**For petrol powertrains:** * **AVOID the 2.4 Tigershark** if you don't want to deal with excessive oil consumption and potential camshaft issues. It's a known problem engine. * The **1.4 MultiAir** should be negotiated with caution. The MultiAir unit is a costly weak point. An impeccable service history and an attractive price are essential. * The **1.3 GSE T4 and 1.5 GSE T4 MHEV** are more modern and generally more reliable choices, especially after the initial software updates. They represent a good compromise between performance and consumption. * The **1.3 GSE PHEV (4xe)** is interesting for its urban efficiency, but its complexity and the potential cost of repairing the hybrid system make it a choice to be carefully evaluated, especially on the used market. * The **2.0 GME T4** is promising, but still new to the Compass.

**For diesel powertrains:** * The **1.6 and 2.0 MultiJet II** are generally more robust and reliable choices, provided they receive rigorous maintenance and appropriate use (avoid repeated short trips for the DPF).

**In summary:** Prioritize a post-facelift model (2021+) with the 1.3 GSE T4 engines or the MultiJet II diesels. A clear service history is non-negotiable. The Compass can be a good purchase if you choose the right powertrain and are diligent with maintenance. Otherwise, it can quickly become a financial drain.
