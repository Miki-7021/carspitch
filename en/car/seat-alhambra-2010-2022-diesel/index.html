Summary

The second-generation Seat Alhambra (internal code 7N) is a large family minivan launched in 2010, sharing its platform and most of its components with the Volkswagen Sharan II. It represents Seat's offering in the minivan segment, emphasizing practicality, generous interior space, and exemplary modularity (up to 7 seats, sliding doors).

Aesthetically, it adopted Seat's design language of the time, with a more dynamic touch than its VW twin. A major facelift occurred in 2015, bringing minor aesthetic changes (headlights, taillights, grille) but, more importantly, significant technical updates, including the introduction of more efficient engines (transition from EA189 TDI engines to EA288, and from EA111 TSI to EA211/EA888 Gen3) and new infotainment and driver assistance technologies.

Overall, the Alhambra II is a reliable and robust vehicle, particularly appreciated for its ability to comfortably transport a large family and their luggage. Its reliability is closely linked to that of the Sharan, benefiting from Volkswagen Group engineering. Diesel versions, very popular in Europe, are the core of the range and offer a good compromise between performance and consumption, provided rigorous maintenance is performed.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional spaciousness and modularity (7 seats, sliding doors)
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Serious build quality and finish (close to VW)
  • Safe and balanced road handling
  • Efficient and torquey diesel engines (especially EA288)
  • Good residual value on the used car market

Weaknesses 5

  • Bulky in urban environments
  • Potentially high maintenance costs for certain parts (dual-mass flywheel, DPF, injectors on EA189)
  • Reliability of early versions of the 1.4 TSI (chain) and 2.0 TDI (EGR/DPF/injectors)
  • DSG gearbox sometimes temperamental (mechatronics, neglected oil changes)
  • Oil consumption on some 2.0 TSI EA888 Gen2 engines

General note on diesels (EA189 & EA288): Problems related to the EGR and DPF are often exacerbated by predominantly urban use or short trips. Regular maintenance and the use of quality fuel are crucial. The dual-mass flywheel is a wear part whose lifespan depends heavily on driving style.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for DSG oil changes and wear part replacements)
  • EGR valve and DPF operation (no engine light, no excessive smoke)
  • Abnormal noises on startup or shutdown (dual-mass flywheel)
  • Condition of the timing chain (1.4 TSI EA111, 2.0 TSI EA888 Gen2) or belt (1.4 TSI EA211)
  • DSG gearbox gear changes (smoothness, jerks)
  • Operation of electric sliding doors (if equipped)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (heavy vehicle)

Official recalls

Official Manufacturer Recalls

  • 2016 (Rappel Conso): Potential issue with brake pedal assembly. Risk of pedal detachment. Vehicles produced between 2010 and 2016.
  • 2017 (Rappel Conso): Engine control unit software issue (related to 'Dieselgate') on some 2.0 TDI. May result in non-compliant NOx emissions. Vehicles produced between 2009 and 2014.
  • 2018 (Rappel Conso): Malfunction of the automatic emergency braking system (Front Assist). May not brake as expected. Vehicles produced between 2017 and 2018.
  • 2019 (Rappel Conso): Rear spoiler attachment issue. Risk of detachment. Vehicles produced between 2018 and 2019.
  • 2020 (Rappel Conso): Central rear seat attachment issue. Risk of injury in case of collision. Vehicles produced between 2019 and 2020.
  • Takata Airbags: Like many vehicles from this period, some Alhambras may have been affected by Takata airbag recalls. Check with a dealership using the chassis number.

Tip: Always check if recalls concerning the targeted vehicle have been performed by contacting a Seat dealership with the chassis number (VIN).

Verdict

The Seat Alhambra II is an excellent choice for large families or those needing a large cargo volume and exemplary modularity. Its reliability is generally good, especially after the 2015 facelift which brought more refined engines (EA288 for diesels, EA211/EA888 Gen3 for petrols).

For family and road use, the 2.0 TDI (150 hp EA288) versions are the most recommendable. They offer an excellent compromise between consumption, torque, and reliability. The 1.4 TSI (EA211) versions are also good choices for less intensive or predominantly urban use.

Purchase conditions: Prioritize a model with a clear service history, ideally from Seat or a VAG group specialist. Check that all manufacturer recalls have been performed. Pay particular attention to the condition of the dual-mass flywheel and the proper functioning of the DSG gearbox (if present), as well as the absence of engine warning lights related to the EGR or DPF, especially on diesel versions prior to 2015.

TO NEGOTIATE: 2.0 TDI EA189 models (before 2015) or 1.4 TSI EA111 models (before 2012) without a clear history of timing chain or EGR valve/DPF replacement.

TO AVOID: Vehicles with signs of neglected maintenance, abnormal gearbox or engine noises, or persistent engine warning lights.
