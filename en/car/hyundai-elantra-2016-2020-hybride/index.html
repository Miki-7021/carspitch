Summary

The sixth-generation Hyundai Elantra (code AD) marked a clear move upmarket for the Korean compact sedan, with a stiffened chassis (composed of 53% high-strength steel), improved soundproofing, and a more mature design (Fluidic Sculpture 2.0). A major facelift took place in 2019, profoundly modifying the front end (triangular headlights) and updating the driving aids.

Clarification on the hybrid engine: Although this sheet targets the hybrid engine, it is imperative to specify that generation VI (AD) was never marketed with a true hybrid engine (HEV or PHEV). The true Elantra Hybrid only appeared with the following generation (CN7, from 2021). On the AD generation (2016-2020), the role of high energy efficiency vehicle was held by the 1.4 T-GDI "Eco" version (in North America) and by 1.6 LPi (liquefied petroleum gas) versions in South Korea. We will detail these "green" versions while exhaustively covering the rest of the combustion engine range.

Strengths 5

  • Generous manufacturer warranty (often 5 years unlimited mileage in Europe, or 10 years/100,000 miles on the powertrain in the USA)
  • Soundproofing and ride comfort significantly improved compared to the MD generation
  • Intuitive interior ergonomics and responsive infotainment system
  • Very controlled fuel consumption on the 1.4 T-GDI (Eco) version, compensating for the lack of a hybrid
  • Safe and secure road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Absence of a true hybrid engine (HEV) on this generation
  • Critical recall on the 2.0 MPI engine (piston rings) for the 2019-2020 model years
  • Dual-clutch gearbox (7DCT) sometimes hesitant and prone to jerking at low speeds
  • 2017 model year subject to numerous early-life recalls (steering, brakes, airbags)
  • Quality of some lower interior plastics

Check before buying

  • Recall history: This is the crucial point on this model (especially for the 2.0L engine and the 2017 model year).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior: On the 1.4T and 1.6T (DCT) versions, check for the absence of shuddering during hill starts.
  • Oil consumption: Particularly on the 2.0 MPI (Nu) engine.
  • Power steering (MDPS): On 2017 models, check that there is no loss of assistance or abnormal noises in the steering column.
  • Trunk lock: Check its proper operation (ongoing recall for 2016 models).

Official recalls

The AD generation has been the subject of several major recalls, particularly in its early years of production and on the 2.0L engine:

  • [2024-11-04] Transmission (Software): The transmission software may not activate the fail-safe mode during a fault, resulting in sudden deceleration (Rappel Conso France / Global).
  • [2024-02-23] Trunk lock (NHTSA 24V146000): On 2015-2016 models, the trunk lock can become damaged, preventing opening from the inside.
  • [2021-04-28] 2.0L Nu MPI Engine (NHTSA 21V301000): CRITICAL RECALL. On 2019-2020 models, the oil control piston rings may not have been properly heat-treated, leading to engine damage, stalling, or a risk of fire.
  • [2019-10-09] Running gear (NHTSA 19V720000 / 19V721000): On certain 2020 models, insufficient tightening of the right rear wheel nuts and the lower suspension arm ball joint fasteners.
  • [2017-03-28] Power steering (NHTSA 17V213000): On 2017 models, the electric power steering (MDPS) connector can be damaged, resulting in a loss of assistance.
  • [2017-01-26] Braking (NHTSA 17V063000): On 2017 models, possible failure of the brake booster (Mastervac), resulting in a loss of braking assistance.
  • [2016-12-30 & 2016-04-21] Airbags (NHTSA 16V956000 / 16V232000): Risk of rupture of the driver's airbag inflator upon deployment on 2016-2017 models.

Verdict

The sixth-generation Hyundai Elantra (AD) is an overall competent, comfortable, and well-equipped compact sedan. However, if you are specifically looking for a hybrid engine, this model is not for you: you will have to turn to the next generation (CN7, 2021+) or to the Hyundai Ioniq from the same era.

For those interested in the Elantra AD, the 1.4 T-GDI Eco version offers an excellent low-consumption alternative, although its DCT gearbox requires attention. Buying a used model requires extreme vigilance regarding the recall history, in particular to avoid 2017 models (early-life problems with the steering and brakes) and 2019-2020 models equipped with the 2.0L MPI engine (risk of engine failure linked to the piston rings). Favor the 1.6 MPI or 1.6 T-GDI versions, which are significantly less problematic.
