Summary

The Porsche Taycan (J1 generation) marks the spectacular entry of the Stuttgart firm into the 100% electric era. Based on the J1 platform (shared with the Audi e-tron GT) and equipped with an innovative 800-volt architecture, it offers ballistic performances, ultra-fast charging times, and dynamic handling worthy of the Porsche crest. Available as a sedan, Cross Turismo (adventurous estate), and Sport Turismo (shooting brake), it has redefined the standards of the electric sports car. However, this first foray into electric vehicles was accompanied by numerous teething problems: recurring software bugs, heating (PTC) failures, 12V battery issues, and several critical recall campaigns concerning the high-voltage battery. A major software update in 2022 corrected some of the electronic erratic behaviors, but the maintenance history remains crucial.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling, worthy of a 911
  • 800V architecture allowing very fast charging (5 to 80% in 22 min)
  • Top-flight interior finish quality and soundproofing
  • Spectacular lines and highly versatile Cross/Sport Turismo variants
  • Maintenance of performances even after several hard accelerations (thermal management)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very temperamental electronic and software reliability (especially 2020-2021 models)
  • Recurring cabin heating (PTC) failures
  • Numerous critical recalls (high-voltage battery, brakes, suspension)
  • Disappointing real-world range on early versions (before software update)
  • Limited rear roominess and trunk volume for its size (sedan)

Check before buying

  • Heating operation: set the temperature to maximum and check for hot air flow (very common PTC failure).
  • Recall history: imperatively check if the recalls on the high-voltage battery (fire risk) and brake hoses have been performed.
  • Infotainment system (PCM) stability: test the rearview cameras and ensure no screen remains black.
  • Condition of the front brake hoses (prone to cracking, ongoing recall).
  • Suspension noises: listen for clunking on rough roads (suspension arms or pneumatic shock absorbers).

Verdict

The Porsche Taycan (J1) is a feat of dynamic engineering, offering an unparalleled driving experience in the world of electric vehicles. However, its reliability record is heavily marred by teething problems typical of the first generations of EVs from traditional manufacturers. Heating (PTC) failures, immobilizing software bugs, and multiple critical recalls concerning the high-voltage battery and braking dictate great caution. **The purchase of a used Taycan (especially the 2020-2021 model years) should only be considered with a valid "Porsche Approved" warranty and a clear history proving that all recalls have been performed.** Without this coverage, the financial risk associated with out-of-warranty repairs is extremely high.
