MINI Mini

II (R56) · 2006 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 42/100 — faible

All MINI models

Summary

The second-generation MINI (code name R56) had the difficult task of succeeding the R50, which had brilliantly relaunched the brand under BMW's aegis. Aesthetically similar to its predecessor, the R56 is nevertheless a 100% new car. It stands out for the introduction of the "Prince" engine family, developed jointly by BMW and PSA Peugeot Citroën.

While the road handling remains exceptional (the famous "go-kart" effect), the improved finish quality, and undeniable charm, the reliability of the gasoline engines (especially before the 2010 facelift) was catastrophic. Problems with the timing chain, oil consumption, and high-pressure pump ruined the model's reputation. The late 2010 facelift (LCI) brought new engines (N16/N18) that corrected some of the flaws, without making the car irreproachable.

Expert's overall rating: A car you'll love to drive, but an extremely risky used car choice that requires a clear service history and in-depth knowledge of engine codes.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (ultra-direct steering, incisive chassis)
  • Timeless design and intact appeal
  • Interior presentation quality and ergonomics significantly improved compared to the R50
  • Efficiency and driving pleasure of gasoline engines (when they work)
  • Controlled fuel consumption on Cooper and diesel versions

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of pre-2010 gasoline engines (N12/N14)
  • Endemic oil consumption on gasoline engines
  • Very firm ride comfort, especially with 17-inch wheels and Runflat tires
  • Symbolic rear passenger space and tiny trunk (160 liters)
  • Maintenance and spare parts costs worthy of the premium segment (BMW)

Check before buying

  • Metallic noise on cold start (clanking/scraping): sign of a loose timing chain (urgent).
  • Oil level: many owners drive with too low a level, which destroys the timing chain and VANOS.
  • Service history: avoid any model without clear invoices. "Long Life" oil changes (30,000 km) are to be avoided.
  • Operation of electric windows and lighting: the FRM (Footwell Module) is very fragile and expensive to replace.
  • Soot traces around the injectors on 1.6 diesels (W16/DV6).

Official recalls

  • January 2012 (Auxiliary water pump): Risk of overheating of the auxiliary water pump's circuit board for the turbo (Cooper S/JCW), potentially leading to fire. Pump replacement.
  • May 2018 (FRM Module): On certain models, risk of short circuit in the footwell module (FRM) due to water or salt ingress.
  • Technical campaigns (Non-safety recalls): Multiple dealership actions have taken place to replace timing chain tensioners on N14 engines, often partially covered depending on age and mileage.

Verdict

Buying a MINI R56 is a perilous exercise. If you are looking for a gasoline engine, avoid at all costs models produced between 2006 and late 2010 (N12 engines and especially N14 on the Cooper S), unless the engine has been completely rebuilt (timing chain, HP pump, turbo) with supporting invoices. It is imperative to prioritize facelifted (LCI) versions from 2011 onwards equipped with N16 (Cooper) or N18 (Cooper S) engines. Even on these more reliable versions, rigorous monitoring (annual oil changes, monthly oil level checks) is mandatory. For diesels, both PSA and BMW blocks have their own costly flaws (turbo/injectors for one, chain for the other); only consider them for high-mileage drivers with a perfect service history.
