Volvo XC90

II (L) · 2014 – 2025

NÉGOCIER Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The Volvo XC90 Generation II (L) marked a renaissance for the Swedish brand, being the first vehicle built on the SPA (Scalable Product Architecture) platform. It introduced Volvo's controversial but forward-looking strategy to exclusively use 2.0-liter 4-cylinder engines (VEA - Volvo Engine Architecture) across all trims, relying on turbocharging, supercharging, and electrification to match the performance of rivals' 6-cylinder engines.

Positioned as a premium luxury mid-size/large SUV, it is renowned for its class-leading safety, minimalist Scandinavian interior, and excellent packaging (offering a genuine 7-seat option). A mild facelift was introduced in 2019 (Model Year 2020), bringing subtle styling tweaks, an upgraded kinetic energy recovery braking system (introducing the 'B' badged mild-hybrid engines), and improved reliability. In 2022, the Sensus infotainment system was replaced by a Google Android Automotive OS.

**Overall Note:** While early models (2015-2017) suffered from significant teething issues (software glitches, EGR problems on diesels), post-2018 models and especially the mild-hybrid 'B' variants are much more reliable. The diesel engines (D4, D5, B5) offer the best real-world fuel economy for heavy highway users, but require strict maintenance.

Strengths 6

  • Class-leading safety features and crash test ratings.
  • Stunning, minimalist Scandinavian interior design with high-quality materials.
  • Excellent packaging: a true 7-seater with usable third-row space.
  • Outstanding Bowers & Wilkins premium audio system (optional).
  • Very comfortable ride, especially with the optional air suspension.
  • Strong fuel economy on diesel variants for a vehicle of this size.

Weaknesses 5

  • 4-cylinder engines lack the refinement and sound character of rival 6-cylinder engines.
  • Early models (2015-2017) are plagued with electrical and software gremlins.
  • Sensus infotainment system can be laggy upon startup.
  • High repair costs for air suspension and PHEV (T8) components.
  • EGR and intake manifold issues on early diesel engines.

Check before buying

  • **PowerPulse Hose (D5):** Open the hood and check the thick black hose near the airbox. If it's detached or split, the car will lack low-end punch.
  • **EGR System (Diesels):** Verify service history for EGR cooler replacement. Look for signs of coolant loss or soot buildup.
  • **Water Ingress:** Check the front footwells (under the carpets) for dampness. Clogged sunroof drains can flood the main electrical modules.
  • **Air Suspension:** Cycle the suspension through its highest and lowest settings. Listen for excessive compressor noise or uneven lifting.
  • **ERAD Unit (T8):** Test drive in pure electric mode ('Pure'). Listen carefully for a high-pitched whining noise from the rear, indicating failing bearings.
  • **Door Locks:** Check that all doors lock and unlock properly. Actuator failures are common.

What sets it apart 14

Safety

  • Unrivaled Safety and Advanced Systems — The Volvo XC90 II is a benchmark in safety, achieving a maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with exceptional scores for adult (97%) and child (87%) protection. It integrates advanced safety systems as standard, such as City Safety and Pilot Assist, ensuring optimal protection for all occupants.
  • Precise Braking in All Conditions — Equipped with an excellent braking system, the XC90 II ensures short stopping distances and a reassuring feel. It typically brakes from 100 km/h to a standstill in just 36 to 38 meters, an impressive feat for an SUV of its size, offering enhanced safety.

Verdict

The Volvo XC90 II is a brilliant family SUV that prioritizes safety, comfort, and design over sporty driving dynamics. However, its complex 4-cylinder-only strategy and heavy reliance on early-generation tech made the 2015-2017 models quite problematic.

**If you are buying a Diesel:** Avoid the 2015-2017 D5 unless it has a flawless, fully documented Volvo service history proving the EGR recall and PowerPulse fixes were done. The sweet spot is a **2019 or newer B5 Mild Hybrid Diesel**, which ironed out the major flaws of the early VEA engines while providing excellent fuel economy for long-distance hauling.

Overall, prioritize post-facelift models (2019+) regardless of the powertrain, and consider an extended warranty if opting for the highly complex T8 PHEV or a model equipped with air suspension.
