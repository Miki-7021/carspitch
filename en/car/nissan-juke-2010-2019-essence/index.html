Nissan Juke

I (F15) · 2010 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

All Nissan models

Summary

The first-generation Nissan Juke (F15) is the undisputed pioneer of the urban SUV (B-SUV) segment. Launched in 2010, it shook up the market with its polarizing design, inspired by buggies and motorcycles, and its dynamic road handling.

Major evolutions: - 2010: Commercial launch. - 2013: Introduction of the Nismo sports version (200 hp). - 2014 (Facelift - Phase 2): Mid-cycle refresh. New bumpers, boomerang-shaped LED daytime running lights, integration of the 1.2 DIG-T engine (replacing the 1.6 naturally aspirated 117 hp on higher trims), and most importantly, an increase in trunk volume from 251 to 354 liters on 2-wheel drive versions (thanks to the removal of the spare wheel). Arrival of the Nismo RS version (214/218 hp).

Although its rear passenger space and trunk (especially in Phase 1 and on 4x4 versions) are limited, it remains a popular choice. In terms of reliability, the assessment is extremely contrasted: the Japanese naturally aspirated engines are indestructible, while the 1.2 DIG-T (Renault origin) and the CVT automatic gearbox (Jatco) are veritable minefields.

Strengths 5

  • Original and timeless design that has aged very well
  • Dynamic and agile road handling (especially with Torque Vectoring on 4WD versions)
  • Exceptional reliability of the 1.6 naturally aspirated engine (HR16DE)
  • Nismo and Nismo RS versions with a strong sporty character
  • Rich equipment on Tekna trims (360° cameras, heated leather seats)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.2 DIG-T engine (Renault origin)
  • Chronic fragility of the CVT automatic gearbox (Jatco CVT7)
  • Ridiculous trunk volume on Phase 1 (251L) and on all 4WD versions (207L)
  • Very cramped rear passenger space (legroom and headroom)
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on highways

Check before buying

  • CVT Gearbox: Test drive the car when cold and hot. Any jerking, excessive slipping, whistling, or limp mode indicates imminent failure (cost: 5000€+).
  • 1.2 DIG-T Engine: Demand full service history. Check the oil dipstick. Flee if the idle is unstable or if a rattling noise is heard on startup.
  • 1.6 DIG-T Engine (pre-2014): Check if the recall for the timing chain and fuel pressure sensor has been performed.
  • Running gear: Premature wear of front suspension wishbone bushings (clunking over speed bumps).
  • Air conditioning: Condenser highly exposed to gravel, frequent gas leaks.

Official recalls

- February 2012: Risk of fuel leak at the fuel rail pressure sensor (1.6 DIG-T Engines).,- May 2014: Replacement of the timing chain tensioner and chain on 1.6 DIG-T models produced between 2010 and 2013 (risk of engine failure).,- December 2014: Start/Stop button potentially sticking, leading to engine shutdown while driving.,- July 2017: Ignition switch defect (return spring) potentially cutting off the engine while driving.,- November 2018: Reprogramming or replacement of the lambda sensor on the 1.2 DIG-T (risk of short circuit).

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 2008 1st gen (more spacious, beware of the 1.2 PureTech), Renault Captur 1st gen (more practical, shares the same 1.2 TCe/DIG-T to avoid), Mini Countryman R60 (more premium, go-kart handling).
  • North America: Honda HR-V (more reliable, more robust CVT, Magic Seats), Mazda CX-3 (dynamic, very reliable, but also cramped in the back), Chevrolet Trax.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Vezel (HR-V), Toyota C-HR (similar polarizing design, very reliable hybrid).

Verdict

The first-generation Nissan Juke is an emotional purchase that requires a surgical selection of its mechanics.

The verdict is clear: - BUY with your eyes closed a 1.6 naturally aspirated (94 or 117 hp) manual gearbox version. It's a rock of reliability, ideal for city and suburban driving. - NEGOTIATE a 1.6 DIG-T (190 hp or Nismo) manual gearbox version, provided the service history is clear and the timing chain has been checked/replaced on pre-2014 models. - ABSOLUTELY AVOID the 1.2 DIG-T 115 hp engine (a financial black hole in perspective) and all versions equipped with the CVT automatic gearbox (Xtronic), whose lifespan is unpredictable and replacement cost prohibitive.
