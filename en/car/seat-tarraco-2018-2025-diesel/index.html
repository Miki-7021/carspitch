Summary

The Seat Tarraco, launched in 2018, is the largest SUV from the Spanish brand, positioned above the Ateca and Arona. Based on the Volkswagen Group's modular MQB-A platform, it shares many technical elements with its cousins, the Skoda Kodiaq and the Volkswagen Tiguan Allspace, of which it is the technical twin. Offered in 5 or 7-seater configurations, it targets the family SUV segment, offering a good compromise between space, practicality, and a more dynamic design than its VW/Skoda counterparts.

The Tarraco has not undergone a major facelift, but software updates and engine evolutions (notably the arrival of the plug-in hybrid) have marked its career. Its general reliability is in the good average for the Volkswagen Group, with strengths and specific points of vigilance for each engine, particularly the diesels and the plug-in hybrid.

Strengths 6

  • Spaciousness and modularity (5 and 7-seater versions)
  • Balanced and dynamic road handling for a family SUV
  • Build and finish quality in the segment average
  • Good price/performance ratio compared to its cousins VW Tiguan Allspace and Skoda Kodiaq
  • Sportier and more assertive exterior design
  • Wide choice of powerful and efficient engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Suspension comfort sometimes firm on large wheels
  • Infotainment system sometimes slow or prone to bugs on early versions
  • High fuel consumption for 4Drive gasoline versions in dynamic driving
  • Reliability of the AdBlue system on diesels (common problem in the VW group)
  • Access to the third row of seats a bit tight for adults

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for diesels and PHEV)
  • AdBlue system operation (no warning light, correct level)
  • DPF condition (no excessive smoke, no loss of power)
  • Absence of abnormal noises from the dual-mass flywheel (on DSG diesel)
  • Tire and brake condition (rapid wear on heavy/powerful versions)
  • Infotainment and driving assistance system operation
  • Battery and charging system condition on e-Hybrid versions
  • Check for manufacturer recalls performed (VIN history)

Verdict

The Seat Tarraco is an excellent choice for families looking for a spacious, well-equipped SUV with an assertive design, backed by the technical robustness of the Volkswagen Group.

  • For gasoline versions (1.5 TSI, 2.0 TSI): This is a recommended purchase, especially if the service history is clear. The 1.5 TSI is a good compromise for versatile use, while the 2.0 TSI offers very pleasant performance for long journeys or towing.
  • For diesel versions (2.0 TDI): NEGOTIATE is the keyword. These engines are fundamentally robust, but the AdBlue system and DPF are major points of vigilance. A purchase is relevant if and only if the usage is predominantly road/highway, the service history is impeccable (with proof of AdBlue/DPF maintenance), and the price reflects the potential risks. Avoid it for exclusive urban use. A thorough inspection of the exhaust system is crucial.
  • For plug-in hybrid versions (1.4 e-Hybrid): NEGOTIATE as well. The technology is more complex, and battery replacement costs can be very high out of warranty. It's a good choice if you can recharge regularly and your daily use matches the electric range, but be vigilant about battery condition and software updates.

In all cases, prioritize a model with a complete and up-to-date service history, ideally performed at a dealership. The Tarraco remains a solid proposition in its segment, offering good value for money compared to its more premium cousins.
