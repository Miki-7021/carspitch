Hyundai i30

III (PD) (2017–2025) · 2017–2025

BUY Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The third-generation Hyundai i30 (PD code) was launched in 2017, marking a significant evolution in terms of design, technology, and dynamism compared to its predecessors. Positioned in the highly competitive compact segment, it aims to rival established benchmarks such as the Volkswagen Golf, Ford Focus, or Peugeot 308.

Its more mature and European design was refined during a first **facelift in 2020** (introducing new MHEV engines, a revised front/rear design, and a modernized interior) and a **second facelift in 2024** (primarily aesthetic and technological). The range was enriched with sporty N-Line versions and the high-performance i30 N, praised by critics.

Overall, the i30 PD is a well-built car, offering good value for money, generous equipment, and an attractive manufacturer warranty. Its reliability is generally good, especially for naturally aspirated engines and well-maintained diesels. Direct injection turbo petrol versions (T-GDI) require particular attention to maintenance to prevent carbon buildup.

Strengths 7

  • Excellent value for money/equipment
  • Very advantageous 5-year (Europe) / 7-year (Australia) manufacturer warranty
  • Balanced and comfortable road handling (standard versions)
  • N-Line and N versions offering high-level dynamism and performance
  • Generous cabin space and boot volume for the category
  • Naturally aspirated engines (MPI/DPi) of great reliability
  • Perceived build quality on the rise

Weaknesses 6

  • T-GDI engine fuel consumption sensitive to driving style
  • Valve carbon buildup on direct injection engines (T-GDI)
  • 7-speed DCT gearbox sometimes hesitant or lacking fluidity on early model years
  • Noise insulation could be improved at high speeds on some versions
  • Functional interior design but less 'premium' than some competitors
  • Potentially lower resale value than German benchmarks

Transmission * **Manual (MT)**: Generally very reliable across all engines. The clutch is a consumable. On i30 N, the clutch may wear faster with intensive use. * **Automatic (7-speed DCT)**: Smooth and fast. Cases of jerky operation or slipping have been reported on early models, but software updates and improvements have made the gearbox more reliable. Check for regular oil changes if used intensively. On 1.6 T-GDI and 2.0 T-GDI, the DCT is more robust (wet clutch gearbox).

Check before buying

  • **Maintenance history:** Up-to-date logbook, invoices, regular oil changes (especially for T-GDI and DCT).
  • **T-GDI Engines:** Check for abnormal noises (turbo, timing chain), exhaust smoke, and spark plug condition. Ask if a decarb has been performed.
  • **DCT Gearbox:** Test the smoothness of gear changes when cold and hot, absence of slipping or jerking.
  • **DPF (Diesel):** Ensure the vehicle has made sufficiently long trips for regeneration. Check for absence of engine warning light.
  • **MHEV System (if equipped):** Check for absence of warning lights related to the 48V system.
  • **Braking:** Wear of discs and pads, especially on N-Line and N versions.
  • **Suspension:** Abnormal noises (clunking, squeaking) on degraded roads.
  • **Electronics/Infotainment:** Test all functions (touchscreen, GPS, Bluetooth, driving aids).
  • **Manufacturer Recalls:** Check with a Hyundai dealership if all recalls have been performed on the targeted vehicle.

Verdict

The third-generation Hyundai i30 is an excellent choice in the compact segment, offering a very comprehensive package and generally solid reliability.

**For urban and suburban use prioritizing reliability and reduced maintenance costs, the naturally aspirated 1.4 MPI, 1.5 DPi, and 2.0 MPI engines (depending on the markets) are the most recommended.** They are simple, robust, and less prone to carbon buildup issues common in direct injection engines.

**For versatile use with a good balance of performance/consumption, the 1.0 T-GDI and 1.4 T-GDI (pre-facelift) or 1.5 T-GDI (post-facelift) are good candidates.** However, rigorous maintenance (frequent oil changes, quality fuel, preventive decarb) is crucial to prevent valve carbon buildup. The DCT gearbox, although improved, deserves careful testing.

**High-mileage drivers will appreciate the 1.6 CRDi** for its fuel efficiency and torque, provided short trips that clog the DPF are avoided.

**The i30 N is a race beast** for enthusiasts, but it demands a higher maintenance and consumables budget, and increased vigilance on mechanical condition due to its potentially intensive use.

**In summary, the i30 PD is a wise purchase, especially if you opt for a well-maintained version and all manufacturer recalls have been performed.** Prioritize post-facelift models (2020+) for technological improvements and more efficient MHEV engines. The remaining manufacturer warranty is a major asset in the used car market.
