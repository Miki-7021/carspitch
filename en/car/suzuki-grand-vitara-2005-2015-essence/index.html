Suzuki Grand Vitara

II (JT) · 2005 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The **Suzuki Grand Vitara II (JT)** marks a major evolution compared to its predecessor. It abandons the separate ladder chassis for a monocoque structure integrating a pseudo-ladder frame (IBLF - Integrated Built-in Ladder Frame), offering better road handling while retaining true off-road capabilities. Unlike most compact SUVs of its era, it features permanent 4x4 transmission with a central differential lock and a low-range transfer case (reducer) on most 5-door versions.

**Positioning:** An off-roader disguised as a family SUV. **Evolutions:** - **2008 (Phase 2):** New petrol engines (2.4L and 3.2L V6), slight aesthetic revisions, improved sound insulation, rear disc brakes. - **2012 (Phase 3):** New grille, redesigned front bumper, removal of the spare wheel from the rear door on some urban trims.

**Expert's overall rating:** A robust and atypical vehicle, penalized by thirsty petrol engines and a temperamental Renault-sourced diesel, but which remains a master choice for those who truly need off-road capabilities.

Strengths 6

  • True off-road capabilities (low-range gearbox, central differential lock)
  • Robust chassis (monocoque + integrated ladder frame)
  • Exceptional reliability of 1.6 and 2.0 petrol engines
  • Safe road handling compared to the previous generation
  • Good towing capacity (up to 2000 kg depending on version)
  • Timeless design that ages well

Weaknesses 6

  • Very high fuel consumption for all petrol engines
  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.9 DDiS diesel engine (Renault-sourced)
  • Hard interior plastics prone to scratches, rattling noises
  • Very small boot on the 3-door version
  • Firm suspension comfort, especially at low speeds
  • Imperfect sound insulation on Phase 1 models (before 2008)

Check before buying

  • Transfer case operation: switch to 4H Lock and 4L Lock (when stationary, in neutral) to check for any locking issues or flashing warning lights.
  • Leaks at the front differential and transfer case (oil seals).
  • Coolant level on the 2.4L engine (risk of cracked block on 2008-2010 models).
  • Metallic noises on cold start on 2.0L, 2.7L, and 3.2L engines (sign of a stretched timing chain).
  • Service history of the 1.9 DDiS: invoices for DPF, EGR, Turbo, and timing belt.
  • Corrosion of the underbody and suspension wishbones if the vehicle has been off-road or comes from a mountainous region.

Official recalls

  • - **February 2011:** Recall concerning the accessory belt tensioner pulley (petrol engines). Risk of breakage leading to loss of power steering and water pump.
  • - **June 2006:** Recall on manual gearbox models (2005-2006) for a defect in the gear linkage that could prevent gear changes.
  • - **October 2013:** Recall (primarily in North America) concerning the passenger seat occupant classification sensor (airbag).

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Toyota RAV4 III (more road-oriented, less capable off-road), Nissan X-Trail II (good space/off-road compromise), Subaru Forester III (excellent AWD, Boxer engines). - **North America:** Honda CR-V, Ford Escape, Jeep Liberty (true off-road competitor). - **Emerging Markets / Asia:** Mitsubishi Outlander, Chevrolet Captiva.

Verdict

The Suzuki Grand Vitara II is a paradoxical niche vehicle: it has the appearance of an urban SUV but the soul of a true 4x4. **In petrol**, it is an extremely relevant choice for those who live in the mountains, tow, or drive on unpaved roads, provided they accept a fuel consumption of 10 to 12L/100km. The **2.0L (J20A)** engine is the safest and most balanced choice. The **2.4L** is more pleasant but requires absolute vigilance regarding the production year (avoid 2008-2010 models that haven't had the block replaced). The **1.6L** is too underpowered, except in the 3-door version for local use. As for the **1.9 DDiS** diesel, it is to be avoided unless its history is clear and your use is exclusively on paved roads.
