Summary

The **first-generation Kia Sorento (Type BL)** marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer, establishing it in the global SUV market. Unlike modern unibody SUVs, the Sorento I is built on a robust **ladder frame (body-on-frame)** with a rigid rear axle, making it a true off-roader disguised as a family vehicle. It offers rear-wheel drive with an engageable front axle, or an automatic all-wheel-drive system (TOD - Torque On Demand) with a low-range transfer case. A significant facelift occurred in late 2006, bringing aesthetic improvements, revised suspension, and, most importantly, new gasoline engines (Lambda family) replacing the older units. Although heavy and ponderous on the road, it excels in its towing and off-road capabilities.

Strengths 6

  • True off-road capabilities (ladder frame, low-range gearbox)
  • Excellent towing capacity (up to 2800 kg depending on version)
  • Overall reliability of Lambda V6 engines (3.3 and 3.8)
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume
  • Very rich standard equipment for its time (EX/Major trims)
  • Very attractive used price today

Weaknesses 6

  • Excessive fuel consumption on all gasoline V6s (13 to 18 L/100km)
  • Ponderous road handling, significant body roll
  • Very firm and bouncy suspension (especially before the 2006 facelift)
  • Critical design flaw in the 3.5 V6 intake manifold
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (chassis and wheel arches)
  • Poor quality interior plastics that age badly

Check before buying

  • Thorough inspection of the ladder frame for penetrating rust (especially on mountain/coastal vehicles).
  • 4x4 transmission operation: switch to 'Low' mode (low range) and check for absence of jerks or metallic noises.
  • Vibrations at steady speed (80-100 km/h): sign of an unbalanced driveshaft or worn U-joints.
  • On the 3.5 V6: demand proof of timing belt replacement and ask if the intake manifold has been secured.
  • Oil leaks from valve cover gaskets (common on V6s, oil can drip onto the alternator).

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Nissan Pathfinder (more spacious, 7 seats), Mitsubishi Pajero (excellent off-roader), Hyundai Santa Fe (technical cousin but unibody chassis, more road-oriented). - **North America**: Toyota 4Runner (absolute benchmark in reliability but much more expensive), Jeep Grand Cherokee (better on road, V8s available), Nissan Xterra. - **Asia / Emerging Markets**: Toyota Fortuner, Ford Everest, Isuzu MU-7.

Verdict

The Kia Sorento I (BL) is a polarizing choice. If you're looking for a dynamic city SUV, look elsewhere. However, if you need a robust tow vehicle or a true 4x4 at a low price, it's formidable. **Regarding gasoline engines, avoid the 3.5 V6 (2002-2006)** unless the intake manifold swirl flap issue has been formally corrected by a specialist. **Absolutely prioritize facelifted models (2007-2009) equipped with the 3.3 V6 or 3.8 V6**. These Lambda family engines are infinitely more reliable, feature a timing chain, and offer superior driving pleasure, although their fuel consumption remains tank-like. A rigorous inspection of the chassis (rust) and transfer case is mandatory before purchase.
