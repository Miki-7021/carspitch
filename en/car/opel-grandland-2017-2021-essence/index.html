Summary

The Grandland X is Opel's compact SUV, launched in 2017 and marking an important step in Opel's integration into the PSA Group (now Stellantis). Based on the EMP2 platform, common to models like the Peugeot 3008 and Citroën C5 Aircross, it shares many technical elements with its French cousins. The "X" in its name designated Opel's SUV range at the time, before being removed during the 2021 facelift to become simply "Grandland".

Positioned in the highly competitive compact SUV segment, the Grandland X stands out with a more sober and classic design than its PSA counterparts, appreciable suspension comfort, and decent interior space. It was designed to offer a more pragmatic and less exuberant alternative, while benefiting from the group's proven powertrains and technologies. Its reliability is generally average for the segment, with specific points of attention related to shared powertrains.

Check before buying

  • **1.2 PureTech Engine**: Complete service history, timing belt replacement invoices, visual inspection of the belt (by a professional), presence of sludge in the oil.
  • **Diesel Engines (BlueHDi)**: AdBlue system operation (warning lights, error messages), history of AdBlue tank/sensor replacements, absence of leaks.
  • **General**: Functionality of all electronic equipment (multimedia, driving aids), condition of tires and brakes, body panel alignment (sign of impact).
  • **PHEV**: Battery capacity, charging time, absence of error messages related to the hybrid system, software update history.

Verdict

The Opel Grandland X is a compact SUV that offers a good compromise between comfort, interior space, and road handling. Its sober design and positioning make it attractive to those looking for an alternative to more exuberant French SUVs, while benefiting from the same proven technical basis (EMP2 platform).

**For petrol engines:** * The **1.2 PureTech 130 hp** is to be **NEGOTIATED** strongly and bought with extreme caution. It is imperative to check the service history, proof of preventive timing belt replacement, and absence of oil-related problems. Rigorous maintenance is non-negotiable. If these conditions are not met, it is best to **AVOID**. * The **1.6 PureTech 180 hp** is a more reliable and enjoyable option to drive. It is a **BUY** if maintenance has been followed (regular oil changes, quality fuel) and if monitoring points (oil consumption, fouling) are under control.

**For diesel engines:** * The **BlueHDi (1.5, 1.6, 2.0)** are generally mechanically reliable, but the AdBlue system is a recurring weak point. They are to be **NEGOTIATED** by checking the history of AdBlue failures and ensuring that recalls or fixes have been applied. The 2.0 BlueHDi is the most robust for high-mileage drivers.

**For plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions:** * They are to be **NEGOTIATED** due to their complexity and potential electronic bugs. They are suitable for profiles with easy access to charging and primarily making short trips to maximize fuel economy.

In summary, the Grandland X is a good choice if you opt for the most reliable powertrains (1.6 PureTech 180 or 2.0 BlueHDi) and if you are vigilant about the maintenance of other powertrains. It offers excellent value for money on the used market.
