Summary

Tesla Model 3 Generation II (Project Highland)

Introduced in late 2023, the 'Highland' update represents a massive overhaul of the Tesla Model 3. While technically a heavy facelift rather than a ground-up new platform, the changes are so extensive that it is widely considered the second generation. Tesla focused heavily on addressing the main criticisms of the previous model: ride harshness and cabin noise.

The Highland introduces frequency-selective dampers, revised suspension geometry, 360-degree acoustic glass, and improved aerodynamics (Cd reduced to 0.219), resulting in a significantly quieter and more comfortable ride. Inside, the cabin features upgraded materials, ventilated front seats, an 8-inch rear entertainment screen, and customizable ambient lighting.

Controversially, Tesla removed all steering column stalks, moving turn signals and wiper controls to steering wheel buttons, and gear selection to the touchscreen. It also relies entirely on 'Tesla Vision' (cameras) for parking assistance, having removed ultrasonic sensors. Build quality, particularly from the Shanghai Gigafactory (which supplies Europe and Asia), is exceptionally high, showing a maturity in Tesla's manufacturing processes.

Strengths 6

  • Class-leading energy efficiency and real-world range
  • Massive improvement in ride comfort and cabin quietness (NVH) compared to the previous generation
  • Seamless integration with the Tesla Supercharger network
  • High-quality infotainment system with snappy response and excellent OTA update support
  • Standard LFP battery on the RWD model is incredibly durable
  • Excellent build quality, especially from Giga Shanghai (panel gaps are largely a thing of the past)

Weaknesses 5

  • Removal of indicator and gear stalks is highly polarizing and ergonomically flawed on roundabouts
  • Tesla Vision (camera-only) parking assist is inferior to traditional ultrasonic sensors (USS)
  • Phantom braking on Autopilot/Traffic-Aware Cruise Control still occasionally occurs
  • Wipers on 'Auto' mode can be erratic (relying on cameras rather than a dedicated rain sensor)
  • No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto integration

Check before buying

  • Ambient lighting strip: Check for uniform brightness across the dashboard and doors.
  • Wheel alignment: Some vehicles leave the factory with slight alignment issues (steering wheel not perfectly centered).
  • Tesla Vision calibration: Ensure cameras are clean and free of condensation inside the housing.
  • Tire wear: Especially on AWD and Performance models, check for uneven inner tread wear.
  • Panel gaps: While vastly improved, Fremont-built cars (North America) should still be inspected upon delivery.

Verdict

The Tesla Model 3 'Highland' is a masterclass in iterative improvement. By addressing the harsh ride and noisy cabin of the first generation, Tesla has transformed the Model 3 into an exceptionally refined daily driver. The electric powertrains remain the benchmark for efficiency, and the Supercharger network provides unmatched convenience. The build quality, particularly from the Shanghai plant, is now excellent.

However, the user interface choices—specifically the removal of indicator stalks and ultrasonic parking sensors—require a significant adjustment period and remain the car's biggest flaws. If you can adapt to the steering wheel buttons and the camera-only parking system, the Highland Model 3 is arguably the best all-around EV on the market in its price bracket. The **Standard Range RWD** is the smartest financial choice due to its indestructible LFP battery, while the **Long Range AWD** is perfect for those needing maximum all-weather capability.
