MINI Clubman

I (R55) · 2007 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 55/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation MINI Clubman (code name R55) is the estate (or 'Shooting Brake') variant of the MINI Hatch R56. It stands out with its extended wheelbase (+8 cm), its split rear doors ('Barn doors'), and its famous asymmetrical 'Clubdoor' (a half-door hinged at the rear, located only on the right side to facilitate access to the rear seats).

Positioned as a chic and practical alternative to premium city cars, it retains the brand's typical 'go-kart' handling. However, its reputation has been severely tarnished by the disastrous reliability of its 'Prince' petrol engines (co-developed by BMW and PSA) before the late 2010 facelift (LCI). Buying a Clubman R55 requires in-depth knowledge of engine codes (N12/N14 vs N16/N18) to avoid financial pitfalls.

Strengths 5

  • Unique design and undeniable charm ('Barn doors' rear doors)
  • Exceptional road handling (direct steering, sharp chassis)
  • Rear legroom and boot volume (260L to 930L) superior to the Hatch
  • Improved interior finish quality after the 2010 facelift
  • Wide choice of customization (colors, wheels, inserts)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of petrol engines before 2010 (N12/N14)
  • The 'Clubdoor' is on the right: perfect in Europe (pavement side), dangerous in the UK (road side)
  • Rear visibility obstructed by the central pillar of the split doors
  • Very firm suspension, especially with 17-inch wheels and Runflat tires
  • Maintenance and spare parts costs worthy of the premium segment (BMW)

Check before buying

  • Metallic rattling noise when cold on the passenger side (sign of a loose timing chain - urgent)
  • Clear oil change history (crucial for all engines, petrol and diesel)
  • Traces of coolant leak under the plastic water housing (thermostat)
  • Smooth operation of the rear doors (check the struts and locks of the 'Barn doors')
  • Condition of the FRM (Footwell Module): check that all lights and electric windows work correctly
  • Oil consumption: ask the seller about the frequency of top-ups (max tolerance 0.5L/1000km, beyond that is suspicious)

Official recalls

  • - **January 2012 (Auxiliary water pump)**: On Cooper S and JCW (2007-2011), risk of overheating of the turbo water pump's circuit board potentially leading to fire.
  • - **May 2018 (FRM Module)**: Warranty extension/recall in some countries for the footwell module (FRM3) which controls lighting, prone to failures during voltage drops.
  • - **Various (Chain Tensioner)**: No global safety recall, but multiple 'Service Bulletins' (PUMA) for the replacement (partially or totally depending on age) of the chain tensioner on N14 engines.

Verdict

The MINI Clubman R55 is a beloved vehicle, but it can quickly turn into a financial nightmare if the wrong version is chosen. **The golden rule is to absolutely avoid petrol engines from before late 2010 (N12 and especially N14 engines of the Cooper S/JCW)**, unless the timing chain, HP pump, and turbo have been recently replaced with supporting invoices. It is imperative to prioritize facelifted (LCI) models from 2011 onwards equipped with N16 (Cooper) or N18 (Cooper S) engines. For diesel, the blocks are more durable but require meticulous maintenance (turbo on the 1.6 PSA, chain on the N47 BMW). Buy the service history before buying the car.
