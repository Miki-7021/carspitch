Mitsubishi Outlander

II (CW0) (2006–2012) · 2006 – 2012

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The second-generation Mitsubishi Outlander (chassis code CW0) was launched in 2006 and marked a turning point for the Japanese manufacturer. Based on the GS platform shared with other group models (Lancer, ASX, as well as the Peugeot 4007 and Citroën C-Crosser clones), it positions itself as a compact family-oriented SUV, often available in 5 or 7-seat configurations. Its design, initially more understated, was refreshed in 2010 with a notable facelift ('Jet Fighter' grille inspired by the Lancer Evolution) and interior improvements.

The Outlander II offers sound road handling, efficient all-wheel drive, and appreciable interior space. However, its reliability is highly dependent on the chosen engine, with major differences between gasoline engines, generally very robust, and certain diesels, which have experienced recurrent and costly problems. It is a vehicle that can be an excellent choice if one selects the right engine and an impeccable service history.

Strengths 6

  • Generous interior space (7-seat option)
  • Balanced and safe road handling
  • Efficient and versatile all-wheel drive
  • Gasoline engines (MIVEC) with exemplary reliability
  • Often generous equipment for the segment
  • Good towing capacity (V6)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of the 2.0 DI-D (VW) engine
  • Interior finish a bit dated before the 2010 facelift
  • High fuel consumption of V6 gasoline engines
  • Engine noise of diesels (especially 2.0 DI-D) and 2.4 MIVEC at high RPMs
  • Maintenance cost of diesels (DPF, dual-mass flywheel, injectors)
  • Firm suspension on some versions

Check before buying

  • Complete and detailed service history (with invoices)
  • Condition and operation of the DPF (for diesels): regenerations, warning lights, smoke
  • Abnormal engine noises (ticking, knocking, turbo whistling)
  • Exhaust smoke (blue, white, black)
  • Operation of air conditioning and multimedia system
  • Tire and brake wear (heavy vehicle)
  • Operation of the all-wheel drive (2WD/4WD switching, locking)
  • Presence of oil or coolant leaks (especially on 2.0 DI-D)
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel (noises on start/stop, vibrations)

Verdict

The Mitsubishi Outlander II is an SUV that offers good performance in terms of interior space and road capabilities, particularly thanks to its efficient all-wheel drive. However, its purchase is highly conditional on the engine.

**Our advice is to strongly favor the MIVEC gasoline versions (2.0, 2.4 or 3.0 V6)**, which offer remarkable reliability and unparalleled peace of mind. The V6 fuel consumption should be considered, but robustness largely compensates.

**The Volkswagen-sourced 2.0 DI-D is to be ABSOLUTELY AVOIDED.** Its recurrent and costly problems make it too risky a bet, even at a low price.

The 2.2 DI-D diesels (PSA or Mitsubishi 4N14) are more acceptable choices for high-mileage drivers, provided they have an impeccable service history and are vigilant regarding known weaknesses (DPF, dual-mass flywheel). The 2.2 DI-D 4N14 is the most recommendable of the diesels.

If you find a well-maintained gasoline model, the Outlander II can be an excellent family companion, reliable and versatile, offering good value for money on the used market.
