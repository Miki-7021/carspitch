Opel Corsa

D · 2006 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

All Opel models

Summary

The Opel Corsa D (fourth generation) marks a major turning point for the 'blitz' city car. Developed on the SCCS (Small Common Components and Systems) platform in partnership with Fiat (shared with the Grande Punto), it grew considerably to almost 4 meters. It stands out for its safe road handling, increased interior space, and perceived finish quality superior to the C generation.

The model underwent two major evolutions: a technical update in 2010 (optimization of chassis connections, recalibrated steering, and engines brought up to Euro 5 standards) and an aesthetic facelift in 2011 (new 'Eagle Eyes' front end). Although the demand here is for diesel (very popular at the time), the Corsa D is a heavy car for its category, which penalizes smaller engines. The diesel units, from Fiat (1.3 CDTI) and Isuzu (1.7 CDTI), offer good fuel efficiency but suffer from chronic weaknesses related to emission control systems and transmission on certain versions.

Strengths 5

  • Healthy and safe road handling (chassis shared with the Grande Punto)
  • Generous interior space for the category (especially in 5-door version)
  • Design that has aged well, particularly after the 2011 facelift
  • Very affordable used purchase cost
  • Naturally aspirated petrol engines (1.2 and 1.4) generally very reliable

Weaknesses 5

  • High weight penalizing performance and fuel consumption of small engines
  • Disastrous reliability of the 6-speed manual gearbox (M32) on powerful diesels and 1.6 Turbo
  • Fragility of the timing chain on the 1.3 CDTI (linked to DPF cycles)
  • Uneven interior finish (hard plastics that scratch easily)
  • Poor rear visibility (especially in 3-door version)

Check before buying

  • **M32 Gearbox (6-speed manual)**: On 1.3 CDTI 90, 1.7 CDTI and 1.6T. Test 5th and 6th gears on the highway. A whistling sound or a lever that moves when lifting off the accelerator = worn bearings (costly repair).
  • **Metallic noise on startup (1.3 CDTI)**: Indicates a stretched timing chain. Imminent risk of engine failure.
  • **Oil level (1.3 CDTI)**: If the level is above maximum, the oil is diluted by diesel fuel (DPF regeneration failures). Danger for the engine.
  • **Cabin ventilation** : Test all speeds. The blower motor resistor frequently burns out (speeds 1, 2 and 3 inoperative).
  • **Steering rack** : Audible clunking during low-speed maneuvers (steering column or universal joint to be replaced).
  • **Water ingress** : Check for dampness under the front carpets (BCM housing sealing issue or clogged windshield scuttle drains).

Official recalls

  • - **2007 (Suspension)** : Risk of front lower suspension arm fracture (2006-2007 models).
  • - **2010 (Parking Brake)** : Handbrake cable may detach or loosen.
  • - **2011 (Steering)** : Defect in the steering column intermediate shaft potentially leading to loss of control.
  • - **2016 (Bonnet)** : The bonnet safety catch may corrode and no longer properly lock the bonnet (risk of opening while driving).
  • - **2016 (Electrical)** : Risk of short circuit in the Body Control Module (BCM) due to water ingress.

Verdict

The Opel Corsa D is a city car with an attractive price/performance ratio on the used market, but **diesel versions require extreme vigilance**. The 1.3 CDTI, although fuel-efficient, falls victim to its emission control system (DPF) which degrades the oil and, by extension, the timing chain if used in the city. Furthermore, the notoriously unreliable M32 gearbox (on diesels over 75 hp) is a financial sword of Damocles.

**Buying advice**: If you absolutely need a diesel for long journeys, opt for a 1.3 CDTI 75 hp (reliable 5-speed manual gearbox) with an impeccable service history (strict annual oil changes), or a 1.7 CDTI whose M32 gearbox has already been rebuilt. However, for 80% of buyers, **the 1.2 16V or 1.4 16V petrol versions are infinitely more recommendable** and will save you exorbitant repair bills.
