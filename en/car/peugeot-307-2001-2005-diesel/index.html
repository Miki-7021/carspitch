Peugeot 307

I (3A/C) · 2001 – 2005

All Peugeot models

Summary

The first-generation Peugeot 307 (Phase 1, internal code 3A/C for the sedan) marked a radical break from the 306. Voted Car of the Year 2002, it adopted a semi-high architecture favoring interior space and safety. Dynamically, it offered an exceptional comfort/road holding compromise, typical of Peugeot's expertise. However, this generation introduced a new multiplexed electronic architecture (VAN/CAN network) which proved to be a real industrial disaster during the first years of production. Electronic failures (BSI, COM2000) and problems related to new diesel emission control technologies (FAP) and transmission (dual-mass flywheel) heavily tarnished its reputation. This is a model to approach with extreme caution on the used market.

Strengths 5

  • Superb road handling (precise and safe chassis)
  • Generous interior space and boot volume for its time
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (on high-end versions)
  • Mechanical robustness of the 2.0 HDi 90 block and petrol engines
  • Passive safety (solid structure, 4 Euro NCAP stars)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic multiplexed electronics (2001-2003): BSI, COM2000, various bugs
  • Disastrous reliability of the dual-mass flywheel (2.0 HDi 110 and 1.6 HDi 110)
  • Recurrent turbo failures on the 1.6 HDi 110
  • Complex and costly FAP management (cerine, differential pressure sensors)
  • Premature wear of interior plastics and stalks
  • High weight penalizing small engines (1.4i, 1.4 HDi)

Check before buying

  • Operation of ALL electrical equipment (indicators, wipers, windows, AC) to test the COM2000 and BSI.
  • Absence of vibrations or knocking at idle and when the clutch slips (symptom of a failed dual-mass flywheel on HDi 110).
  • Presence of black tar or exhaust gas smell around the injectors (seal leak on 1.4 HDi and 1.6 HDi).
  • Excessive turbo whistling on acceleration (especially on 1.6 HDi 110).
  • Dashboard error messages: 'Anti-pollution fault' (FAP, EGR valve, mass air flow sensor).
  • Condition of the Damper pulley (metallic knocking on the accessory belt side on 2.0 HDi).

Official recalls

- 2002: Risk of short-circuit on the starter wiring harness (2001 models).,- 2003: Replacement of the ignition switch (risk of jamming or non-starting).,- 2004: BSI unit update to correct unexpected headlight or dashboard shutdowns.,- 2005: Inspection and replacement of the brake assist vacuum pump on 2.0 HDi engines.,- 2005: ABS/ESP unit sealing issue potentially leading to a short circuit (massive recall).,- 2007: Recall on 1.6 HDi for a vacuum pump valve defect (loss of braking assistance).

Verdict

The Peugeot 307 Phase 1 is a car full of dynamic and family qualities, but it bore the brunt of multiplexing and the first generations of FAP at PSA. As an expert, I strongly advise against buying models from 2001 to 2003, which are true nests of electronic failures. If you are looking for an economical diesel, the 2.0 HDi 90 is the only truly safe choice, as it lacks a FAP and a dual-mass flywheel. The 1.6 HDi 110 is to be avoided unless the turbo, injector seals, and flywheel have been recently replaced with supporting invoices. Always prefer a late Phase 1 model (2004-2005) or opt for a significantly more reliable Phase 2 (after mid-2005).
