I (GG/GY) (2002–2007) · 2002–2007

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Mazda 6 (chassis codes GG for the sedan/hatchback and GY for the wagon) was launched in 2002, marking a turning point for the brand with its 'Zoom-Zoom' slogan. It positioned itself in the family car segment, offering a more dynamic and stylish alternative to established competitors. Available as a 4-door sedan, 5-door hatchback, and wagon, it appealed with its agile road handling, precise steering, and sporty design.

A **facelift** was carried out in **2005**, bringing minor aesthetic changes (bumpers, headlights, taillights), interior improvements (materials, instrumentation), and technical evolutions, particularly for the diesel engines. The Mazda 6 I is generally appreciated for its reliability in gasoline versions and its driving pleasure, although the diesel versions faced specific challenges.

Strengths 6

  • Dynamic and agile road handling
  • Still current and sporty exterior design
  • Exemplary reliability of MZR gasoline engines
  • Good level of equipment for its time
  • Decent interior space and trunk volume (especially in wagon)
  • Precise and pleasant steering

Weaknesses 5

  • Poor reliability of 2.0 MZR-CD diesel engines (injectors, turbo, DPF, flywheel)
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (wheel arches, sills, rear subframe)
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speed
  • Quality of some interior plastics (pre-facelift)
  • Slightly large turning radius

Check before buying

  • **Corrosion:** Meticulously inspect wheel arches, sills, rear subframe, and suspension mounting points.
  • **Diesel Engine:** Check for injector leaks (diesel smell, seepage), proper turbo functioning (no excessive whistling, no blue/black smoke), the condition of the flywheel (noises on start/stop, vibrations), and the DPF service history (for RF7J).
  • **Gasoline Engine:** Listen for any abnormal noises (timing chain, ignition coils). Check oil level.
  • **Gearbox:** Smooth gear changes, absence of abnormal noises.
  • **Running Gear:** Tire wear, play in ball joints, shock absorbers.
  • **Electronics:** Functioning of all equipment (air conditioning, power windows, radio, cruise control).
  • **Service History:** Up-to-date logbook, invoices, especially for diesels.

Verdict

The first-generation Mazda 6 is a car that has aged well, offering an excellent compromise between dynamism and practicality. **My verdict is clear: prioritize MZR gasoline engines.** They are remarkably reliable, with a timing chain and few costly breakdowns. The 2.0 MZR is the most balanced choice for versatile use.

**Regarding the 2.0 MZR-CD diesels (RF5C and RF7J), caution is advised.** Recurring problems with injectors, turbo, flywheel, and DPF (on the 143hp) can lead to expensive bills. If you are considering a diesel, ensure you have an impeccable service history, invoices proving the replacement of weak parts, and budget for potential repairs. A thorough test drive and inspection by a professional are essential.

**Regardless of the engine, corrosion is the weak point of this model.** A meticulous inspection of the underbody and wheel arches is crucial. If the bodywork is sound and the engine is gasoline, the Mazda 6 I remains an excellent choice for its driving pleasure and value for money on the used market.
