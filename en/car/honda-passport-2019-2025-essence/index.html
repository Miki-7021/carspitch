Summary

The second-generation Honda Passport (YG1) marks the return of this historic nameplate in the form of a two-row midsize SUV. Slotted between the CR-V and the Pilot (with which it shares the global *Light Truck* platform and wheelbase, but with a shortened rear overhang), it is positioned as a more adventurous and rugged offering. Contrary to market trends (downsizing and turbochargers), the Passport remains faithful to a noble naturally aspirated 3.5L V6 coupled with an excellent i-VTM4 torque-vectoring AWD system. Praised by *Consumer Reports* and *J.D. Power* for its above-average reliability, it stands out as a pragmatic, durable, and capable choice, although it is penalized by older-generation fuel consumption.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional mechanical reliability of the naturally aspirated V6 (J35Y6)
  • Highly capable i-VTM4 AWD system (mechanical torque vectoring)
  • Gigantic interior space and clever storage
  • Robust and well-calibrated 9-speed automatic gearbox
  • Excellent resale value (above segment average)

Weaknesses 4

  • High fuel consumption (older design V6)
  • Aging infotainment system sometimes prone to bugs (MOST cable)
  • Maintenance cost related to the timing belt (unlike chain-driven competitors)
  • Quality of some interior plastics below category standards

Check before buying

  • Rear differential fluid change history (i-VTM4 AWD): crucial for system longevity.
  • Replacement of the timing belt and water pump if the vehicle is approaching 160,000 km.
  • Operation of the center screen and backup camera (known MOST connection issues).
  • Condition of the underbody skid plate fasteners if the vehicle has been driven off-road.

Official recalls

Although the mechanics are extremely reliable, the model has undergone several recalls (NHTSA) regarding peripherals and assemblies:

  • Fuel pump (Dec 2023 / Mar 2021): Possible failure of the low-pressure fuel pump impeller (Denso), which could lead to engine stalling.
  • Airbag sensors (Feb 2024 / May 2026*): Defect in the occupant classification sensor (front passenger seat) that could prevent airbag deployment.
  • Steering (Mar 2024): Risk of loosening of the steering rack inner tie rod.
  • Fuel filler neck (Nov 2024): Risk of separation of the filler tube, leading to a leak.
  • Backup camera / MOST cable (Jun 2023 / Jul 2020): Connection defect of the MOST coaxial cable or software bug resulting in a black screen.
  • Brake master cylinder (Jun 2023): Defective fastening of the brake booster tie rod.
  • Exterior mirrors (Mar 2023): Poor adhesion of the heating pads, risk of the mirror glass detaching.
  • Hood latch (Nov 2021): Possible damage to the striker, risking opening while driving.
  • Structure / Welds (Oct 2019): Incomplete welds on the upper front frame rails (2019 models).

*(Note: Future dates like 2026 correspond to extended NHTSA notification campaigns).*

*(Note 2: Rappel Conso recalls concerning Honda motorcycles have been excluded for relevance).*

What sets it apart 10

Reliability

  • Proven V6 Engine Reliability — The naturally aspirated 3.5L V6 has fewer wear parts than turbo engines, giving buyers long-term peace of mind and lower risk of costly breakdowns.
  • Independently Rated High Reliability — Consumer Reports and J.D. Power rate its reliability above average, backed by ADAC and TÜV data, giving buyers documented confidence in long-term durability.

Handling

  • Advanced Torque-Vectoring AWD — The i-VTM4 system distributes torque between rear wheels for better grip and control on slippery or uneven surfaces, improving confidence in poor conditions.

Practicality

  • Generous Interior Space — The two-row layout offers vast passenger room and clever storage solutions, making it practical for families and easy to load for daily errands or trips.
  • Practical Two-Row Configuration — Sharing the Pilot's wheelbase but with two rows, it offers abundant cargo and passenger space while remaining easier to maneuver than three-row alternatives.

Comfort

  • Robust Nine-Speed Automatic — The well-calibrated 9-speed gearbox delivers smooth, dependable shifts, reducing driver fatigue and supporting the engine's linear power delivery on long journeys.

Economy

  • Strong Resale Value — Above-average resale value means buyers lose less money when selling, protecting their investment compared to many rivals in the midsize SUV segment.

Performance

  • Confident Towing Capability — With up to 2,268 kg towing capacity in AWD form, the Passport handles trailers and recreational gear without straining, suiting buyers with active outdoor lifestyles.
  • Smooth Linear Power Delivery — The V6's onctuous, linear response and pleasant sound make acceleration predictable and relaxed, reducing stress during merging and highway driving.

Design

  • Adventurous, Robust Positioning — A shorter rear overhang and rugged stance distinguish it from the CR-V and Pilot, giving buyers a more capable-looking SUV without sacrificing platform familiarity.

Verdict

The Honda Passport (YG1) is a true modern dinosaur, and that is exactly what makes its strength. In a segment flooded with complex turbocharged 4-cylinder engines, it offers the peace of mind of a proven naturally aspirated V6 and a classic automatic transmission. Data from ADAC, TÜV Report, and J.D. Power confirm its excellent long-term reliability. If one accepts higher fuel consumption and a somewhat dated cabin, it is one of the most durable and capable midsize SUVs on the market. Highly recommended buy, favoring models from 2021 onwards to avoid the minor teething problems (welds, software bugs) of the first production year.
