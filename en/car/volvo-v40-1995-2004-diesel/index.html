Summary

The first-generation Volvo V40 (and its S40 sedan variant) marked a turning point for the Swedish manufacturer. Developed in partnership with Mitsubishi (sharing its platform with the Carisma) and assembled at the NedCar factory in the Netherlands, it abandoned Volvo's traditional cubic lines for a more fluid design.

Major Evolution: The model underwent a crucial facelift in 2000 (Phase 2). This update corrected many early flaws: wider tracks, completely redesigned suspension for better road handling, improved sound insulation, and the introduction of more modern powertrains.

Diesel Focus: As Volvo did not have its own 4-cylinder diesel engine at the time, the brand turned to Renault. The V40 thus housed three generations of the French 1.9-liter block (F8Q and F9Q): from rustic indirect injection to modern Common Rail. While these engines offer good fuel efficiency, their reliability requires rigorous attention, particularly on dCi versions.

Weaknesses 6

  • Random reliability of Renault-sourced diesel engines (turbos, EGR, flywheel)
  • Problematic 1.8i GDI engine (Mitsubishi) (chronic fouling)
  • Clumsy road handling and harsh suspension on Phase 1 (1995-1999)
  • Uneven interior finish quality (rattles, plastics that age poorly)
  • Limited rear passenger space and trunk volume for a wagon
  • Large turning radius

Check before buying

  • Service history of the turbo and EGR valve on 102/115 hp diesels.
  • Metallic noises at idle or vibrations in the clutch pedal (sign of a faulty dual-mass flywheel on diesels).
  • Traces of oil leaks around the cylinder head gasket on the 1.9 TD 90 hp.
  • Condition of front wishbone bushings and anti-roll bar links (clunking on degraded roads).
  • Operation of on-board electronics: central locking, power windows, air conditioning.
  • Corrosion on rear wheel arches and sills (especially on models before 2000).

Verdict

The first-generation Volvo V40 is an elegant and safe compact wagon, but buying it used today requires caution, especially with a diesel engine. If you are looking for a diesel: avoid the 90hp and 95hp versions, which are too old and rough. Target a Phase 2 model (2000-2004) equipped with the 1.9 D of 102 or 115 hp, but demand a clear service history (turbo, EGR, timing belt, clutch/flywheel replaced).

However, currently, the best deals on the V40 are found with the naturally aspirated gasoline engines (1.8 or 2.0), of Volvo origin, which are infinitely more reliable and less expensive to maintain than the aging Renault diesels. Avoid the Mitsubishi-sourced 1.8i GDI at all costs.
