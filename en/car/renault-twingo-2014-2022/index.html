Summary

The Renault Twingo III (AH8) marks a radical departure from its predecessors. Co-developed with the Daimler group (Smart Fortwo/Forfour), it adopts a rear-engine and rear-wheel-drive architecture. This technical choice gives it an exceptional turning radius (4.30 m), ideal for the city, but reduces trunk volume and raises the loading sill.

It benefited from a facelift (Phase 2) in 2019, bringing aesthetic touches (C-shaped light signature), an update to the multimedia system (Easy Link), and the introduction of the 100% electric Z.E. version (which became E-Tech). Overall, it's a very endearing city car, whose mechanical reliability is reassuring, as Renault had the good idea not to install the controversial 1.2 TCe, preferring chain-driven 3-cylinder blocks instead.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable turning radius (4.30 m) facilitating parking
  • Overall very satisfactory mechanical reliability (chain-driven engines)
  • Charming look and numerous customization possibilities
  • 22 kW Chameleon charger on the electric version (very rare in this segment)
  • Absence of the problematic 1.2 TCe engine

Weaknesses 5

  • Limited trunk volume (188 liters) and very high loading sill
  • Sensitivity to crosswinds on highways (rear-engine architecture)
  • Plastic front hood opening system, fragile and impractical
  • Heat generated by the engine in the trunk (despite insulation)
  • EDC gearbox sometimes harsh in the city on early models

Check before buying

  • Smooth operation of the EDC gearbox (absence of juddering on startup)
  • Asymmetrical tire wear (sensitive front axle geometry)
  • Condition of the plastic front hood fasteners (often broken by clumsy handling)
  • History of R-Link multimedia system updates (frequent bugs on Phase 1)
  • Presence of coolant traces under the engine (water pump on SCe)

Official recalls

  • October 2015: Risk of front shock absorber failure due to poor welding (models manufactured until 2015).
  • November 2016: Non-conformity of rear seat belt buckles (replacement required).
  • April 2017: Risk of rear spoiler detachment on the GT version (bonding defect).
  • May 2019: Inspection and possible replacement of the steering column due to a machining defect.
  • 2021 (Z.E.): ECU reprogramming to prevent an unexpected discharge of the 12V battery.

Verdict

The Renault Twingo III is an excellent used city car. Its atypical architecture makes it the queen of cities, even if it penalizes it on the road (wind sensitivity) and in practical aspects (trunk). Mechanically, it's a very good vintage for Renault: the SCe and TCe engines are robust and spared from the reliability scandals of other engines from the brand.

Buying advice: Opt for a 1.0 SCe 70/75 hp version for strictly urban use (indestructible and economical). If you drive outside cities, the 0.9 TCe 90/95 hp is essential, ideally a Phase 2 (after 2019) to benefit from a better calibrated EDC gearbox and the Easy Link system. The electric E-Tech version is one of the best deals on the used market for commuting thanks to its 22 kW charging.
