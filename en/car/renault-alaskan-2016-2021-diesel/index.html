Summary

The Renault Alaskan (D23 generation) marks Renault's entry into the highly competitive one-ton payload pickup segment. A product of the Renault-Nissan Alliance, it is a technical clone of the Nissan Navara NP300 and the Mercedes X-Class. It primarily distinguishes itself with a specific front end, integrating Renault's visual identity (imposing grille, C-shaped light signature). Its major asset lies in its multi-link rear suspension with coil springs (on Double Cab versions), offering significantly superior ride comfort compared to the traditional leaf springs of competitors, without sacrificing its towing capacity (3.5 tons). Although its European career was cut short (ending in 2020/2021), it remains a robust and stately vehicle.

Weaknesses 5

  • Interior plastic quality (hard and utilitarian)
  • Dated and unresponsive multimedia system
  • Large turning radius (cumbersome in urban areas)
  • Sensitivity of the emissions control system (DPF/AdBlue) to short trips
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speeds

Check before buying

  • Condition of the ladder frame: check for perforating corrosion and signs of impact (intensive off-road use).
  • AdBlue system: check for any warning lights on the dashboard (faulty pump, injector, or sensor).
  • Turbo hoses (on the 190 hp): check for oil leaks or abnormal whistling sounds during acceleration.
  • 4x4 transmission: test engagement of 4H and 4LO modes, as well as the rear differential lock (if equipped).
  • Rear suspension: check for sagging coil springs if the vehicle has been frequently overloaded.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Nissan Navara (technical twin), Ford Ranger, Toyota Hilux, Volkswagen Amarok, Isuzu D-Max. - **South America**: Chevrolet S10, Toyota Hilux, Mitsubishi L200 Triton, Volkswagen Amarok. - **Asia / Oceania**: Very rare or absent model, the market being covered by the Nissan Navara.

Verdict

The Renault Alaskan is an excellent choice on the used market for those seeking the dynamic qualities of the Nissan Navara (especially its suspension comfort) with a more distinctive design. The 2.3 dCi engine is robust, but like any modern diesel, it requires regular long-distance driving to preserve its DPF and AdBlue system. Opt for the 190 hp version for heavy towing. A thorough inspection of the chassis and service history is essential before purchase.
