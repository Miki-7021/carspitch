Ford F150

· 2015 – 2020

NÉGOCIER Reliability 60/100 — moyen

Summary

The 13th-generation Ford F-150 (2015-2020) marked a revolutionary shift in the pickup truck industry by introducing a military-grade aluminum-alloy body mounted on a high-strength steel frame. This drastic change shed up to 700 lbs (317 kg) compared to its predecessor, significantly improving payload, towing capacity, and fuel efficiency.

Throughout its lifecycle, Ford heavily pushed its turbocharged 'EcoBoost' V6 engines, which eventually outsold the traditional V8. The generation also saw the introduction of the 10-speed automatic transmission (10R80), co-developed with General Motors. While the aluminum body solved the notorious rust issues of older trucks, this generation is plagued by specific, well-documented powertrain issues—most notably cam phaser failures on the 3.5L EcoBoost, severe oil consumption on the 2018+ 5.0L V8, and erratic shifting from the 10-speed transmission. Choosing the right year and engine combination is absolutely critical for long-term reliability.

Strengths 6

  • Aluminum body completely eliminates traditional body panel rust.
  • Class-leading towing and payload capacities.
  • Spacious and highly ergonomic interior (especially SuperCrew).
  • SYNC 3 infotainment (2016+) is responsive and supports Apple CarPlay/Android Auto.
  • Excellent crash test and safety ratings.
  • 2.7L EcoBoost offers an impressive balance of torque and fuel economy.

Weaknesses 6

  • 10-speed automatic transmission (10R80) is notoriously clunky, hesitates, and suffers from CDF clutch drum failures requiring rebuilds.
  • 3.5L EcoBoost cam phaser failures are incredibly common and expensive to fix.
  • 2018-2020 5.0L V8 suffers from excessive oil consumption.
  • IWE (Integrated Wheel Ends) vacuum system for 4WD frequently fails, causing grinding noises.
  • Panoramic sunroof tracks are made of fragile plastic, prone to breaking and leaking.
  • Frozen door latches in cold weather (doors won't latch shut or won't open).

Reliability by engine

EngineReliabilityKnown Issues & Notes
**3.5L / 3.3L V6 (Naturally Aspirated)**ExcellentThe most reliable engines in the lineup. Simple, no turbos, port injection (3.5L) or dual injection (3.3L). Highly recommended for basic utility.
**2.7L EcoBoost V6**GoodGen 1 (2015-2017) suffers from leaking plastic oil pans. Gen 2 (2018+) added port injection to prevent carbon build-up. Generally the most reliable EcoBoost option.
**3.5L EcoBoost V6 (incl. H.O.)**Average to Poor**Major Issue:** Cam phaser rattle on cold starts (especially 2017-2020 Gen 2). Requires expensive timing job. Gen 1 (2015-2016) prone to timing chain stretch. Also suffers from cracked exhaust manifolds.
**5.0L Coyote V8**Varies by Year**2015-2017 (Gen 2):** Very reliable, minor water pump issues. **2018-2020 (Gen 3):** Poor reliability. Switched to Plasma Transfer Wire Arc (PTWA) cylinder liners, leading to **severe oil consumption** (TSB 19-2365).
**3.0L Power Stroke Diesel**AverageExpensive to maintain. Sensitive emissions system (DPF/EGR). Bosch CP4 fuel pump can fail catastrophically if fuel quality is poor.

Check before buying

  • **Cold Start (Crucial):** Listen carefully for a loud rattle lasting 2-5 seconds on cold start (indicates failing cam phasers on 3.5L EcoBoost).
  • **Transmission Shifting:** Test drive to check for harsh shifts, especially from 1st to 3rd, or 3rd to 5th gear on the 10-speed automatic.
  • **Oil Level (5.0L V8):** On 2018-2020 V8 models, check the oil level and ask for oil consumption test records.
  • **4WD Engagement:** Switch to 4H while driving at low speed. Listen for grinding or humming from the front hubs (IWE vacuum leak).
  • **Sunroof Operation:** Open and close the panoramic sunroof fully. Listen for popping or cracking noises from the tracks.
  • **Door Latches:** Ensure all doors open and latch securely, especially if testing the vehicle in freezing temperatures.

Alternatives

**Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500 (2014-2018):** Offers reliable 5.3L V8s, though plagued by AFM (Active Fuel Management) lifter failures and frame rust. **Ram 1500 (2013-2018):** Superior ride quality due to coil-spring rear suspension and a great interior, but suffers from HEMI 'tick' (exhaust manifold/lifters) and air suspension failures. **Toyota Tundra (2014-2021):** The undisputed king of reliability. The 5.7L V8 is bulletproof, but it suffers from terrible fuel economy, a dated interior, and lower towing capacities compared to the F-150.

Verdict

The 2015-2020 Ford F-150 is a highly capable, innovative truck, but it is a minefield of powertrain configurations. The aluminum body is a massive advantage for long-term ownership in rust-prone areas. However, Ford's push for complex technology resulted in significant reliability issues.

**The Best Buy:** A 2015-2017 model with the 5.0L V8 and the 6-speed automatic transmission. This combination avoids the oil consumption of the Gen 3 V8 and the clunkiness of the 10-speed auto. Alternatively, the 2.7L EcoBoost (especially 2018+) is an excellent daily driver. **The One to Avoid:** The 2018-2019 5.0L V8 (due to severe oil consumption) and any 3.5L EcoBoost without documented proof of a recent cam phaser replacement.
