Summary

The Grandland, in its post-facelift version launched in late 2021 (often referred to as Grandland II for convenience, although it is a major evolution of the first-generation Grandland X), is Opel's compact SUV. It shares its EMP2 V3 platform with other Stellantis group models (Peugeot 3008, Citroën C5 Aircross). This redesign brought a new front design (Vizor), a modernized interior with the digital Pure Panel, and technological improvements. Positioned in the C-SUV segment, it targets family customers looking for comfort, space, and a more assertive aesthetic. The true second generation of the Grandland, based on the STLA Medium platform and including a 100% electric version from its launch, is expected in late 2024/early 2025.

Overall reliability score: The post-facelift Grandland inherits powertrains known from the Stellantis group. While the chassis and assembly are generally robust, the 1.2 PureTech gasoline and 1.5 BlueHDi diesel powertrains present significant points of concern that impact overall reliability. The plug-in hybrid versions, although powerful, add a layer of technical complexity.

Strengths 6

  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Modernized and assertive exterior design (Vizor)
  • Spacious and ergonomic interior (Pure Panel)
  • Good road holding and balanced road behavior
  • Powerful and efficient plug-in hybrid versions (if regularly recharged)
  • Smooth and responsive EAT8 automatic gearbox

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of the 1.2 PureTech engine (belt, oil consumption, fuel recalls)
  • Potential AdBlue system issues on the 1.5 BlueHDi
  • Complexity and maintenance cost of plug-in hybrid versions
  • Slightly reduced boot volume on PHEV versions
  • Interior materials sometimes inferior to certain premium competitors
  • Absence of 100% electric powertrain on this generation (before 2024/2025)

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for 1.2 PureTech and 1.5 BlueHDi)
  • Condition of the timing belt on the 1.2 PureTech (request proof of replacement or inspection)
  • Absence of engine or AdBlue warning lights on the dashboard
  • Operation of the hybrid system and electric range on PHEVs
  • Check if all manufacturer recalls have been performed (especially for the 1.2 PureTech fuel line and PHEV battery)
  • Engine oil level and quality (on the 1.2 PureTech)

Verdict

The post-facelift Grandland is a compact SUV that is appealing for its design, comfort, and spaciousness. However, the reliability of its powertrains is a key factor to consider. The 1.2 PureTech gasoline should be approached with great caution due to recurring timing belt issues and numerous fuel leak recalls. An impeccable service history and verification of all recalls are imperative. The 1.5 BlueHDi diesel is a more robust choice for high-mileage drivers, provided the AdBlue system is monitored. The plug-in hybrid versions offer good performance and controlled consumption if regularly recharged, but their technical complexity and potential repair costs must be taken into account. For a confident purchase, prioritize diesel or plug-in hybrid versions with rigorous follow-up. The 1.2 PureTech should be strongly negotiated and only purchased with an extended warranty and a clear history of interventions on the belt and fuel line.
