Summary

The Mercedes-Benz G-Class (W463) is a true automotive icon. Launched in 1989, this generation marks the transition of the G from a pure military/agricultural utility vehicle (W460/W461) to a luxury vehicle, while retaining its ladder frame chassis, rigid axles, and three differential locks. Produced for nearly 30 years with multiple interior and technological updates, it has seen a wide variety of engines. In diesel, its history is mixed: it ranges from the indestructible inline-6 (OM606) to the highly problematic V8 CDI (OM628), passing through the widespread V6 CDI (OM642). It is a vehicle with undeniable charm and exceptional off-road capabilities, but whose road handling remains rustic and maintenance very expensive.

Overall design rating: 7.5/10 (Penalized by aerodynamics, fuel consumption, and the reliability of certain engines, but saved by its structural robustness and charisma).

Strengths 6

  • Unmatched off-road capabilities (3 differential locks, low range)
  • Structural robustness (massive ladder frame chassis)
  • OM606 (Diesel) and M113 (Petrol) engines of legendary reliability
  • Exceptional resale value, almost zero depreciation on certain models
  • Commanding driving position and peripheral visibility
  • Charisma and timeless design

Weaknesses 6

  • Outdated road handling (vague steering, body roll, barely adequate braking)
  • Gargantuan fuel consumption (aerodynamics of a brick)
  • Disastrous reliability of the G 400 CDI engine (OM628)
  • Prohibitive cost of maintenance and spare parts
  • Limited rear seat space compared to the exterior dimensions
  • Soundproofing at high speeds (wind noise)

Check before buying

  • Corrosion: This is the #1 enemy of the W463. Check the windshield frame, door hinges, rocker panels, rear shock mounts, and the bottom of the rear door.
  • 4x4 System: IMPERATIVELY test the 3 differential lock buttons (Center, Rear, Front) on a loose surface. If the red lights do not turn on, the hydraulic/pneumatic actuators are seized (expensive repair).
  • Swivel housings: Check for the absence of grease leaks at the front wheel spheres.
  • OM642 Engine (V6 CDI): Inspect the bottom of the engine V (under the turbo) with a flashlight to detect traces of oil (cooler leak).
  • OM612 Engine (270 CDI): Remove the plastic cover and look for a hard, black substance (carbon buildup) around the injectors.
  • Maintenance history: A G-Class without a rigorous service book (including axle and transfer case oil changes) is a money pit in the making.

Verdict

The Mercedes G-Class W463 is a passionate purchase that defies all economic logic. If you are looking for a diesel, the choice is crucial. Absolutely avoid the G 400 CDI (OM628), a true mechanical nightmare. For pure collection/off-road use, the G 300 TD (OM606) is the Holy Grail of reliability, although it is starting to age. For a more modern and versatile use, the G 350 CDI/BlueTEC (OM642) is the best compromise, provided you verify that the oil cooler issue has been addressed and accept high maintenance costs. Never forget that a G-Class rusts: a meticulous inspection of the bodywork and the operation of the differential locks is mandatory before any purchase.
