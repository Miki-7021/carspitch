Suzuki Alto

VII (GF) · 2009 – 2015

BUY Reliability 86/100 — excellent

Summary

The seventh-generation Suzuki Alto (factory code GF), also marketed as the Maruti Suzuki A-Star in India and technical twin of the Nissan Pixo, is an A-segment micro city car. Primarily designed for emerging markets and Europe, it positions itself as an entry-level, ultra-economical, and pragmatic vehicle. It does not excel in its finish (ubiquitous hard plastics), sound insulation, or boot volume (129 liters, one of the smallest in its category). However, it excels in its role as an urban 'commuter' thanks to exceptional mechanical reliability, negligible maintenance costs, and great maneuverability. A slight facelift occurred in late 2012, bringing subtle aesthetic touches and a slight reduction in CO2 emissions. Overall design note: basic but indestructible.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall mechanical reliability (K10B engine)
  • Extremely low purchase, insurance, and maintenance costs
  • Chain timing (no belt to replace)
  • Very low fuel consumption
  • Smooth and very reliable torque converter automatic gearbox (BVA4) (unlike competing automated manual gearboxes)
  • Perfect maneuverability and turning radius for the city

Weaknesses 6

  • Almost non-existent sound insulation (very noisy on highways)
  • Ridiculous boot volume (129 liters) and high loading sill
  • Very low-end interior plastic quality and assembly
  • Outdated road handling outside urban areas (body roll, sensitivity to crosswinds)
  • Mediocre passive safety (3 Euro NCAP stars in 2009)
  • Rear compass windows (tilt-only)

Check before buying

  • **Clutch (manual gearbox):** Often premature wear (sometimes before 60,000 km) due to exclusively urban use. Test the biting point and pedal stiffness.
  • **Water pump:** Known issue with the K10B engine. Look for traces of coolant (pink/blue color) around the accessory belt or listen for a whistling/growling sound at idle.
  • **Wheel bearings:** Especially at the rear. Listen for a dull humming sound starting from 50 km/h.
  • **Exhaust system:** Tendency for surface corrosion, or even perforation on the rear silencer for models parked outdoors in humid/salty regions.
  • **Front brakes:** Discs tend to wear quickly or warp slightly with repeated heavy braking.

Economy

  • Total Budget Control — Benefit from some of the lowest purchase, insurance, and maintenance costs on the market, making the Alto a smart choice for your wallet.
  • Exemplary CO2 Emissions — Contribute to the environment and benefit from advantageous taxation thanks to very low CO2 emissions, notably 99g/km for post-2012 models.

Practicality

  • Queen of the City — Maneuver with astonishing ease thanks to its compactness and optimal turning radius, making it perfect for urban traffic and parking.

Comfort

  • Smooth and Serene Urban Driving — Appreciate the smoothness and reliability of its torque converter automatic transmission (BVA4), a rarity in this segment, offering superior driving comfort in the city.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Toyota Aygo I / Peugeot 107 / Citroën C1 (the triplets, better road handling), Hyundai i10 I (better finished and more versatile), Renault Twingo II (more spacious), Fiat Panda II/III. - **Japan / Asia:** Daihatsu Mira, Hyundai Santro, Chevrolet Spark / Beat. - **Emerging Markets (India):** Hyundai Eon, Maruti Suzuki Wagon R.

Verdict

The Suzuki Alto VII (GF) is the archetype of the rational car. If you're looking for comfort, technology, or performance, look elsewhere. However, if your goal is to get from point A to point B in the city for the lowest possible cost, it's a masterful choice. Its 1.0 K10B engine is one of the most robust of its generation. Prioritize a post-2012 (facelifted) model with a clear service history. The automatic gearbox (BVA4) version is an excellent find for city dwellers avoiding manual gearboxes, offering much superior reliability compared to competing automated manual gearboxes (PSA/Toyota or VW).
