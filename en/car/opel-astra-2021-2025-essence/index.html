Opel Astra

L · 2021 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The sixth-generation Opel Astra (Astra L) marks a complete break with the General Motors era. Based on the Stellantis group's EMP2 V3 platform (shared with the Peugeot 308 III and DS 4), it adopts the brand's new stylistic language, characterized by the 'Opel Vizor' grille and a minimalist 'Pure Panel' interior. Positioned as a dynamic mainstream compact, it offers an excellent compromise between comfort and road holding, with slightly firmer damping than its French cousin to appeal to the German market. While the design and road qualities are praised, the reliability of its internal combustion engines (especially the PSA-derived 1.2 petrol) remains a major point of concern for used car buyers, although recent versions have benefited from significant technical developments (switch to a timing chain on the hybrid).

Strengths 6

  • Assertive and distinctive design (Opel Vizor)
  • Interior ergonomics (retention of physical buttons for air conditioning)
  • Superb road handling and precise steering
  • AGR certified seats (excellent for back comfort on long journeys)
  • Very powerful Intelli-Lux LED matrix lighting
  • Crucial introduction of the 1.2 Hybrid engine with timing chain in 2024

Weaknesses 5

  • Uncertain reliability of 1.2 Turbo 110/130 versions (wet timing belt)
  • Frequent electronic bugs on the Pure Panel system (black screens, reboots)
  • Average rear passenger space for the category
  • Reduced boot volume on PHEV versions (352 liters vs 422 liters)
  • Poor rear visibility (thick C-pillars)

Check before buying

  • On 1.2 Turbo (non-hybrid): Condition of the timing belt (cracks, swelling) and service history (zero tolerance for delays).
  • On 1.5 Diesel: Metallic rattling noise at idle (sign of wear on the 7mm camshaft chain).
  • Electronics: Test all Pure Panel functions, wireless CarPlay/Android Auto connection, and the rearview camera.
  • PHEV: Check battery health (SOH) and ensure the T2 charging cable is supplied and functional.
  • Bodywork: Panel alignment (some assembly defects reported early in production in Rüsselsheim).

Official recalls

  • November 2023 (Software): Engine management unit update on PHEV versions to prevent sudden loss of motive power.
  • October 2023 (Airbags): Possible incorrect deployment of curtain airbags in the event of a side impact (models produced between early 2022 and mid-2023).
  • May 2023 (Steering): Risk of steering loss due to an improperly tightened nut on the steering column (concerns a small series of Stellantis vehicles).
  • February 2022 (Tires): Incorrect pressure indicated on the door label, requiring label replacement.

Verdict

The Opel Astra L is a very successful compact car in terms of style, comfort, and driving dynamics. However, the choice of engine is absolutely crucial. The 1.2 Turbo 110 and 130 hp petrol versions (with timing belt) should be approached with extreme caution on the used market due to the PureTech engine's history, requiring an impeccable service record. The true 'sweet spot' of the range is the recent **1.2 Turbo Hybrid 136 hp**, which finally adopts a timing chain, offering peace of mind and driving pleasure. PHEV and electric versions are good alternatives, although more expensive. Negotiate firmly for 2021-2023 models and prioritize recent models or those under warranty.
