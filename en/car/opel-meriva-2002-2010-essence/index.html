NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Opel Meriva (Meriva A) is a compact mini-MPV produced from 2002 to 2010. Based on the Opel Corsa C platform, it is positioned as a versatile family vehicle, offering remarkable interior modularity for its size, notably thanks to its FlexSpace rear seating system. It underwent a facelift in 2006, bringing aesthetic touches (bumpers, lights) and some mechanical evolutions. The Meriva A is appreciated for its spaciousness, ease of city driving, and overall comfort, but its reliability has been marred by some recurring problems, particularly with certain electronic components and the power steering.

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of the electric power steering (EPS)
  • Electronic problems (CIM module, power windows)
  • Fragile EGR valve and ignition coils on some gasoline engines (Twinport)
  • Fragile timing chain on 1.4 Twinport and 1.3 CDTI
  • Somewhat austere interior finish and scratch-sensitive materials
  • Oil consumption on some gasoline engines

Check before buying

  • **Electric Power Steering (EPS)**: Check for any hard spots, noise, or loss of assistance. This is a major and costly defect.
  • **CIM Module (Column Integration Module)**: Test all steering wheel controls (horn, radio, cruise control), turn signals. A defect can prevent starting.
  • **Engine Noises**: Listen carefully to the engine on cold start (clattering, whistling) to detect a timing chain problem (1.4 gasoline, 1.3 CDTI) or hydraulic lifters.
  • **EGR Valve and Ignition Coils**: Check the service history to see if these parts have been replaced, and be attentive to jerks or loss of power.
  • **Oil Consumption**: Ask for the oil consumption history, especially on 1.4 and 1.6 Twinport engines.
  • **General Electronics**: Test all electrical equipment (power windows, air conditioning, car radio, lights).
  • **Bodywork and Chassis Condition**: Check for significant corrosion, especially on the sills and wheel arches.

Verdict

The Opel Meriva A is a practical and modular vehicle, ideal for urban or suburban families looking for generous space in a compact format. However, its reliability is mixed. The 1.6 8V (Z16SE) and 1.8 16V (Z18XE) gasoline engines are the most recommendable for their relative robustness, provided that the ignition coils and CIM module are monitored. The Twinport versions (1.4 and 1.6) and the 1.3 CDTI are riskier due to recurring problems (EGR, timing chain, injectors). The electric power steering is the major weak point, potentially leading to costly repairs. A well-maintained Meriva A, with a clear repair history (especially for the EPS and CIM), can be a good used car deal. Prioritize post-facelift models (after 2006) which may have benefited from some improvements. Negotiate the price based on repairs to be anticipated or already carried out.
