Summary

The first-generation Citroën C4 Cactus (Phase 1) is an atypical crossover that marked a stylistic departure for the chevron brand. Based on the PF1 platform (that of the Peugeot 208 I and Citroën C3, not the classic C4), it stands out for its weight reduction philosophy (under 1,000 kg for base versions), its sleek design integrating the famous *Airbumps* (polyurethane side protectors), and a minimalist cabin where almost all controls are grouped on a touchscreen.

While this approach allowed for very low fuel consumption and typically Citroën suspension comfort, it also imposed compromises: pop-out rear windows, a single-piece rear bench (until 2015), and light soundproofing. In terms of reliability, this generation is unfortunately hit by the two biggest mechanical scandals of the PSA group of the decade: the wet belt failures of the PureTech petrol engines and the chronic breakdowns of the AdBlue emissions control system on the BlueHDi diesels. A purchase that therefore requires extreme vigilance regarding the maintenance history.

Strengths 5

  • Extremely low fuel consumption (especially in diesel)
  • Soft suspension comfort, typical of Citroën's DNA
  • Original design and practical Airbumps in the city
  • Wide and comfortable front seats (sofa-style on ETG gearbox versions)
  • Featherweight promoting agility and saving tires/brakes

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the PureTech petrol engines (belt)
  • Chronic and costly breakdowns of the AdBlue system on the BlueHDi 100
  • ETG6 automated gearbox is slow, jerky, and unreliable
  • Annoying all-touch ergonomics (climate control adjustment via the screen)
  • Petty cost-cutting details: pop-out rear windows, no tachometer, single-piece rear bench (before 2015)
  • Light soundproofing on the highway (wind and road noise)

Check before buying

  • On BlueHDi: Demand the history of interventions on the AdBlue system (tank, pump, injector, NOx sensors). If nothing has been done, a breakdown is imminent.
  • On PureTech: Check the condition of the belt through the oil cap. Demand invoices proving strict adherence to oil change intervals and belt replacement.
  • ETG6 Gearbox: Test the vehicle at low speeds and while maneuvering. Shuddering or violent jerks indicate a clutch or actuator at the end of its life.
  • Touchscreen: Test all functions (climate control, radio, GPS). A black or very slow screen often requires an expensive replacement.
  • Bodywork: Check the condition of the Airbumps (UV discoloration on early models) and the absence of corrosion on the door edges (weatherstrip defect).

Verdict

The first-generation Citroën C4 Cactus is an endearing vehicle, economical to run, and very comfortable. However, it is undermined by the mechanical flaws of its PSA parts bin. If you are looking for a diesel, avoid the 1.6 BlueHDi 100 unless the AdBlue tank has been replaced very recently with supporting invoices. The absolute best choice is the 1.6 e-HDi 92, preferably with a manual gearbox: it is a robust Euro 5 engine, without AdBlue, which offers ridiculously low fuel consumption and excellent longevity if routine maintenance is respected. The PureTech petrol versions are to be strictly avoided due to the constant risk of engine failure linked to the wet belt.
