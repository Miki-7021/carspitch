Summary

The second-generation Audi A5, known by the internal code B9 (or F5), was launched in 2016, adopting the MLB Evo platform from the Audi A4 B9. It is available as a coupe, Sportback (five-door), and convertible, offering a blend of sportiness, elegance, and technology. The design, while evolutionary, has maintained the model's strong identity.

A **major facelift** occurred in late 2019 (2020 models), bringing aesthetic revisions (grille, headlights, bumpers), a technological update to the interior (new MMI touchscreen, removal of the rotary controller), and the widespread integration of mild-hybrid (MHEV) technology across many engines, particularly diesel and gasoline.

The A5 B9 is generally perceived as a reliable, well-built, and pleasant car to drive, with a high level of interior finish quality. Its reliability is solid, but like any modern vehicle, it is not without points of vigilance, particularly concerning emission control systems and onboard technologies.

Weaknesses 7

  • High purchase price new and used

  • Limited rear legroom in coupe and convertible

  • Touchscreen MMI (post-facelift) less intuitive for some than the previous rotary controller
  • Tire and brake wear on sporty versions (S/RS)

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for S tronic gearbox oil changes)
  • AdBlue system operation (warning lights, error messages) on diesels
  • Absence of leaks from the water pump and thermostat on TFSI
  • Condition of brake discs and pads, especially on sporty versions
  • Functioning of all electronic equipment (MMI, driving aids, air conditioning)
  • Condition of tires and rims (pay attention to large rims and sports suspensions)
  • Absence of abnormal engine noises (timing chain on high-mileage V6 TDI, turbo)
  • Functioning of the MHEV system (Start/Stop, energy recovery) on relevant models

Verdict

The Audi A5 B9 is an excellent choice for anyone looking for an elegant, comfortable, and technologically advanced car. Its reliability is generally good, particularly for the most common diesel and gasoline engines (2.0 TDI and 2.0 TFSI), which represent an excellent compromise between performance and fuel consumption.

**For diesel engines (2.0 TDI and 3.0 TDI)**: They are particularly recommended for high-mileage drivers and long journeys. Fuel efficiency and torque are major assets. Vigilance should be paid to the AdBlue system and the EGR valve, which can incur costs if maintenance is not rigorous or if the vehicle is primarily used in the city. Post-facelift MHEV versions are slightly more complex, and the 12V/48V battery is a point to monitor.

**For gasoline engines (2.0 TFSI)**: They offer superior driving pleasure for more varied use, with good responsiveness. Water pump issues are known but manageable. MHEV versions also need monitoring for their electrical system.

**Purchase Verdict**: **BUY** if the service history is clear and recalls have been performed. Prioritize post-facelift models (2019+) for technological improvements and more integrated MHEV systems, but be aware of potential costs related to the 12V/48V battery. For diesels, ensure the vehicle has primarily done long journeys to preserve the DPF and EGR. A pre-purchase inspection is always recommended.
