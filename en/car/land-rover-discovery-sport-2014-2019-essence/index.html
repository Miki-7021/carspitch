Summary

The first-generation Land Rover Discovery Sport (L550) succeeded the Freelander 2 in 2014. Positioned as a premium family compact SUV, it stands out with its 5+2 seating configuration (rare in the category) and its real off-road capabilities thanks to the Terrain Response system. Mechanically, its career is divided into two phases: a launch with engines inherited from the Ford era (2.2 Diesel and 2.0 EcoBoost petrol), then a transition to Jaguar Land Rover's 'Ingenium' modular engine family from 2015/2016. Although very attractive, this model suffers from a mediocre reliability record (TÜV, ADAC, What Car?), particularly in the early years of production (ZF9 gearbox, electronics) and on the Ingenium diesels. The 2019 facelift brought notable improvements.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional versatility: 7 seats (5+2) in a compact format (4.59 m)
  • Real off-road capabilities (Terrain Response, approach/departure angles)
  • High-level suspension comfort and soundproofing
  • Timeless and rewarding design
  • Petrol engines (especially the 2.0 Ford) spared from the worst flaws of the Ingenium diesels

Weaknesses 5

  • Very disappointing overall reliability record (TÜV defect rate at 3 years: 20.5%, What Car? 2.5/5)
  • Poorly calibrated 9-speed ZF automatic gearbox on 2014-2016 models (jerks, slowness)
  • Slow and dated InControl Touch infotainment system before the facelift
  • Very high fuel consumption of the petrol engines
  • Fragile Ingenium diesels (2015+) (timing chain, EGR, FAP)

Check before buying

  • Automatic gearbox (ZF9): imperative to test gear shifts both cold and hot (no jerking should be tolerated).
  • AWD transmission (Haldex): demand the rear axle oil change invoices (every 60,000 km).
  • Underbody and running gear: TÜV reports weaknesses on the underbody (corrosion, off-road impacts) and suspension arms (recall in 2016).
  • Electronics and sensors: check the proper functioning of all equipment. ADAC reports frequent failures of the ABS sensors.
  • Maintenance history: avoid models that have followed the manufacturer's oil change interval of 34,000 km / 2 years (especially on Ingenium engines).

Official recalls

The Discovery Sport has been the subject of numerous safety recalls. Here are the main ones:

  • Airbags (May 2025 - France): Recall related to Takata airbags. The propellant gas can deteriorate, projecting metal fragments in the event of deployment.
  • Turbocharger (Oct 2024 - France): Potential leak from the turbo oil supply pipe on 2021-2025 models (spillover into later years of the generation).
  • Seat belts (Sep 2022 & Dec 2016 - France/USA): Manufacturing defect in the seat belt pretensioners (damaged tubes).
  • Fuel system (Sep 2021 - France): Defect in the support grid of the injected rubber return pipe.
  • MHEV fire risk (May 2021 - France): Electrical overload in the 48V system (MOSFET) on 2019-2020 models.
  • Electricity / Lighting (May 2021 - France): Poor contact of the battery ground stud leading to a lighting failure.
  • Steering wiring harness (May 2021 - France): Rubbing of the electrical wiring harness inside the steering column.
  • Petrol Fuel Rail (Feb 2018 - USA NHTSA 18V087000): Fuel leak on the fuel rail of the 2.0L petrol engines (Ingenium).
  • Front suspension (Dec 2016 - USA NHTSA 16V889000): Defect on the front lower suspension arm (2016-2017 models).
  • Automatic gearbox (Aug 2016 - USA NHTSA 16V614000): Sensor wiring harness problem on the ZF9 transmission (late 2014 models).
  • License plate lighting (May 2016 - USA NHTSA 16V274000): Failure of the license plate lighting.

What sets it apart 5

Practicality

  • Versatile 5+2 Seater Family SUV — Enjoy exceptional modularity with its 7 seats (5+2) in a compact 4.59m format, ideal for large families or occasional transport needs.

Performance

  • Unrivaled Off-Road Capabilities — Equipped with the Terrain Response system, the Discovery Sport offers genuine off-road capabilities, allowing you to explore beyond the beaten path with confidence and ease.
  • Proven and Powerful Petrol Engine — The 2.0 Si4 (Ford) petrol engine delivers lively performance and quiet operation, ensuring a dynamic and enjoyable driving experience.

Comfort

  • Premium Ride Comfort and Sound Insulation — Experience high-level suspension comfort and refined sound insulation, transforming every journey into a serene and pleasant experience for all passengers.

Safety

  • Comprehensive Safety and Controlled Road Behavior — With its 5-star Euro NCAP rating (89% adult, 82% child), the Discovery Sport offers optimal protection. Its solid and reassuring braking, combined with stable and predictable handling, ensures a serene and safe drive in all circumstances.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Audi Q5, BMW X3, Mercedes-Benz GLC, Volvo XC60 (often more reliable, but rarely with 7 seats)., * Japan/Asia: Lexus NX (absolute benchmark in hybrid reliability), Honda CR-V (available with 7 seats)., * North America: Acura RDX, Lincoln Corsair, Lexus RX.

Verdict

The first-generation Land Rover Discovery Sport is a vehicle full of family and dynamic qualities, but weighed down by overall reliability well below premium standards. With an ADAC breakdown rate of 4.8 and a TÜV defect rate of 20.5% at 3 years, purchase must be extremely cautious.

If you are looking for a petrol version: Favor models from 2017 onwards (Ingenium engine) for slightly better controlled fuel consumption, or the pre-2017 2.0 Si4 (Ford) if you drive little, provided the ZF9 gearbox has been updated and its oil changed.

To avoid: The Ingenium diesels (2.0 TD4/SD4 from 2015 to 2018) in urban use, and any model lacking a clear maintenance logbook with shortened oil change intervals (max 15,000 km). The 2019 facelift greatly improved the electronics and finish.
