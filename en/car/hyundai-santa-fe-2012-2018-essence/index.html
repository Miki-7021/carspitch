Summary

The third-generation Hyundai Santa Fe, known by the internal code DM, was launched in 2012 and marked a turning point for the Korean family SUV. More modern, more dynamic, and better equipped than its predecessors, it positioned itself as a serious alternative to European and Japanese SUVs. Available in 5-seater (often called Santa Fe Sport in some markets) and 7-seater (Santa Fe or Grand Santa Fe) versions, it offered excellent value for money, appreciable comfort, and generous space. A mid-cycle facelift occurred in 2015, bringing aesthetic touches (bumpers, lights) and technological improvements (infotainment systems, driving aids). Overall, the Santa Fe DM is a reliable vehicle, but some engines, particularly direct-injection gasoline ones, have experienced specific problems that are crucial to be aware of before purchase.

Strengths 6

  • Overall comfort and sound insulation
  • Generous interior space (especially in 7-seater)
  • Comprehensive standard equipment
  • Attractive value for money on the used market
  • Reliable and powerful diesel engines (2.2 CRDi)
  • Modern and stately design

Weaknesses 6

  • High fuel consumption of GDI gasoline engines
  • Major reliability issues on some GDI gasoline engines (2.4 GDI, 2.0 T-GDI) not resolved by recall
  • DPF and EGR sensitivity on diesels in urban use
  • Less dynamic road handling than some competitors
  • Quality of some interior plastics before the facelift
  • Power steering sometimes considered too light

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for engine recalls)
  • Abnormal engine noises (ticking, knocking) on GDI gasoline versions
  • DPF and EGR operation on diesels (warning lights on, loss of power)
  • Condition of running gear (silent blocks, shock absorbers)
  • Automatic gearbox operation (smooth shifts, no jerks)
  • Sealing and operation of the panoramic sunroof
  • Condition of tires and brakes (heavy vehicle)

Verdict

The Hyundai Santa Fe III (DM) is a very interesting family SUV on the used car market, offering an excellent level of equipment, appreciable comfort, and generous space. However, the choice of engine is crucial.

**For maximum reliability and peace of mind, prioritize the 2.2 CRDi (D4HB) diesel engines or, if available, the 2.4 MPI (G4KE).** These engines are generally robust, provided that specific diesel maintenance (DPF, EGR) is respected and frequent short trips are avoided.

**Direct-injection gasoline engines (2.4 GDI G4KJ and 2.0 T-GDI G4KH) should be approached with the utmost caution, or even avoided, due to documented problems with connecting rod bearing failure and engine breakdown, particularly in North America.** Although recalls have taken place, the residual risk and complexity of repair make these versions less recommendable. If you are considering purchasing such a version, a complete history of recalls and repairs is absolutely essential, as well as a thorough mechanical inspection.

The 3.3 GDI V6 (G6DH) is more reliable than the 4-cylinder GDI engines, but its consumption is high and valve fouling remains a concern. In any case, an automatic gearbox oil change (even if not recommended by Hyundai) is strongly advised to extend its lifespan.

In summary, a well-maintained diesel Santa Fe DM is an excellent choice. The 4-cylinder GDI gasoline versions are a risky bet.
