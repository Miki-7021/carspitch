Summary

The **first-generation Suzuki Grand Vitara (GT/HT)**, also known as Suzuki Escudo in Japan or Chevrolet Tracker in North America, is a compact SUV that retains the DNA of a true off-roader. Unlike its direct competitors of the era (Toyota RAV4, Honda CR-V) which opted for unibody chassis, the Grand Vitara is based on a **separate ladder frame**, a rigid rear axle, and features a transfer case with **low range (reducer)**. It was offered in 3-door, 5-door, and convertible body styles. Although its on-road behavior is rustic, it excels off-road and offers excellent overall robustness. A minor facelift occurred in 2001 (revised interior) and 2003 (front fascia).

Strengths 5

  • True off-road capabilities (ladder frame, low range gearbox)
  • Overall reliability of gasoline engines (especially 1.6 and 2.0)
  • Robustness of the transmission and running gear
  • Compact size ideal for narrow trails
  • Timeless and appealing design (especially in 3-door/convertible)

Weaknesses 5

  • Rustic on-road behavior (bouncy suspension, body roll)
  • Significant susceptibility to corrosion (chassis, wheel arches)
  • High fuel consumption of gasoline engines (especially the V6)
  • Basic interior finish (hard plastics that age poorly, rattles)
  • Tiny trunk on the 3-door version

Check before buying

  • **Corrosion:** Thoroughly inspect the ladder frame, suspension mounts, rocker panels, and trunk floor.
  • **4x4 System:** Check the engagement of the front axle. The pneumatic system (vacuum pump behind the bumper) can leak or fail, preventing hub locking.
  • **Timing Chain (J20A / H25A):** Listen to the engine when cold. A persistent metallic rattling indicates worn chain tensioners.
  • **Oil Leaks:** Frequent at the crankshaft oil seal, valve cover, and oil pan.
  • **Suspension:** Wear of rear axle trailing arm bushings and shock absorbers.

Official recalls

  • - **November 2001:** Risk of moisture freezing in the accelerator cable in very cold weather, potentially blocking the pedal (1998-2001 models). - **February 2002:** Replacement of the ignition switch (Neiman) due to a risk of overheating and fire (concerns certain specific markets). - **June 2006:** Potential issue with front seat belt buckles that might not latch correctly.

Verdict

The Suzuki Grand Vitara I is an excellent choice for those looking for a true compact, robust, and affordable 4x4. It is more suited for rural dwellers, hunters, or off-road enthusiasts than for city dwellers seeking a dynamic SUV. **The 2.0L (J20A) gasoline engine is the best compromise** between performance, reliability, and maintenance cost, provided that the oil change history is clear to ensure the survival of the timing chain. The 1.6L is indestructible but a bit underpowered on the road. Avoid heavily corroded models or those where the 4x4 system does not engage. Diesels should only be considered for intensive utility use, favoring the 2.0 HDi if maintenance has been rigorous.
