Summary

The fourth-generation Mitsubishi Outlander (internal code GN0W) marks a significant turning point for the Japanese manufacturer. Launched in 2021, it is the brand's first model to fully benefit from the Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi alliance, sharing its CMF-CD platform with the Nissan Rogue/X-Trail (T33). This mid-size family SUV stands out with a bold and modern design, an upgrade in terms of interior materials and embedded technologies, and a seven-seat option in some markets.

Positioned as a versatile vehicle, the Outlander IV aims to offer comfort, safety, and light off-road capability thanks to its improved S-AWC (Super All-Wheel Control) all-wheel drive system. Its reliability, although difficult to assess in the very long term for such a recent model, is based on proven technical foundations, notably Mitsubishi's plug-in hybrid (PHEV) system, known for its robustness, and gasoline engines shared with Nissan.

**Overall reliability rating (preliminary): 7.5/10** (Good, with reservations about the model's newness and the gasoline engine's CVT).

Strengths 6

  • Modern and distinctive exterior design.
  • Quality interior, significantly improved compared to the previous generation.
  • Highly effective S-AWC all-wheel drive system.
  • PHEV version known for its reliability and efficiency.
  • Good spaciousness and 7-seat option.
  • Generous manufacturer's warranty (often 5 years/100,000 km or more depending on the market).

Weaknesses 5

  • 2.5L gasoline engine a bit underpowered for the vehicle's size.
  • CVT gearbox on the gasoline version, potentially less durable than conventional automatic transmissions if poorly maintained.
  • Gasoline version's fuel consumption can be high on the highway.
  • Higher purchase price for the PHEV version.
  • Limited long-term reliability data for the IV model, as it is very recent.

Check before buying

  • **Service history:** Essential, especially for CVT fluid changes on the gasoline version and specific PHEV maintenance.
  • **CVT operation:** Absence of slipping, abnormal noises, or jerks.
  • **PHEV system:** Check actual electric range, battery charge status, and absence of warning lights.
  • **Onboard electronics:** Test all systems (infotainment, driving aids, air conditioning).
  • **Tire and brake wear:** A heavy SUV can quickly wear out these consumables.

Verdict

The fourth-generation Mitsubishi Outlander is a competent and well-equipped family SUV, marking a significant improvement over its predecessor.

**For the 2.5L Gasoline version:** It's a reasonable choice for versatile use, provided you don't expect sporty performance and strictly adhere to CVT maintenance. Its engine reliability is good, but the CVT remains a point of vigilance. Ideal for tighter budgets at purchase.

**For the 2.4L MIVEC PHEV version:** This is undoubtedly the version to prioritize if your budget allows and if you can recharge regularly. Mitsubishi's plug-in hybrid system is proven and offers excellent efficiency, comfortable power, and first-rate reliability. It's an excellent choice for family and eco-friendly use.

Overall, the Outlander IV is a vehicle to **BUY** if the service history is clear and recalls have been performed. The PHEV version is particularly recommended.
