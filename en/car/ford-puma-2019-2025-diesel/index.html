Summary

The **Ford Puma II** marks the return of the legendary nameplate, no longer as a small coupé, but as an urban SUV (B-segment) based on the Fiesta Mk8 platform. Launched in late 2019, it quickly established itself thanks to its athletic design, very dynamic chassis (one of the best in its category), and practicality, embodied by the famous « MegaBox » (an 80-liter washable compartment under the trunk floor).

Although the range is largely dominated by micro-hybrid (mHEV) gasoline engines and the excellent Flexifuel (E85) version, Ford briefly offered a **1.5 EcoBlue 120 hp diesel engine** between mid-2020 and late 2021. This version targeted high-mileage drivers and company fleets but was prematurely withdrawn due to falling demand for diesel in this segment and the success of E85. The model received a facelift in early 2024 (new light signature, redesigned interior with a 12-inch touchscreen and SYNC 4 system).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis: dynamic and precise road handling (the best in the B-SUV segment)
  • Practicality: voluminous trunk (456 L) and ingenious 80 L « MegaBox » under the floor
  • Factory-original Flexifuel E85 engine (very rare and economical)
  • Very low fuel consumption of the 1.5 EcoBlue diesel engine on the highway
  • Distinctive design and comprehensive technological equipment (especially post-2024 facelift)

Weaknesses 5

  • 1.5 EcoBlue diesel engine unsuitable for city driving (rapid clogging, AdBlue issues)
  • Very firm suspension comfort (especially in ST-Line trim and on the Puma ST)
  • Poor rear visibility (thick C-pillars)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (lower parts and door panels)
  • Reliability of the oil pump belt (immersed) on 1.0 EcoBoost engines requiring absolute vigilance

Check before buying

  • For the Diesel (1.5 EcoBlue): Journey history (avoid former urban vehicles), AdBlue/DPF warning lights on the dashboard, turbo whistling.
  • For the Gasoline (1.0 EcoBoost): 100% network service book or invoices proving the use of specific Ford oil. Condition of the oil pump belt.
  • Electronics: Test all touchscreen functions (SYNC), check that the Start&Stop system works (indicator of a healthy 12V/48V battery).
  • Bodywork/Interior: Body panel alignment (sometimes imperfect from the factory), parasitic noises in the dashboard on degraded roads.

Verdict

The Ford Puma II is an excellent choice for those looking for a small SUV with dynamic sedan-like handling. **Regarding the Diesel version (1.5 EcoBlue 120)**: it is to be **NEGOTIATE**d with caution. Although the engine is economical and powerful on the road, it suffers from the typical ailments of modern diesels (AdBlue, DPF) if it doesn't drive enough. Given the rarity of this version and traffic restrictions (ZFE), its purchase is only justified for very high-mileage drivers primarily doing highway driving. For 90% of buyers, the **1.0 Flexifuel E85** or **mHEV 125 hp** version is an infinitely more relevant, economical, and easy-to-resell choice, provided the service history is scrupulously checked (specific oil mandatory).
