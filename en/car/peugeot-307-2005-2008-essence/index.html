Peugeot 307

I phase 2 (3A/C) · 2005 – 2008

BUY Reliability 76/100 — bon

Summary

The Peugeot 307 Phase 2 (facelifted in June 2005) marks a crucial turning point for the model. Aesthetically, it adopts the 'big mouth' front end inspired by the 407. Technically, it abandons the VAN/CAN multiplexed electronic architecture (source of countless breakdowns on Phase 1) in favor of the 'Full CAN' network. This change radically improved electronic reliability. Mechanically, the petrol engines (which are the focus of this report) prove to be particularly robust and are today the best choice on the used market, contrasting sharply with the misfortunes of the 1.6 HDi diesels of the same era. Available as a sedan (3/5 doors), station wagon (Estate/SW), and Coupé-Cabriolet (CC).

Strengths 5

  • Remarkable reliability of petrol engines (especially the 1.6 16v 110hp)
  • Superb road handling (precise and dynamic Peugeot chassis)
  • Spaciousness and brightness (especially in SW version with panoramic roof)
  • Electronics finally made reliable compared to Phase 1
  • Very attractive used prices for petrol versions

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 HDi 110hp (peripherals)
  • High weight penalizing smaller engines (1.4 16v)
  • Aging of some interior plastics and the leather steering wheel
  • Fragility of automatic air conditioning mixing flaps
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (short 5-speed manual gearboxes in petrol versions)

Check before buying

  • Test all COM2000 functions (turn signals, wipers, headlights).
  • Check air conditioning mixing (request hot and cold on both sides).
  • On petrol engines: check for absence of jerks during acceleration (sign of worn coils).
  • On SW versions: ensure proper functioning of the panoramic roof blind (fragile motor).
  • On diesels: listen for any excessive turbo whistling and check for absence of 'hissing' at the injectors (seal leak).

Official recalls

  • - **February 2006**: Reprogramming of the ABS/ESP unit (risk of malfunction during emergency braking). - **November 2007**: Replacement of the vacuum pump valve on 2.0 HDi engines (risk of loss of braking assistance). - **May 2008**: Update of the intelligent service unit (BSI) to correct a fault of intermittent light activation.

Verdict

The Peugeot 307 Phase 2 is an excellent used car deal **provided you choose a petrol engine**. The transition to the 'Full CAN' multiplexed network has erased the bad reputation of Phase 1. The 1.6 16v 110hp (TU5JP4) engine is the top choice: indestructible, flexible, and inexpensive to maintain. The 2.0 16v 143hp is perfect for high-mileage drivers allergic to diesel. However, absolutely **AVOID** the 1.6 HDi 110hp, a real money pit due to its fragile peripherals (turbo, dual-mass flywheel, DPF).
