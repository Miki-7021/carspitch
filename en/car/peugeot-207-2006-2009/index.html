Peugeot 207

I (WA/WC) · 2006 – 2009 (Phase 1 / WA-WC)

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

All Peugeot models

Summary

The Peugeot 207 succeeded the legendary 206 with the daunting task of performing just as well. More imposing, heavier (up to +150 kg) but significantly better finished and safer (5 Euro NCAP stars), it marked a clear move upmarket. This Phase 1 (2006-2009) is a pivotal period mechanically: it began its career with the old and robust TU/ET engines, before gradually introducing the "Prince" petrol engines (VTi and THP) co-developed with BMW, which would prove to be disastrous in terms of reliability. In diesel, the 1.6 HDi 110hp of this generation is also notoriously famous for its turbo failures. It's a model with two faces: very reliable in some versions, a financial black hole in others.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent road handling, sharp and safe chassis
  • Interior finish significantly improved compared to the 206 (soft-touch plastics on high trims)
  • High level of passive safety (5 Euro NCAP stars)
  • Reliability of older petrol engines (TU/ET)
  • Suspension comfort preserved despite dynamism

Weaknesses 6

  • Significant weight increase, penalizing smaller engines
  • Disastrous reliability of VTi and THP petrol engines (2007-2009)
  • Endemic turbo failures on the 1.6 HDi 110
  • Electric power steering sometimes faulty (high replacement cost)
  • Average rear passenger space and boot volume for the category
  • Premature wear of seat cushions and leather steering wheel

Check before buying

  • Meticulous service history (especially on VTi, THP, and HDi 110)
  • Chain noise when cold on 1.4 VTi, 1.6 VTi, and 1.6 THP petrol engines
  • Presence of black tar around injectors on 1.4 HDi and 1.6 HDi engines
  • Smoothness and absence of jerks in the electric power steering
  • Functioning of on-board electronics (radio, windows, dashboard warning lights)
  • Condition of the flywheel on the 1.6 HDi 110 (clunking noise at idle or clutch slip)

What sets it apart 7

safety

  • Benchmark Passive Safety — The Peugeot 207 achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, guaranteeing optimal occupant protection in the event of a collision.

braking

  • Efficient and Stable Braking — Enjoy a powerful and reassuring braking system, offering short stopping distances and great stability for perfect vehicle control.

handling

  • Dynamic and Safe Road Handling — Its sharp chassis and precise steering provide exemplary road holding, offering driving pleasure and great peace of mind on all journeys.

reliability

  • Proven TU/ET Petrol Engines — Versions equipped with TU and ET petrol engines are renowned for their robustness and longevity, ensuring daily peace of mind.

comfort

  • Optimized Suspension Comfort and Pleasant Cabin — Despite its dynamism, the 207 maintains excellent suspension comfort, complemented by a well-designed cabin for serene journeys.

quality

  • Improved Interior Finish — Benefit from a significantly improved interior compared to its predecessor, with quality materials and a refined finish, offering a modern and pleasant environment.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Renault Clio III (category benchmark, generally more reliable), Ford Fiesta Mk6/Mk7, Volkswagen Polo IV/V, Opel Corsa D, Toyota Yaris II (reliability champion).
  • Asia / South America: Honda Fit/Jazz, Chevrolet Onix/Agile, Volkswagen Gol/Fox.

Verdict

The Peugeot 207 Phase 1 is an excellent versatile city car, provided you choose the right engine. Its chassis is superb and its safety was top-notch for its time. However, it introduced engines that ruined its reputation. Absolutely avoid the 1.6 THP and 1.6 HDi 110 from those years, as well as VTi engines without an impeccable service history (chain replaced). For a worry-free purchase, prioritize the older petrol engines (1.4 75hp, 1.4 16v 90hp, 1.6 16v 110hp) or the less powerful diesels (1.4 HDi 70, 1.6 HDi 90), making sure to check for any injector leaks.
