Renault Laguna

I (B56) · 1993 – 2001

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The Renault Laguna I (codename B56 for the sedan, K56 for the Nevada wagon) succeeded the aging R21. It marked a spectacular leap forward for Renault in terms of manufacturing quality, suspension comfort, and ergonomics. A true road car, it went through two phases: Phase 1 (1993-1998) and Phase 2 (1998-2001), which brought smooth headlights, new taillights, improved passive safety, and new engines. While the gasoline versions proved very reliable, the diesel range (which is our focus) underwent a major technological transition, moving from atmospheric indirect injection (2.2 D) to high-pressure direct injection (1.9 dCi), with varying degrees of reliability.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional suspension comfort (typical of 90s Renaults)
  • Generous interior space and large trunk (especially in Nevada wagon)
  • Overall reliability of gasoline engines (F-series blocks)
  • Very decent corrosion resistance for its era
  • Today's ridiculously low used purchase price

Weaknesses 5

  • Very fragile heater core (coolant leak into the cabin, repair requiring dashboard removal)
  • Capricious electronics (infrared key immobilizer on Phase 1 that desynchronizes)
  • Poor sound insulation on older generation diesels (2.2 D and 1.9 dTi)
  • Catastrophic mechanical accessibility on the 2.2 dT and V6s
  • Phase 1 interior plastics that deform in the sun (dashboard cowl)

Check before buying

  • Front floor mats (passenger and driver side): if they are greasy or smell of coolant, the heater core is faulty.
  • Operation of central locking and immobilizer (keys).
  • Timing belt history, particularly on 2.2 dT (prohibitive cost) and 1.9 dTi (risk of breakage).
  • Airbag light on (connector under the seats).
  • Condition of rear wheel bearings (frequent issue).

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Peugeot 406 (the benchmark for road holding and HDi engines), Citroën Xantia (hydraulic comfort), Volkswagen Passat B5 (superior finish, robust but noisy TDI engines), Ford Mondeo I/II. - **Japan/Asia**: Honda Accord, Toyota Carina E / Avensis (unbeatable reliability). - **North America**: Model not sold (market dominated by Honda Accord, Toyota Camry, Ford Taurus).

Verdict

The Renault Laguna I is now a 'youngtimer' or a very low-cost 'daily driver'. If you're looking for a diesel, the **2.2 dT** is the most robust choice for towing or driving loaded, provided the timing belt has been done recently. The **1.9 dCi** offers better driving pleasure but requires rigorous monitoring (EGR, turbo). The **1.9 dTi** is to be avoided unless the accessory belt history is crystal clear. The atmospheric **2.2 D** is indestructible but too slow for modern traffic. Paradoxically, the best deals on the Laguna I are found among the gasoline engines (1.8 and 2.0), which are indestructible and have fewer kilometers. The absolute black spot to check before purchase remains the heater core leak.
