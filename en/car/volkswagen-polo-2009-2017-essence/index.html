Volkswagen Polo

V (6R) · 2009 – 2017

NÉGOCIER Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The Volkswagen Polo V (Mk5) is a benchmark in the B-segment (supermini) class, renowned for bringing 'Golf-like' refinement, build quality, and ergonomics to a smaller footprint. It is divided into two distinct phases: the **6R (2009–2014)** and the facelifted **6C (2014–2017)**. This distinction is absolutely critical for reliability, particularly regarding petrol engines. The 6R generation relied heavily on the EA111 engine family (notorious for timing chain issues) and early iterations of the DQ200 DSG7 transmission (plagued with mechatronic failures). The 6C facelift introduced the vastly superior EA211 engine family (reverting to a reliable timing belt) and improved infotainment systems. While it remains an excellent all-rounder, buyers must navigate the engine and transmission options very carefully to avoid costly pitfalls.

Strengths 5

  • Class-leading interior build quality and ergonomics
  • Excellent ride comfort and sound insulation for a B-segment car
  • Timeless, understated exterior design that ages very well
  • EA211 petrol engines (post-2014) are exceptionally reliable and efficient
  • Strong resale value across all markets

Weaknesses 5

  • DQ200 DSG7 dry-clutch transmission is a major reliability liability
  • Pre-2014 EA111 1.2 TSI engines suffer from fatal timing chain issues
  • 1.4 TSI Twincharger (GTI 6R) is one of VW's most unreliable modern engines
  • 1.6 TDI engines are plagued by expensive injector and EGR failures
  • Relatively firm ride on larger alloy wheels (GTI / R-Line)

Check before buying

  • Cold start sound: Any metallic rattling from the right side of the engine indicates timing chain wear (EA111 engines).
  • DSG7 operation: Must shift seamlessly. Any shuddering when pulling away or shifting from 1st to 2nd means the dual-clutch pack is worn.
  • Service history: Crucial for TSI engines. Oil changes must be strictly adhered to (max 15,000 km / 1 year, ignore 'LongLife' 30k km intervals).
  • EGR/DPF history on TDI models: Check if the EGR valve has already been replaced, especially after the EA189 emissions recall.
  • Water pump/Thermostat housing: Check for pink crust (G12/G13 coolant leaks) on EA211 and EA888 engines.

Verdict

The VW Polo V is a tale of two halves. If you buy a **Phase 1 (6R, 2009-2014)**, you are navigating a minefield of timing chain failures (1.2 TSI), catastrophic engine deaths (1.4 TSI GTI), and fragile DSG gearboxes. However, if you buy a **Phase 2 (6C, 2014-2017)** with a manual gearbox and an EA211 engine (1.2 TSI 90/110hp with a timing belt), you are getting one of the best, most reliable, and refined superminis ever made.

**Buying Advice:** - **Best Buy:** 2015-2017 Polo 1.2 TSI (90 or 110 hp) Manual. - **Avoid:** Any 1.4 TSI GTI (180 hp), any pre-2014 1.2 TSI without proof of chain replacement, and be highly skeptical of any high-mileage DSG7 or 1.6 TDI.
