Summary

The first-generation Subaru Crosstrek (known as the Subaru XV in Europe and other international markets) is a compact crossover based on the Impreza hatchback platform. It stands out with its raised ground clearance (22 cm / 8.7 inches), its rugged look with raw plastic cladding, and its symmetrical all-wheel drive (Symmetrical AWD) as standard in almost all markets. This model has met with immense success, particularly in North America (California, Pacific Northwest) and mountainous regions, thanks to its versatility and real capabilities on snow and dirt roads. Although praised for its overall robustness and excellent resale value, it has often been criticized for its slightly underpowered engines and cabin soundproofing. Overall rating: An excellent choice for harsh climates, provided you choose the year and engine carefully.

Strengths 5

  • Real all-road capabilities (22 cm ground clearance)
  • Extremely effective and robust Symmetrical AWD (viscous differential)
  • Excellent passive and active safety (excellent IIHS/Euro NCAP crash tests)
  • Very solid resale value, particularly in mountainous areas
  • Excellent all-around visibility (thin pillars, large mirrors)

Weaknesses 5

  • Chronic excessive oil consumption on 2.0L petrol engines (2012-2015)
  • Modest performance, especially with the 1.6i and CVT gearbox
  • Improvable soundproofing (road and wind noise on the highway)
  • Dated, slow, and buggy infotainment system (Starlink)
  • Paint quality (fragile against stone chips and scratches)

Check before buying

  • Engine oil level (crucial on FB20s from 2012 to 2015)
  • Oil change history of the Lineartronic CVT automatic gearbox
  • Operation of brake lights and keyless start (linked to the brake pedal switch)
  • Rear rolling noises (fragile rear wheel bearings, often need changing around 100,000 km)
  • Condition of front CV joint boots (premature wear due to raised ground clearance)

Official recalls

  • [2019-02-28] NHTSA 19V149000 (USA) / [2021-06-16] Rappel Conso SRT270 (France): Massive recall concerning the brake light switch. Exposure to certain chemicals (such as interior cleaners containing silicone) can insulate the switch. Consequences: the brake lights do not illuminate, inability to start the vehicle (push button) or to shift the gear lever out of the "Park" position. Affects 2013-2017 models.

What sets it apart 7

safety

  • Recognized Top-Tier Safety — The Subaru Crosstrek (XV) achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP crash tests, with impressive scores for adult (89%) and child (82%) protection. It has also been repeatedly awarded by the IIHS as "Top Safety Pick" and "Top Safety Pick+" (with EyeSight), attesting to its robust design and advanced safety systems.

handling

  • Unrivaled Grip and Stability — Thanks to its standard Symmetrical AWD and low-center-of-gravity Boxer engine, the Crosstrek offers stable, predictable, and secure handling. It ensures perfect control on all surfaces, with well-controlled body roll for an SUV, guaranteeing a serene and confident drive.

braking

  • Powerful and Reliable Braking — The Subaru Crosstrek (XV) demonstrates excellent braking performance for its class, with a measured distance of only 36.5 meters to stop from 100 km/h. This result, combined with stable and predictable behavior, ensures optimal active safety in all situations.

practicality

  • Versatility and Real All-Road Capabilities — With its generous 22 cm ground clearance and robust Symmetrical AWD, the Crosstrek is designed to tackle more than just paved roads. It excels on snow, slippery roads, and unpaved tracks, offering unparalleled freedom of exploration and peace of mind.

reliability

  • Long-Term Robustness and Reliability — The Subaru Crosstrek (XV) is renowned for its overall robustness and durable design. The gasoline engines, particularly the 2.0i versions from 2016 onwards, benefit from above-average reliability, ensuring peace of mind for many miles.

economy

  • Excellent Investment with High Resale Value — The Subaru Crosstrek (XV) maintains a very solid resale value in the used car market, particularly appreciated in regions with harsh climates or mountainous areas. It is a guarantee of confidence and an excellent long-term investment.

comfort

  • Exceptional Peripheral Visibility — Designed for safety and ease of driving, the Crosstrek offers excellent peripheral visibility thanks to its thin windshield pillars and large rearview mirrors. This facilitates maneuvers and enhances active safety by providing better environmental awareness.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Nissan Qashqai, Dacia Duster (for affordable 4x4), Suzuki Vitara, Skoda Yeti.
  • North America: Honda HR-V, Jeep Renegade, Mazda CX-3, Chevrolet Trax.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Vezel, Toyota C-HR, Mitsubishi RVR.

Verdict

The first-generation Subaru Crosstrek (XV) is a utility vehicle in the noble sense of the term: robust, practical, and unbeatable in precarious grip conditions. Its AWD system is superior to almost all of its direct competitors. However, buying a used model requires vigilance. The 2.0L petrol models produced between 2012 and 2015 present a proven risk of excessive oil consumption (piston ring defect). It is therefore strongly advised to target the facelifted models (2016-2017), which benefit from a more reliable engine, better soundproofing, and a modernized cabin. The diesel (in Europe) should be strictly reserved for high-mileage drivers to avoid DPF/FAP and EGR problems.
