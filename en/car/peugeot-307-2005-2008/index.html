Summary

The Peugeot 307 Phase 2 (internal code T6) was introduced in mid-2005 as a mid-life facelift to the highly successful 307 (T5). It is easily recognizable by its revised front fascia featuring a larger 'gaping mouth' grille, redesigned headlights, and new bumper designs, aligning it with the styling of the newer 407. Under the skin, the Phase 2 brought a major electrical upgrade, transitioning to a full CAN-bus multiplex architecture. While this allowed for more advanced features (like dual-zone climate control and better infotainment), it also introduced new electronic gremlins. Mechanically, the older 2.0 HDi (90/110) engines were entirely replaced by the Ford/PSA co-developed 1.6 HDi and a new 16-valve 2.0 HDi. The 307 Phase 2 remains a highly practical, comfortable, and safe compact car (5-star Euro NCAP), but its reliability is heavily dependent on the chosen engine and the electrical system's health.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent ride comfort and chassis dynamics (classic Peugeot road holding)
  • Spacious interior with a high roofline, giving an MPV-like feel
  • Very robust petrol engines (TU5 and EW10)
  • High safety standards for its era (5-star Euro NCAP)
  • Excellent rust protection (fully galvanized body)

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.6 HDi 110hp if poorly maintained
  • Electronic and multiplexing gremlins (BSI, COM2000 stalk unit)
  • Interior plastics and soft-touch materials peel and scratch easily over time
  • Heavy curb weight blunts the performance of entry-level engines
  • Frequent headlight bulb burnouts due to voltage spikes

Check before buying

  • Injector Seals (1.6 HDi): Remove the plastic engine cover and look for black, tar-like deposits around the injectors. Listen for a 'chuffing' sound.
  • Turbocharger (1.6 HDi 110): Check for excessive whistling, loss of power, or oil leaks around the turbo housing.
  • COM2000 Unit: Test all steering column stalks. Ensure the turn signals stay on when engaged and the airbag warning light is off.
  • Dual-Mass Flywheel (HDi 110/136): Listen for a rattling noise at idle, or vibrations through the clutch pedal when half-depressed.
  • Climate Control: On dual-zone models, check if both sides blow hot/cold correctly. The internal heater flap gears are known to strip, requiring dashboard removal to fix properly.

Verdict

The Peugeot 307 Phase 2 is a mixed bag. As a used buy today, it offers excellent value, comfort, and space. However, your experience will entirely depend on the engine under the hood. Buy with confidence if you choose a petrol model (especially the 1.6 16v) or the simpler 1.6 HDi 90hp, provided the electronics behave during your test drive. Avoid at all costs the 1.6 HDi 110hp unless the seller provides a massive binder of receipts proving strict 10,000km oil changes, injector seal replacements, and turbo health. Always thoroughly test every single button and switch inside the cabin to rule out BSI or COM2000 failures.
