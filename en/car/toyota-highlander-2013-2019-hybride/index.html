Toyota Highlander

III (XU50) · 2013 – 2019

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

Summary

The third-generation Toyota Highlander (XU50) is a large family SUV (E-segment) with 3 rows of seats, designed primarily for the North American market, but also sold in Asia, Russia, and Oceania (under the name Kluger). Not officially marketed in Western Europe at that time (the model would only arrive there with the XU70 generation in 2021), it established itself as the absolute benchmark for family SUVs across the Atlantic thanks to its outstanding reliability.

It received a major facelift in late 2016 (2017 Model Year) bringing a new front fascia, a modernized V6 with dual injection (D-4S), a new 8-speed automatic gearbox, and increased power for the hybrid version. The **Hybride (Hybrid Synergy Drive)** engine is the jewel of the lineup: it combines exceptional durability (often praised by Consumer Reports and J.D. Power) with an unbeatable total cost of ownership (TCO) for a vehicle of this size.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall reliability, particularly on the Hybrid version
  • Very low total cost of ownership (TCO) for the Hybrid version (fuel consumption of 7 to 8 L/100 km)
  • Generous interior space for the first two rows and numerous storage compartments
  • Extremely high resale value on the used market
  • Ride comfort and silence on board (especially post-2017 facelift)
  • Proven Toyota hybrid system (HSD), without clutch or timing belt (chain)

Weaknesses 5

  • Cramped third row of seats, reserved for children
  • Dated, slow infotainment system (Entune) without native Apple CarPlay/Android Auto on this generation
  • Clumsy road handling, focused on comfort at the expense of dynamism
  • Anemic and noisy 2.7L 4-cylinder engine under load
  • Limited trunk volume when all 3 rows are deployed

Check before buying

  • Recall history: it is imperative to check if the fuel pump recall (Denso) has been performed (2017-2019 models).
  • On the 2GR-FE V6s (2013-2016): inspect the passenger side of the engine to detect any potential oil seepage at the timing chain cover (labor-intensive repair).
  • On the Hybrid version: check that the battery ventilation vents (in the rear) are not obstructed by pet hair or dust.
  • Operation of the 8-speed automatic gearbox (2017-2019 models): ensure there are no jerks at low speeds (requires a software update if present).
  • Brake wear: on the hybrid, the pads last a very long time (regenerative braking), but the calipers can seize if not maintained.

Verdict

The 3rd generation Toyota Highlander is one of the most reliable family SUVs in the world. **The Hybrid engine (3.5L V6) is the absolute choice**: it erases the main flaw of large petrol SUVs (urban fuel consumption) while offering exceptional mechanical reliability, superior even to 100% combustion versions thanks to the absence of a traditional gearbox (eCVT) and reduced brake wear.

If you opt for a standard V6 model, favor the facelifted versions (2017-2019) for their more efficient dual-injection engine, ensuring that the fuel pump recall has been performed. Avoid the 2.7L 4-cylinder, which is too weak for the vehicle's weight. It is a 'blind trust' purchase if the maintenance history is crystal clear.
