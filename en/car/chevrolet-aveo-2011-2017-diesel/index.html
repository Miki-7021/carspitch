Chevrolet Aveo

II (T300) · 2011 – 2017

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Chevrolet Aveo (code name T300), also sold as the Chevrolet Sonic in North America and other markets, marks a complete break from the previous generation (T250). Based on General Motors' global Gamma II platform (shared with the Opel Corsa E and Opel Mokka), it offers transformed road handling, top-tier safety (5 Euro NCAP stars), and a bold design, characterized by its unfaired round headlights and motorcycle-inspired instrument cluster.

In Europe, its career was abruptly interrupted in late 2014 / early 2015 following General Motors' decision to withdraw the Chevrolet brand from the Old Continent to protect Opel. This led to a drastic drop in its value on the used market. Although the official network has disappeared, maintenance remains easy because almost all of its mechanical components (Ecotec petrol engines, Fiat-sourced diesels, running gear) are shared with widely distributed Opel models. Today, it is an excellent used car bargain for those looking for a rational and economical vehicle, provided the right engine is chosen.

Strengths 6

  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Sound and secure road handling (Gamma II chassis)
  • Generous roominess for the B-segment and decent trunk (290 L in 5-door, 502 L in 4-door)
  • Original exterior and interior design (motorcycle-style gauges)
  • Easy maintenance thanks to shared parts with Opel (Corsa/Mokka)
  • Very low fuel consumption of the 1.3 VCDi engine on the highway

Weaknesses 6

  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and very sensitive to scratches)
  • Imperfect soundproofing (wind and road noise on the highway)
  • Rough and noisy 1.3 VCDi engine under acceleration
  • Reliability of the DPF and EGR on the diesel in urban use
  • Brand image in Europe (difficult resale following Chevrolet's departure)
  • Catchy 5-speed manual gearbox on smaller engines

Check before buying

  • On the 1.3 VCDi: Oil change history (crucial for the chain), condition of the DPF (warning light on or jerking), dual-mass flywheel noise (clunking in neutral on the 95 hp).
  • On petrol engines: Traces of oil in the coolant (leaking oil cooler gasket, a common issue on Ecotec engines).
  • Running gear: Play in the anti-roll bar links (clunking on rough roads, very common but inexpensive to repair).
  • Electronics: Operation of the MyLink car radio (touchscreen bugs on high trims) and climate controls.
  • Automatic gearbox (6T30/6T40): Check that gear shifts are smooth (oil change recommended every 60,000 km, often neglected).

Alternatives

Alternatives by market

* **Europe:** Opel Corsa D / E (its technical cousin, easier to resell), Peugeot 208 I (better finish, superior comfort), Renault Clio IV (more dynamic), Ford Fiesta Mk7 (benchmark in road handling), Dacia Sandero II (for pricing position). * **North America (vs Sonic):** Honda Fit (unbeatable in modularity), Ford Fiesta (more fun to drive), Hyundai Accent / Kia Rio (excellent warranty and finish). * **Emerging markets (South Amer., Asia):** Hyundai HB20, Volkswagen Gol, Toyota Yaris (regional versions).

Verdict

The Chevrolet Aveo II (T300) is the archetype of a smart used purchase, provided you accept its "orphan" badge in Europe. It offers modern features, a secure chassis, and often comprehensive equipment for a negligible price.

**Regarding the diesel engine (1.3 VCDi):** It is a Fiat-sourced engine that is extremely frugal and capable in its 95 hp version. However, it requires strictly highway or extra-urban use. If you drive in the city, the DPF will clog, the EGR will foul, and oil dilution will lead to fatal wear of the timing chain. For mixed or urban use, it is imperative to favor the petrol engines (1.2 or 1.4), which are certainly less torquey, but much more tolerant and less expensive to maintain in the long run.
