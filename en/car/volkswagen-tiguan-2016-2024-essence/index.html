Summary

The second-generation Volkswagen Tiguan (code name AD1, and BW1 for the long Allspace version) is based on the famous VAG group's modular MQB platform. Launched in 2016 and facelifted in late 2020, it has established itself as one of the best-selling compact SUVs worldwide. More spacious, more technological, and more dynamic than its predecessor, it offers a plethora of engine options. Although this report focuses on petrol engines (TSI) which have seen a strong resurgence in interest, the Tiguan was also massively sold with diesel (TDI) and, towards the end of its career, with plug-in hybrid (eHybrid) powertrains. Overall, the reliability of this generation is very satisfactory, with EA211 and EA888 petrol engines having been largely made more reliable compared to the previous generation (abandonment of problematic timing chains on small blocks, resolution of excessive oil consumption on 2.0 TSI).

Strengths 6

  • Spaciousness and modularity (standard sliding rear bench)
  • Overall ergonomics and build quality
  • Safe and reassuring road handling
  • Reliability of EA211 and EA888 petrol engines (significant improvement vs Gen 1)
  • Onboard technology (Digital Cockpit, driving aids)
  • Allspace version (7 seats) very practical for families

Weaknesses 5

  • Hard plastics in the lower part of the cabin (step back vs Tiguan 1)
  • DSG7 gearbox (DQ200) on small petrol engines sometimes temperamental in city driving
  • MIB3 infotainment system (post-2020) prone to bugs
  • Very high used car prices
  • Firmness of suspension (especially with 19 or 20-inch wheels and without DCC)

Check before buying

  • Service history of DSG gearboxes (oil change at 60,000 km for wet clutches).
  • Maintenance of the 4Motion system (Haldex) if equipped.
  • Traces of moisture around the panoramic sunroof (clogged drains).
  • Coolant level (frequent water pump leak on 1.4/1.5/2.0 TSI).
  • Smooth operation of the touchscreen and multimedia system (especially 2020-2021 models).
  • Cold-start judder on the 1.5 TSI (check if the software update has been performed).

Official recalls

  • 2022 (Engine cover) : Risk of fire. The decorative engine cover can detach and touch hot parts (concerns certain 2.0 TSI). Cover removal by VW.
  • 2021 (Brake pedal) : Poor welding on the brake pedal plate on automatic models (2020 production).
  • 2019 (Shock absorbers) : Risk of weld fracture on the rear shock absorbers (early 2019 production).
  • 2018 (Sunroof) : Possible short circuit in the panoramic roof's LED ambient lighting module due to moisture, risk of fire.
  • 2017 (Airbags/Seatbelts) : Defect in the seatbelt retractor or airbag igniter on a small series.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Tiguan II is a safe bet on the used car market. Focusing on petrol engines, the 1.5 TSI 150 hp represents the best compromise: it is powerful, economical, and generally very reliable once the initial issues (cold-start judder) are corrected by an update. The 2.0 TSI is robust but requires a higher maintenance/fuel budget (water pump to monitor). Avoid models equipped with the dry-clutch DSG7 gearbox (DQ200) if the vehicle has been used exclusively in the city, or budget for dual-clutch replacement. Prioritize a model with a clear service history, especially for 4Motion and wet-clutch DSG versions.
