Chevrolet Bolt EV

I · 2017 – 2023

BUY Reliability 87/100 — excellent

All Chevrolet models

Summary

The first-generation Chevrolet Bolt EV (also briefly marketed in Europe as the Opel Ampera-e) was one of the first affordable electric cars to offer true long range. Launched in 2017 and facelifted in 2022 (with the introduction of the Bolt EUV variant), it stands out for its highly accommodating compact MPV format. Its history is inseparable from a massive and historic recall concerning its LG Chem batteries (fire risk), which forced General Motors to replace the battery packs of almost all 2017-2022 models. Paradoxically, this early defect makes it one of the most recommendable used electric cars today: many examples benefit from a new battery under warranty. Post-recall, it is ranked among the most reliable electric vehicles in its segment by Consumer Reports and J.D. Power.

Strengths 6

  • Excellent real-world range (often exceeding 380 km in mixed conditions)
  • Exceptional reliability of the electric motor and power electronics
  • Negligible maintenance costs (no oil, no belts, minimal brake wear thanks to regeneration)
  • Unbeatable price/range ratio on the used market
  • Surprising roominess for a 4.16-meter vehicle
  • Possibility of acquiring an older model with a new battery (8-year/160,000 km warranty reset to zero following the recall)

Weaknesses 5

  • Obsolete and very slow fast charging (DC) speed (limited to 55 kW maximum), making long highway trips tedious
  • Very thin and uncomfortable front seats on pre-facelift models (2017-2021)
  • Front axle traction easily overwhelmed by the immediate torque of the electric motor
  • Basic interior plastic quality, prone to scratching
  • Slightly harsh suspension at low speeds

Check before buying

  • High-voltage battery recall status (MANDATORY VIN CHECK via GM/NHTSA)
  • Pronounced front tire wear (due to the high torque of 360 Nm on the front axle alone)
  • Proper operation of the infotainment system (some black screen bugs reported, often resolved by an update)
  • Verification of the application of recalls concerning seatbelt pretensioners (risk of carpet fire)

Official recalls

The Chevrolet Bolt EV has been the subject of several major recalls, documented by the NHTSA:

  • High-Voltage Battery (November 2020, July/August 2021) - Recalls 20V701, 21V560, 21V650: Massive recall of all 2017-2022 models. Fire risk from the LG Chem battery when fully charged. Solution: Replacement of defective battery modules.
  • Battery diagnostic software (June/October 2024) - Recalls 24V481, 24V812: Possible failure of the installation of the advanced diagnostic software (intended to monitor the new battery) on 2020-2022 models. Requires reprogramming at the dealership.
  • Seatbelt pretensioners (December 2022, December 2023) - Recalls 22V930, 23V845: Upon deployment of the pretensioners in the event of an accident, exhaust gases can ignite the carpet fibers near the B-pillar. Affects 2017-2023 models. Solution: Installation of protective metal foil tape.
  • Airbags (July 2021, August 2023) - Recalls 21V517, 23V567: Defective attachment of the driver airbag (2022) or lack of perforation on the dashboard preventing proper deployment of the passenger airbag (2022-2023).
  • Brake calipers (August 2018, December 2020) - Recalls 18V576, 20V808: Risk of fracture of the front left brake caliper during high-pressure braking.
  • Door handles (March 2020) - Recall 20V184: Rear door handle cable too long, which can come into contact with the window and get damaged, preventing the door from opening (2019-2020 models).

What sets it apart 11

Performance

  • Real-world range above 380 km — Mixed-condition driving often returns more than 380 km per charge, so daily commutes and regional trips need fewer charging stops than many rivals.
  • Brisk acceleration from electric torque — The electric motor delivers immediate pull, reaching 0-100 km/h in around 7 seconds, which makes merging and overtaking feel responsive in daily driving.
  • Higher-capacity later battery option — The 65/66 kWh versions deliver over 400 km of real range, giving buyers of 2020-2023 cars more flexibility between charging sessions.

Reliability

  • Fresh battery under renewed warranty — Many recalled examples received a new battery pack with an 8-year/160,000 km warranty restarted from zero, meaning the costliest component is often new and covered.
  • Proven electric motor reliability — The motor and power electronics are described as exceptionally reliable with no major defects, reducing the risk of costly unexpected repairs for the buyer.
  • Recognised post-recall reliability — After the battery recall, Consumer Reports and J.D. Power rank it among the most reliable EVs in its segment, supporting confident used purchase decisions.

Economy

  • Very low running maintenance costs — No engine oil, no timing belt and minimal brake wear thanks to regeneration keep routine servicing cheap and simple compared with combustion cars.
  • Strong price-to-range ratio used — On the used market the Bolt EV offers an unmatched price-to-range balance, letting buyers access long electric range for a modest outlay.
  • Regeneration reduces brake wear — Energy recuperation under deceleration means the friction brakes are used less, lowering replacement costs and extending service intervals over the vehicle's life.

Practicality

  • Surprising room in compact body — Its compact monospace layout packs unexpected passenger and cargo space into a 4.16-metre footprint, easing parking without sacrificing everyday practicality.
  • Improved AC charging from 2022 — Later models raise AC charging to 11 kW, cutting home and public destination charging times for buyers who pick a 2022 or newer example.

Alternatives

  • North America: Nissan Leaf (less range, non-liquid-cooled battery), Hyundai Kona Electric / Kia Niro EV (excellent alternatives, slightly faster DC charging, higher quality interior), Tesla Model 3 Standard Range (more expensive, but Supercharger network and very fast charging).
  • Europe (against the Opel Ampera-e): Renault Zoé ZE50 (smaller, 22 kW AC charging), Peugeot e-208 (lower range, 100 kW DC charging), Hyundai Kona Electric 64 kWh (the most direct and formidable competitor).

Verdict

The Chevrolet Bolt EV is a textbook case in the automotive industry. Its major design flaw (the LG Chem battery) was addressed by General Motors via a recall of unprecedented scale, consisting of purely and simply replacing the batteries. Result: buying a used Bolt EV today often means acquiring a vehicle whose most expensive and sensitive component is new and under warranty. With the reliability of the electric motor and power electronics being otherwise flawless, the Bolt EV is a highly recommended purchase for daily or suburban use. Its only real crippling flaw is its fast charging (DC) speed capped at 55 kW, which disqualifies it for heavy highway drivers. Absolute condition of purchase: verify via the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) that the battery recall has indeed been performed.
