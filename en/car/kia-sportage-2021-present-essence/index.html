Kia Sportage

V (NQ5) · 2021 – Présent

BUY Reliability 86/100 — excellent

Summary

The fifth-generation Kia Sportage (code name NQ5) marks a radical departure from its predecessors. Adopting the 'Opposites United' design language, it stands out with a bold front end featuring boomerang-shaped daytime running lights. Unprecedentedly, Kia offers two wheelbases: a short-wheelbase (SWB) version specifically calibrated for Europe, and a long-wheelbase (LWB) version for North America, South Korea, and Australia. The interior takes a spectacular leap forward in terms of perceived quality and technology, integrating a curved dual display. Mechanically, this generation emphasizes electrification (MHEV, HEV, PHEV) around the 1.6 T-GDI gasoline engine, while retaining classic internal combustion engines (gasoline and diesel) depending on the market. It is a well-rounded, comfortable, and reassuring family SUV, backed by a 7-year (Europe) or 10-year (USA) manufacturer's warranty.

Strengths 5

  • Bold design and very modern interior presentation (curved dual screen).
  • Excellent spaciousness, even on the European short-wheelbase (SWB) version.
  • Very reassuring manufacturer's warranty (7 years / 150,000 km in Europe).
  • Smooth and efficient transition on hybrid powertrains (HEV/PHEV).
  • Refined sound insulation and significantly improved suspension comfort.

Weaknesses 5

  • Sensitivity of the gasoline DPF (GPF) to clogging in hybrid versions during urban use.
  • Significant weight of PHEV and HEV versions, impacting agility and tire wear.
  • Ergonomics of the dual-function touch bar (climate control/multimedia) is not very intuitive while driving.
  • Some persistent hard plastics in the lower part of the cabin.
  • Limited 3/4 rear visibility due to the thick C-pillar.

Check before buying

  • History of software updates (infotainment and engine/battery management).
  • Presence of exhaust warning lights (GPF/DPF) during the test drive, particularly on HEV/PHEV models.
  • Smooth operation of the dual-clutch gearbox (DCT7) when cold and at low speeds (no juddering).
  • Wear condition of the front tires, especially on heavy and torquey hybrid versions.
  • Body panel alignment and wind noise around the front doors at high speed.

Verdict

The Kia Sportage V (NQ5) is an extremely rational and reassuring choice in the compact SUV market. Its technological upgrade and assertive design make it a very attractive vehicle. In terms of reliability, the gasoline engines (especially the electrified 1.6 T-GDI family) prove robust, although the emission control system (gasoline DPF) requires regular trips outside city centers to prevent clogging. The HEV version (230 hp) represents the best overall compromise. The purchase is highly recommended, especially since the long manufacturer's warranty covers any potential teething issues of this recent generation.
