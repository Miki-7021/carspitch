Vauxhall Corsa

D · 2006 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 64/100 — moyen

All Vauxhall models

Summary

The Vauxhall Corsa D (marketed under the Opel brand in continental Europe) marks a major break from the previous generation. Based on the SCCS platform co-developed by General Motors and Fiat (shared with the Fiat Grande Punto), it offers a significantly increased size (nearly 4 meters) and modernized road performance. It underwent two notable evolutions: a technical update in 2010 (optimization of chassis connections, steering, and compliance with Euro 5 standards) and an aesthetic facelift in 2011 (new front end). Although very popular and affordable, its reliability is mixed, particularly for entry-level petrol engines and certain gearboxes. Overall reliability score: Average.

Strengths 5

  • Design that has aged well, especially after the 2011 facelift
  • Generous interior space for the category (SCCS platform)
  • Very affordable used purchase cost
  • Abundant and inexpensive spare parts
  • Safe and sound road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 6-speed manual gearbox (M32) on 1.6T and 1.7 CDTI models
  • Fragile timing chains on small petrol engines (1.0, 1.2, 1.4)
  • Easytronic automated manual gearbox is slow, jerky, and prone to actuator failures
  • Frequent water ingress (front floor, boot, rear lights)
  • Quality of some interior plastics and rapid wear of the steering wheel/gear knob

Check before buying

  • Rattling/metallic clicking noise on cold start (sign of a stretched timing chain on petrol and 1.3 CDTI).
  • Oil pressure sensor on 1.2 and 1.4 petrol engines: disconnect the plug and check for the absence of oil inside.
  • Gear lever movement during acceleration/deceleration (symptom of worn M32 gearbox bearings).
  • Heating/ventilation operation at speeds 1, 2, and 3 (the resistor often burns out, leaving only speed 4).
  • Dampness under the front floor mats (defective water box/brake booster seals) and in the spare wheel well.
  • Spongy brake pedal or long travel (known ABS unit problem on 2006-2008 models).

Official recalls

Official Manufacturer Recalls

  • 2007 (Braking): Risk of handbrake cable failure, potentially detaching from its guide.
  • 2008 (Braking): Critical recall concerning the ABS module. A sealing defect can lead to water ingress into the hydraulic unit, causing a risk of fire or loss of braking.
  • 2010 (Steering): Replacement of the intermediate shaft of the steering column (risk of yoke fracture).
  • 2011 (Electrical): Risk of short circuit on the starter cable, which may rub against other components.
  • 2016 (Bonnet): The secondary bonnet latch can seize (corrosion), risking opening while driving.

Alternatives

Competing Alternatives

  • Europe: Ford Fiesta Mk7 (better chassis, reliable petrol engines except first-generation 1.0 EcoBoost), Volkswagen Polo V (superior finish, but chain issues on TSIs), Renault Clio III / IV (very comfortable, robust TCe petrol engines on Clio III), Peugeot 207 / 208.
  • Japan/Asia: Toyota Yaris II / III (significantly superior reliability, hybrid available), Honda Jazz (record spaciousness, impeccable reliability), Suzuki Swift.
  • North America: Not marketed in this form (Chevrolet Sonic/Aveo as a GM alternative).

Verdict

The Vauxhall/Opel Corsa D is an attractive city car on paper thanks to its low used price and modern look. However, it requires great vigilance when purchasing. Entry-level petrol engines (1.0, 1.2, 1.4) suffer from chronic weaknesses in the timing chain and leaking sensors that can destroy the ECU. Sporty versions (VXR/SRi) are plagued by the fragility of the M32 gearbox and pistons.

Buying advice: Prioritize a 1.4i petrol model (100 hp) from after 2011 (facelifted), with a clear service history (strict annual oil changes) and a 5-speed manual gearbox. Absolutely avoid the Easytronic automated manual gearbox and models exhibiting metallic noises on startup. A thorough inspection is mandatory.
