Summary

The first-generation MG HS (phase 2 / facelift) has established itself as a major player in the compact SUV (C-SUV) segment thanks to highly aggressive pricing. Designed by the Chinese giant SAIC Motor, it benefits from internal combustion engines developed in partnership with General Motors (SGE and NLE families). The 2021 facelift (which appeared later in some European markets) gave it a more aggressive front end and a technological update. Although the plug-in hybrid version (EHS) attracts the bulk of sales in Western Europe for tax reasons, the 1.5T petrol version (and 2.0T in other markets) remains the core of the global range. It is a rational vehicle, offering excellent interior space and a reassuring warranty (7 years in Europe), but it falls short with sluggish road handling and an unresponsive multimedia system.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable price-to-equipment ratio in the segment
  • Generous rear passenger space and seat comfort
  • 7-year / 150,000 km manufacturer warranty (depending on markets)
  • Neat interior finish (soft-touch materials, good quality synthetic leather)
  • GM-sourced 1.5T engine is generally very mechanically robust

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption on 100% internal combustion versions (1.5T and 2.0T)
  • Slow and sometimes jerky 7-speed dual-clutch automatic gearbox (DCT7) in the city
  • Slow, complex, and bug-prone infotainment system
  • Road handling focused solely on comfort (significant body roll, vague steering)
  • Dealer network still under development in some regions

Check before buying

  • DCT7 gearbox behavior during a cold test drive (look for excessive jerking or slipping)
  • Touchscreen responsiveness and Apple CarPlay / Android Auto functionality
  • Front tire wear (the front axle sometimes struggles to handle the torque, especially on the PHEV)
  • Body panel alignment (variable quality control from the factory)
  • Software update history (crucial on this model)

Alternatives

  • Europe: Dacia Duster (for budget), Hyundai Tucson / Kia Sportage (for warranty and reliability), Peugeot 3008 (for driving dynamics).
  • Asia / Oceania: Haval H6 (direct Chinese competitor), Chery Tiggo 7 Pro, Toyota RAV4.
  • Latin America / Emerging markets: Ford Territory (also of Chinese origin), Jeep Compass.

Verdict

The facelifted MG HS is the quintessential pragmatic choice. If you are looking for a spacious, well-equipped family SUV with a long warranty without breaking the bank, it perfectly fulfills its brief. The 1.5T petrol engine is mechanically reliable (General Motors base) but suffers from higher-than-average fuel consumption for the category and an automatic gearbox that leaves room for improvement. Purchase is recommended, provided you accept somewhat dated onboard technology (multimedia) and favor a smooth driving style. Negotiate firmly on used models, as depreciation is steeper than on established European or Japanese brands.
