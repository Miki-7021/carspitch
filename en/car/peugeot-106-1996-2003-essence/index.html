Summary

The Peugeot 106 Phase 2, launched in 1996, is a major evolution of the lion-badged city car that appeared in 1991. It now shares its platform and engines with its cousin, the Citroën Saxo. More rounded, better finished, and slightly safer than Phase 1, it retains what made it successful: a featherlight weight (often under a ton), formidable agility, and simple, proven mechanics. While the sporty versions (S16, Rallye) are highly sought-after 'youngtimers' today, the classic versions (1.1i, 1.4i) remain excellent economical daily drivers. Be aware, however: its old design makes it vulnerable to current passive safety standards (crash tests).

Strengths 6

  • Negligible maintenance and insurance costs
  • Excellent overall mechanical reliability (TU and TUD engines)
  • Exceptional road handling (agility, precise front axle)
  • Mechanical accessibility (ideal for learning mechanics)
  • Abundant and very cheap spare parts
  • Strong 'Youngtimer' potential for Sport/Rallye/S16 versions

Weaknesses 6

  • Obsolete passive safety (fragile structure in case of a crash)
  • Chronic rear axle issue (trailing arm bearings)
  • Fragile cylinder head gasket on TU petrol engines
  • Almost non-existent sound insulation on the highway
  • Interior plastic quality and seat wear
  • Very sparse standard equipment on lower trims

Check before buying

  • **The rear axle:** Look at the car from behind. If the rear wheels have negative camber ('Gordini' effect /-\) or rub in the wheel arches, the rear axle is dead (cost: 400 to 600€ for a standard exchange).
  • **The cylinder head gasket (Petrol):** Check for oil at the front right of the engine, just above the alternator. A slight seepage is normal; a major leak requires replacement.
  • **Corrosion:** Inspect the rear wheel arches, boot floor, chassis rails, and sills (especially under the plastic extensions of sporty versions).
  • **Power steering:** On Phase 2, the power steering pump is electric. Listen for abnormal whistling or if the steering has stiff spots.
  • **Timing belt history:** TU/TUD engines are not 'freewheeling' (a broken belt destroys the valves). Demand recent invoices.

Official recalls

  • - **1997:** Recall concerning a possible defect on the front seat belt pretensioners. - **2000:** Inspection and modification of the airbag harness under the front seats (risk of inadvertent deployment or warning light due to connectors). - **2001:** On diesel engines (1.5D), recall to check the vacuum pump valve for brake assistance (risk of loss of assistance).

Verdict

The Peugeot 106 Phase 2 is the archetype of the 90s city car: light, communicative, and mechanically indestructible, with the exception of its two chronic ailments (cylinder head gasket on TU engines and the rear axle). With the 1.1i or 1.4i petrol engine, it's an excellent choice for a new driver, a student, or as an economical second car. The S16 and Rallye versions are pure collectors' items offering driving sensations that have disappeared today. The purchase is highly recommended, provided the rear axle is rigorously inspected and keeping in mind that its crash safety no longer meets current standards.
