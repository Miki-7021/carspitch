Summary

The second-generation Hyundai Santa Fe (CM) marks a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer. Gone is the rustic design of the first iteration; this generation adopts more fluid lines, a significantly increased size, and a distinctly more family-oriented and prestigious vocation (available in 5 or 7 seats).

Launched in 2006, it benefited from a significant facelift in late 2009 / early 2010, which not only modernized its aesthetics and interior but above all introduced a brand new generation of diesel engines ('R' series) advantageously replacing the old blocks.

On the European market, it established itself as a formidable alternative to premium SUVs thanks to its unbeatable price/equipment ratio. Although demand is mainly for diesel, the Santa Fe had a global career with a wide variety of gasoline engines, particularly in North America and Asia.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent spaciousness and generous trunk volume (up to 7 seats)
  • Remarkable reliability and performance of the 2.2 CRDi 197 hp engine (post-2010)
  • Very competitive price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Suspension comfort geared towards a family 'cruiser'
  • Good towing capacity (especially with 2.2 CRDi 197 and V6)

Weaknesses 5

  • Variable reliability of early 2.2 CRDi 150/155 hp diesels (2006-2009)
  • Fragile 4x4 (AWD) system: wear of transfer case splines rendering the vehicle 2-wheel drive
  • Quality of some interior plastics that age poorly (peeling paint)
  • Clumsy road handling, significant body roll
  • Frequent engine failure on the 2.4 gasoline (North American market)

Check before buying

  • AWD system operation: Test the vehicle on a slippery surface or gravel. If the front wheels slip without the rear engaging, the transfer case splines are likely stripped (very costly repair).
  • DPF (Diesel) service history: Check for absence of engine warning light and invoices for cleaning/replacement if the vehicle has been driven extensively in the city.
  • Flywheel and clutch (Manual gearbox): Listen for knocking at idle and check for absence of vibrations during clutch slip (especially on 2006-2009 models).
  • Alternator (V6 3.3L): Inspect for oil presence on the alternator (valve cover gasket leak).
  • Running gear: Wear of steering ball joints, front wishbone bushings, and anti-roll bar links (heavy vehicle).

Official recalls

  • ABS Module (2013 / 2018 / 2022): Risk of short circuit in the ABS module potentially leading to an engine compartment fire, even with the ignition off. Critical recall.
  • Brake Light Switch (2009 / 2013): Switch failure preventing the brake lights from illuminating and locking the automatic gear lever in 'P' position.
  • Front Suspension Springs (2015): In cold regions (road salt), risk of corrosion and breakage of the front coil springs, potentially puncturing the tire.
  • Theta II 2.4L Engine (North America): Massive recalls for premature connecting rod bearing wear leading to engine stalling or fire.
  • Airbag Clock Spring (2012): Breakage of the clock spring in the steering wheel, disabling the driver's airbag (warning light on).

Verdict

The Hyundai Santa Fe II (CM) is a family SUV with two faces. Phase 1 models (2006-2009) equipped with the 2.2 CRDi 155 hp engine should be approached with caution due to peripheral weaknesses (flywheel, turbo, EGR) and require an impeccable service history.

On the other hand, Phase 2 (2010-2012) equipped with the 2.2 CRDi 197 hp (R engine) is an excellent deal. This engine transforms the vehicle, offering high-level reliability (chain timing) and first-class performance.

Regardless of the model, the black spot remains the all-wheel drive (AWD) system whose splines wear prematurely. If you don't need 4x4, opt for a 2-wheel drive (2WD) version, which is cheaper to buy and maintain. Avoid the 2.4 gasoline versions imported from North America.
