MINI Clubman

I (R55) · 2007 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 50/100 — faible

Summary

The first-generation MINI Clubman (code name R55) is the estate version of the famous city car (R56). It stands out with its atypical architecture: an antagonistic side door on the passenger side (the *Clubdoor*) to facilitate access to the rear seats, and hinged rear doors (*Splitdoors*) replacing the classic tailgate. With a wheelbase extended by 8 cm compared to the hatchback, it offers better interior space and a more accommodating trunk (260 to 930 liters).

Mechanically, the R55 went through two distinct phases, separated by the late 2010 facelift (LCI). This transition is crucial for reliability, particularly for diesel engines (the main subject of this report): it moved from PSA-sourced blocks (DV6/W16) to BMW-sourced blocks (N47). Although very endearing with its go-kart handling and inimitable style, the Clubman R55 requires extreme vigilance when purchasing due to major design flaws in almost all of its engines before 2011.

Strengths 5

  • Unique design and undeniable appeal
  • Exceptional road handling (go-kart feel)
  • Trunk volume and rear passenger space superior to the R56 hatchback
  • Rear doors (Splitdoors) practical in confined spaces
  • Very low fuel consumption for diesel engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous overall reliability on pre-2011 models (both petrol and diesel)
  • The side door (Clubdoor) is always on the right (roadside in the UK, but curbside in Europe, which is good, but limits access on the left side)
  • Very firm ride comfort, especially with 17-inch wheels and Runflat tires
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway
  • Maintenance and repair costs worthy of the premium segment

Check before buying

  • Diesel W16 (109hp): Remove the engine cover and inspect the base of the injectors (presence of black soot/tar = leaking seals = danger to the turbo).
  • Diesel N47 (90/112/143hp): Listen carefully to the engine at idle, when cold, and around 1500 rpm. A cicada-like noise or metallic rubbing indicates a loose timing chain.
  • Petrol (all): Check the oil level when cold. A dipstick at minimum indicates a negligent owner for an engine that naturally consumes oil.
  • Electronics: Test the proper functioning of all lights, power windows, and turn signals (the FRM module managing the cabin is prone to failures).
  • Running gear: Irregular tire wear (sensitive geometry) and clunking over speed bumps (anti-roll bar bushings).

Verdict

The MINI Clubman R55 is a beloved vehicle, but it can quickly turn into a financial black hole. If you're targeting a **diesel**, caution is advised. The 1.6 109hp (W16/PSA) from Phase 1 is to be avoided unless the injector seals and turbo have been recently replaced with invoices. Phase 2 diesels (BMW-sourced N47) offer a much better driving experience, but the sword of Damocles of the timing chain means you must buy a model where the chain has already been replaced, or budget for this intervention (€1,500 to €2,500). Generally, favor a facelifted model (post-2011), demand a clear service history, and avoid vehicles that have had long oil change intervals (more than 15,000 km).
