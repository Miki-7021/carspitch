Summary

The fifth-generation Mercedes-Benz E-Class (W213 for the sedan, S213 for the wagon, C238/A238 for coupe/cabriolet) marks a major technological turning point for the star brand. Launched in 2016 and facelifted in 2020 (Phase 2), it introduced the famous 'Widescreen Cockpit' and advanced semi-autonomous driving aids. Under the hood, it debuted the brand new family of modular aluminum diesel engines (OM654 as a 4-cylinder, then OM656 as an inline 6-cylinder), replacing the aging OM651 and OM642. Although the market is shifting towards hybrid and petrol, the E-Class W213 remains an absolute benchmark for high-mileage drivers thanks to its diesel engines (notably the E 220 d) offering an exceptional performance/consumption ratio. Manufacturing quality is up compared to the W212, although some electronic and mechanical teething problems (on the first OM654s) marred the early years of production.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional fuel consumption of OM654 and OM656 diesel engines
  • Superb ride comfort (especially with Airmatic air suspension)
  • High-quality interior finish and sound insulation
  • Highly advanced onboard technology and driving aids (Drive Pilot)
  • Overall reliability significantly improved from the 2020 facelift (Phase 2)

Weaknesses 5

  • Costly early issues on OM654 diesels (2016-2018): rocker arms, water pump
  • PHEV battery integration (E300e / E300de) severely reduces trunk volume
  • Frequent electronic bugs on early models (black screens, GPS, ambient lighting)
  • Very high maintenance and spare parts costs in the official network
  • Airmatic air suspension prone to leaks (air springs/compressor) after 120,000 km

Check before buying

  • On pre-2019 OM654 (E220d): Listen carefully to the top end of the engine when cold (clatter = worn rocker arms/camshafts).
  • On OM654: Check for absence of coolant in the vacuum hoses (tandem pump leak).
  • Electronics: Test all Widescreen functions, ambient lighting, and 360° cameras.
  • Airmatic suspension (if equipped): Ensure the car raises and lowers without suspicious compressor noise and does not sag after a night of parking.
  • 9G-Tronic gearbox: Check for absence of jerks during downshifts at low speed (3-2-1).

Official recalls

  • - **Coolant pump (2022)**: Risk of coolant leakage into the vacuum circuit on OM654 and OM656 diesel engines, potentially leading to a fire risk (deactivation of a switching valve). Concerns models produced between 2017 and 2021. - **Steering (2021)**: Steering gear wiring harness incorrectly routed, which can wear out and cause loss of assistance. - **12V Battery (2020)**: Potentially loose fastening of the 12V battery's ground terminal in the trunk, fire risk. - **Roof spoiler (2019)**: On Wagon versions (S213), the rear spoiler may detach while driving. - **eCall system (2021)**: Software update because the emergency call system could transmit an incorrect vehicle position.

Verdict

The Mercedes E-Class W213 is an exceptional road car, particularly brilliant in its diesel versions. The OM654 engine (E 220 d) offers an ideal compromise, but **it is imperative to favor models produced after late 2018** to avoid the serious rocker arm and water pump issues of the early series. The 6-cylinder diesel (OM656) is a masterpiece of reliability and driving pleasure. The plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions are fiscally attractive but compromise trunk space too much. For a used car, a clear service history within the Mercedes network is non-negotiable given the electronic and mechanical complexity.
