Summary

The third-generation Audi TT, known by its internal code 8S, was launched in 2014 and continued Audi's tradition of a compact, elegant, and sporty coupé. Based on the Volkswagen Group's MQB platform, it shares its foundations with models like the Golf 7 and A3 8V, giving it a solid and proven technical base.

Aesthetically, the 8S refined the lines of its predecessors, adopting a more angular and aggressive look, notably with its wider Singleframe grille and LED headlights (or optional Matrix LED). The interior marked a revolution with the introduction of the Virtual Cockpit, a fully digital dashboard that centralized information and infotainment display, eliminating the central screen and drastically simplifying the dashboard.

A minor facelift occurred in 2018, bringing minor aesthetic tweaks (bumpers, wheels) and engine updates, notably the introduction of the gasoline particulate filter (GPF) on some TFSI blocks. Production ceased at the end of 2023, marking the end of an era for this iconic coupé.

Overall, the Audi TT 8S is a well-built vehicle, offering an engaging driving experience and a high-quality interior. Its reliability is generally good, especially with well-maintained TFSI gasoline engines and the robust 2.0 TDI diesel engine.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for sporty versions)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (rapid wear on TTS/TTRS)
  • Virtual Cockpit functionality (no dead pixels, fluidity)
  • Absence of water pump leaks (TFSI engines)
  • Condition of DPF and EGR valve (TDI engine, check regenerations)
  • Abnormal timing chain noises (less frequent than Gen2, but listen for on TFSI)
  • S tronic gearbox operation (smooth shifts, no jerks)
  • Condition of wheels (sensitive to impacts on sporty versions)

Verdict

The Audi TT 8S is an excellent choice for anyone looking for a sporty, elegant, and well-finished coupé. Its general reliability is very good, especially with the third-generation TFSI gasoline engines (EA888 Gen3/3B) which have corrected most of the flaws of their predecessors. The 2.0 TDI (EA288) diesel engine is also a wise choice for high-mileage drivers, offering record fuel consumption for such a vehicle, provided that the specific maintenance requirements for the DPF and AdBlue are respected.

**Buying Tips:** * **For the 2.0 TDI:** Ideal for long journeys and highway driving. Ensure the vehicle has been regularly used for long distances to prevent DPF and EGR clogging. A rigorous service history is crucial. * **For TFSI:** The 2.0 TFSI versions offer the best compromise. Check the decarbonization history if mileage is high. TTS and TTRS versions are performance beasts, but their maintenance and insurance costs are proportional. * **Regardless of the engine:** Prioritize a model with a complete and clear service history, ideally at an Audi dealership or a recognized specialist. Check the condition of consumables (tires, brakes) and the proper functioning of the Virtual Cockpit. The S tronic gearbox is generally reliable but requires regular fluid changes (every 60,000 km).

In summary, the Audi TT 8S is a recommended purchase, offering definite driving pleasure and reassuring reliability, provided it has been well-maintained. Negotiate the price if the history is incomplete or if major maintenance points are anticipated.
