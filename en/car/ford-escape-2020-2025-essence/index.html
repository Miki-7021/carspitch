Ford Escape

IV (CX430) · 2020 – 2025

All Ford models

Summary

The fourth-generation Ford Escape (codename CX430), sold as the Ford Kuga in Europe, marks a stylistic departure from its predecessor by adopting softer, more urban lines. Based on the C2 platform, it focuses on electrification with the introduction of hybrid (HEV) and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) powertrains. A mid-cycle facelift occurred in late 2022 (2023 models). Despite its dynamic qualities and roominess, the reliability record of this generation is particularly bleak. According to ADAC and J.D. Power, the Escape/Kuga IV ranks among the least reliable vehicles in its segment, weighed down by an astronomical number of major safety recalls (NHTSA and Rappel Conso) affecting the engines, high-voltage batteries, and electronics.

Strengths 4

  • Generous roominess and sliding rear bench seat
  • Excellent energy efficiency of the hybrid versions (when they work)
  • Dynamic road handling, typical of Ford
  • SYNC 4 infotainment system (on 2023+ facelifted versions)

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic overall reliability (ranked below average by Consumer Reports, ADAC, and J.D. Power)
  • Industrial quantity of safety recalls (fire risks, loss of power, brakes)
  • 1.5L EcoBoost engine 2020-2022 to be avoided (wet belt, injectors)
  • Recurring electronic bugs (black screens, inverted rearview camera)
  • Interior finish and plastic quality lagging behind the competition

Check before buying

  • Recall history: This is the crucial point. Never buy without a certificate of conformity from Ford proving that all NHTSA/Rappel Conso recalls have been done.
  • 1.5L Engine: Check for the absence of fuel leaks (smell) and the history of the timing system/oil pump.
  • Hybrid System (PHEV/HEV): Check the operation of the transmission (no jerking or shifting to neutral) and the absence of battery-related error messages.
  • On-board electronics: Test the center screen, the digital dashboard (which may refuse to turn on), and the rearview camera.

Official recalls

The Ford Escape/Kuga IV holds a sad record when it comes to recalls. Here are the main ones (sources NHTSA and Rappel Conso):

  • Fire Risk / 1.5L Engine (NHTSA 25V467, 25V165, 24V187, 24V634): On 2020-2024 models, fuel injectors can crack or leak, leading to a fire risk under the hood.
  • Fire Risk / PHEV Battery (Rappel Conso France 2021-05-04 & NHTSA 24V954): An internal or charging defect can cause hot venting of the high-voltage battery or an internal short circuit.
  • HEV/PHEV Engine Failure (NHTSA 25V345): On 2020-2023 models, potential failure of the 2.5L engine leading to oil and flammable vapor leaks.
  • Loss of Drive Power (NHTSA 25V133, 24V330): The hybrid powertrain control module (HPCM) software may force the vehicle to shift into neutral while driving (2020-2022).
  • Cylinder Head Oil Leak (NHTSA 25V372): On 1.5L and 2.0L (2023-2025), cylinder head manufacturing defect (cup plugs).
  • Electronics / Display (NHTSA 25V049, 23V506, 26V123): The instrument panel may fail to illuminate on startup. The rearview camera may display an inverted image.
  • Active & Passive Safety: Wiper motor failure (26V117), brake assist ECU overheating (25V787), improperly secured seat belts (25V862), misaligned adaptive cruise control radar (24V440).

Verdict

The 4th generation Ford Escape / Kuga (2020-2025) is a textbook case of a botched launch in terms of reliability. Data from ADAC, TÜV, J.D. Power, and the endless list of NHTSA recalls paint an alarming picture. The 2020 to 2022 model years, particularly with the 1.5L EcoBoost engine or the early PHEV versions, are to be ABSOLUTELY AVOIDED on the used market, unless the vehicle benefits from an extended manufacturer warranty and a flawless recall history. The facelifted models (2023+) seem to correct some early defects, but remain under surveillance. If you are looking for a reliable hybrid compact SUV, turn to the Japanese or Korean competition.
