Volkswagen Polo

VI (AW) · 2017 – 2025

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The sixth-generation Volkswagen Polo (Type AW) marks a major turning point for the Wolfsburg city car. Based on the modular MQB-A0 platform, it grew considerably (exceeding 4 meters) to offer interior space worthy of the upper category (Golf). Launched in late 2017, it stands out for its unprecedented technological content for the segment (Active Info Display, advanced driving aids). A mid-career restyling (facelift) occurred in 2021, bringing aesthetic touches (front LED strip, widened rear lights), an infotainment update (MIB3), and an improvement in interior plastics, which were criticized on early versions for their 'hard' and scratch-sensitive appearance. Overall, it is a very mature, safe, and well-balanced car, with generally well-controlled mechanical reliability, particularly on its EA211 family gasoline engines.

Strengths 5

  • Record-breaking spaciousness for the category (rear legroom and 351L trunk)
  • Very sound, safe road handling, close to a compact car
  • Overall reliability of gasoline engines (very mature EA211 family)
  • Ergonomics of controls and technological features (Digital Cockpit)
  • Careful sound insulation on TSI versions

Weaknesses 5

  • Disappointing interior plastic quality on pre-facelift models (2017-2020)
  • DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox still prone to clutch wear in city driving
  • Recurring infotainment system bugs in the early years
  • 1.0 MPI engines undersized for the car's weight
  • Used prices often overpriced compared to competitors

Check before buying

  • Software update history (1.5 TSI engine and central screen)
  • DSG7 gearbox behavior: no jerks or juddering should be felt when shifting from 1st to 2nd gear
  • Coolant level (trace of pink powder around the water pump on 1.5 TSI and 2.0 TSI)
  • Functioning of rear seat belts (check if the recall has been performed on 2018 models)
  • Metallic noises on acceleration on the 1.0 TSI (wastegate rod)

Verdict

The Volkswagen Polo VI (AW) is an extremely rational choice in the used car market. It shines with its versatility, offering 'mini-Golf' performance. If you are looking for a gasoline engine, the **1.0 TSI 95 hp with manual gearbox** represents the best buy: it is reliable, economical, and sufficiently powerful. The 1.0 TSI 110/115 hp is ideal for regular road trips. We advise caution with the DSG7 (DQ200) automatic gearbox if the vehicle has been driven extensively in the city or if it is approaching 100,000 km without a clear service history. Avoid the 1.0 MPI versions, which are too sluggish, and opt for a facelifted model (post-2021) if your budget allows, to benefit from a significantly improved interior finish.
