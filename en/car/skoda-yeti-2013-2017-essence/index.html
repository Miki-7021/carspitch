Skoda Yeti

I facelift (5L) · 2013 – 2017

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The Skoda Yeti I Phase 2 (Type 5L), facelifted in late 2013, lost its original round headlights to adopt a more angular front end, in line with the brand's new style. It was then offered in two versions: an urban version (painted bumpers) and an 'Outdoor' version (black plastic protections, skid plates). This compact SUV stands out for its exceptional spaciousness and modularity inherited from minivans, notably thanks to the independent and removable *VarioFlex* rear seat system. Mechanically, the 2013-2017 period marks a crucial transition: the gradual shift from Euro 5 engines (EA111 generation for petrol, EA189 for diesel) to Euro 6 engines (EA211 for petrol, EA288 for diesel) around 2015, which had a major impact on the model's overall reliability. ADAC and TÜV data confirm that the facelifted Yeti is among the best in its category, provided you choose the right engine and transmission.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional modularity (independent, sliding, and removable VarioFlex rear seats)
  • Very positive overall reliability record according to TÜV (14% defects at 3 years, among the best compact SUVs)
  • Robust build and finish quality, aging very well
  • Excellent peripheral visibility thanks to vertical glazing
  • Efficient 4x4 transmission (5th generation Haldex system)
  • Sound and safe road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.8 TSI engine (oil consumption, piston rings)
  • Capricious and fragile DSG7 automatic gearbox (dry-clutch DQ200) on smaller engines
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind noise due to cubic profile)
  • Firm suspension, especially with 17-inch wheels
  • Average boot volume (416 liters) if rear seats are pushed back to the maximum

Check before buying

  • DSG gearbox service history: the DSG6 (DQ250) requires an oil change every 60,000 km.
  • Haldex coupler oil change (4x4 versions) every 3 years or 60,000 km (often overlooked).
  • Oil level on all TSI engines (particularly 1.4 and 1.8).
  • Smooth operation of the DSG7 gearbox (DQ200) during test drives: no jerking or juddering should be tolerated when shifting from 1st to 2nd gear.
  • History of software updates on EA189 TDI engines (Dieselgate recall campaign) which can lead to EGR issues.
  • VarioFlex seat mechanism: check that they fold and extract without jamming.

Official recalls

  • - **2023-08-07 (Takata Airbag)**: The airbag may deteriorate over time. If deployed, excessive internal pressure can cause the inflator body to rupture and project metallic fragments, risking injury to occupants.

Reliability

  • Proven and Recognized Reliability — The Skoda Yeti is praised for its robustness and overall reliability. With a defect rate of only 14% at 3 years according to the TÜV Report 2024, it ranks among the best compact SUVs, ensuring daily peace of mind.

Safety

  • Maximum Safety Certified by Euro NCAP — Safety is an absolute priority. The Skoda Yeti achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 92% for adult protection and 78% for child protection, ensuring total serenity on board.

Handling

  • Agile and Secure Road Handling — Appreciate agile road handling and dynamic behavior similar to a sedan. Body roll is perfectly controlled, steering is precise, and the car proves stable and predictable, offering a comfortable and secure driving experience.

Braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — The Skoda Yeti ensures efficient and reassuring braking performance. Distances are optimized for its category, and the pedal offers excellent feel, allowing for easy modulation and increased safety in all circumstances.

Comfort

  • Robust Build Quality and Optimal Visibility — Benefit from robust build quality and finish, designed to last and age very well. Peripheral visibility is excellent thanks to its vertical glazing, contributing to superior driving comfort and better environmental awareness.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Volkswagen Tiguan I (technical cousin), Nissan Qashqai II, Peugeot 2008 / 3008 I, Dacia Duster, Suzuki Vitara. - **Japan/Asia**: Honda HR-V / Vezel, Subaru XV. - **Emerging markets**: Hyundai Creta, Renault Kaptur (Russia).

Verdict

The facelifted Skoda Yeti is an extremely endearing, practical, and overall very reliable compact SUV, as evidenced by its excellent scores from ADAC (2.2 breakdowns/1,000 veh/year) and TÜV. However, the choice of engine is absolutely crucial. **We strongly recommend** the 1.2 TSI 110 hp petrol versions (post-2015, EA211 engine) for daily use, or the very robust 2.0 TDI (140 or 150 hp) for high-mileage drivers and all-wheel drive enthusiasts. Conversely, **AVOID** the 1.8 TSI due to its serious piston ring issues (excessive oil consumption), and be extremely vigilant about the condition of the DSG7 automatic gearbox (DQ200) on smaller engines. Prioritize a manual gearbox or the DSG6 (associated with 2.0 TDI and 1.8 TSI 4x4 engines).
