Citroën C3 Aircross II (2024+)

The second generation of the Citroën C3 Aircross marks a complete break with its predecessor. Based on Stellantis' *Smart Car* platform (shared with the new Opel Frontera), the vehicle has grown considerably to reach 4.39 m and now offers a configuration with up to 7 seats, a unique offering in the B-SUV segment.

**⚠️ IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION REGARDING DIESEL:** Although your search targets diesel powertrains for the years 2024-2025, **Citroën has definitively abandoned diesel for this second generation**. Buyers looking for a diesel C3 Aircross registered in 2024 will exclusively find **1st generation (Phase 2) end-of-stock models** equipped with the 1.5 BlueHDi. The new generation (Gen II) relies entirely on new gasoline engines with timing chains, mild-hybridization (MHEV), and 100% electric. This report will cover the Gen II exhaustively, while also including an analysis of the 1.5 BlueHDi for buyers of transitional 2024 models.

Strengths 5

  • Record spaciousness for the segment (up to 7 seats).
  • Typical Citroën suspension comfort (Advanced Comfort).
  • Abandonment of the wet belt on gasoline engines (finally a chain!).
  • Very aggressive pricing against the competition.
  • Assertive cubic design maximizing interior space.

Weaknesses 5

  • Total disappearance of the diesel engine on the new generation.
  • Limited range for the electric version (approx. 300 km WLTP).
  • Hard and basic interior plastics (cost reduction).
  • Infotainment ergonomics sometimes slow.
  • Unknown long-term reliability of the new e-DCS6 gearbox.

Check before buying

  • On hybrid models: smoothness of e-DCS6 gearbox shifts (jerks may require reprogramming).
  • On electric versions: check that all software recall campaigns have been carried out.
  • If purchasing a 2024 Diesel model (Gen 1): check the AdBlue system history and ensure the engine is equipped with the housing with an 8.5 mm chain (reference engraved on the housing).

Alternatives

Verdict

The Citroën C3 Aircross II (2024+) changes dimension and philosophy. **For diesel enthusiasts, it's a disappointment: the powertrain has disappeared from the catalog.** If you absolutely must drive a diesel, you will have to look for a previous generation model registered in early 2024, accepting the risks associated with the 1.5 BlueHDi's AdBlue system.

However, the new generation corrects Stellantis' biggest flaw: the gasoline engines finally adopt a timing chain. The most relevant choice is the **1.2 Hybrid 136 hp**, which offers an excellent power/consumption compromise and makes up for the absence of diesel for most uses. The electric version is best reserved for peri-urban use given its modest battery.
