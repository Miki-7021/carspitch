Summary

The 11th generation Ford F-150 (codename P415) marked a major turning point for the model with an all-new fully boxed frame, a redesigned suspension for better ride comfort, and a significantly higher quality cabin. ATTENTION - EXPERT CLARIFICATION REGARDING DIESEL: Although the request targets a diesel engine, the Ford F-150 (2004-2008) was NEVER offered with a factory diesel engine. The first diesel F-150 (3.0L Power Stroke V6) did not appear until 2018 (13th generation). At that time, buyers requiring a diesel had to turn to the higher *Super Duty* range (F-250/F-350) equipped with the highly problematic 6.0L Power Stroke V8. Consequently, and to maintain factual accuracy, this sheet focuses exhaustively on the gasoline engines actually available on this model, while integrating official reliability data (ADAC, NHTSA).

Strengths 5

  • Extremely robust chassis (fully boxed)
  • Exemplary reliability of the 4.6L V8 engine (highlighted by ADAC)
  • Excellent payload and towing capacities for the time
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing significantly improved compared to the 10th generation
  • Massive availability of spare parts (especially in North America)

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of a diesel offering on this generation (contrary to the expectations of some markets)
  • 5.4L 3V V8 engine plagued by costly design flaws (spark plugs, cam phasers)
  • Very high fuel consumption on all V8s
  • Imposing size making European urban use very difficult
  • Very plasticky interior finish on lower trims (XL, XLT)

Check before buying

  • On the 5.4L V8: Listen carefully to the engine when hot at idle. A knocking sound (diesel-like) indicates an imminent failure of the camshaft adjusters (*cam phasers*).
  • On the 5.4L V8: Demand invoices proving the replacement of the spark plugs with the revised version.
  • Check the condition of the brake booster vacuum hose (subject to an NHTSA recall on 2005-2006 models).
  • Inspect the fuel tank for any deformation or leaks at the welds (several NHTSA recalls).
  • Test the automatic transmission (4R70E/4R75E): look for jerks, slipping, or a delay when shifting into reverse.

Official recalls

This generation has been the subject of numerous safety recalls, mainly in the United States (NHTSA). Here are the most critical: - 2011 (NHTSA 11V107000): Airbags. The clockspring ribbon cable can wear out and cause the inadvertent deployment of the driver's airbag (2004-2006 models). - 2008 (NHTSA 08V208000): Braking. Swelling and loss of retention of the brake booster vacuum supply hose on 2005-2006 models equipped with the 5.4L V8, reducing braking assistance. - 2007 (NHTSA 07V336000): Fire risk. The cruise control deactivation switch can leak, overheat, and catch fire, even with the ignition off. - 2004 & 2007 (NHTSA 04V200000, 04V443000, 07V278000): Fuel tank. Risk of local depression at the tank weld, which can lead to fuel leaks. - 2005 (NHTSA 05V520000): Visibility. Wiper motor assembled without grease on the output shaft gear, which can lead to wiper failure. *(Note: Multiple minor recalls also concern non-compliant aftermarket headlight assemblies).*

Verdict

The 11th generation Ford F-150 is a robust pickup with an indestructible chassis, but its reliability record depends entirely on the chosen engine. The expert is categorical: avoid the 5.4L 3V V8 unless the seller provides recent and invoiced proof of the replacement of the complete timing system (with a high-volume oil pump) and spark plugs. Absolutely favor the 4.6L 2V V8, which is significantly more reliable and validated by ADAC statistics. Finally, for buyers specifically looking for a diesel engine on an F-150 from those years: this configuration simply does not exist. You will either have to opt for an F-250 (beware of the very fragile 6.0L Power Stroke), or turn to a much more recent model (2018+).
