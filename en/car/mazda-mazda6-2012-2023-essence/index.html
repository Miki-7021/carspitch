Summary

The third-generation Mazda 6 (chassis code GJ, then GL after the 2015 facelift) was launched in 2012, marking a stylistic and technological turning point for the brand. Adopting the 'Kodo – Soul of Motion' design language and integrating the suite of Skyactiv technologies (engines, transmissions, chassis), it positions itself as an elegant and dynamic family sedan, offering a serious alternative to German and French benchmarks.

It benefited from two major facelifts: the first in 2015 (GL) with interior improvements, enhanced sound insulation, and the introduction of G-Vectoring Control (GVC); the second in 2018, bringing a more significant front-end redesign, an even more refined interior, and the introduction of the 2.5 Skyactiv-G Turbo engine in certain markets.

Overall, the Mazda 6 GJ/GL is recognized for its driving pleasure, meticulous finish, and timeless design. Its reliability is generally good, particularly for the gasoline engines, which are the strong point of this generation.

Strengths 7

  • Elegant and timeless design (Kodo)
  • Pleasant to drive, balanced and dynamic chassis
  • Quality of finish and interior materials (especially after 2015)
  • Exemplary reliability of Skyactiv-G gasoline engines
  • Smooth and responsive Skyactiv-Drive automatic gearbox
  • Reasonable fuel consumption for naturally aspirated gasoline engines
  • Good spaciousness and large trunk (wagon version)

Weaknesses 6

  • Imperfect sound insulation on early model years (improved after 2015)
  • Problematic 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engine on early model years (timing chain, injector seals)
  • MZD Connect infotainment system sometimes slow or temperamental on early versions
  • Firm suspension for some, especially with large wheels
  • No hybrid or plug-in hybrid version
  • Resale value sometimes lower than German competitors

Check before buying

  • Complete service history: Essential, especially for diesels.
  • 2.2 Skyactiv-D engine (2012-2015): Proof of timing chain and injector seal replacement. Listen for any suspicious noise on startup (ticking/rattling).
  • Engine oil level: Check for absence of dilution (diesel smell) on diesels.
  • DPF (Diesel): Check for absence of warning light, regeneration history.
  • Bodywork: General condition, presence of rust (wheel arches, sills) in saline regions.
  • MZD Connect system: Functioning of the screen, rotary controller, connections (Bluetooth, USB).
  • Suspension: Abnormal noises, wear of shock absorbers and silent blocks.
  • Tires: Regular wear, sign of good alignment.
  • Automatic gearbox: Smooth gear changes, without jerks or slipping.

What sets it apart 7

Reliability

  • Unrivaled Skyactiv-G Petrol Reliability — The Mazda 6's Skyactiv-G petrol engines are renowned for their exemplary robustness and longevity, offering exceptional peace of mind for daily driving.

Safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — The Mazda 6 achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 92% for adult protection and 77% for child protection, confirming its commitment to your safety and that of your passengers.
  • Ultra-Efficient Braking — Enjoy enhanced safety thanks to remarkable braking performance, with very short stopping distances (often between 37 and 39 meters from 100 km/h) and excellent stability, even in emergency situations.

Verdict

The third-generation Mazda 6 is an excellent family sedan, offering a rare blend of style, driving pleasure, and reliability, especially with its Skyactiv-G gasoline engines.

For a confident and recommended purchase: Prioritize gasoline versions (2.0L or 2.5L Skyactiv-G). They are remarkably reliable, economical to maintain, and offer undeniable driving pleasure. The 2.5L Turbo is an excellent choice for those seeking more power without sacrificing reliability.

For a purchase to negotiate or with caution: The 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel models from the early years (2012-2015) should be considered with the utmost caution. It is imperative to verify that recalls concerning the timing chain and injector seals have been carried out and that the service history is impeccable. Post-2016 diesel models are more reliable but still require appropriate use (long journeys) to avoid DPF and clogging issues.

Overall, the Mazda 6 GJ/GL is a wise choice for anyone looking for a reliable, well-finished, and pleasant-to-live-with sedan, provided the engine is chosen carefully and the history is verified.
