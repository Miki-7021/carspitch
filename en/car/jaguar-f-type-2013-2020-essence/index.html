Summary

The first-generation **Jaguar F-TYPE (X152)** (prior to the major facelift in late 2020) is considered the spiritual successor to the legendary Type E. First launched as a convertible in 2013 and then as a coupe in 2014, it sits on an aluminum chassis. It is a sports car that leans towards Grand Touring (GT), renowned for its timeless design by Ian Callum and, above all, for the epic sound of its supercharged V6 and V8 engines. Although its weight is higher than that of its direct competitors (such as the Porsche 911 or the Cayman), it offers a rare raw and emotional character. The mechanical reliability of the V6 and V8 blocks is generally good, provided the cooling system is monitored very closely, as it is the model's true Achilles heel.

**Overall generation rating: 7.5/10** - A passion purchase par excellence, requiring rigorous maintenance.

Strengths 5

  • Spectacular and timeless exterior design
  • Sound of the V6 and V8 engines (active exhaust)
  • Highly responsive 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox
  • Rigid aluminum chassis
  • Assertive character, less clinical than a Porsche

Weaknesses 5

  • High weight for the category (impacts agility)
  • Very fragile cooling system on V6/V8 (plastic parts)
  • Dated and slow infotainment system (especially before 2018)
  • "Soft-touch" interior plastics that become sticky over time
  • Ridiculous trunk volume on the convertible

Check before buying

  • Traces of coolant (sweet smell, white marks) around and under the supercharger (V6/V8).
  • Metallic or rattling noise coming from the top of the engine at idle (supercharger isolator).
  • Oil seepage at the rear axle (differential oil seal).
  • Operation of the retractable rear spoiler and flush door handles.
  • Condition of interior plastics (center console buttons, handles).
  • Clear maintenance history, ideally with frequent oil changes (15,000 km / 1 year max).

Performance

  • An Unforgettable Mechanical Symphony — Be captivated by the inimitable sound of the supercharged V6 and V8 engines, amplified by the active exhaust. Every acceleration is a pure auditory experience, a roar that vibrates the driver's soul.
  • ZF 8-Speed Automatic Gearbox: Responsiveness and Fluidity — The ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox is renowned for its lightning-fast responsiveness and exemplary fluidity. It allows for instantaneous gear changes, optimizing performance while offering superior driving comfort.

Safety

  • Absolute Control with State-of-the-Art Braking — Equipped with highly efficient braking systems, featuring large discs and powerful calipers, the F-TYPE ensures short and controlled stops. This exceptional braking capability guarantees optimal safety, even at high speeds.
  • Dynamic Handling and Remarkable Agility — Enjoy exceptional road holding, characterized by precise and responsive steering, excellent body control, and impressive agility. The F-TYPE offers a perfect balance between performance and maneuverability, for a rewarding driving experience.

Reliability

  • V6 and V8 Engines: Robustness and Lasting Performance — The supercharged V6 and V8 engine blocks of the F-TYPE are renowned for their robust design and their ability to deliver exceptional performance over the long term, ensuring an exhilarating and reliable driving experience.

Alternatives

* **Europe:** Porsche 911 (Type 991) (the absolute benchmark, more rigorous but more expensive), Porsche 718 Cayman/Boxster (more agile, mid-engine), Mercedes-AMG GT (similar architecture, biturbo V8), Aston Martin Vantage (V8, very close positioning). * **North America:** Chevrolet Corvette C7 (unbeatable price/performance ratio, naturally aspirated or supercharged V8). * **Japan/Asia:** Lexus LC 500 (naturally aspirated V8, more GT and luxurious approach, exceptional reliability).

Verdict

The first-generation Jaguar F-Type is a purchase of the heart. It is bought for its sculptural lines and the symphony of its V6 and V8 engines, which are among the most beautiful sounds in modern automotive production. However, it is not a car you buy with your eyes closed. The known and documented problem of plastic cooling pipes on the AJ126 (V6) and AJ133 (V8) engines is a real sword of Damocles. If you are aiming for a V6 or a V8, demand a clear history and set aside a budget to replace these parts with aluminum preventively if it has not been done. The 2.0L 4-cylinder is to be avoided if you are looking for the authentic F-Type experience. In summary: a future classic, but one that requires an informed and meticulous owner.
