Ram 1500

5th gen (DT) · 2019 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The 5th generation Ram 1500 (codename DT) revolutionized the North American *full-size* pickup segment upon its release. It stands out with unprecedented cabin luxury for the category, superior ride comfort thanks to its rear coil spring suspension (or optional air suspension), and cutting-edge onboard technology (12-inch Uconnect screen).

**Focus on the Diesel (3.0L EcoDiesel V6 - Gen 3):** Reintroduced for the 2020 model year, this revised VM Motori engine offers a generous torque of 650 Nm (480 lb-ft) and excellent fuel efficiency for long trips and towing. However, it requires rigorous maintenance and has been affected by critical recalls (injection pump, crankshaft sensor). The Ram 1500 received a major facelift for 2025, marking the discontinuation of the Hemi V8 and EcoDiesel V6 in favor of the new 'Hurricane' twin-turbo inline-6 engines.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional ride comfort (multi-link rear suspension with coil springs or air suspension)
  • Cabin finish quality and materials (Laramie, Limited, Tungsten versions)
  • Highly responsive and intuitive Uconnect infotainment system
  • Fuel efficiency and range of the 3.0L EcoDiesel version
  • Ingenious storage spaces (RamBox, flat load floor in the rear)

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the 3.0L EcoDiesel engine peripherals (HPFP, EGR, DPF)
  • Payload often lower than that of the Ford F-150 and Chevrolet Silverado
  • Recurring exhaust stud issues on the 5.7L Hemi V8
  • High number of electronic and software recalls (cameras, dashboard)
  • Replacement cost of eTorque mild hybrid system components out of warranty

Check before buying

  • Recall history: It is imperative to check if the HPFP (EcoDiesel) has been replaced.
  • Air suspension (Active-Level): Test the different height modes. The system can freeze in winter or develop leaks in the air springs with age.
  • Cold engine noise: On the Hemi V8, a ticking noise (tick) that disappears when warm indicates a cracked exhaust manifold or broken studs. If it persists, it is a lifter issue (more serious).
  • Onboard electronics: Test the rearview camera, the Uconnect screen, and the dashboard (prone to numerous software bugs).
  • Water leak in the cabin: Check for moisture under the rear carpets and around the rear window (faulty third brake light seal).

Alternatives

- **Ford F-150 (Gen 13/14):** The main rival. Offers the 3.0L PowerStroke V6 (diesel, discontinued in 2021) and the excellent 3.5L EcoBoost V6. Better payload, equivalent onboard technology, but slightly inferior ride comfort (leaf springs). - **Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500:** Offers the 3.0L Duramax inline-6 (diesel), which is generally considered more reliable and smoother than the Ram's EcoDiesel. Interior lags behind prior to the 2022 facelift. - **Toyota Tundra (Gen 3 - 2022+):** No diesel, but a very powerful hybrid twin-turbo V6. Toyota reliability reputation (despite some initial recalls on the new engine), but fewer customization options than the Ram.

Verdict

The Ram 1500 (DT) is arguably the most comfortable and luxurious pickup of its generation. If you are targeting the **3.0L EcoDiesel** engine, caution is advised: although fantastic in use (torque, fuel consumption), this engine has suffered from serious issues (HPFP) that require scrupulously checking the recall history. A model whose pump has been replaced with the corrected version and which has done a lot of highway driving is a good buy. If you drive in the city or take short trips, avoid the diesel and opt for the 5.7L Hemi V8 (budgeting for the exhaust manifolds) or the very pragmatic 3.6L Pentastar V6. Also, beware of the numerous electronic bugs that have plagued this model's career.
