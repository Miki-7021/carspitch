Summary

The first-generation Volkswagen T-Roc (Phase 1) is a compact SUV based on the modular MQB A1 platform, shared with the Golf 7 and Audi Q2. Positioned between the T-Cross and the Tiguan, it achieved immense commercial success thanks to its dynamic design and excellent road qualities. While its dynamic behavior and spaciousness are praised, this Phase 1 was sharply criticized for the quality of its interior plastics, deemed too hard and unworthy of the brand's usual standards (a flaw corrected during the late 2021 facelift). Mechanically, it offers a wide range of engines, with a particularly frugal Diesel (TDI) offering suitable for high-mileage drivers, although anti-pollution standards have complicated these blocks (AdBlue, DPF, EGR).

Strengths 5

  • Dynamic and safe road handling (similar to a Golf)
  • Ergonomics of controls and intuitive infotainment system
  • Excellent fuel consumption for TDI engines
  • Generous boot volume (445 liters in 2-wheel drive)
  • Availability of 4Motion all-wheel drive (rare in the segment)

Weaknesses 5

  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and scratch-prone on this Phase 1)
  • Reliability of the dry-clutch DSG7 gearbox (DQ200) in intensive urban use
  • Firm suspension, especially with 18 or 19-inch wheels
  • Capricious AdBlue system on Diesel engines
  • High used car purchase price (strong popularity)

Check before buying

  • Coolant level: a drop indicates a water pump leak (common issue on TDI and 2.0 TSI).
  • DSG gearbox behavior: no jerks or slipping should be felt when shifting gears, especially when cold.
  • Dashboard warning lights (TDI): check for absence of engine light or AdBlue alert (costly repair).
  • Maintenance history: DSG gearbox oil change (if DQ381) and Haldex coupler (if 4Motion) every 60,000 km.
  • Electronics: frequent bugs with the central screen (Discover Media) requiring reboots or updates.

Official recalls

  • November 2018 : Risk of rear spoiler detachment (bonding defect). Concerns models manufactured in 2018.
  • February 2019 : Welding problem on the front seat frame. Concerns a small series from 2018.
  • August 2019 : Electric parking brake defect (unintended release). Software update.
  • December 2020 : Risk of brake pedal pad detachment on automatic transmission models (2020 production).
  • March 2022 : Risk of fire related to the engine cover melting onto the turbocharger (concerns only the 2.0 TSI R).

Alternatives

  • Europe : Peugeot 2008 (more comfortable but problematic PureTech engines), Renault Captur (excellent value for money, E-Tech hybridization), Ford Puma (dynamic, mild hybridization), Audi Q2 (technical cousin, superior finish).
  • Asia / China : Honda XR-V, Toyota C-HR, Nissan Qashqai.
  • North America : The T-Roc is not sold there (Volkswagen offers the Taos instead).

Verdict

The Volkswagen T-Roc Phase 1 is a very well-rounded compact SUV, penalized only by a disappointing interior finish for the price. For Diesel, the 2.0 TDI is an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers: it is torquey, economical, and durable, provided the water pump and AdBlue system are monitored. The 1.6 TDI should be avoided if you drive in the city (guaranteed clogging). For Petrol, the 1.5 TSI 150 hp represents the best overall compromise. Beware of the DSG7 gearbox (DQ200) on smaller engines: opt for a manual gearbox if you do a lot of city driving, or choose the 2.0 versions (TDI or TSI) equipped with the robust wet-clutch DSG7 (DQ381).
