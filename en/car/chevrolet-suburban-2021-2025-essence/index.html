Summary

The 12th generation Chevrolet Suburban (T1XX), launched for the 2021 model year, marks a major evolution with the adoption of an independent rear suspension (IRS). This modification radically transforms the road handling and frees up a colossal amount of space for the third row and the trunk. An extended twin of the Chevrolet Tahoe and cousin to the GMC Yukon XL / Cadillac Escalade ESV, the Suburban is a behemoth designed for large families, towing, and professional fleets (luxury ride-hailing, government). Although its comfort and capabilities are undeniable, its overall reliability is rated below average by organizations like J.D. Power and Consumer Reports, mainly due to complex electronics (Electronic Precision Shift) and mechanical weaknesses on the 5.3L V8 (DFM lifters). However, models used in fleets often benefit from more rigorous maintenance, which improves their actual longevity on the used market.

Strengths 5

  • Gigantic interior space (3rd row finally comfortable for adults thanks to the IRS)
  • Unmatched trunk volume in the category
  • Excellent towing capabilities
  • Ride comfort (especially with the air suspension and Magnetic Ride Control)
  • Surprisingly low fuel consumption with the 3.0L Duramax Diesel engine

Weaknesses 5

  • Capricious electronic reliability (infotainment system, sensors)
  • Push-button gear selector (Electronic Precision Shift) prone to bugs
  • Documented fragility of the DFM system lifters on the V8s
  • Size unsuited for narrow urban environments
  • High maintenance cost (tires, brakes, MRC suspension)

Check before buying

  • Metallic ticking noise from the engine (warning sign of DFM lifter failure on the V8s)
  • Automatic gearbox shifting (jerking, hesitations requiring reprogramming)
  • Operation of the Magnetic Ride Control suspension (shock absorber leaks, sagging)
  • Recall history (very numerous on this model, notably software and seatbelts)
  • Operation of all electronic equipment (screens, 360 cameras, driving aids)

What sets it apart 6

praticity

  • Unrivaled Interior Space and Cargo Volume — The Chevrolet Suburban stands out with its gigantic interior space, offering a spacious and comfortable third row for adults, along with an absolutely unrivaled cargo volume in its category.
  • The Master of Versatile Transport — Ideal for large families or professional fleets, the Suburban is the undisputed king of passenger and cargo transport, offering unparalleled versatility and capacity.

comfort

  • First-Class Ride Comfort — Enjoy an exceptionally comfortable driving experience, notably thanks to the independent rear suspension and advanced options like air suspension and Magnetic Ride Control, for serene journeys.

Performance

  • Impressive Towing Capabilities — Built for power, the Suburban offers excellent towing capabilities, allowing you to effortlessly pull caravans, boats, or heavy trailers with complete confidence.
  • Powerful and Smooth V8 Engines — The EcoTec3 V8 powertrains deliver generous power and remarkable smoothness, ensuring strong acceleration and superior driving pleasure in all circumstances.

handling

  • Refined and Stable Road Handling — The integration of independent rear suspension gives the Suburban significantly more refined road handling, improved stability, and enhanced maneuverability, even with its imposing size.

Alternatives

  • North America / Middle East: Ford Expedition Max (high-performance but complex EcoBoost V6), Jeep Wagoneer L / Grand Wagoneer L (new Hurricane turbo L6 engines), Toyota Sequoia (Hybrid, but less space in the 3rd row and trunk).
  • Europe: Individually imported model. Local alternatives: BMW X7, Mercedes-Benz GLS, Land Rover Defender 130 (although less voluminous).

Verdict

The Chevrolet Suburban T1XX is the undisputed king of space and troop transport. The addition of the independent rear suspension has made it a much more refined vehicle than the previous generation. However, its reliability record is marred by annoying electronic issues and, more seriously, by the fragility of the DFM system on the V8 engines (particularly the 5.3L). Buying a used model requires a scrupulous check of the maintenance history and the very numerous recalls. Paradoxically, a former fleet vehicle (high-end ride-hailing) with highway mileage and strict maintenance can prove to be a better choice than a neglected family vehicle. The 3.0L Duramax Diesel engine is the sensible choice for high-mileage drivers, while the 6.2L V8 offers the best drivability, subject to checking the recent engine recall.
