Summary

The T6.2 generation Ford Ranger (codename P703) marks a major evolution for the best-selling pickup in Europe. Developed in Australia and sharing its platform with the second-generation Volkswagen Amarok, it profoundly modernizes its road performance, on-board technology (vertical SYNC 4 screen), and utility capabilities. The big news is the return of a diesel V6 engine (3.0L from the F-150) which replaces the old 3.2L 5-cylinder, offering superior drivability. The Raptor version abandons diesel for an explosive 3.0L biturbo petrol V6 (although a diesel remains available in some markets). Reliability data (TÜV, ADAC, What Car?) confirm excellent overall robustness of the mechanical base, although the early production years suffered numerous teething recalls (electronics, assembly).

Strengths 6

  • Drivability and torque of the new 3.0L diesel V6 engine
  • Transformed road handling (widened tracks, new damping)
  • Highly responsive and ergonomic SYNC 4 infotainment system
  • Towing capacity maintained at 3,500 kg (except Raptor)
  • Excellent technical inspection results (TÜV: 10.5% defect rate at 3 years, among the best pickups)
  • Overall platform reliability validated by ADAC (2.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year)

Weaknesses 5

  • Imposing size (over 5.35 m) difficult to park in Europe
  • Numerous teething recalls (assembly, electronics, fasteners)
  • Very high fuel consumption on the V6 EcoBoost (Raptor)
  • 10-speed automatic gearbox sometimes hesitant at low speeds
  • Purchase price sharply up compared to the previous generation

Check before buying

  • Recall history: the vehicle has been the subject of numerous campaigns (see Recalls section), verify that they have been carried out.
  • Underbody: check for impacts, rust, or accumulated dirt if the vehicle has been off-roading.
  • Gearbox: ensure there is no jerking when engaging reverse or the first gears (valve body).
  • Electronics: test all functions of the SYNC 4 central screen (frequent reversing camera or display bugs before OTA update).
  • DPF/EGR (Diesels): check that the idle is stable and no engine light is on (sign of short urban trips clogging the system).

Official recalls

The T6.2 Ranger has undergone numerous teething recalls (NHTSA / Rappel Conso). Here are the main ones affecting 2022-2025 models: * Engine / Valves (2025-2026): Risk of failure of a valve assembly leading to a loss of power or engine failure. * Gearbox (March 2025 / 2024): Incorrect machining of the automatic transmission valve body that can cause unintended engagement (NHTSA 25V164000). * Braking (2025): Possible malfunction of the electronic brake booster (EBB) module, increasing braking distance. * Suspension (2024-2025): The front upper suspension arm ball joint nut may be loose or missing, risking detachment. * Driveshaft (Nov 2023): The driveshaft may detach from the transfer case due to faulty installation. * Fuel (2024): High-pressure fuel pump line nuts loose (risk of leak/fire). * Rear differential (Oct 2022): A heavy load can cause rear differential failure on early models. * Airbags / Safety (2024-2025): Recalls concerning curtain airbag deployment, passenger airbag cover, and driver seatbelt routing. * Assembly (2024-2025): Windshield bonding defects, loose front seat mounting bolts, improperly positioned sun visor wiring harness.

Verdict

The Ford Ranger T6.2 is undoubtedly the most accomplished pickup in its class on the European market. ADAC and TÜV statistics confirm that the mechanical fundamentals (EcoBlue engines, chassis) are very solid. However, the impressive number of manufacturer recalls on the 2022-2024 model years testifies to a rushed industrial launch.

Buying advice: Favor the 3.0 V6 EcoBlue diesel, which transforms the driving experience and proves extremely robust. The 170 hp 2.0 EcoBlue remains the rational choice for professionals. It is imperative to ensure that all recalls (especially on the transmission, suspension, and braking) have been carried out at a dealership before purchasing a used model.
