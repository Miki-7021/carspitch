Summary

The fourth-generation Hyundai Santa Fe (internal code TM) was launched in 2018, succeeding the DM model. It positioned itself as the brand's premium family SUV, offering up to seven seats, enhanced comfort, and a rich technological endowment. Its design evolved towards more modernity and robustness, with a distinctive lighting signature.

A significant facelift occurred in late 2020 (sometimes referred to as TM PE for Product Enhancement), bringing significant aesthetic changes (front fascia, rear lights), a revised interior with a new center console and a larger touchscreen, as well as the introduction of new hybrid and plug-in hybrid powertrains, and an update to existing diesel engines.

Overall, the Santa Fe IV is a reliable and well-built vehicle, offering excellent value for money on the used car market. Its versatility and comfort make it a relevant choice for families.

Strengths 7

  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Generous interior space and modularity (7 seats)
  • Very comprehensive standard equipment, especially after the facelift
  • Improved build and finish quality
  • Long manufacturer's warranty (5 years in Europe)
  • Efficient and economical diesel engines (2.2 CRDi)
  • Very efficient hybrid and PHEV versions

Weaknesses 6

  • High fuel consumption for naturally aspirated petrol engines
  • Sensitive AdBlue and DPF systems on diesels if used improperly
  • Valve carbon buildup on GDi engines (direct injection)
  • DCT automatic gearbox (post-facelift) requiring rigorous maintenance
  • Pre-facelift infotainment less intuitive than competitors
  • Some hard plastics in the lower parts of the cabin

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for diesels and DCT)
  • AdBlue system operation and absence of engine warning lights
  • Condition of the DPF (diesel particulate filter) on diesels (no excessive smoke, regular regenerations)
  • Absence of abnormal engine noises (injectors, turbo, timing chain)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (heavy vehicle)
  • Functioning of all electronic equipment and driving aids
  • For hybrids/PHEVs: battery charge status, absence of specific warning lights

Verdict

The Hyundai Santa Fe IV is an excellent choice for anyone looking for a spacious, comfortable, and well-equipped family SUV. Its overall reliability is good, provided rigorous maintenance is followed, especially for diesel engines and DCT gearboxes.

For diesels (2.0 and 2.2 CRDi): Prioritize models with a history of long journeys and meticulous maintenance of the AdBlue system and DPF. The post-facelift 2.2 CRDi with the 8-speed DCT gearbox is particularly recommendable for its driving pleasure and economy, but gearbox oil changes are imperative.

For GDi petrols: Be vigilant about maintenance and plan for decarbonization if the vehicle has high mileage or a history of short journeys. T-GDi versions are more powerful and generally reliable.

For hybrids/PHEVs: These are modern and very relevant choices for city and daily commutes, offering excellent fuel economy. The reliability of the hybrid system is good, backed by the Hyundai warranty.

Overall, the Santa Fe IV represents a safe bet on the used car market, offering a high level of performance for a controlled budget. A thoughtful and well-verified purchase will guarantee many years of satisfaction.
