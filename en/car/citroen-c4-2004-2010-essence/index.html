Citroën C4

I (LC) · 2004 – 2010

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Citroën C4 (internal code name LC for the 5-door sedan, LA for the 3-door coupé) marked a major stylistic and technological break for the chevron brand, replacing the aging Xsara. Launched in 2004, it stood out with its bold design (especially the Coupé version with its split rear window), its fixed-hub steering wheel grouping controls, its central digital display, and unprecedented equipment for the category (perfume diffuser, AFIL lane departure warning).

The late 2008 facelift (Phase 2) brought slight aesthetic touches, an improvement in interior finish, and a multimedia system update. However, this facelift primarily marked a turning point under the hood: the abandonment of the robust historical petrol engines (TU/EW) in favor of the "Prince" engine family (VTi and THP) co-developed with BMW. Paradoxically, while Phase 2 corrected the electronic teething problems of Phase 1, it introduced petrol engines with disastrous reliability. For a used car, the choice of engine is therefore absolutely crucial.

Strengths 6

  • Exemplary reliability of Phase 1 petrol engines (1.4i, 1.6i, 2.0i)
  • Typically Citroën suspension comfort (excellent filtering)
  • Safe and dynamic road handling
  • Original design that has aged well (especially the Coupé)
  • Rich equipment for its time (directional xenon headlights, AFIL on high trims)
  • Ergonomics of steering wheel controls (fixed hub) once accustomed

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of VTi and THP petrol engines (Phase 2)
  • Recurrent turbo failures on the 1.6 HDi 110
  • Capricious electronics on 2004-2006 models (BSI, sensors, cruise control)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (peeling) and fragility of the glove box handle
  • Poor rear visibility on the Coupé
  • AL4 automatic gearbox slow, energy-consuming, and prone to solenoid failures

Check before buying

  • **Fixed-hub steering wheel wiring harness:** Test ALL buttons (cruise control, radio, horn). Wires can break over time.
  • **Dual-zone climate control:** Check that cold/hot air comes out correctly on both sides (known problem of mixing flap breakage, costly repair as it requires dashboard removal).
  • **VTi/THP engines:** Listen to the engine when cold (metallic rattling noise = loose chain). Check oil consumption history.
  • **HDi engines:** Remove the plastic engine cover and inspect the base of the injectors. The presence of a black paste (carbon build-up) indicates a seal leak.
  • **Onboard electronics:** Check the proper functioning of electric windows, central locking, and the absence of phantom error messages on the central display.
  • **Rear shock absorbers:** The shock absorber bump stops tend to disintegrate or make noise over speed bumps.

Official recalls

  • - **2010 (Braking):** Recall of 2.0 HDi models for a defect in the brake assist vacuum pump valve (loss of assistance). - **2008 (Electronics):** Software update for the BSI (Built-in Systems Interface) to correct lighting malfunctions. - **2007 (Safety):** Replacement of the ABS/ESP hydraulic unit on a series of vehicles produced in 2006. - **2006 (Airbag):** Inspection and modification of the driver's airbag wiring harness located in the fixed-hub steering wheel. - **2005 (Fuel):** Risk of leakage on the diesel return hose on the first HDi engines.

Verdict

The first-generation Citroën C4 is a car with two faces. If you are looking for a petrol engine, **it is imperative to prioritize a Phase 1 (2004-2008) equipped with the 1.6i 16V (110 hp) or 2.0i 16V (138 hp) engines**. These older generation (TU/EW) blocks are indestructible and will protect you from ruinous mechanical failures, even if you have to accept sometimes capricious electronics.

Conversely, **avoid the petrol versions of Phase 2 (2008-2010) equipped with VTi and THP engines**, whose reliability is disastrous (timing chain, oil consumption). For diesel, the 1.6 HDi 90 or 2.0 HDi 136 are good choices for high-mileage drivers, but the very common 1.6 HDi 110 requires a pristine maintenance history to avoid turbo failure.
