Summary

The first-generation Ford Maverick marks the manufacturer's major return to the unibody compact pickup segment. Based on the C2 platform (shared with the Ford Escape and Bronco Sport), it stands out with its urban size, practical bed (Flexbed), and highly aggressive pricing at launch. A true commercial success, it generated very high demand resulting in long delivery times. It is offered with two main powertrains: a classic 2.0L EcoBoost turbo and a highly efficient 2.5L hybrid powertrain. Its steel body (unlike the aluminum of the F-150) allows for less expensive repairs. It establishes itself as the most reliable compact pickup in its segment according to data from J.D. Power and the ADAC.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent reliability/price ratio (among the best in its class in initial quality according to J.D. Power)
  • Very low fuel consumption on the 2.5L hybrid version
  • Steel body making bodywork repairs less expensive
  • Very well thought-out and modular 'Flexbed' bed
  • Handling close to a compact SUV

Weaknesses 4

  • Extremely long delivery times due to high demand
  • Hard interior plastics sensitive to scratches
  • Some transmission jerks reported on the 2.0L EcoBoost
  • Numerous early recalls (electronics, airbags, 12V battery)

Check before buying

  • 12V battery: Tendency for rapid degradation and sudden failures (especially on the hybrid).
  • Recall history: The vehicle has been the subject of dozens of NHTSA recalls, verify that all have been performed at a dealership.
  • Transmission (2.0L): Test the smoothness of gear shifts when cold and at low speeds.
  • Interior fit and finish: Check the fastening of dashboard panels and trims.

What sets it apart 12

Economy

  • Hybrid delivers very low fuel consumption — The 2.5L hybrid powertrain keeps fuel use very low, which cuts running costs for daily urban and suburban driving compared with a conventional compact pick-up.
  • Steel body lowers repair costs — The steel body, unlike the aluminium used on the F-150, makes bodywork repairs less expensive, reducing the cost of fixing dents and panels after minor impacts.

Reliability

  • Strong reliability-to-price ratio — Initial quality ranks among the best in its category according to J.D. Power, so buyers get dependable build quality without paying a premium for the segment.
  • Proven hybrid system from the Escape — The 2.5L hybrid uses an e-CVT derived from the Escape hybrid system, a well-established setup that supports smooth operation and long-term dependability.
  • Above-average segment reliability — The Maverick ranks above the average of its segment for reliability, and J.D. Power and ADAC data place it as the most reliable compact pick-up.

Practicality

  • Flexbed is practical and modular — The well-designed Flexbed can be configured in multiple ways, making it easier to carry different loads and organise cargo for work or weekend use.
  • Compact footprint suits city use — Its urban-sized dimensions make the Maverick easier to park and manoeuvre in tight spaces than larger pick-ups, while still offering an open cargo bed.
  • Available 4K Tow Package — The 2.0L EcoBoost can be specified with the 4K Tow Package, giving buyers verified towing capability for light trailers and equipment without stepping up a vehicle class.

Comfort

  • Drives like a compact SUV — The monocoque platform shared with the Escape and Bronco Sport gives road behaviour close to a compact SUV, improving comfort on everyday journeys.
  • Smooth hybrid operation — The 2.5L hybrid delivers smooth operation in urban and peri-urban use, making repeated daily trips more comfortable and less tiring for the driver.

Performance

  • EcoBoost offers generous power — The 2.0L EcoBoost turbo provides generous power and is available with all-wheel drive, supporting confident driving in mountainous regions and with light towing.

Handling

  • All-wheel drive option available — All-wheel drive is offered on the 2.0L EcoBoost, which helps traction and stability in mountainous areas and on slippery surfaces during daily driving.

Verdict

The first-generation Ford Maverick is a true masterstroke by Ford. By combining the practicality of a pickup with the comfort and economy of use of a compact SUV, it has managed to win over a large audience. In terms of reliability, it ranks above the average for its segment. The 2.5L hybrid powertrain (e-CVT) shines with its proven robustness (inherited from the Escape) and its fuel efficiency, while the 2.0L EcoBoost offers solid performance despite some reported transmission jerks. Its main flaw lies in a long list of early recalls (electronics, 12V battery, assemblies) that must be checked before buying used. If the service book is up to date and the recalls have been performed, it is a highly recommended purchase.
