Volkswagen Passat

B5.5 (3B) · 2001 – 2005

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The Volkswagen Passat B5.5 (internal code 3BG) is a significant facelift of the B5 generation (3B). Launched in late 2000 for the 2001 model year, it retains the longitudinal architecture inherited from the Audi A4 (B5) but benefits from increased rigidity, an interior finish that was a benchmark for its time, and a modernized front end with chrome accents. Mechanically, it marks the pinnacle of Volkswagen's 'Pumpe-Düse' (Unit Injector) technology for its diesel engines. Although it offers exceptional ride comfort and excellent corrosion resistance thanks to its galvanized body, it is known for the complexity of its multi-link front suspension and certain design flaws related to the windshield scuttle's sealing. It is a stately grand tourer, particularly popular in its wagon (SW) body style.

Strengths 6

  • Interior finish quality and material aging (excluding 'soft-touch' plastics)
  • Legendary reliability and fuel efficiency of the 1.9 TDI 130 hp engine (AWX/AVF)
  • Corrosion resistance (fully galvanized body)
  • Gigantic trunk volume on the wagon version (Variant/SW)
  • Efficiency of the 4Motion all-wheel drive (Torsen system, identical to the longitudinal Audi Quattro)
  • Significantly improved sound insulation compared to the B5

Weaknesses 6

  • Complex, fragile, and expensive multi-link front suspension to replace (clunking, silentblock wear)
  • Catastrophic water ingress via the windshield scuttle (clogged drain plugs)
  • Location of the comfort control module (CCM) under the passenger carpet, vulnerable to water
  • Interior plastics covered with a 'soft-touch' film that peels and becomes sticky over time
  • Disastrous reliability of the first V6 TDI (AKN/AKE) and 2.0 TDI (BGW)
  • Timing belt replacement requiring 'service position' (bumper removal and tilting of the front end)

Check before buying

  • Lift the carpet on the driver and passenger sides to check for moisture (sign of water ingress destroying the comfort module).
  • Check the condition of the water drains under the battery in the windshield scuttle (must be clear).
  • Listen carefully to the front suspension on degraded roads: any clunking indicates control arms need replacement (there are 4 per side).
  • On 1.9 TDI models: check for excessive vibrations at idle or clunking when the engine is switched off (worn dual-mass flywheel).
  • On 1.9 TDI models: ensure the turbo does not go into 'Safe Mode' (sudden loss of power) during strong acceleration in 3rd or 4th gear (stuck variable geometry).
  • Demand oil change history to confirm the use of VW 505.01 oil (TDI Unit Injector).

Official recalls

  • - **2001 (Suspension)**: Massive recall concerning premature wear of the lower control arms on the front suspension (affects models produced until 2002). - **2004 (Braking)**: Replacement of a vacuum hose connecting the tandem pump to the brake booster, risk of loss of braking assistance (TDI). - **2005 (Fuel System)**: Possible fuel leak at the tandem pump (vacuum/fuel pump) on 3 and 4-cylinder TDI engines. - **2006 (Heated Seats)**: Risk of overheating and burning of the driver's seat heating element.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Passat B5.5 is a car with two faces. Equipped with the legendary 1.9 TDI 130 hp (AVF/AWX) with a manual gearbox, it's a true workhorse capable of exceeding 500,000 km with rigorous maintenance, offering a performance/consumption ratio still relevant today. In contrast, the 2.5 V6 TDI (first generation), 2.0 TDI, and W8 engines are financial black holes to be absolutely avoided. Regardless of the engine, the buyer must meticulously inspect the front suspension (control arms) and absolutely ensure that the windshield scuttle is watertight to prevent the destruction of the onboard electronics. This is a 'NEGOTIATE' purchase: an excellent deal if you choose the right version and check the critical points.
