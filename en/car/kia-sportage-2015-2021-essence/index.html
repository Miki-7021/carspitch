Summary

The fourth-generation Kia Sportage (codename QL), launched in late 2015 (model year 2016), marked a turning point for the Korean manufacturer with a significant move upmarket. A technical cousin of the Hyundai Tucson (TL), it stands out with a bold design (raised headlights in the style of a Porsche Macan), a significantly improved interior finish, and a 7-year (or 150,000 km) manufacturer's warranty that strongly reassured buyers.

A facelift occurred mid-2018, bringing slight aesthetic touches, a modernized infotainment system, and especially a redesign of the diesel range with the introduction of the new 1.6 CRDi (replacing the 1.7) and the appearance of micro-hybridization (MHEV 48V).

Although this sheet focuses on petrol engines, it is crucial to note that reliability varies drastically by market: European petrol engines (Gamma family) are generally very robust, while North American engines (Theta II family) have suffered major problems.

Strengths 6

  • Distinctive design that ages very well
  • 7-year manufacturer's warranty (often still active on 2018-2021 models)
  • Generous interior space and decent boot volume (except MHEV versions where the boot is reduced)
  • Exemplary reliability of the 1.6 GDI petrol engine (Europe)
  • Intuitive interior ergonomics with physical controls retained
  • Sound and safe road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Capricious DCT7 automatic gearbox (dry dual-clutch) prone to premature wear
  • High fuel consumption for petrol engines (especially the 1.6 T-GDI with all-wheel drive)
  • North American petrol engines (Theta II) at high risk of failure
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speeds (wind and road noise)
  • Suspension a bit firm at low speeds, especially with 19-inch wheels (GT Line trim)

Check before buying

  • DCT7 gearbox behavior: no jerks, juddering, or hesitation should be felt during low-speed maneuvers or in reverse.
  • Recall history: it is imperative to check if the ABS/HECU module has been inspected (fire risk).
  • For North American models: presence of the KSDS update certificate for 2.4 and 2.0T engines.
  • Tire wear: the Sportage is heavy and can prematurely wear out tire sets, especially in 4WD version.
  • Onboard electronics: test the multimedia system (touchscreen sometimes frozen), the rearview camera, and the air conditioning operation.

Official recalls

  • ABS / HECU Module (2021/2022): Risk of short circuit in the hydraulic electronic control unit (HECU) which can lead to a fire in the engine compartment, even with the ignition off. Recommendation to park outdoors before repair (fuse/module replacement).
  • Tow Hitch Wiring Harness (2022): On models equipped with an accessory tow hitch, risk of short circuit and fire due to water infiltration into the hitch module.
  • Theta II Engines (North Amer. - 2017/2020): Premature wear of connecting rod bearings. Recall for inspection, KSDS software installation, and engine block replacement if necessary.
  • Airbags (2022): On some models, the airbag warning light may illuminate due to a software interference defect, preventing deployment in the event of a crash.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 3008 II (better chassis, but problematic PureTech engines), Volkswagen Tiguan II (more expensive, superior finish), Nissan Qashqai II (more compact), Hyundai Tucson III (technical clone), Renault Kadjar.
  • Japan / Asia: Toyota RAV4 (hybrid benchmark), Honda CR-V (very spacious), Mazda CX-5 (excellent naturally aspirated petrol engines, premium finish).
  • North America: Chevrolet Equinox, Ford Escape, Subaru Forester, Toyota RAV4, Honda CR-V.

Verdict

The Kia Sportage IV (QL) is a generally highly recommendable compact SUV, provided you choose the right engine. In Europe, the petrol versions (1.6 GDI and 1.6 T-GDI) offer excellent mechanical reliability. The naturally aspirated 1.6 GDI is an absolute sensible choice for its robustness, although it lacks vigor. The 1.6 T-GDI is more pleasant but requires caution if coupled with the DCT7 gearbox (to be rigorously tested). In contrast, in North America, the 2.4 GDI and 2.0 T-GDI (Theta II) engines are to be avoided or bought with extreme caution (clear history and active warranty are essential) due to the risk of engine failure. The frequent presence of a remaining 7-year warranty on the European used car market makes it a particularly safe purchase.
