Summary

The Vauxhall Grandland X (which became simply Grandland after its facelift) is a compact SUV and technical cousin of the Peugeot 3008 II, based on the PSA group's (now Stellantis) EMP2 platform. It stands out with a more conservative design and more traditional ergonomics than its French counterpart. The plug-in hybrid (PHEV) version, introduced in 2019, represents the top of the range with FWD (225 hp) and AWD (Hybrid4 300 hp) variants. While comfort and interior space are present, the overall reliability of the model is heavily marred by the PSA-sourced combustion engines (1.2 PureTech and 1.5 BlueHDi). The hybrid versions offer excellent drivability but require increased vigilance regarding the electronics and the high-voltage battery.

Strengths 5

  • Drivability and performance of the hybrid versions (especially Hybrid4 300 hp)
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing
  • More intuitive interior ergonomics than the Peugeot 3008 (physical climate controls)
  • Interior space and trunk volume (on combustion versions)
  • Generous standard equipment on high trim levels

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the mass-market combustion engines (1.2 PureTech and 1.5 BlueHDi)
  • Reduced trunk volume on hybrid versions (PHEV)
  • Slow and dated infotainment system
  • Significant weight of the hybrid versions impacting agility
  • Critical safety recalls (battery fire, steering)

Check before buying

  • On PHEV: Formal proof of the execution of the high-voltage battery recall (fire risk).
  • On PHEV: Smooth operation of the electric/combustion transition and condition of the air conditioning compressor.
  • On 1.2 Turbo: Condition of the timing belt (swelling, cracks) and strict maintenance history (1 year / 15,000 km).
  • On 1.5 Diesel: Metallic clattering noise at the top of the engine (warning sign of the 7 mm chain breaking).
  • On all Diesels: Absence of the 'Anti-pollution fault' warning light (failing AdBlue system).

Official recalls

  • [2025-09-19] High-voltage battery (PHEV): The vehicle may have a defective high-voltage battery, which could cause overheating and lead to a fire in the battery or the vehicle.
  • [2026-06-15] Power steering: Possible loss of steering assistance due to a failure on dual-pinion steering systems (risk of injury).
  • [2026-02-23] & [2025-01-10] Front suspension: The fixing screws on the left and right front support of the ball joint on the front lower suspension arm may break.
  • [2026-01-28] & [2025-01-31] Fuel system: Possible fuel leak in the high-pressure pipe or weakness of the quick coupling connector on the high-pressure pump (fire risk).
  • [2025-07-25] Timing (1.5 Diesel): Due to oil carbonization and pressure, the strength of the 7 mm camshaft chain may be reduced (risk of breakage).
  • [2025-06-19] Fuel line: The low-pressure line may touch the ignition coil screw, become damaged, and leak (fire risk).
  • [2025-03-07] Wiring harness: Incorrect routing of the front seat wiring harness could cause a short circuit (risk of fire/injury).
  • [2023-11-27] AdBlue software: A software error prevents the malfunction indicator light (MIL) from illuminating if the urea injector stops working.

Verdict

The Vauxhall/Opel Grandland X is an SUV full of family qualities, but undermined by the Stellantis parts bin. If you are targeting this model, the Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV) version is paradoxically the safest mechanical choice, as it avoids the catastrophic 1.2 PureTech and 1.5 BlueHDi. However, this hybrid version is subject to a critical recall regarding a fire risk of the high-voltage battery. Buying a Grandland PHEV is only recommendable under one strict condition: that the dealer can prove the battery has been checked, updated, or replaced as part of the official recall campaigns. Flee from the 1.2 petrol and 1.5 diesel versions.
