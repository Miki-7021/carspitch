MINI Mini

I (R50/R53) · 2001 – 2006

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The first generation of the modern MINI, developed under BMW's aegis, is divided into two internal codes: **R50** (naturally aspirated One and Cooper versions) and **R53** (supercharged Cooper S version). Launched in 2001, it redefined the premium city car segment thanks to its timeless neo-retro design and legendary road handling, often compared to that of a go-kart.

However, this generation faced the teething problems of the brand's rebirth. Models produced before the **July 2004 facelift (Phase 2)** suffer from major early-life defects, particularly concerning the gearboxes and power steering. The facelift brought an improved interior finish, new, more reliable manual gearboxes for the R50, and a slight power increase for the Cooper S. It's a future collector's item, but one that requires an extremely cautious purchase.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional road handling (go-kart feel, ultra-direct steering)
  • Timeless exterior and interior design, strong appeal
  • Cooper S (R53) engine full of character thanks to the supercharger
  • Indestructible Getrag 6-speed manual gearbox of the Cooper S
  • Reliability of the Tritec engine block itself (robust Chrysler/Rover design)
  • Indestructible Toyota-sourced diesel engine (One D)

Weaknesses 7

  • Catastrophic Midland manual gearbox (R50 before 2004)
  • Extremely unreliable and expensive-to-replace CVT automatic gearbox (R50)
  • Very fragile electro-hydraulic power steering pump (risk of fire in extreme cases)
  • Very firm ride comfort, exacerbated by original Run-Flat tires
  • Interior finish of Phase 1 models (hard plastics, numerous rattles)
  • Ridiculous rear passenger space and trunk volume
  • High fuel consumption for gasoline engines (especially Cooper S)

Check before buying

  • Gearbox history on One and Cooper models before July 2004 (rebuild or replacement invoices).
  • Front strut towers under the hood: they should not be bulging or cracked ('Mushrooming' defect due to potholes).
  • Power steering whine: a slight noise is normal, a shrill noise or heavy steering indicates a pump at the end of its life.
  • Traces of coolant around the expansion tank (Cooper S) and thermostat housing.
  • Cold knocking noise on the passenger side (worn timing chain tensioner).
  • Operation of electric windows and central locking (fickle window regulator motors and door actuators).
  • Condition of the exhaust line (frequent corrosion on the rear muffler).

Verdict

The MINI R50/R53 is a true passion car par excellence, offering pure driving sensations that have become rare. However, it's a purchase that requires great caution. **Avoid at all costs One and Cooper (R50) models from before July 2004 equipped with the Midland manual gearbox, as well as all versions with the CVT automatic gearbox.**

The best naturally aspirated choice is a post-facelift Cooper (late 2004-2006) with the Getrag gearbox. For enthusiasts, the **Cooper S (R53)** is a fantastic future collector's item: its mechanics are generally more robust (Getrag 6-speed gearbox as standard), but it requires meticulous maintenance (supercharger oil change, cooling system monitoring). Budget for peripherals (power steering pump, silentblocks).
