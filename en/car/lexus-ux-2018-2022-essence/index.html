Lexus UX

I (AYX10) · 2018 – 2022

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

Summary

The **Lexus UX (Generation I - AYX10)** is a premium compact SUV based on the Toyota/Lexus TNGA-C modular platform. Designed to compete with the Audi Q3, BMW X1, and Volvo XC40, it stands out with its bold design, flawless finish quality, and ride comfort. Although the hybrid version (UX 250h) accounts for the overwhelming majority of sales in Europe, the 100% combustion version **UX 200** (gasoline) was marketed in many markets (North America, Asia, Eastern Europe, and briefly in Western Europe). The model shines with exceptional reliability, true to the brand's reputation, although it suffers from practical shortcomings (tiny trunk, cramped rear passenger space) and a touchpad infotainment system (Remote Touch) that lacked ergonomics before its update in late 2022.

**Overall reliability rating: Excellent.** The Lexus UX regularly features on the podium of the most reliable SUVs in J.D. Power and What Car? surveys.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional mechanical and electronic reliability (M20A engines)
  • Finish and assembly quality (interior materials, soundproofing)
  • UX 200 Direct-Shift CVT gearbox very smooth and more responsive than a traditional CVT
  • Very low fuel consumption on the 250h hybrid version
  • Suspension comfort and highly ergonomic front seats

Weaknesses 5

  • Ridiculously small trunk volume for the category (especially on 250h E-Four and 300e)
  • Very limited rear passenger space (legroom and headroom)
  • Distracting and dated touchpad multimedia system (Remote Touch)
  • Disappointing charging performance and range on the UX 300e (CHAdeMO)
  • UX 200 gasoline engine a bit noisy during hard acceleration

Check before buying

  • 12V battery: Tendency to discharge if the vehicle is driven infrequently (a common problem with modern hybrids and combustion vehicles).
  • Battery cooling filter (UX 250h): Ensure it is not obstructed by pet hair or dust.
  • Tire wear on E-Four (AWD) versions: Check the wheel alignment if wear is asymmetrical.
  • Recall history: Verify that software updates (camera, ECU) have been performed at the dealership.

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Volvo XC40 (more spacious, mild-hybrid gasoline engines), BMW X1 (more dynamic, better trunk), Audi Q3 (superior onboard technology). - **North America**: Acura RDX (larger, turbo engine), Mercedes-Benz GLA (more technologically luxurious), Mazda CX-30 (affordable premium alternative). - **Asia / Japan**: Toyota C-HR (technical cousin, cheaper), Honda Vezel/HR-V (much more spacious).

Verdict

The Lexus UX is an absolute sensible choice for anyone looking for a premium compact SUV without mechanical worries. The **UX 200** gasoline version is a model of reliability thanks to its dual-injection naturally aspirated engine (M20A-FKS) and its innovative CVT gearbox, ideal for markets where a hybrid is not essential. However, if your budget and market allow it, the **UX 250h** remains the best buy for its drivability and fuel consumption. Conversely, the **UX 300e** is to be avoided except for very specific uses, due to its outdated electric technology (air cooling, CHAdeMO). The main compromise of the UX remains its interior space: buy it as a raised coupe for two people, not as a family car.
