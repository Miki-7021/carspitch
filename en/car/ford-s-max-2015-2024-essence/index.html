Summary

The second-generation Ford S-Max (internal code CD539), launched in 2015, successfully succeeded the first iteration, consolidating its position as a dynamic family MPV. Based on the CD4 platform, shared with the Mondeo and Galaxy, it stands out with a sharper design, improved spaciousness, and a more engaging driving experience than most of its competitors. Available in 5 or 7-seat configurations, it offers exemplary modularity and appreciable comfort on long journeys. A minor facelift occurred in late 2019/early 2020, bringing minor aesthetic changes, technological updates (notably the SYNC 3.4 infotainment system), and the introduction of new powertrains, including the highly anticipated hybrid version. Overall, the S-Max II is a well-designed vehicle, but its reliability can vary significantly depending on the chosen engine.

Strengths 6

  • Modularity and spaciousness (5 or 7 seats)
  • Dynamic and precise road handling for an MPV
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Successful exterior design, less 'utilitarian' than some competitors
  • Efficient and reliable diesel and hybrid powertrains (post-2018)
  • Modern technological equipment (depending on trim level)

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of the 1.5 EcoBoost engine (pre-2020) problematic
  • Fuel consumption of gasoline engines (excluding hybrid) can be high
  • AdBlue system on diesels can be a source of costly breakdowns
  • Quality of some interior plastics inferior to German standards
  • SYNC 2/3 infotainment system sometimes slow or buggy (improved with SYNC 3.4)
  • Third-row access a bit tight for adults

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for the 1.5 EcoBoost)
  • Condition of the timing belt and water pump on 1.5 EcoBoost
  • Absence of coolant leaks (all engines)
  • AdBlue system operation (warning lights, refilling, fault history)
  • Absence of excessive smoke or abnormal noises (diesel engines)
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel (noises on start/stop, vibrations)
  • Operation of all electronic equipment (SYNC, driving aids)
  • Wear of tires and brakes (particularly on heavy or powerful versions)
  • Condition of seats and modularity (folding mechanisms)

Official recalls

Official Manufacturer Recalls

  • 2016-2017 (various dates) : Potential corrosion issue on the electric parking brake cable, which could lead to a loss of function. Affects vehicles produced between 2014 and 2017. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2017 (May) : Risk of fuel leakage at the fuel filter, which could lead to a fire. Affects vehicles produced between 2015 and 2017 equipped with certain diesel engines. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2018 (February) : Powertrain Control Module (PCM) software issue that could lead to engine stalling or loss of power. Affects vehicles produced in 2017. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2019 (July) : Risk of assisted braking system (vacuum pump) failure, which could reduce braking effectiveness. Affects vehicles produced between 2014 and 2018. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2020 (October) : Windshield bonding issue that could lead to its detachment in the event of an accident. Affects vehicles produced between 2019 and 2020. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2021 (March) : Risk of engine overheating due to a coolant level sensor issue, which could lead to engine damage. Affects vehicles equipped with the 1.5 EcoBoost engine produced between 2014 and 2019. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)
  • 2022 (June) : Transmission Control Module (TCM) software issue that could lead to incorrect gear shifting or loss of propulsion. Affects vehicles equipped with automatic transmission. (Source: Rappel Conso, ADAC)

*Note: This list is not exhaustive, and it is recommended to check with a Ford dealership whether the desired vehicle has been subject to all recalls and if the corrective actions have been applied.*

Verdict

The Ford S-Max II is an excellent choice for families looking for a spacious, modular, and enjoyable-to-drive MPV. However, the choice of engine is crucial for long-term reliability. We recommend avoiding 1.5 EcoBoost versions produced before 2020 due to recurring timing belt and cooling issues, unless an impeccable service history and proof of preventive replacement of at-risk parts are provided.

2.0 TDCi/EcoBlue diesel engines are generally reliable and an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers, provided the AdBlue system is monitored and rigorous maintenance is performed. The 2.0 EcoBoost 240 hp version is a powerful and more reliable option than the 1.5, but its fuel consumption should be considered.

The 2.5 Duratec FHEV (hybrid) is the most recommendable engine for its smoothness, urban fuel efficiency, and potentially exemplary reliability, but it is newer and therefore more expensive on the used market.

In summary: prioritize 2.0 TDCi/EcoBlue diesels or the 2.5 FHEV hybrid. If you opt for a 1.5 EcoBoost, be extremely vigilant about the history and budget for potential fixes. The S-Max II remains a very relevant vehicle for its segment.
