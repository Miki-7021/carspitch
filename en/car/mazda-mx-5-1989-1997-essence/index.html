Mazda MX-5

I (NA) · 1989 – 1997

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The first-generation Mazda MX-5 (codename NA), also known as Miata in North America and Eunos Roadster in Japan, is an icon of automotive history. Launched in 1989, it resurrected the segment of small sports roadsters (until then dominated by British and Italian manufacturers) by bringing Japanese reliability to it. Characterized by its retractable (pop-up) headlights, its featherlight weight (around 950 kg), and its ideal weight distribution (50/50), it embodies the *Jinba Ittai* philosophy (the oneness of horse and rider). Today, it is a highly sought-after collector's vehicle (youngtimer). Its mechanical reliability is legendary, but its worst enemy remains corrosion.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional mechanical reliability (indestructible engines and gearboxes if maintained)
  • Unrivaled driving pleasure at this price level (rear-wheel drive, lightness, perfect gearbox handling)
  • Negligible maintenance cost and excellent mechanical accessibility
  • Almost infinite availability of spare parts (OEM and aftermarket)
  • Rising popularity and resale value (a true Youngtimer)

Weaknesses 5

  • Extreme susceptibility to corrosion (the model's true weak point)
  • 'Short Nose' crankshaft issue on early 1.6 models (1989-1991)
  • Non-existent soundproofing and spartan comfort on long journeys
  • Passive safety from another era (no ABS or airbag on early European models)
  • Original soft top with plastic rear window that fogs up and cracks in the cold

Check before buying

  • **CORROSION (Crucial)** : Thoroughly inspect the sills (in front of the rear wheels), wheel arches, floor pans, and front frame rails.
  • **Crankshaft (1.6 115hp 89-91)** : Check if the crankshaft pulley (damper) wobbles with the engine running (symptom of the 'Short Nose' failing).
  • **Soft top and humidity** : Check the condition of the soft top, the rear window, and lift the carpets (trunk and cabin) to track down water ingress (often due to clogged drains).
  • **Oil leaks** : Look at the rear of the valve cover (CAS seal) and under the gearbox.
  • **Cooling system** : Radiator color (if it's brown/khaki, it's ready to burst; it should be black).
  • **Clutch** : Check the clutch fluid level (the slave cylinder leaks very often, making gear changes difficult).

Performance

  • Pure and Unrivaled Driving Pleasure — Thanks to its lightness, ideal 50/50 weight distribution, precise steering, and perfect gearbox handling, it offers an exhilarating driving experience.

Economy

  • Optimized Maintenance and Running Costs — Its maintenance is negligible, mechanical accessibility excellent, and the availability of spare parts (OEM and aftermarket) almost infinite.

Handling

  • Exceptional Road Holding — Its dynamic balance, communicative steering, and benchmark handling make it a vehicle offering an engaging and predictable driving experience.

Braking

  • Adequate and Predictable Braking — The braking system, consisting of front and rear discs, is effective for its light weight and performance, providing good and predictable pedal feel.

Safety

  • High Active Safety — Its lightweight and well-balanced chassis, combined with a well-tuned suspension, makes it very forgiving and easy to control, even at the limit, contributing to remarkable active safety.

Practicality

  • A Wise Youngtimer Investment — With a growing fan base and constantly increasing resale value, the MX-5 NA represents a 'love at first sight' purchase with no financial risk, a truly prized Youngtimer.

Alternatives

- **Europe** : MG F (mid-engine, Hydragas suspension, beware of head gasket issues), Fiat Barchetta (front-wheel drive, lively engine), BMW Z3 (heavier, 4 or 6-cylinder engines, superior finish). - **Japan / Asia** : Honda CR-X Del Sol (front-wheel drive, targa top, reliable VTEC engines), Toyota MR2 W20 (mid-engine, sharper handling). - **North America** : Few direct competitors at this price point at the time, the Miata dominated the segment.

Verdict

The Mazda MX-5 NA is a masterpiece of automotive design. Mechanically, it is one of the most reliable sports cars ever produced, capable of exceeding 300,000 km without opening the engine. However, its advanced age makes body inspection absolutely vital: rust is the only real reason for concern. If you find a healthy example (or one properly restored with a photo file), it's a 'love at first sight' purchase with no financial risk. Prioritize a 1.6 115hp 'Long Nose' (post-1991) or a 1.8 130hp for the full experience, although the 1.6 90hp remains an excellent choice for cruising.
