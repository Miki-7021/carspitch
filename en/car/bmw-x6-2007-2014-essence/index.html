BMW X6

E71 (2007–2014) · 2007 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation BMW X6, known by the E71 chassis code, was launched in 2007 and marked the introduction of the "Sports Activity Coupé" (SAC) concept. Based on the X5 E70 platform, it stands out with its sloping roofline and sportier appearance, sacrificing some rear passenger space and trunk volume for style. Positioned as a high-end niche vehicle, it combines the attributes of an SUV (ground clearance, xDrive all-wheel drive) with the driving dynamics of a coupé. A minor facelift (LCI) occurred in 2012, bringing minor aesthetic changes (headlights, LED taillights) and some mechanical updates. The X6 E71 is appreciated for its performance, highway comfort, and interior quality, but its reliability is highly dependent on the chosen engine, with notable weaknesses on certain V8 petrol versions.

Strengths 6

  • Distinctive and exclusive design
  • Interior finish quality and materials
  • Dynamic road handling for an SUV
  • High engine performance (especially V8 and M)
  • Comfort on long journeys
  • Efficient xDrive all-wheel drive

Weaknesses 6

  • Very uneven reliability depending on engine (problematic N63 V8)
  • High fuel consumption (especially V8 petrol)
  • Limited rear visibility
  • Reduced rear passenger space and trunk volume compared to the X5
  • High maintenance and parts cost
  • Firm suspension on some versions (M Sport)

Check before buying

  • **Complete service history and detailed invoices** (especially for N63/S63 V8 petrol engines).
  • **Oil level and consumption** (N63/S63 engines).
  • Abnormal engine noises (ticking, knocking, turbo whistling, timing chain noises).
  • Oil leaks (valve cover, oil filter housing, oil pan).
  • Transmission operation (jerks, slipping, differential/transfer case noises).
  • Suspension condition (shock absorbers, air springs if equipped).
  • Operation of iDrive and all electronic equipment.
  • Tire condition (even wear, matching brands/models to preserve the transfer case).
  • Presence of unperformed manufacturer recalls.

Verdict

The BMW X6 E71 is a vehicle with a strong character, offering a unique driving experience and a certain level of luxury. However, its purchase as a used car must be approached with great caution, especially regarding the choice of engine. The **diesel versions (M57 and N57)** are generally the most reliable and recommendable for daily use and long journeys, offering an excellent compromise between performance and fuel consumption. The **xDrive35i with the N55 engine** is also a solid option for those who prefer petrol, being significantly more reliable than its N54 predecessor.

In contrast, the **V8 petrol versions (xDrive50i N63 and X6 M S63)** should be considered with the utmost suspicion. The N63 engine is notoriously famous for its major and costly reliability issues (oil consumption, timing chain, turbos). A purchase is only advisable if a complete history of the "Customer Care Package" (CCP) program is available and the vehicle has been meticulously maintained, with a substantial budget for future maintenance. The **ActiveHybrid X6** should be avoided on the used market due to the complexity and potential costs associated with the hybrid system combined with the weaknesses of the N63.

**Buying advice:** Prioritize a model with a clear service history, ideally from BMW. Have the vehicle inspected by an independent BMW specialist. For V8s, demand a compression test and a check of the timing chain condition. Post-facelift models (LCI, from 2012 onwards) benefit from some improvements and are generally a safer choice. If the budget allows, the diesels or the 35i N55 are the safest bets to enjoy the X6 experience without financial headaches.
