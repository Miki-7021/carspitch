Summary

Volkswagen T-Cross I Facelift (Phase 2 - C11)

The Volkswagen T-Cross received a mid-cycle facelift unveiled in mid-2023, with deliveries starting in 2024. This facelift corrects the main flaw of phase 1: the perceived quality of the interior. The dashboard now features soft-touch materials, the infotainment is updated (floating screens from 8 to 9.2 inches), and the exterior receives new bumpers as well as the IQ.Light (Matrix LED) lighting signature on high trim levels.

**IMPORTANT NOTE REGARDING DIESEL:** In line with the B-SUV segment trend and anti-pollution standards (Euro 6e), **Volkswagen has definitively abandoned diesel for the T-Cross since late 2020**. The facelift (2023-2025) is therefore **exclusively offered with gasoline engines** (and Flexfuel in some markets). To acquire a diesel T-Cross (1.6 TDI 95 hp), one must necessarily turn to the used car market for Phase 1 models produced between 2019 and 2020. This sheet focuses on the reality of the 2023-2025 range while providing a historical reminder about the TDI to answer your request.

Strengths 5

  • Significantly improved finish quality on this facelift (soft-touch plastics).
  • Excellent modularity for the category (sliding rear bench seat over 14 cm as standard).
  • Very efficient, economical, and proven TSI EVO2 engines.
  • Physical ergonomics preserved (return of real buttons on the steering wheel, separate climate controls).
  • Very powerful IQ.Light Matrix LED lighting technology (depending on trim).

Weaknesses 4

  • Total absence of diesel powertrain for very high mileage drivers (since 2020).
  • No hybridization available (neither MHEV nor HEV), which penalizes it against competitors (Captur, Yaris Cross).
  • DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox still a bit rough at very low speeds or in traffic jams.
  • Prices have become elitist on high trim levels (R-Line, Style).

Check before buying

  • Infotainment system operation (slowness or black screen bugs may occur, requiring a software update at the dealership or OTA).
  • DSG7 gearbox behavior during a test drive: check for absence of jerks or metallic noises during 1st -> 2nd -> 3rd gear changes.
  • Alignment of body panels and new bumpers (sometimes uneven from the factory).

Verdict

The facelifted Volkswagen T-Cross (2023-2025) has reached maturity. By correcting its main flaw (the hard plastics of phase 1), it once again becomes one of the most well-rounded B-SUVs on the market. **Regarding diesel, this powertrain must be mourned for new vehicles.** However, the 1.0 TSI EVO2 gasoline engines (especially the 115 hp) have become so efficient that highway fuel consumption remains very low (5.5 to 6 L/100 km), making the absence of the TDI painless for 90% of users. It is a highly recommendable, safe, and practical purchase, although the lack of mild hybridization is regrettable at this price point. Opt for the 1.0 TSI 115 hp with a manual gearbox for absolute reliability, or with DSG7 for urban comfort (accepting the slight jerks inherent to this transmission).
