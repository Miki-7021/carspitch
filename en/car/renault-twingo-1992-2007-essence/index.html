Summary

The first-generation Renault Twingo (C06) is an icon of French automotive design. Designed under the direction of Patrick Le Quément, it revolutionized the A-segment (mini-city cars) thanks to its monobox profile offering record interior space for a length of only 3.43 meters. Its major asset lies in its sliding rear bench seat, allowing priority to be given to either legroom or trunk volume.

Major evolutions: - Phase 1 (1992-1998): Launch with the old Cléon-Fonte engine, orange turn signals. - Phase 2 (1998-2000): New headlights with integrated turn signals, painted bumpers, standard airbags introduced, revised chassis (anti-roll bar). - Phase 3 (2000-2004): 14-inch wheels, smooth-lens headlights, new door panels, 16v engine introduced. - Phase 4 (2004-2007): Renault logo on the tailgate, thick side moldings, end of European career.

A rustic but brilliantly designed vehicle, the Twingo I is today a sought-after 'youngtimer', particularly mechanically reliable if certain automated gearboxes are avoided.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional interior space for its size (sliding rear bench seat)
  • Overall mechanical reliability of gasoline engines (simplicity)
  • Negligible maintenance and insurance costs
  • Abundant and very cheap spare parts
  • Excellent peripheral visibility (large glass surfaces)
  • Likeability factor and emerging 'Youngtimer' status

Weaknesses 6

  • Outdated passive safety (Euro NCAP crash test from another era)
  • Almost non-existent sound insulation on the highway
  • Recurring immobilizer problems (key desynchronizes)
  • Catastrophic reliability of semi-automatic gearboxes (Easy and Quickshift)
  • Very basic road holding of Phase 1 models (without anti-roll bar)
  • Susceptibility to corrosion on early vintages (front subframe)

Check before buying

  • Immobilizer: Check that the small red diode on the dashboard turns off when the ignition is switched on. If it flashes quickly, the key is not recognized.
  • Front shock absorber springs: A known issue, they tend to break cleanly at the base due to corrosion (metallic noise when steering).
  • Wheel bearings: Frequent premature wear (dull humming noise while driving).
  • Sunroof (if equipped): Check the sealing of the fabric roof (shrinks with age) or the glass roof (dry seals, seized mechanism).
  • Corrosion: Inspect the front engine subframe, rear wheel arches, and sills, especially on models before 1998.
  • Gearbox: Avoid 'Easy' models. On manual gearboxes, check that gears shift without grinding (the linkage develops play over time).

Official recalls

- April 2002: Recall for risk of front suspension spring breakage (installation of retaining cups to prevent the spring from puncturing the tire in case of breakage).,- November 2000: Inspection and possible replacement of the airbag control unit (unintended deployment).,- 1998: Recall for front seat belt buckles (risk of unlatching).

Verdict

The Renault Twingo I is an incredibly rational purchase for urban or peri-urban use, provided you choose it carefully. Definitely opt for a manual gearbox, as the Easy and Quickshift systems are costly sources of breakdowns. The 1.2 16v (D4F) 75 hp engine is the most versatile, but the 1.2 60 hp (D7F) is perfectly sufficient for city driving. Be aware of the temperamental immobilizer system: make sure you have the 4-digit emergency code. It's an economical, endearing car whose value is starting to rise for well-preserved examples (especially colorful Phase 1 models or special editions like the Kenzo or Initiale).
