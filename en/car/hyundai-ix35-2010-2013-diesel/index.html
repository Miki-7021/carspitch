Summary

The Hyundai ix35 (LM generation) marks a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer in Europe. Replacing the Tucson (although it retains this name in other markets such as North America), it introduces the 'Fluidic Sculpture' design, offering much more dynamic and modern lines. Sharing its platform with the Kia Sportage (SL), the ix35 has established itself thanks to an excellent price/equipment ratio and its famous 5-year unlimited mileage warranty. On the used market, the diesel versions (CRDi) are the most widespread in Europe and enjoy an excellent reputation for mechanical robustness, despite some weaknesses in the peripherals and interior finishes. Overall rating: 7.5/10.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of the CRDi diesel blocks (especially the 2.0L)
  • Very competitive price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume (465 liters)
  • Design that has aged well compared to the previous Tucson
  • Timing chain on the majority of engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and very sensitive to scratches)
  • Leather on the steering wheel and gear knob peeling quickly
  • Soundproofing could be improved at high speeds (wind and road noise)
  • Damping a bit firm and jittery at low speeds
  • Electric power steering lacking feel (vague at the center point)

Check before buying

  • Replacement history of the clutch and flywheel (especially on the 1.7 CRDi manual).
  • Suspension noises (clunking): stabilizer bar links and silent blocks often worn before 80,000 km.
  • Operation of the touchscreen/GPS and reversing camera (frequent bugs, camera getting moisture inside).
  • Condition of the DPF on diesels: check that the idle is stable and no warning lights are on.
  • Check if the recalls concerning the ABS module (fire risk) have been carried out at the dealership.

Official recalls

Major safety recalls (Sources: Rappel Conso / Manufacturer)

  • [2023-02-09] ABS Module (Fire risk): The anti-lock braking system (ABS) modules can develop an internal electrical short circuit, which can increase the risk of fire, even with the ignition off. Affects ix35 (EL) models. Fix: Replacement of the ABS module fuse and/or the module itself.
  • [2022-01-11] 12V Socket (Fire risk): The 12V socket can overheat when the tire repair kit compressor is used for a prolonged period. This could increase the risk of fire. Affects several models including the ix35. Fix: Replacement or modification of the 12V socket.
  • [Historical] Brake light switch: Very frequent recall on Hyundai/Kia group models of this era. The faulty switch prevents the brake lights from turning on and can lock the gear lever on automatic gearboxes.

practicality

Alternatives

Direct competitors by market

  • Europe: Nissan Qashqai (segment leader, but dCi diesels sometimes temperamental), Volkswagen Tiguan (more premium but more expensive), Peugeot 3008 I (watch out for the 1.6 THP and 1.6 HDi), Kia Sportage (technical cousin, identical reliability).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda CR-V (benchmark for petrol reliability), Toyota RAV4 (very robust, hybrid on subsequent generations).
  • North America (Tucson): Ford Escape, Chevrolet Equinox, Subaru Forester.

Verdict

The Hyundai ix35 (2010-2013) is a very rational and safe choice on the used market, provided you favor the CRDi diesel engines. The 2.0 CRDi block (136 or 184 hp) is particularly recommendable for its foolproof robustness, capable of handling high mileages without flinching if routine maintenance is respected. The 1.7 CRDi is a good economical compromise, but requires vigilance regarding the clutch. The interior ages moderately (scratched plastics, peeling steering wheel), but the mechanics largely compensate for these cosmetic flaws. Make absolutely sure that the critical recall concerning the ABS module has been carried out to rule out any risk of a short circuit.
