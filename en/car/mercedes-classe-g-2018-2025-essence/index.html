Summary

Launched in 2018, the W463A generation (often marketed under the W463 designation for historical reasons) marks the greatest evolution of the Classe G since its creation in 1979. While retaining its iconic cubic design, ladder frame, and three differential locks, it finally adopts an independent front suspension (double wishbone), rack-and-pinion steering, and a significantly widened and modernized cabin (integration of the dual screen, then the MBUX system). Road handling takes a giant leap, going from a rustic off-roader to a high-performance luxury SUV, although its brick-like aerodynamics still limit efficiency. In 2024, a facelift introduces mild-hybrid (MHEV) engines and a 100% electric version (G 580).

Strengths 5

  • Transfigured road handling compared to the previous generation (independent front suspension)
  • Intact off-road capabilities (3 differential locks, low range)
  • Exceptional interior finish quality and significantly improved soundproofing
  • V8 engines (G 500 and G 63) with captivating character and sound
  • Extremely low depreciation (residual value among the highest on the market)

Weaknesses 5

  • Gargantuan fuel consumption (especially with the petrol V8)
  • Very noticeable wind noise above 130 km/h (brick-like aerodynamics)
  • Rear seat space remains average despite the widening of the vehicle
  • Stratospheric maintenance, insurance, and tire costs (especially on AMG)
  • Windshield highly exposed to stone chips

Check before buying

  • Strict maintenance history within the Mercedes-Benz network (essential for resale).
  • Condition of the windshield (chips are almost systematic if the vehicle does a lot of highway driving).
  • Underbody and side exhausts (on G 63): check for the absence of impacts related to off-road use.
  • Operation of the 3 differential locks (must engage in order: center, rear, front).
  • Wear of tires and brakes (the 2.5 tons wear out consumables very quickly, especially on the G 63).

Official recalls

*(Note: Only recalls specifically affecting the Classe G among the provided data and official databases are listed here)*

  • [2022-01-28] Electronics / Safety (Classe G and other models): The electrical connector of the side impact sensors might not be properly locked and could detach. This would cause a delay in the deployment of the airbags in the event of an accident. Risk of injury.
  • [2021-09-01] Transmission (Classe G): The bolts in the transfer case may not meet specifications and could fracture while driving. In such a case, power transmission to the wheels could be interrupted. Risk of loss of control and injury.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Land Rover Defender V8 (more modern, excellent road/off-road compromise), Range Rover (more luxurious and comfortable, less of an off-roader), Porsche Cayenne Turbo (significantly more dynamic on the road).
  • North America: Cadillac Escalade V (for the excess of the V8), Ford Bronco Raptor (for extreme off-road, but inferior finish).
  • Emerging markets / Middle East: Lexus LX 600 (legendary reliability, luxury, but less mechanical charisma), Nissan Patrol Nismo.

Verdict

The Mercedes Classe G W463A is an automotive paradox: irrational, overpriced to buy and to run, but endowed with unparalleled charisma. In terms of reliability, this generation has corrected the dynamic wanderings of the past while retaining engines (notably the M176/M177 V8s and the OM656 diesel L6) that are particularly robust if maintained without sparing any expense. Buying a G 63 AMG or a G 500 is a highly recommendable passionate choice for those who can afford it, provided they scrupulously check the history, the condition of the windshield, and the application of the transfer case recall. The diesel (G 400 d) remains the most pragmatic choice for high mileage drivers.
