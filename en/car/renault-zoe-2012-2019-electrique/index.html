Summary

The Renault Zoe Phase 1 (internal code BFM) is a pioneer of mass-market electric mobility in Europe. Designed from the outset as a 100% electric vehicle (unlike its competitors, often derived from internal combustion models), it democratized access to EVs.

Its positioning is that of a versatile city car (B segment). Throughout its career, it underwent significant technical evolutions, transitioning from a 22 kWh battery (real-world range of approximately 130-150 km) to a 41 kWh battery, known as "ZE 40," in late 2016 (real-world range of approximately 250-300 km). It stands out with its onboard "Caméléon" charger, capable of charging up to 22 kW (or even 43 kW on Q motors) with alternating current (AC), a very practical rarity on public charging stations.

Expert's overall rating: An excellent choice for a first step into electric mobility on a controlled budget, provided you fully understand the battery rental system (DIAC/Mobilize contract) which concerned 90% of sales of this generation, and avoid the very first (2012-2014) models which were more temperamental.

Strengths 5

  • 22 kW AC charger as standard (on R motors), unbeatable on urban public charging stations.
  • Range of the ZE 40 (41 kWh) version still very relevant today.
  • Driving pleasure (smoothness, silence, city maneuverability).
  • Very attractive used purchase cost.
  • Generous interior space and boot volume (338 liters) for the B segment.

Weaknesses 5

  • Battery rental contract (DIAC) often mandatory for used cars: heavy administrative procedures and incompressible monthly cost.
  • Very basic interior plastic quality and prone to scratches.
  • Reflection of the light dashboard in the windshield on early Zen trims (corrected late 2015 with a dark dashboard).
  • R-Link 1 multimedia system slow, dated, and prone to bugs (black screens).
  • No DC fast charging (DC / Combo CCS) on this Phase 1.

Check before buying

  • Traction battery SOH (State of Health): to be checked via an OBD2 dongle and the CanZE app, or via a Renault certificate.
  • Battery status: check if it is rented (DIAC) or fully purchased (rare on Phase 1).
  • Air conditioning and heating (heat pump) operation: listen for abnormal compressor noise.
  • Age of the 12V battery (must be less than 3 years old).
  • Front axle noises on degraded roads (stabilizer bar links, wishbones, fragile shock absorber mounts).
  • High-pitched whistling on acceleration/deceleration (sign of wear in motor or reducer bearings).

Official recalls

  • March 2016: Risk of premature wear of front brake hoses due to rubbing on wheel arches (models produced until October 2014).
  • August 2017: Reprogramming or replacement of the gear selector. The "P" (Parking) position might not engage correctly.
  • Various technical campaigns (non-safety related): Updates to the battery management unit (BMS) and the R-Link multimedia system.

Verdict

The Renault Zoe Phase 1 is an excellent entry point into the world of electric vehicles, particularly in its R90 or R110 versions with the ZE 40 (41 kWh) battery. These models offer reassuring range and solid mechanical reliability.

However, purchasing requires particular vigilance on two crucial points: the condition of the air conditioning compressor and the administrative status of the battery (rental vs. purchase). The first Q210 versions (2012-2014) are to be avoided due to their temperamental charger and costly motor failures. If you accept the constraints related to the battery rental contract (which in return guarantees replacement if the SOH drops below 75%), it is an unbeatable city car in terms of price/performance.
