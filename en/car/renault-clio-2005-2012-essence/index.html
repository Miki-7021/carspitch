Renault Clio

III · 2005 – 2012

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The Renault Clio III marks a major turning point for the diamond-badged city car. Larger, heavier, and significantly better finished than its predecessor, it offers road performance worthy of the upper category (C-segment). The first in its category to achieve 5 stars in the Euro NCAP crash test, it excels in comfort, sound insulation, and imperial road handling.

**Generations:** - **Phase 1 (2005 - 2009):** Launch with a wide range of engines. Interior finish significantly improved but some early issues (electronics, early dCi). - **Phase 2 (2009 - 2012):** Facelift (refined front end, new lights). Significant improvement in electronic reliability and diesel engine reliability. *(Note: A "Collection" version was sold until 2014 in parallel with the Clio IV).*

In the used car market, petrol versions (our main focus here) are particularly recommendable for their overall robustness, contrasting with the misfortunes of the early 1.5 dCi diesels.

Strengths 6

  • High-level suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Very safe and balanced road handling
  • Overall reliability of petrol engines (especially 1.2 and 2.0 140hp)
  • Spaciousness and boot volume (288 litres, excellent for its time)
  • Passive safety (5 stars Euro NCAP)
  • Very practical and spacious Estate (wagon) version

Weaknesses 5

  • Capricious electronics on Phase 1 (2005-2008)
  • Aging of certain interior plastics (peeling steering wheel, sticky 'soft-touch' plastics)
  • Disastrous reliability of early 1.5 dCi (metal filings, bearings)
  • Difficult mechanical accessibility on larger engines (1.6, 2.0 RS)
  • Notchy gearbox on some versions

Check before buying

  • Operation of all electrical equipment: stalks (headlights/wipers), power windows, air conditioning.
  • Condition of the steering wheel and interior door handles (very frequent wear).
  • Timing belt history (every 5 years or 120,000 km on most petrol engines, except 2.0 140hp chain-driven).
  • Noises from the front axle: fragile shock absorber top mounts and steering ball joints.
  • On 1.6 16v: listen to the engine when cold. A knocking sound (diesel-like) indicates a faulty dephaser pulley.
  • On Clio RS: test gear changes at high revs (crunching = worn synchros).

Verdict

The Renault Clio III is an excellent versatile city car, provided you choose the right engine. In **petrol**, it is highly recommendable. The 1.2 16v, 1.2 TCe, and 1.4/1.6 16v units are proven engines that, with classic maintenance (timing belt, coils), will take you very far. The rare 2.0 140hp chain-driven engine is a gem of reliability.

Prioritize a **Phase 2 (after 2009)** to benefit from more reliable electronics and a finish that ages slightly better. Absolutely avoid 1.5 dCi diesels from before 2009 without a clear history (risk of engine or injection failure). In petrol, it's a smart, economical, and very safe purchase.
