Renault Megane

II · 2002 – 2009

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

All Renault models

Summary

The Renault Mégane II made an impression with its bold design (abrupt tailgate) and unprecedented level of passive safety (first compact car with 5 EuroNCAP stars). Voted Car of the Year 2003, it achieved immense commercial success. However, its career is split into two: a Phase 1 (2002-2005) notoriously known for its catastrophic breakdowns (faltering electronics, turbo failures on diesels, injection), and a Phase 2 (2006-2009) largely made more reliable. As an expert, the analysis of this model requires a strict separation between these two periods, particularly for the diesel (dCi) engines which concentrate the majority of serious failures.

Strengths 5

  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially in Phase 2)
  • Excellent passive safety (5 EuroNCAP stars)
  • Exceptionally robust and powerful 2.0 dCi 150hp engine
  • Safe road handling and biting brakes
  • Renault Sport (RS) versions legendary for their chassis

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of 1.5 dCi and 1.9 dCi diesels before 2006
  • Capricious electronics (unrecognized hands-free cards, sensors)
  • Extremely fragile electric window regulators (cables break)
  • Aging interior plastics ('soft touch' coating that peels and becomes sticky)
  • Water ingress in the windshield cowl drowning the UPC (fuse box)

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: avoid any Phase 1 diesel without turbo/injection replacement invoices.
  • Operation of ALL electric windows (chronic issue).
  • Detection and operation of both start cards (high replacement cost).
  • Engine knocking noises on 1.5 dCi (connecting rod bearings).
  • Excessive whistling or blue/white smoke on acceleration on 1.9 dCi 120hp (Turbo at end of life).
  • Condition of the windshield cowl (dead leaves blocking water drains).

Official recalls

  • 2005 (Window regulators) : Recall for replacement of front window regulator clips (risk of window falling).
  • 2006 (Seat belts) : Inspection and replacement of front seat belt anchorages.
  • 2006 (dCi Injection) : Reprogramming of the injection computer on some 1.5 dCi to avoid jerking.
  • 2007 (Handbrake) : Risk of inadvertent loosening of the manual parking brake, modification of the pawl.
  • 2007 (Steering) : Replacement of steering column mounting screws on a small series.

  • Optimal Suspension Comfort and Soundproofing — Enjoy superior ride comfort and excellent sound insulation, particularly appreciated on long journeys.

economy

  • 1.5 dCi Phase 2: Fuel Efficiency and Reliability — The 1.5 dCi Phase 2 version is a smart choice for its controlled consumption and recognized reliability, perfect for mixed use.

  • 1.9 dCi Phase 2: The Perfect Road Tripper — The 1.9 dCi engine in Phase 2 offers generous torque and great versatility, making every journey pleasant and efficient.

Alternatives

  • Europe : Peugeot 307 (suffers from similar electronic and flywheel problems in Phase 1), Volkswagen Golf V (TDI with robust but noisy unit injectors), Ford Focus II (shares the 1.6 TDCi/HDi, excellent chassis), Honda Civic VIII (superior 2.2 i-CTDi reliability).
  • Asia / North America : Toyota Corolla, Honda Civic (markets where the Mégane was marginal or absent).
  • Emerging markets : Chevrolet Astra, Ford Focus I & II.

Verdict

The Renault Mégane II is a textbook example of a two-faced car. Absolutely avoid Phase 1 diesel models (2002-2005), especially the 1.9 dCi 120hp and 1.5 dCi (80/100hp), whose failures can cost more than the car's value. However, Phase 2 (from 2006 onwards) is an excellent used car deal. The 1.5 dCi 105hp and 1.9 dCi 130hp have been corrected, and the chain-driven 2.0 dCi 150hp is a model of reliability. Gasoline versions are generally trouble-free, with the exception of ignition coils. Buy a Phase 2 with a clear history, and you'll have a comfortable, safe, and very affordable compact car.
