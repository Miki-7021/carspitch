Summary

The Jeep Renegade (internal code BU) is the American brand's first compact SUV, launched in 2014. It marks a stylistic departure from traditional Jeep codes, adopting a more urban and unconventional look, while retaining surprising off-road capabilities for its category, particularly in its Trailhawk versions. Developed on Fiat Chrysler Automobiles' Small Wide 4x4 platform (shared with the Fiat 500X), it is produced in Italy (Melfi), Brazil (Goiana), and China (Guangzhou).

The Renegade received a significant facelift in 2018, bringing aesthetic changes (headlights, grille, bumpers) and, most notably, the introduction of new gasoline engines (FireFly GSE T3/T4) and an infotainment update. In 2020, the 4xe plug-in hybrid version was launched, strengthening its technological positioning.

Overall, the Renegade is appreciated for its distinctive style, decent interior space, and off-road capabilities. Its reliability is mixed, with strengths in certain diesel engines and weaknesses in other gasoline units and electronics.

Strengths 6

  • Distinctive design and strong personality
  • Surprising off-road capabilities for a compact SUV (especially 4x4 and Trailhawk versions)
  • Decent interior space and modularity for its category
  • High driving position and good visibility
  • Diesel engines (MultiJet II) generally reliable and fuel-efficient
  • 9-speed automatic gearbox (ZF) smooth after updates

Weaknesses 6

  • High fuel consumption on certain gasoline engines (2.4 Tigershark, 1.6 E.torQ)
  • Pronounced aerodynamic and road noise at high speeds
  • Interior plastic quality could be improved
  • Electronic and Uconnect system reliability sometimes temperamental
  • 9-speed automatic gearbox (ZF) sometimes hesitant before updates
  • DPF and EGR problems on diesels in urban use

Check before buying

  • **Complete service history**: Essential, especially for diesels (DPF, EGR) and MultiAir gasoline engines (oil changes).
  • **DPF and EGR valve operation**: On diesels, check for absence of engine warning lights, regeneration history, and overall condition.
  • **Oil level and consumption**: On gasoline engines (GSE T3/T4, Tigershark), check the level and ask for consumption history.
  • **Automatic gearbox**: Test gear changes when cold and hot, ensure software updates have been performed.
  • **Electronics and Uconnect**: Test all functions (GPS, Bluetooth, air conditioning, power windows), check for absence of abnormal warning lights.
  • **Suspension and running gear**: Listen for abnormal noises (clunks, squeaks) on degraded roads.
  • **Sealing**: Check for absence of moisture in the cabin, especially around the sunroof or door seals.
  • **AdBlue system**: On recent diesels, check for proper functioning and absence of error messages.

What sets it apart 14

Safety

  • Maximum Safety for All Passengers — The Jeep Renegade achieved a maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 89% for adult protection and 87% for child protection, ensuring peace of mind on all your journeys.
  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — The Renegade's braking system is deemed adequate and powerful, offering a stopping distance of approximately 55 meters (180 feet) from 112 to 0 km/h (70 to 0 mph). The pedal feel is firm and predictable, ensuring optimal control in all circumstances.
  • Stable and Comfortable Road Handling — The Renegade offers stable and predictable road behavior, with appreciable suspension comfort. Its precise steering and good road holding ensure a pleasant and safe drive, even on long journeys.

Verdict

The Jeep Renegade is a vehicle with a strong personality, capable of offering a unique driving experience in its category, especially for those who appreciate its rugged look and off-road capabilities.

**For a confident purchase, prioritize the 1.6 or 2.0 MultiJet II diesel engines**, provided that the service history is impeccable and that usage is not exclusively urban (to preserve the DPF and EGR). These units are the most reliable and best suited to the vehicle's character.

The FireFly gasoline engines (1.0 and 1.3 GSE T3/T4) are acceptable for more urban use, but require careful monitoring of oil consumption and strict adherence to timing belt maintenance. The 4xe (PHEV) versions should be considered with caution due to their complexity and reports of electronic bugs. **The 2.4 Tigershark engines are to be avoided** due to their recurring oil consumption and MultiAir unit problems.

A thorough road test and meticulous inspection (ideally by a professional) are essential to ensure the proper functioning of the electronics and transmission. If you find a well-maintained model, especially a diesel, the Renegade can be an excellent companion, offering a good compromise between style, versatility, and capabilities.
