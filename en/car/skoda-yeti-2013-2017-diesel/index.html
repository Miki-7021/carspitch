Skoda Yeti

I facelift (5L) · 2013 – 2017

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The Skoda Yeti I phase 2 (facelift, type 5L) was introduced in late 2013. This restyling abandons the original round headlights to adopt Skoda's new, more angular visual identity. It is now available in two versions: a 'City' version with painted bumpers and an 'Outdoor' version with raw plastic protections, more adventurous. The Yeti retains its immense practical qualities, notably the independent and removable *VarioFlex* rear seat system, offering minivan-like modularity in a compact SUV format (4.22 m). Mechanically, the 2013-2017 period marks a crucial transition: the gradual shift from Euro 5 engines (EA111/EA189 generation) to Euro 6 engines (EA211/EA288 generation) in 2015, significantly improving overall reliability.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional modularity (VarioFlex sliding, folding, and removable rear seats)
  • Very high overall reliability (TÜV defect rate at 3 years: 14%, among the best compact SUVs)
  • Robustness of 2.0 TDI engines (especially the post-2015 EA288 generation)
  • Sound road handling and precise steering
  • Excellent peripheral visibility thanks to vertical glazing and cubic shape
  • Manufacturing and assembly quality (ages very well over time)

Weaknesses 6

  • DSG7 automatic gearbox (DQ200) with dry clutches, capricious and expensive to repair
  • 1.2 TSI (EA111) and 1.8 TSI (EA888) engines from before 2015 prone to serious failures (chain, oil)
  • Consequences of the 'Dieselgate' update on EA189 TDIs (EGR/DPF clogging)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind noise due to cubic aerodynamics)
  • Somewhat firm damping, especially with 17-inch wheels
  • Base boot volume (416 L) a bit small for the category (but compensated by modularity)

Check before buying

  • DSG gearbox service history: mandatory oil change every 60,000 km for the DSG6 (DQ250).
  • Haldex system maintenance (4x4 versions): oil change every 60,000 km or 3 years.
  • On EA189 TDIs (2013-2015): check if the anti-pollution update has been done and look for acceleration hesitations or engine warning lights (sign of a clogged EGR valve).
  • On EA288 TDIs (2015-2017): check for coolant leaks at the water pump.
  • On TSIs from before 2015: listen carefully to the cold engine (rattling noise = stretched timing chain).
  • Air conditioning operation (the compressor can sometimes show signs of weakness).

Practicality

  • Unrivaled VarioFlex Versatility — Enjoy an ultra-flexible interior space thanks to the VarioFlex system. The sliding, folding, and even removable rear seats transform your Yeti into a versatile vehicle, capable of adapting to all your needs, from passenger transport to bulky cargo.

Reliability

  • Proven Diesel Reliability — The Skoda Yeti, particularly with its latest generation 2.0 TDI (EA288) engines, is renowned for its robustness and durability. It ranks among the most reliable compact SUVs in its category, ensuring many worry-free kilometers.
  • Durable Build Quality — The Skoda Yeti stands out for its remarkable build and assembly quality. The chosen materials and rigorous design ensure excellent longevity, preserving its value and your satisfaction over time.

Performance

  • Driving Pleasure and Agility — Benefit from dynamic road behavior and precise steering that give the Yeti surprising agility for an SUV. Its balanced handling and minimal body roll provide an enjoyable and secure driving experience.

Comfort

  • Panoramic Visibility and Comfort — The Yeti's intelligent design, with its generous glazing and cubic shape, offers exceptional peripheral visibility. This facilitates maneuvers and contributes to an increased sense of control and comfort for the driver.

Alternatives

Verdict

The facelifted Skoda Yeti (2013-2017) is an excellent choice on the used car market. According to ADAC and TÜV, it ranks among the most reliable compact SUVs of its generation. Its VarioFlex modularity makes it a formidable alternative to traditional minivans.

**For a Diesel purchase**: Prioritize models from after 2015 equipped with the **2.0 TDI 110 or 150 hp (EA288)**. They are extremely robust, avoid the 'Dieselgate' update issues of the older EA189s, and offer superior driving pleasure. The 4x4 (Haldex) versions coupled with the DSG6 gearbox are excellent towing vehicles, provided that the oil changes (gearbox and differential) have been scrupulously respected every 60,000 km.

**For a Gasoline purchase**: Avoid the 1.8 TSI and be wary of 1.2/1.4 TSI models from before 2015 (chain-driven EA111). Absolutely favor versions from after 2015 equipped with the **1.2 TSI 110 or 1.4 TSI 125/150 (belt-driven EA211)** engines, which are exemplarily reliable. If possible, avoid the DSG7 automatic gearbox (DQ200) on smaller engines, as its history of failures (mechatronics, clutches) is too significant.
