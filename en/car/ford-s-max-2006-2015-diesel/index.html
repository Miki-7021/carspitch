Summary

The first-generation Ford S-Max (chassis code WS) was launched in 2006, positioning itself as a 7-seater family minivan, but with a touch of dynamism and a sportier design than its larger sibling, the Galaxy. It quickly appealed with its agile road handling, modular interior, and good value for money. Voted European Car of the Year in 2007, it received a significant facelift in 2010 (phase 2), bringing aesthetic changes (front fascia, LED lights) and the introduction of EcoBoost gasoline engines and more powerful versions of the TDCi diesels. Overall, the S-Max I is a reliable vehicle for its category, but like any minivan, it is subject to wear of certain components and specific problems depending on the engine, particularly the widely used diesels in Europe.

Strengths 6

  • Dynamic and pleasant road handling for a minivan
  • Cabin modularity (individual retractable seats)
  • Successful and timeless exterior design
  • Good spaciousness and trunk volume
  • Overall comfort (suspension, soundproofing)
  • Good value for money in the used market

Weaknesses 6

  • Uneven reliability of certain engines (1.6 EcoBoost, 1.8 TDCi)
  • Rapid wear of certain consumables (brakes, tires) due to weight
  • High maintenance cost for high-end or poorly maintained versions
  • Minor electronic problems (sensors, modules)
  • Quality of certain interior plastics (scratches, aging)
  • Powershift gearbox (on some post-2010 models) can be problematic if poorly maintained

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices: Essential for all engines, especially diesels and EcoBoost.
  • Dual-mass flywheel and clutch: On all diesels, check for absence of noises (rattling at idle, when engine stops), vibrations, or slipping.
  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter): On diesels, check for absence of engine light, excessive smoke, and regeneration/replacement history.
  • EGR valve: On diesels, check for absence of misfires, loss of power, or engine light.
  • Cooling system (1.6 EcoBoost): Check for absence of leaks, fluid level, fluid color (no mayonnaise), and history of overheating-related repairs.
  • Powershift gearbox (if present): Check for smooth gear changes, absence of jerks, and oil change history (every 60,000 km).
  • Electronics: Test all equipment (air conditioning, GPS, electric windows, cruise control, etc.).
  • Running gear: Check for absence of noises (clunks, squeaks) on degraded roads (bushings, ball joints).
  • Bodywork: Check general condition, alignments, and absence of corrosion (wheel arches, sills).

Official recalls

Official Recalls (Ford S-Max I)

  • 2007 (Europe): Potential fuel leak problem at the fuel filter on certain diesel vehicles produced between 2006 and 2007. Risk of fire.
  • 2008 (Europe): Windscreen attachment problem that could lead to its detachment in the event of an accident. Concerns vehicles produced in 2007.
  • 2010 (Europe): Risk of braking system (assistance) failure due to a manufacturing defect in a component. Concerns vehicles produced in 2009-2010.
  • 2011 (Europe): Engine cooling fan control module overheating problem, potentially leading to a fire risk. Concerns vehicles produced between 2006 and 2010 (mainly 2.0 Duratec and 2.0 TDCi).
  • 2012 (Europe): Corrosion problem on the electric power steering (EPAS) system that could lead to loss of assistance. Concerns vehicles produced between 2006 and 2010.
  • 2014 (Europe): Engine overheating problem on 1.6 EcoBoost due to a coolant leak, potentially leading to a cracked cylinder head. Ford issued fixes and recalls for this major problem.
  • 2015 (Europe): Recall related to Takata airbags, requiring replacement of the gas generator on certain models. Concerns a wide range of Ford vehicles, including the S-Max I.

*Note: This list is not exhaustive, and it is imperative to check with a Ford dealership whether the vehicle in question has received all necessary recalls and updates based on its chassis number (VIN).*

Verdict

The first-generation Ford S-Max remains an excellent choice for families looking for a spacious, modular, and pleasant-to-drive minivan. Its design has aged well, and its road handling is one of its major assets.

For a confident purchase, prioritize the following engines: * Diesel: The 2.0 TDCi (140 or 163 hp) is the most balanced and reliable choice. It offers a good compromise between performance/consumption and proven robustness, provided rigorous maintenance (DPF, EGR, flywheel). It is the core of the range and the most recommended for high-mileage drivers. * Gasoline: The atmospheric 2.0 and 2.3 Duratec are safe bets in terms of pure reliability, but their consumption is high and their performance is just adequate. The 2.5 Duratec (Volvo T5) is a pleasure engine, but very thirsty and requires specific maintenance.

To avoid or buy with extreme caution: * The 1.6 EcoBoost is to be avoided due to its recurring and costly overheating and engine failure problems. If you find one, ensure that all major recalls and fixes have been performed and documented. * The 1.8 TDCi should be strongly negotiated due to the fragility of its flywheel and injectors. Budget for these repairs if they haven't already been done.

Buying tips: * Look for a model with a complete and transparent service history. * Prioritize post-facelift versions (from 2010) for aesthetic improvements and more modern engines (except the 1.6 EcoBoost). * Check the condition of the flywheel/clutch on diesels, and the general condition of the interior (seats, plastics) which can suffer from family use.

With the right engine and consistent maintenance, the S-Max I is a reliable and versatile road companion.
