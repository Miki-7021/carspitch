Tesla Model S

I · 2012 – 2021

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Tesla Model S (2012-2021) is a 100% electric luxury sedan that revolutionized the automotive industry. Before the major interior refresh in 2021 (introduction of the Yoke steering wheel and Plaid/Palladium versions), it underwent several significant evolutions: an exterior facelift in 2016 (removal of the fake black grille) and a major 'Raven' technical update in 2019 (new variable reluctance motors at the front, adaptive air suspension). Although it offers ballistic performance, benchmark range, and access to the Supercharger network, early model years (2012-2015) suffered from serious teething problems (motors, screens, door handles). Reliability greatly improved from 2017 onwards, making the 100D and 'Raven' versions much safer choices.

Strengths 6

  • Real-world range and efficiency (especially post-2017 and Raven)
  • Integrated and reliable Tesla Supercharger network
  • Exceptional acceleration performance
  • Over-the-air (OTA) software updates that slow down car aging
  • Gigantic interior space (rear hatchback trunk + large front 'Frunk' trunk)
  • Free lifetime Supercharging (SC01) transferable on some models before March 2017

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of early model years (2012-2015)
  • Assembly and finish quality unworthy of the luxury segment (body panel alignment, parasitic noises)
  • Very imposing size (2.20m wide with mirrors) poorly suited for European cities
  • Cost of out-of-warranty repairs (Tesla proprietary parts, high hourly rate)
  • Premature wear of running gear (suspension arms, control arms) due to weight (>2.1 tons)

Check before buying

  • History of warranty replacements (Motor, Battery, MCU).
  • Condition of the eMMC chip (if MCU1) or presence of MCU2 (smooth screen, no yellow borders).
  • Functionality of all 4 retractable door handles.
  • Suspension noises (clunking over speed bumps = suspension arms need changing).
  • Vibrations in the steering wheel under strong acceleration (worn driveshafts/half-shafts, especially on Dual Motor versions).
  • Battery State of Health (SOH): charge to 100% and compare displayed range with original.
  • Presence of the CCS modification (for Europe) allowing charging at recent stations.

Verdict

Purchasing a first-generation Tesla Model S requires great caution. Models from 2012 to 2015 (especially the 85 and P85) are to be avoided unless there is a clear history proving recent replacement of the battery, motor (LDU), and screen (MCU2). However, from 2017 onwards (100D versions) and especially the 'Raven' versions (2019-2021), the Model S becomes a formidable and reliable electric road car. The current depreciation makes the 75D and 100D versions very attractive, but keep a budget aside for the running gear and door handles.
