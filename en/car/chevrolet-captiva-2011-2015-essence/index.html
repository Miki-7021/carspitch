Summary

The facelifted first-generation Chevrolet Captiva (code name C140) was introduced in 2011. It distinguishes itself from phase 1 (C100) by a much more aggressive front end, a modernized interior, reworked soundproofing and, above all, all-new engines paired with new 6-speed gearboxes. Positioned as an affordable family SUV, available in 5 or 7 seats, it offered an excellent price-to-equipment ratio. However, the withdrawal of the Chevrolet brand from the European market in late 2014 / early 2015 brought its career on the Old Continent to a halt, leading to heavy depreciation. The petrol versions, although a minority in Europe compared to diesel, are sought after today to escape low-emission zone (ZFE) traffic restrictions, but they suffer from the vehicle's substantial weight (nearly 1.9 tons).

Strengths 5

  • Generous roominess and modularity (true 7-seater version)
  • Unbeatable price-to-equipment ratio on the used market
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing significantly improved (C140)
  • Modernized and imposing exterior design
  • Attractive towing capacity (especially in V6 and Diesel)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very high fuel consumption on petrol engines (often > 10L/100km)
  • Excessive weight penalizing driving dynamics and braking
  • 6-speed automatic gearbox (6T45/6T50) slow and sometimes prone to jerking
  • Chevrolet's withdrawal from Europe: sparse maintenance network (although taken over by Opel/GM)
  • Quality of some interior plastics still lagging behind

Check before buying

  • Metallic noises on cold start (sign of a stretched timing chain on 2.4 and 3.0 V6).
  • Engine oil level (Ecotec petrol engines can consume up to 1L/1,000km as they age).
  • Automatic gearbox behavior: gear shifts must be smooth, without slipping or jerking (gearbox oil change imperative every 60,000 km).
  • Running gear wear: given the vehicle's weight, control arm bushings, tie rod ends, and shock absorbers wear out prematurely.
  • Operation of onboard electronics (reversing camera, multimedia system, ABS/ESP sensors).

Official recalls

  • 19/12/2011 (NHTSA 11V591000): Power steering. Affects 2011-2012 models produced between 02/10/2009 and 21/04/2011. Driving the vehicle with the transmission in manual mode and in first gear for more than 5 minutes at high engine speeds can cause the power steering fluid to overheat. This can lead to a fluid leak, a loss of power steering assist, and a risk of fire if the fluid comes into contact with hot engine parts.

Verdict

The Chevrolet Captiva C140 is a rational choice for families on a tight budget looking for a large 7-seater SUV. However, the choice of a petrol engine must be carefully considered. The 2.4 Ecotec and 3.0 V6 are fuel-thirsty and require rigorous maintenance (very regular oil changes) to avoid costly timing chain problems, a known weakness of these GM blocks. If you drive little, the 2.4 can do the job provided it has a crystal-clear maintenance history. For high mileage drivers, the 2.2 diesel paradoxically remains the most coherent choice to move the vehicle's 1.9 tons, despite the risks inherent to modern diesels (FAP/EGR). Negotiate the price firmly due to the absence of the Chevrolet network in Europe.
