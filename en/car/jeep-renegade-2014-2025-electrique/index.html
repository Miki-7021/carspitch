Summary

The first-generation Jeep Renegade (internal code BU) marked Jeep's entry into the highly competitive urban compact SUV segment, while retaining the brand's adventurous DNA. Launched in 2014, it shares its Small Wide 4x4 platform with the Fiat 500X. Its cubic and distinctive design allowed it to stand out, offering a robust and stylish alternative to more conventional SUVs.

Over the years, the Renegade has benefited from several updates: a first facelift in 2018 brought aesthetic changes (headlights, grille) and new gasoline engines (FireFly 1.0 and 1.3). A second facelift in 2022 modernized the interior with a new Uconnect 5 infotainment system and introduced mild-hybrid (e-Hybrid) and plug-in hybrid (4xe) powertrains.

The Renegade is appreciated for its unique look, its off-road capabilities (especially in Trailhawk and 4x4 versions), and its decent interior space for its category. Its general reliability is average, with specific points of attention depending on the engines and model years. The 4xe (plug-in hybrid) version is particularly relevant for those seeking reduced consumption and electric driving capability in the city, while retaining Jeep's 4x4 aptitudes.

Strengths 6

  • Distinctive design and strong personality
  • Genuine off-road capabilities for a compact SUV (4x4 and Trailhawk versions)
  • Decent interior space and appreciable modularity
  • 4xe (PHEV) versions offering good electric range and high performance
  • Modern and intuitive Uconnect 5 system (post-2022)
  • High driving position and good visibility

Weaknesses 6

  • Fuel consumption sometimes high on certain gasoline engines (excluding PHEV)
  • Quality of certain interior plastics (before 2022)
  • Wind and road noise on the highway
  • Electronic reliability (Uconnect system) on early model years
  • Potentially higher maintenance cost for 4xe (PHEV) versions
  • 2.4 Tigershark engine (N. Am.) prone to oil consumption

Check before buying

  • Complete service history: essential, especially for MultiAir engines and diesels (DPF).
  • MultiAir system operation (on 1.4 MultiAir): no misfires, no loss of power. Check oil invoices.
  • Engine oil level: particularly on the 1.4 MultiAir and 2.4 Tigershark.
  • Hybrid system (4xe and e-Hybrid): check electric range, proper charging function, absence of warning lights. Test different driving modes.
  • Onboard electronics: test the Uconnect screen, Bluetooth, GPS, driving aids. Check software updates.
  • Suspension and running gear: listen for abnormal noises (clunks, squeaks), check tire condition and silentblocks.
  • DPF (on diesels): check trip history, absence of DPF-related engine light.
  • Automatic gearbox: check for smooth shifts, absence of jerks.

Official recalls

Official Recalls (Examples, non-exhaustive list and variable by market)

  • 2015 (Europe/N. Am.): Potential transmission software issue that could lead to engine stalling or loss of propulsion. (Concerns models equipped with the 9-speed automatic gearbox).
  • 2016 (N. Am.): Recall for a risk of short circuit in the engine control module wiring, which could lead to stalling. (Concerns certain 2.4L models).
  • 2017 (Europe/N. Am.): Recall for an electronic parking brake issue that may not engage correctly. (Concerns certain models).
  • 2018 (Europe): Recall for a risk of fuel leakage from the tank on certain diesel models. (Concerns certain 1.6 and 2.0 MultiJet II models).
  • 2020 (Europe/N. Am.): Recall for a powertrain control module (PCM) software issue that could lead to engine stalling. (Concerns certain 1.3 FireFly models).
  • 2021 (Europe): Recall for a risk of overheating of the 12V battery wiring on 4xe (PHEV) versions, which could lead to a fire risk. (Concerns early 4xe models).
  • 2022 (Europe): Recall for an autonomous emergency braking (AEB) system software issue that could lead to unexpected braking. (Concerns certain post-2018 models).

*It is imperative to check whether the desired vehicle has benefited from all manufacturer recalls by inquiring with a Jeep dealership using the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN).*

Verdict

The Jeep Renegade is an interesting choice for those looking for a compact SUV with a strong personality and above-average off-road capabilities.

For urban and suburban use with easy charging: The 1.3 GSE T4 PHEV (4xe) version is the most relevant choice. It offers excellent versatility, reduced consumption in the city thanks to its electric range, and retains Jeep's 4x4 capabilities. This is the powertrain to prioritize if you are looking for an experience close to electric without the constraints of a pure BEV. Monitor the software update history and the condition of the high-voltage battery.

For road and versatile use: The 1.3 GSE T4 FireFly (gasoline) or 2.0 MultiJet II (diesel, if restrictions allow) engines are good compromises, offering good performance and satisfactory driving pleasure. Diesels are preferable for high-mileage drivers.

For a tight budget: The early 1.6 E.torQ or 1.4 MultiAir can be options, but be vigilant about MultiAir maintenance and be prepared for higher consumption.

To avoid: The 2.4 Tigershark (North American market) due to its chronic oil consumption, unless an impeccable service history and regular monitoring are guaranteed.

Overall, the Renegade is an endearing vehicle. Prioritize post-2018 models (with FireFly engines) or post-2020 models (for 4xe) to benefit from improvements and better overall reliability. A thorough inspection and a complete service history are crucial for a confident purchase.
