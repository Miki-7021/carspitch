Summary

The third generation of the Hyundai i20 (code BC3 for the global/European market, BI3 for India) marks a stylistic break with the 'Sensuous Sportiness' design and an impressive technological upgrade (10.25" screens, advanced driver assistance systems).

Expert Note on Diesel: It is crucial to emphasize that Hyundai has completely abandoned diesel engines for the i20 in Europe with this BC3 generation, favoring petrol and mild-hybrid (MHEV 48V) powertrains. However, diesel did not disappear globally at launch: the Indian market (and some emerging markets) benefited from the robust 1.5 U2 CRDi 100 hp engine. This engine was, however, withdrawn from the catalog in early 2023 following the entry into force of BS6 Phase 2 (RDE) emission standards, making its compliance too costly for the segment. This report focuses on this specific diesel engine while exhaustively covering global petrol alternatives.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional fuel consumption of the 1.5 CRDi on the road (often under 4.5 L/100 km)
  • Diesel engine torque (240 Nm) offering excellent acceleration compared to petrol engines
  • Rear spaciousness and boot volume (352 liters) among the best in its class
  • Very rich technological features (10.25" screens, wireless Apple CarPlay/Android Auto on some trims)
  • Sound and safe road handling (very rigid chassis)
  • 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer's warranty (Europe) or very comprehensive local equivalents

Weaknesses 6

  • Total disappearance of diesel in Europe (penalizing for high-mileage B-segment drivers)
  • Premature discontinuation of the 1.5 CRDi in India (2023) making recent models unavailable new
  • DPF and BS6 emission control system of the diesel very sensitive to urban use
  • Hard and easily scratchable interior plastics, below European competition (Clio, 208)
  • Firm suspension, particularly with 17-inch wheels or on N-Line trims
  • DCT7 gearbox (on petrol) sometimes hesitant and prone to wear in the city

Check before buying

  • On the 1.5 CRDi: Trip history. Avoid a diesel model that has only been driven in the city (risk of clogged DPF and fouled EGR).
  • On the 1.5 CRDi: Check the oil level on the dipstick. A level above maximum indicates diesel dilution due to interrupted DPF regenerations.
  • On the 1.0 T-GDI DCT7: Jerking or trembling when starting in first gear or reverse indicates a dry dual-clutch nearing the end of its life.
  • On MHEV 48V versions: Check that no hybrid warning light is on and that 'Sailing' mode activates correctly.
  • Cabin electronics: Test the infotainment system (some cases of black screens requiring a reset or update).

Verdict

The third-generation Hyundai i20 is a versatile, technological, and very well-designed city car. Regarding the 1.5 CRDi diesel engine (markets outside Europe): it's an excellent choice for high-mileage drivers. This U2 engine is torquey, extremely frugal, and generally very reliable if, and only if, it is used on roads and highways. Buying used is highly recommended for this use, but it should absolutely be avoided if your daily routine consists of short urban trips (the DPF will not tolerate it).

For European buyers (where diesel is absent): the 1.2 MPI is the sensible choice for absolute reliability in the city, while the 1.0 T-GDI 100 hp (ideally with a manual gearbox to avoid DCT7 issues) offers the best compromise for mixed use.
