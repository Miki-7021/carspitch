Summary

Renault Fluence I (L30)

The Renault Fluence is a compact three-box sedan based on the Mégane III platform (extended wheelbase of the Estate wagon). Initially designed to replace the Mégane II three-box, it was conceived for markets fond of sedans with trunks (Eastern Europe, Turkey, Russia, South America, Asia). In South Korea, it was marketed under the name **Renault Samsung SM3**.

**Positioning:** It offers generous passenger space, typically French suspension comfort, and an impressive trunk volume (530 liters), all often at a lower price than the Mégane.

**Evolutions:** - **Phase 1 (2009-2012):** Launch with a conservative design. - **Phase 2 (2013-2017):** Restyling adopting the new Renault visual identity (large logo on black grille), LED daytime running lights, and introduction of new engines (1.2 TCe, 1.6 dCi).

**Expert's overall rating:** An excellent economical alternative for anyone looking for a robust family sedan. The naturally aspirated gasoline engines (1.6 16v and 2.0 16v) are reliability benchmarks, although not very dynamic. Conversely, the 1.2 TCe and the electric version (Z.E.) are to be avoided.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional reliability of naturally aspirated gasoline engines (1.6 K4M, 1.6 H4M, 2.0 M4R)
  • Very generous rear passenger space (2.70 m wheelbase)
  • Huge trunk volume (530 liters)
  • Excellent suspension comfort, filtering degraded roads very well
  • Unbeatable price/performance ratio on the used market
  • Very reasonable maintenance and insurance costs

Weaknesses 6

  • 1.2 TCe (H5F) engine catastrophic in terms of reliability
  • Obsolete Z.E. electric version and batteries often at end of life
  • Uneven interior finish (scratch-prone plastics, wear on steering wheel and gear knob)
  • Average sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • 4-speed automatic gearbox (DP0/AL4) on some 1.6 16v versions slow and fragile
  • Exterior design considered clumsy by some (especially in Phase 1)

Check before buying

  • Timing belt history on the 1.6 16v K4M (must be < 5 years old).
  • Cold knocking noise on the 1.6 16v (sign of a faulty dephaser pulley).
  • On models with CVT automatic gearbox (2.0 16v, 1.6 H4M): demand gearbox oil change invoices. Jerky behavior indicates an end-of-life gearbox.
  • Wear of running gear: front wishbone bushings and anti-roll bar links (frequent wear around 80,000 km).
  • Operation of on-board electronics: hands-free card, automatic air conditioning, R-Link multimedia system (frequent bugs on Phase 2).
  • Possible water ingress in the trunk (rear light seals).

Official recalls

  • - **Airbags (2011):** Recall on certain models for a welding defect on the curtain airbag gas generator.
  • - **Seat belts (2012):** Risk of non-locking of the seat belt buckle in the front seats.
  • - **Injection ECU (2015):** On 1.5 dCi models, reprogramming necessary to avoid a risk of engine stalling or untimely illumination of the engine warning light.
  • - **Braking (2010):** Inspection and eventual replacement of the brake booster (Mastervac) valve on a small early production series.

Verdict

The Renault Fluence is a pragmatic and intelligent choice for buyers looking for a spacious sedan on a small budget. **With a naturally aspirated gasoline engine (1.6 16v 110hp or 2.0 16v 140hp), it is a highly recommended purchase**: these engines are indestructible if basic maintenance is respected. The 1.5 dCi is also a good choice for high-mileage drivers. However, absolutely avoid the 1.2 TCe engine (risk of failure) and the electric Z.E. version (obsolete batteries). Opt for a Phase 2 (after 2013) in a high trim level (Privilège or Initiale) for modern equipment and a refreshed look.
