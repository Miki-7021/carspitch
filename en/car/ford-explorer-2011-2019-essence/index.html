Ford Explorer

V (U502) · 2011 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The **fifth-generation Ford Explorer (U502)** marks a radical turning point in the model's history. Abandoning the ladder frame for a unibody structure (D4 platform shared with the Ford Taurus and the first-generation Volvo XC90), it transitions from a true off-roader to a large family SUV (crossover) prioritizing ride comfort. Offered in FWD or AWD, it underwent a major facelift in 2016 (redesigned front end, new 2.3L EcoBoost engine, technological improvements) and a minor update in 2018. Although extremely popular in North America, its overall reliability is rated **below average** by organizations like J.D. Power and Consumer Reports, primarily due to chronic weaknesses in the AWD system (PTU), the water pump on V6 engines, and the SYNC infotainment system.

Strengths 5

  • Generous roominess (up to 7 real seats) and impressive trunk volume
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing worthy of the upper segment
  • Formidable performance of the 3.5L EcoBoost (Sport) version
  • Rich technological equipment for the time (especially after the 2016 facelift)
  • Excellent passive safety (heavy and robust structure)

Weaknesses 6

  • Overall reliability below the segment average (J.D. Power / Consumer Reports)
  • Possible engine failure on the V6s (naturally aspirated 3.5L and EcoBoost) due to the internal water pump
  • Transfer case (PTU) on AWD versions is extremely fragile if the fluid is not changed
  • SYNC 2 multimedia system (2011-2015) is infuriating (sluggishness, crashes)
  • Automatic gearbox jerking when cold (reported by ADAC)
  • Very high fuel consumption on the V6s, particularly the EcoBoost

Check before buying

  • **PTU (AWD) maintenance history**: Demand proof of transfer case fluid changes. If there are oil leaks or a sulfur smell, the PTU is probably dead.
  • **Coolant level and color (V6)**: An unexplained drop or "mayonnaise" in the oil indicates a failing internal water pump (repair > €2000).
  • **Rear suspension**: Listen for clunking noises in the rear. The toe links are prone to breaking (several massive recalls).
  • **Exterior trim**: Check the attachment of the A-pillars and roof rails, known to fly off on the highway.
  • **Automatic gearbox operation**: Test the vehicle when cold to detect abnormal jerking during gear shifts (1st to 2nd).

Verdict

The Ford Explorer V (U502) offers undeniable space and comfort for large families, with an imposing look. However, its reliability record is marred by costly design flaws. Buying a V6 model (naturally aspirated 3.5L or EcoBoost) is a risky bet without formal proof of a recent internal water pump replacement and regular transfer case (PTU) fluid changes for AWD versions. The 4-cylinder versions (2.0L and 2.3L EcoBoost) are generally less problematic mechanically. Favor a facelifted model (2016+) to benefit from the much more stable SYNC 3 system, and ensure that the numerous rear suspension recalls have been performed.
