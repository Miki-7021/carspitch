Land Rover Range Rover

IV (L405) · 2012 – 2022

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

Summary

The **fourth-generation Land Rover Range Rover (L405)** marked a major technological turning point upon its launch in 2012. As the world's first SUV featuring an all-aluminum monocoque structure, it shed up to 420 kg compared to its predecessor (L322), thereby improving its road handling and efficiency. Facelifted in late 2017 (2018 model year) with the integration of the Touch Pro Duo infotainment system and the introduction of plug-in hybrid powertrains (PHEV), it embodies the pinnacle of off-road luxury. However, despite its regal performance, the L405 suffers from the brand's historical reputation: highly temperamental mechanical and electronic reliability. The petrol engines (supercharged V6 and V8) offer exceptional drivability but require rigorous preventive maintenance, particularly regarding the cooling system.

Strengths 5

  • Imperial ride comfort (standard air suspension)
  • Unmatched off-road capabilities in the luxury category
  • Material quality and cabin soundproofing
  • Drivability and sound of the V8 Supercharged
  • Timeless and statutory design

Weaknesses 5

  • Highly hit-and-miss overall electronic reliability
  • Astronomical maintenance and repair costs
  • Fragility of the cooling system on petrol engines (V6/V8)
  • Frequent engine failure on the 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 diesel
  • Substantial weight despite the aluminum, leading to rapid wear of brakes and tires

Check before buying

  • Cooling system (V6/V8 petrol): traces of orange fluid under the supercharger.
  • Air suspension: the vehicle must rise and lower without suspicious noise from the compressor, and not sag after being parked overnight.
  • Onboard electronics: absolutely test all functions (screens, cameras, massaging seats, power tailgate).
  • Rotary gear selector: must rise smoothly upon startup. If it remains stuck, the module needs replacing.
  • Timing noises: metallic clattering on cold start on petrol V6s and V8s (sign of timing chain tensioner wear).

Verdict

The Range Rover L405 is a paradoxical vehicle: it offers an unmatched driving experience and luxury, but its reliability is objectively mediocre. If you opt for a petrol engine (V6 or V8 Supercharged), the engine itself is robust, but its ancillaries (plastic cooling components, water pump) are ticking time bombs if not replaced preventively. **Purchasing a used L405 should only be considered with a crystal-clear maintenance history and, ideally, an extended mechanical breakdown warranty.** Flee from the V6 diesels (risk of crankshaft failure) and beware of the early PHEV models (2018-2019) riddled with bugs.
