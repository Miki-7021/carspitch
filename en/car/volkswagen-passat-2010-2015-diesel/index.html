Volkswagen Passat

B7 (3C) · 2010 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

All Volkswagen models

Summary

The Volkswagen Passat B7 (type 3C/362 for the sedan, 365 for the SW estate) is not an entirely new generation, but a significant facelift of the B6. It retains the PQ46 platform but corrects many early flaws, notably by definitively abandoning pump-injector (IP) diesel engines in favor of common rail. Positioned as a benchmark family road car (D-segment), it shines with its improved sound insulation, finish quality, and spaciousness (especially in the SW estate). While diesel engines (TDI) are the stars of the model for high-mileage drivers, the B7 was hit hard by the *Dieselgate* scandal (EA189 engines), whose software updates paradoxically altered the reliability of certain peripherals (EGR valve, injectors).

Strengths 6

  • Interior finish quality and material aging
  • Excellent sound insulation (especially with acoustic glazing)
  • Gigantic boot volume on the SW version (603 to 1731 liters)
  • Reliability of the 2.0 TDI (Common Rail) engine block much superior to the B6 generation
  • Sound and safe road handling
  • DSG6 gearbox (on 2.0 TDI) very pleasant if well maintained

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the dry-clutch DSG7 gearbox (DQ200)
  • Consequences of the Dieselgate update on TDI engines (EGR, injectors)
  • Astronomical oil consumption on 1.8 and 2.0 TSI (before 2013)
  • Fragility of the timing chain on 1.4 TSI
  • Very conservative design (often perceived as austere)
  • Cost of spare parts and labor in the VW network

Check before buying

  • History of the EA189 (Dieselgate) update on TDI engines: if done, check if the EGR valve has been changed recently.
  • DSG gearbox behavior: no jerks, juddering, or hesitation should be tolerated (especially on DSG7).
  • Service book for DSG6: oil change mandatory every 60,000 km.
  • Metallic noises on cold start on TSI engines (sign of a worn timing chain tensioner).
  • Traces of coolant around the water pump on 2.0 TDI.
  • Operation of the electric parking brake (caliper motors can seize).

Official recalls

  • NOx Emissions (2016): Massive recall (Dieselgate) of all 1.6 and 2.0 TDI (EA189) engines for engine mapping update (and addition of a flow regulator on the 1.6 TDI).
  • DSG7 Gearbox (2013/2014): Replacement of synthetic gearbox oil with mineral oil to prevent corrosion and short circuits of the mechatronic unit.
  • Takata Airbags (2020+): Replacement of the driver airbag gas generator on certain models assembled between 2010 and 2014.
  • Injection Pipes (2012): On 2.0 TDI, risk of high-pressure line cracking due to vibrations. Installation of balancing weights.
  • Panoramic Roof (2011): On SW versions, risk of detachment of the panoramic sunroof frame.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Passat B7 is an excellent road car, particularly accomplished in its 2.0 TDI versions combined with the manual or DSG6 gearbox. It corrected the catastrophic flaws of the B6 (pump-injectors, porous cylinder heads, steering column). However, the overall assessment is tarnished by two major issues: the chronic fragility of the DSG7 gearbox (which equips smaller engines) and the side effects of the *Dieselgate* update on TDI engines (EGR clogging). For a used car, prioritize a 2.0 TDI 140 or 170 hp model whose EGR valve has already been replaced post-update, or a gasoline model post-2013. Avoid the 1.4 TSI 160 and models equipped with the DSG7 without a clear service history.
