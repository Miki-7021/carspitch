Summary

The Peugeot 207 Phase 2 (restyled in June 2009) marks the maturity of the lion brand's flagship model. Aesthetically, it adopts a redesigned front bumper (fog lights moved to the extremities) and LED rear lights. Technically, this period (2009-2012) is crucial as it corresponds to the transition to the Euro 5 standard (around 2010), leading to a major overhaul of the engines, particularly for diesel. The 207 remains a heavy but versatile city car with excellent road handling and a significantly improved finish compared to the 206. While the 'Prince' petrol engines (VTi/THP) tarnished its reputation, the diesel engines (HDi) underwent a salvific evolution by transitioning from 16 to 8 valves, correcting most of the early defects.

Strengths 5

  • Imperial and safe road handling
  • Reliability of 1.6 HDi 8v diesel engines (92 and 112 hp) from 2010
  • Improved interior finish on Phase 2
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing (on high trim levels)
  • Reasonable insurance and maintenance costs for HDi and TU versions

Weaknesses 5

  • High weight penalizing smaller engines (1.4i, 1.4 HDi)
  • Disastrous reliability of VTi and THP petrol engines (Prince series)
  • Average rear passenger space and boot volume for the category
  • Electric power steering sometimes prone to costly failures
  • Premature wear of headlight bulbs (especially H7)

Check before buying

  • DPF maintenance history and additive (Eolys) level on diesels
  • Absence of 'hissing' noise or black carbon buildup around injectors (1.4 HDi and 1.6 HDi 16v)
  • Smooth operation of the power steering (no hard spots or warning light)
  • Metallic noises on cold start for VTi/THP petrol engines (timing chain)
  • Condition of the flywheel on the 1.6 HDi 110hp (clunking noise at idle or clutch slip)

Official recalls

  • November 2010: Inspection and possible replacement of the brake assist vacuum pump (risk of loss of assistance) on models produced until 2010.
  • September 2011: Replacement of the battery ground cable (risk of electrical cut-off or non-start) on models produced between 2009 and 2010.
  • February 2012: Update of the electric power steering control unit to prevent sudden loss of assistance.
  • 2014 (ZNP Campaign): Inspection of the accessory belt tensioner pulley on certain 1.6 HDi engines.

Verdict

The Peugeot 207 Phase 2 is a car with two faces. If you're looking for a petrol model, avoid the VTi and THP engines and opt for the modest but robust 1.4i 75hp. However, for a diesel purchase, the 207 Phase 2 is an excellent choice under one strict condition: opt for the 1.6 HDi 92 hp or 112 hp (8-valve) versions that appeared around 2010. These engines corrected the serious flaws of their predecessors (90/110 hp 16v) and offer one of the best performance/consumption/reliability ratios of their time. The 1.4 HDi remains a solid alternative for small budgets, provided you monitor its injector seals.
