Summary

The second-generation Jeep Compass (MP code) was launched in 2017, replacing both the first-generation Compass and the Patriot. Positioned as a compact SUV, it sits between the Renegade and the Cherokee in the Jeep lineup. It shares its Small Wide 4x4 platform with the Renegade and the Fiat 500X.

Its design, inspired by the Grand Cherokee, gives it a more stately and modern look than its predecessor. A significant facelift occurred in late 2020 (for the European market, sold in 2021), bringing aesthetic changes (headlights, bumpers) and, more importantly, a major interior redesign, with a modernized dashboard, a larger infotainment screen, and new technologies.

Overall, the Compass II offers a good compromise between off-road capabilities (especially in 4x4 Trailhawk versions) and on-road comfort. Its reliability is heterogeneous, strongly depending on the engines and markets. Diesel versions and the most recent gasoline engines (GSE T4) are generally more reliable than older gasoline blocks (MultiAir, Tigershark). Plug-in hybrid versions (4xe) are promising, but their complexity can lead to higher long-term maintenance costs.

  • Uconnect system sometimes slow or buggy (before the latest versions)
  • 9-speed automatic gearbox (ZF9) sometimes hesitant on early model years
  • Price of parts and maintenance at Jeep

Check before buying

  • **Complete maintenance history and invoices:** Essential for all engines, especially MultiAir and Tigershark.
  • **Oil level and consumption:** Imperative on 1.4 MultiAir and 2.4 Tigershark. Request readings.
  • **DPF and AdBlue operation:** On diesel, check for absence of warning lights, regeneration history, and AdBlue level.
  • **EGR valve:** On diesel, check for absence of excessive smoke or loss of power.
  • **Automatic gearbox (ZF9 or DCT):** Test the smoothness of gear changes, absence of jerks. Check if the oil change has been done on the ZF9.
  • **Uconnect system:** Test all functionalities (GPS, Bluetooth, Apple CarPlay/Android Auto).
  • **General electronics:** Check for absence of dashboard warning lights, proper functioning of all equipment (air conditioning, windows, etc.).
  • **Condition of suspensions and running gear:** Especially if the vehicle has been off-road. Abnormal noises, tire wear.
  • **Hybrid system (4xe):** Check electric range, proper charging function, absence of specific warning lights.

Verdict

The Jeep Compass II is a compact SUV with an assertive design, offering real off-road capabilities for its category, especially in the Trailhawk version. Its 2021 facelift significantly improved the interior and technological equipment, making it more competitive.

**For a worry-free purchase, prioritize the diesel engines (1.6 or 2.0 MultiJet II) which have proven robust and reliable, or the more recent 1.3 GSE T4 gasoline blocks.** The 4xe plug-in hybrid versions are also an excellent choice for their versatility and efficiency, provided you accept increased technical complexity.

**STRONGLY NEGOTIATE or AVOID models equipped with the 2.4 Tigershark engine (mainly in North and South America) due to its recurring oil consumption issues.** The 1.4 MultiAir is acceptable if the maintenance history is impeccable and oil changes have been done with the specific oil.

Whatever the choice, a complete maintenance history and a thorough test drive are crucial. Check the status of manufacturer recalls. The Compass is a good choice for those looking for an SUV with character and off-road capabilities, provided they choose the right engine and model year.
