Alfa Romeo 156

I facelift (932) · 2003 – 2007

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

Summary

The Alfa Romeo 156 phase 2 (or facelift), redesigned by Giorgetto Giugiaro in 2003 (internal code 932), relaunched the career of this sedan with its iconic design. It stands out with a more aggressive front end (new grille, tapered headlights) and a slightly reworked interior with higher quality materials. Positioned as a premium family sedan with a sporty vocation (D-segment), it prioritizes driving pleasure, the precision of its front axle, and the character of its engines at the expense of interior space and trunk volume. While the diesel versions (JTD/JTDm) accounted for the bulk of sales, the gasoline engines (Twin Spark, JTS, and the legendary V6 Busso) embody the brand's DNA, but require rigorous, often expensive maintenance. Production of the sedan and the station wagon (Sportwagon) stopped in 2005 to make way for the 159, but the raised Crosswagon Q4 variant continued until 2007.

Strengths 5

  • Timeless exterior design (Giugiaro) and driver-oriented interior
  • Exceptional chassis: incisive front axle, ultra-direct steering (2.2 turns lock-to-lock)
  • Legendary V6 Busso engines (sound, character)
  • Very durable JTD 8V diesel engines
  • Very attractive price-to-pleasure ratio on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Twin Spark and JTS) if maintenance is not obsessive
  • Extremely fragile running gear (control arms, bushings), to be considered as consumables
  • Catastrophic turning radius (worse than some commercial vehicles)
  • Rear passenger space and trunk volume unworthy of the D-segment
  • Capricious electronics (airbag warning lights, various sensors)

Check before buying

  • Crystal clear timing belt history (invoices mandatory, especially on Twin Spark and V6)
  • Suspension noises: squeaks (like an old bed frame) or clunks over speed bumps (failed control arms)
  • Engine oil level and quality (Alfa gasoline engines naturally consume oil, a low level is a red flag)
  • Airbag warning light on (often due to connectors under the front seats)
  • Water temperature while driving: if it drops below 90°C on the road, the thermostat is stuck open (very common)
  • Corrosion: floor pans (especially at the front), rocker panels, and wheel arches (surprisingly common on phase 2 models)

Verdict

The Alfa Romeo 156 phase 2 is a car for aesthetes and enthusiasts. Its road handling and steering remain benchmarks for dynamism. However, with the 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Twin Spark and JTS), it requires an extremely informed buyer: the reliability of these blocks is mediocre without rigorous and expensive preventive maintenance (timing belt every 3 years, constant oil monitoring). The V6 Busso is a mechanical masterpiece but is expensive to maintain. Paradoxically, for peace of mind, the diesel versions (JTD 8V) offer the best reliability. Buy with full knowledge of the facts, with a dedicated budget for running gear and engine maintenance.
