Summary

The third-generation Porsche Cayenne (code name 9YA, and 9YB for the Coupé version introduced in 2019) is based on the Volkswagen Group's MLB Evo platform, shared with the Audi Q8, Bentley Bentayga, and Lamborghini Urus. Lighter, more dynamic, and technologically more advanced than its predecessor (958), it definitively abandons diesel to focus on turbocharged gasoline engines (V6 and V8) and highly popular plug-in hybrid versions (E-Hybrid). A major facelift occurred in 2023, bringing a redesigned interior (inspired by the Taycan), revised chassis settings, and the return of the V8 on the S version. In terms of reliability, this 9YA generation is considered the most accomplished of the lineage, although its electronic complexity (ADAS, infotainment) and air suspension remain points of vigilance in the long term.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling, the absolute benchmark in the luxury SUV segment
  • Flawless interior finish quality and assembly
  • Overall mechanical reliability significantly improved compared to previous generations (ADAC rate of 2.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year for Gen III, the best in the lineup)
  • Highly performing and robust V8 engines (EA825)
  • Smooth and responsive Tiptronic S automatic gearbox (ZF 8-speed)

Weaknesses 5

  • Recurring electronic bugs on early model years (2018-2020): PCM screens, driving aids (ADAS)
  • Very high weight (especially on E-Hybrid versions), leading to rapid wear of brakes and tires
  • Prohibitive maintenance and spare parts costs (Porsche network)
  • Complex air suspension: although made more reliable, it remains a potential long-term weak point (TÜV reports the suspension as a recurring defect)
  • What Car? breakdown rate of 27% over 12 months (score 3/5), mainly related to non-immobilizing electronic issues

Check before buying

  • Operation of the air suspension (PASM): check for the absence of sagging after prolonged parking and the absence of abnormal compressor noises
  • Software update history (PCM, engine management, rearview camera)
  • Wear of brake discs and pads (particularly on versions not equipped with PSCB or PCCB)
  • Condition of tires (irregular wear could indicate a rear axle alignment defect, subject to a recall)
  • On E-Hybrid versions: demand a high-voltage battery capacity test (SOH)

Official recalls

Major safety recalls (NHTSA & Rappel Conso)

  • Rearview camera (Dec 2025 & Feb 2019): The rearview camera image may not display or may display with a delay due to a software bug. Affects 2019-2025 models. Software update required.
  • High-pressure fuel pump (Sep 2025): The HP pump fasteners may be improperly tightened, leading to a fuel leak and a fire risk. Affects 2024-2026 models.
  • Front suspension arm (Jul 2024): The upper left suspension arm of the front axle may show cracks (2024 models). Replacement of the arm.
  • Hazard lighting (Jul 2023): After the vehicle is stopped, the rear hazard lights and turn signals may not activate (2024 models). Software update.
  • Rear axle alignment and nut (Jan 2022 & Apr 2021): The lock nut on the rear suspension arm can break, altering the alignment and affecting control. A second recall was issued because the alignment had not always been checked after the first repair (2021-2022 models).
  • Steering column (Jun 2021 / Dec 2021): The screwed connection between the steering column and the steering box may be defective, risking detachment (2021 models).
  • Seat heating element (May 2021): Defect on the passenger seat backrest heating element (2020-2021 models).
  • Fuel leak (Jul 2020): Possible leak at a quick connector on the fuel line, fire risk.
  • Transmission oil leak (May 2020): Insufficient weld on a transmission oil pipe that can cause an ATF leak (2020 models).
  • Front shock absorbers (Feb 2019): The shock absorber forks may allow the shock absorbers to loosen (affects a very small number of 2019 Turbos).
  • Rear seatbelt buckle (Oct 2018): The rear passenger-side seatbelt buckle can break under load during an accident (2019 models).

*(Note: The January 2022 recalls regarding the emissions of the 4.2L V8 Diesel mentioned in the databases apply to the previous 958 generation, as the 9YA generation was never marketed in diesel).*

Verdict

The Porsche Cayenne III (9YA) is undoubtedly the most dynamic and accomplished luxury SUV in its segment. In terms of mechanical reliability, it marks a clear improvement over the 955 and 958 generations, with robust and well-designed gasoline engines (V6 EA839 and V8 EA825). ADAC statistics confirm this, ranking it as the most reliable model in the current Porsche lineup with a very low breakdown rate (2.8). However, the record is tarnished by temperamental electronics (PCM system bugs, phantom ADAS alerts) and frequent recalls concerning suspension or assembly components. The TÜV also points out that the suspension remains a weak point to watch beyond 3 years.

Buying advice: Favor a post-2020 model (where the initial teething bugs have been fixed) or ideally the 2023 facelifted version. The base 3.0 V6 is the most rational and reliable choice. The E-Hybrid versions are excellent, but their excessive weight accelerates the wear of the running gear and brakes. A purchase under an approved warranty (Porsche Approved) is highly recommended to protect against electronic or air suspension-related repair costs.
