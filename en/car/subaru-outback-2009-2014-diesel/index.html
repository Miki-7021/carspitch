Summary

The Subaru Outback IV, known by the chassis code BR (for the wagon) or BM (for the Legacy sedan from which it derives), represents the fourth generation of the Japanese brand's iconic raised wagon. Produced from 2009 to 2014, it positions itself as a robust and versatile alternative to traditional SUVs, offering generous ground clearance, discreet body protection, and Subaru's essential symmetrical all-wheel drive. This generation focused on comfort, interior space, and safety, while retaining the dynamic qualities inherent to Boxer engines. A minor facelift occurred in 2012 (MY2013), bringing minor aesthetic touches, interior improvements, and an evolution of the Lineartronic CVT transmission for petrol versions. While the petrol engines are generally very reliable, the diesel version had a difficult start, which impacts the model's overall reliability rating.

Strengths 6

  • Highly effective and safe symmetrical all-wheel drive.
  • Suspension comfort and balanced road holding.
  • Generous interior space and large boot volume.
  • Robust and durable overall build quality (except for certain diesel engine aspects).
  • Light off-road capabilities, ideal for unpaved roads or snow.
  • Petrol engines (2.5i and 3.6R) renowned for their reliability and driving pleasure.

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of the 2.0D Boxer Diesel (EE20) engine on early model years (2009-2011).
  • High fuel consumption of the 3.6R Boxer Petrol.
  • Less 'premium' brand image than some European competitors.
  • Less dense dealer network than mainstream brands.
  • Somewhat austere interior design and sometimes dated materials for some.
  • CVT (Lineartronic) gearbox may lack responsiveness for some drivers, especially before the 2012 facelift.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and detailed invoices. Essential, especially for the diesel.
  • 2.0D Engine (EE20): Check if the crankshaft has been replaced (recall or preventive intervention) with proof. Listen for abnormal noises (lower engine, flywheel). Test the clutch (slippage, stiffness). Check DPF status (warning light, regeneration history, smoke).
  • 2.5i Engine (EJ25): Check oil level and consumption history. Ensure timing belt replacement according to recommendations.
  • All-wheel drive: Test the vehicle on different surfaces (gravel, grass) to ensure proper functioning and absence of abnormal noises.
  • Gearbox: Test for smooth shifts (manual) or absence of jerks and slippage (automatic/CVT).
  • Suspension: Check for absence of noises (clunks, squeaks) or shock absorber leaks.
  • Corrosion: Inspect the underbody, wheel arches, and anchor points, especially if the vehicle has been driven in salted areas.
  • Electronics: Test all equipment (air conditioning, GPS, power windows, cruise control, etc.).

Verdict

The Subaru Outback IV (BR) is an endearing and very capable vehicle, offering versatility and light off-road capabilities rare in its category, all with appreciable comfort. It's an excellent adventurous family car for those looking for an alternative to classic SUVs.

If you are aiming for a petrol engine (2.5i or 3.6R), you are making an excellent choice. These engines are renowned for their robustness and reliability, provided regular maintenance and adherence to recommendations (timing belt for the 2.5i). The 2.5i is the most balanced for daily use, while the 3.6R is a delight for power and smoothness enthusiasts, but with a significant fuel budget.

Regarding the 2.0D Boxer Diesel (EE20), caution is advised. Models produced before late 2010 / early 2011 should be viewed with the utmost suspicion, unless irrefutable proof of crankshaft replacement with a reinforced model is provided. Even on post-correction models, the dual-mass flywheel and clutch remain weak points, and the DPF requires appropriate use (avoid city driving and prioritize long journeys). If you find a diesel with a clear history and all interventions performed, it can be an option for high-mileage motorway drivers, but the risk of unforeseen costs remains higher than with petrol versions.

In summary, the Outback IV is an excellent adventurous family car, but prioritize petrol versions for maximum peace of mind. If diesel is your only option, negotiate firmly and ensure an impeccable history.
