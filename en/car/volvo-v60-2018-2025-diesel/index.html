Summary

The second-generation Volvo V60 (codename Z252) is based on the modular SPA (Scalable Product Architecture) platform. A true alternative to German premium wagons, it stands out with its minimalist Scandinavian design, exceptional ride comfort, and advanced safety features. Initially launched with conventional internal combustion engines (including excellent diesels designed for high-mileage drivers), the range gradually shifted towards mild hybrid (B) and plug-in hybrid (Recharge/T6/T8), marking the end of diesel for this model around 2022/2023 depending on the markets. A discreet facelift occurred in 2022, primarily marking the abandonment of the Sensus infotainment system in favor of Android Automotive. Overall score: 8/10.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional seat comfort (among the best on the market)
  • Interior finish quality and materials
  • Very sound and safe road handling
  • Very controlled fuel consumption on diesel engines (D3/D4)
  • Generous and very square trunk volume (529 liters)
  • Electric range of post-2022 PHEV versions (18.8 kWh battery)

Weaknesses 6

  • Sensitivity of diesel engines to clogging (EGR/DPF) in urban use
  • High weight (especially in PHEV), leading to rapid tire and brake wear
  • Aging and sometimes slow Sensus infotainment system (2018-2021)
  • Early bugs on the Android Automotive system (2022-2023)
  • Very high maintenance cost at official Volvo dealerships
  • Aisin automatic gearbox sometimes slow in dynamic driving

Check before buying

  • History of EGR valve cleaning or replacement (on D3, D4, B4 Diesel).
  • Proof of intake manifold recall completion (2018-2019 Diesels).
  • Absence of abnormal noises from the rear axle in electric mode (PHEV T6/T8).
  • Wear of front tires and brake pads/discs (heavy vehicle).
  • Smooth operation of the central screen and rearview camera (frequent bugs).
  • Condition of the air conditioning compressor and high-voltage heater (HVCH) on PHEVs.

Official recalls

  • July 2019 (Intake Manifold): Risk of deformation or melting of the plastic intake manifold on 4-cylinder diesel engines (VEA) due to soot accumulation, potentially leading to fire. Affects 2018-2019 models.
  • March 2020 (AEB Emergency Braking): The autonomous emergency braking system may not detect certain obstacles due to a software issue. Update required.
  • July 2020 (Seat Belts): The steel cable securing the front seat belts may wear prematurely. Replacement with a fixed attachment.
  • July 2021 (Fuel Pump Fuse): A 15A fuse for the low-pressure fuel pump may blow, causing the engine to stop. Replacement with a 20A fuse.

What sets it apart 6

Safety

  • Maximum protection for all occupants. — The Volvo V60 II achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with exceptional scores for adult and child protection, confirming its status as a benchmark in automotive safety.

Braking

  • Control and responsiveness in all circumstances. — Enjoy excellent braking performance, with short stopping distances and a reassuring pedal feel, enhanced by highly effective emergency assistance systems for optimal safety.

Handling

  • Serene and stable driving. — Its road behavior is stable and predictable, offering a comfortable and secure drive. The V60 prioritizes serenity and confidence, ensuring reassuring road holding in all conditions.

Comfort

  • An exceptional cabin for your journeys. — The V60 II stands out with its exceptionally comfortable seats, among the best on the market, and impeccable finish quality with high-end materials, transforming every trip into a relaxing experience.

Verdict

The Volvo V60 II is a highly desirable premium wagon, offering a refreshing alternative to German standards. Regarding diesel engines (D3, D4, B4), they are remarkably efficient for high-mileage drivers, offering generous torque and frugal consumption. However, they require strictly road/highway use: buying a diesel for city driving or short trips will inevitably lead to costly breakdowns (EGR, DPF, manifold). If your profile matches, it's an excellent choice, provided you verify that recalls (especially the intake manifold) have been performed. For mixed use, prefer mild-hybrid petrol engines (B3/B4) or the excellent post-2022 PHEV versions.
