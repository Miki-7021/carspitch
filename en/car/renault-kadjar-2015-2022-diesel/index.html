Summary

Renault Kadjar I (HFE)

Launched in 2015, the Renault Kadjar is the technical cousin of the Nissan Qashqai II, based on the same CMF-C/D modular platform from the Alliance. Positioned in the highly competitive compact SUV (C-SUV) segment, it stands out with its rounded design, excellent suspension comfort, and generous boot volume (472 liters).

Model Evolution:

  • Phase 1 (2015 - 2018): Launch with powertrains shared with Nissan. Interior finish sometimes criticized for its hard plastics and its temperamental R-Link 2 multimedia system in its early days.
  • Phase 2 (Late 2018 - 2022): Mid-life facelift. Notable improvement in perceived interior quality, native integration of Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, and most importantly, a revolution under the hood with the arrival of the excellent 1.3 TCe petrol engine (developed with Mercedes) and the update of diesels to « Blue dCi » (addition of AdBlue) to meet the Euro 6d-Temp standard.

Diesel Focus: The Kadjar was conceived at a time when diesel still reigned supreme in SUVs. The dCi engines (1.5, 1.6 then 1.7) are generally very good performers, offering remarkable fuel efficiency and proven reliability, provided they are used for road driving to avoid DPF clogging and AdBlue issues on recent versions.

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.2 TCe 130 petrol engine (absolutely to avoid)
  • R-Link 2 multimedia system slow and buggy on models before 2018
  • Disappointing interior plastic quality on Phase 1 (2015-2018)
  • Fragility of the AdBlue system on Blue dCi engines (pump/tank)
  • EDC automatic gearbox sometimes harsh and premature wear of dry clutches (EDC6)
  • Significant wind noise on the highway around the mirrors

Check before buying

  • On diesels (dCi): Journey history. Avoid a diesel that has only been driven in the city (risk of clogged DPF/EGR).
  • On Blue dCi: Check for the absence of 'Check Anti-pollution System' or 'AdBlue' warning lights on the dashboard.
  • On EDC gearboxes: Test the vehicle when cold and on a slope. No severe juddering or jerking should be felt on startup.
  • On the 1.6 dCi 130: Check for any oil seepage on the timing side (casing).
  • Electronics: Test all R-Link 2 functions, GPS, Bluetooth, and rearview camera (frequent black screen).
  • Running gear: Asymmetrical wear of front tires (frequent alignment defect from the factory).

Official recalls

  • April 2016: Recall for a risk of incorrect deployment of curtain airbags (models manufactured in 2015).
  • September 2018: Injection ECU reprogramming on the 1.5 dCi 110 to correct a NOx sensor defect.
  • June 2019: Catalytic converter replacement on some 1.5 Blue dCi 115 (risk of anti-pollution non-compliance).
  • November 2019: Inspection and possible replacement of the brake booster vacuum hose (1.3 TCe models).
  • February 2020: Risk of short circuit on the starter motor wiring harness (friction against a metal part) on a small series of 2019 models.

Practicality

  • Generous Cabin Space and Spacious Boot — With a boot volume of 472 liters, the Kadjar is perfect for families and travel, offering comfortable space for all passengers and their luggage.

Verdict

The Renault Kadjar is a compact SUV full of family qualities (comfort, space, smooth driving) that suffered from the shadow of the Peugeot 3008 and the flaws of its 1.2 TCe petrol engine.

If you are looking for a Diesel: This is where the Kadjar shines brightest in the used car market. The 1.5 dCi 110 (Phase 1) is a true workhorse, ultra-reliable if well-maintained, ideal for tight budgets. For towing or driving with a load, the 1.6 dCi 130 is an excellent choice, robust and available with 4x4. The recent Blue dCi (115 and 150) versions are very pleasant but require increased vigilance regarding the AdBlue system (a preventive additive is strongly recommended).

Golden Rule: Avoid the 1.2 TCe 130 like the plague. If you want a petrol engine, absolutely opt for a Phase 2 model equipped with the brilliant 1.3 TCe (140 or 160 hp).
