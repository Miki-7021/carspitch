Audi A6

C5 (4B) · 1997 – 2004

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Audi A6 (C5, Typ 4B) marked a major turning point for the four-ring brand. With its aerodynamic design inspired by the Bauhaus movement (Cd of 0.28), it propelled Audi into the big leagues against the BMW 5 Series E39 and Mercedes E-Class W210. Available as a sedan and a wagon (Avant), it also introduced the 'Allroad' adventurous variant in 1999. While its interior finish quality and Quattro all-wheel drive (Torsen) remain benchmarks, this generation is also sadly famous for the introduction of the 'Multitronic' continuously variable automatic gearbox, a source of ruinous breakdowns, and for major design flaws in the early V6 TDI engines. A restyling (facelift) occurred in mid-2001, bringing aesthetic touches, new engines, and reliability corrections.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional interior finish quality (ages very well if cared for)
  • Corrosion resistance (fully galvanized body)
  • Legendary reliability of the 1.9 TDI 130hp engine
  • Formidable efficiency of the Quattro all-wheel drive (mechanical Torsen system)
  • Ride comfort and sound insulation on the highway
  • Gigantic trunk volume on the Avant (wagon) version

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous Multitronic (CVT) automatic gearbox (jerks, chain breakage, ECU failure)
  • Catastrophic reliability of early V6 2.5 TDI (camshafts, VP44 pump)
  • Fragile multi-link front suspension (bushings and ball joints wear quickly, causing knocking noises)
  • Water ingress into the cabin (clogging of windshield cowl drains, drowning the comfort module under the carpet)
  • Instrument cluster LCD screen (FIS/ODB) whose pixels disappear with heat
  • Maintenance cost of V6 and V8 engines (poor mechanical accessibility, 'Service Position' required for timing belt replacement)

Check before buying

  • Gearbox history: avoid Multitronic (front-wheel drive auto), prefer manual or Tiptronic (Quattro auto) gearboxes.
  • Windshield cowl (under the battery): check for stagnant water (clogged drains). Lift front carpets to check for moisture.
  • Front suspension: listen for knocking noises on degraded roads or speed bumps (suspension arms to be replaced, often by complete kit).
  • On 2.5 TDI: demand invoices proving replacement of the VP44 pump or camshafts, or target only BAU/BDG/BDH codes.
  • On 1.8T: check for invoices of frequent oil changes (max 15,000 km) to rule out the risk of a clogged oil pickup screen.
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (pneumatic central locking, windows, dashboard display).

Verdict

The Audi A6 C5 is a car with two faces. It can be a formidable, economical, and indestructible flagship if you opt for a **1.9 TDI 130hp with a manual gearbox**. Conversely, it can turn into an absolute financial black hole if you fall into the trap of a **2.5 TDI 150/180hp (first generation)** coupled with the disastrous **Multitronic** gearbox. In gasoline, the 1.8T and naturally aspirated V6s are good choices, provided you accept the cost of timing belts. Prioritize buying based on condition and service history, and thoroughly inspect the front axle and the absence of water ingress.
