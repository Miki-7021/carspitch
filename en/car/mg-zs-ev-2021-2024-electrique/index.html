MG ZS EV

I facelift · 2021 – 2024

Summary

The first-generation MG ZS EV benefited from a significant facelift (Phase 2) at the end of 2021, marking a clear move upmarket. Aesthetically, it abandons the fake thermal grille for a solid front fascia, asserting its electric identity. Technically, the evolution is major: the old 44.5 kWh battery is replaced by two new offerings. A 'Standard Range' version with a 50.3 kWh LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) battery, and a 'Long Range' version with a 70 kWh NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt) battery. The iSMART infotainment system makes its appearance, as does the V2L (Vehicle-to-Load) function. Although primarily targeting the electric market in Western Europe, the ZS remains available with internal combustion engines (naturally aspirated and turbocharged) in many markets (UK, Eastern Europe, Asia, South America, Oceania). It is an urban/compact SUV with a formidable price/performance ratio, supported by a 7-year or 150,000 km manufacturer's warranty, although its distribution network is still in the process of densification.

Strengths 6

  • Unbeatable price/equipment/range ratio in the EV market
  • Reassuring manufacturer warranty (7 years or 150,000 km)
  • Generous interior space and boot volume (448 liters) for the category
  • LFP chemistry on the Standard Range version, excellent for longevity
  • Presence of the very practical V2L (Vehicle-to-Load) function
  • Clear improvement in soundproofing and finish compared to Phase 1

Weaknesses 6

  • iSMART infotainment system often slow and buggy
  • Disappointing fast charging (DC) curve (rapid power drop after 50%)
  • Front-wheel traction could be improved (frequent wheelspin in the rain with electric torque)
  • Dealership network still uneven depending on regions (parts delays sometimes long)
  • Slightly high electricity consumption on the highway (aerodynamics could be improved)
  • Recurring 12V battery discharge problems

Check before buying

  • Operation of the front charging flap (mechanism sometimes fragile or blocked by frost)
  • History of software updates (BMS, VCU, iSMART) performed at the dealership
  • Clunking noises from the front axle (stabilizer bar links or bushings)
  • Proper functioning of heating/air conditioning (cases of faulty compressors or sensors have been reported)
  • Condition of the front tires (premature wear due to the immediate torque of the electric motor)
  • Full test of AC (Type 2 cable) and DC (Combo CCS) charging before purchase

Verdict

The facelifted MG ZS EV (2021-2024) is an extremely rational choice for anyone looking for an affordable family electric SUV. The hardware reliability (motor, battery) is very good, supported by a 7-year warranty that covers major risks. The chosen battery chemistries (LFP for city/suburban, NMC for long trips) are relevant. However, the user experience is often marred by a fickle software (iSMART) and minor electronic bugs (12V battery, charging communication). It is a highly recommended purchase as a recent used car, provided that all software updates have been applied and that a competent MG dealership for after-sales service is nearby.
