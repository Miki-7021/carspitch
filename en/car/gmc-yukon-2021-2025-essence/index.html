Summary

The 5th generation GMC Yukon (T1XX) marks a major turning point for the large American SUV. Launched in 2021, it shares its platform with the Chevrolet Tahoe/Suburban and the Cadillac Escalade. The great revolution of this generation is the adoption of an independent rear suspension (multi-link), replacing the historic solid axle. This transforms the road handling and comfort, and frees up a colossal amount of space for 3rd-row passengers and the trunk. Available in standard or extended (Yukon XL) versions, it comes in luxurious (Denali, Denali Ultimate) or off-road oriented (AT4) trims. Although its family and towing capabilities are exceptional, its overall reliability is judged as average by North American organizations (J.D. Power, Consumer Reports), weighed down by early electronic teething problems and a known fragility in the cylinder deactivation system of its gasoline V8 engines.

Strengths 5

  • Gigantic interior space, particularly in the 3rd row and in the trunk (XL version).
  • Exceptional ride comfort thanks to the independent rear suspension (and optional air suspension/Magnetic Ride).
  • High towing capacities (up to 8,400 lbs / 3,800 kg).
  • 3.0L Duramax diesel engine offering rare fuel efficiency in this segment.
  • Intuitive infotainment system with Google integration (since 2022).

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the gasoline V8 engines marred by the cylinder deactivation system (DFM).
  • Oversized dimensions making parking and city driving (especially in Europe) very complex.
  • Gargantuan fuel consumption on the gasoline V8 engines.
  • Interior finish of some lower trims (SLE/SLT) below price standards.
  • Numerous manufacturer recalls (electronics, assembly) on the early production years (2021-2022).

Check before buying

  • Listen carefully to the V8 engine when cold: any pronounced metallic ticking (tick-tick) indicates premature wear of the hydraulic lifters.
  • Check the complete recall history (VIN check essential), as the vehicle has been the subject of more than 20 NHTSA campaigns.
  • Test the gear shifts of the 10-speed automatic gearbox (no jerking or hesitation should be felt).
  • On equipped models, verify that the air suspension raises and lowers without excessive compressor noise and remains level when stationary.
  • Inspect the operation of the center screen and driving aids (360° cameras), which are prone to software bugs.

Official recalls

The T1XX Yukon has undergone numerous recalls (NHTSA data), mainly on the 2021-2022 models:

  • 6.2L V8 Engine (April 2025 - 25V274000): Risk of serious internal mechanical failure that could lead to engine lock-up.
  • Transmission (Feb. 2026 - 26V085000 & Oct. 2024 - 24V797000): Updates to the 10-speed gearbox control module (TCM).
  • Fuel (Sept. 2025 - 25V619000 & Feb. 2021 - 21V064000): Issues with the tank assembly (potential leaks) and fuel pump module (Sept. 2021 - 21V739000).
  • Braking & Steering (Sept. 2024 - 24V674000, July 2021 - 21V579000, June 2021 - 21V455000): Software bugs affecting steering assist, ESC, and brake fluid warning lights.
  • Suspension & Wheels (May 2026 - 26V304000 & Sept. 2023 - 23V642000): Incorrect bolts on 24-inch rims and improperly tightened rear suspension arm bolts.
  • Electronics & Lighting (Late 2022): Multiple recalls for software bugs preventing the deactivation of daytime running lights (DRL).
  • Passive Safety (2020-2022): Several recalls concerning seat belts (tangled 3rd row, defective rivets) and front seat mountings.

What sets it apart 12

Comfort

  • Independent Rear Suspension Transformation — The multi-link independent rear suspension replaces the old solid axle, improving ride comfort and handling while freeing up space for third-row passengers and cargo.
  • Intuitive Google Infotainment — From 2022, the infotainment system integrates Google, providing an intuitive and user-friendly interface for navigation and connectivity.
  • Optional Magnetic Ride Comfort — Optional pneumatic and Magnetic Ride suspension further enhances the already exceptional ride comfort provided by the independent rear suspension.
  • Quiet Diesel Operation — The 3.0L Duramax diesel provides a quiet operating character along with strong low-end torque, improving comfort during long journeys and towing.

Practicality

  • Massive Third-Row and Cargo Space — The independent rear suspension layout liberates a colossal amount of room for third-row passengers and the trunk, with the XL version offering even more.

Performance

  • High Towing Capacity — This SUV can tow up to 8,400 lbs (3,800 kg), making it suitable for heavy trailers and frequent towing needs.
  • Generous V8 Power and Torque — The 6.2L V8 delivers generous power and abundant torque, providing excellent driving pleasure and strong performance for highway trips and heavy towing.
  • Smooth Traditional V8 Flexibility — The 5.3L V8 offers flexibility, a traditional sound, and low base maintenance costs, making it versatile for daily driving and occasional towing.

Economy

  • Efficient Duramax Diesel Engine — The 3.0L Duramax diesel offers rare energy efficiency for this segment, with consumption divided by two compared to the V8 engines.

Reliability

  • Strong Diesel Reliability — The 3.0L Duramax diesel scores 8.5/10 for reliability, described as a robust engine with very good reliability, the best in the range.

Handling

  • Improved Handling from New Platform — The T1XX platform and independent rear suspension transform the road behavior of this large SUV, delivering more controlled handling than the previous solid-axle design.

Design

  • Luxurious Denali Trim Options — Luxurious Denali and Denali Ultimate trims offer upscale appointments, while AT4 versions cater to off-road-oriented buyers seeking a different character.

Alternatives

  • North America: Ford Expedition (very high-performance but complex EcoBoost V6), Jeep Wagoneer (very luxurious, recent Hurricane engines), Toyota Sequoia (Hybrid, Toyota reliability but less practical trunk), Chevrolet Tahoe/Suburban (mechanical clones of the Yukon).
  • Europe: BMW X7, Mercedes-Benz GLS, Land Rover Defender 130 (better suited to European roads, official networks present).

Verdict

The 5th generation GMC Yukon is a behemoth with undeniable family and road capabilities. However, from a reliability standpoint, it requires caution. The gasoline V8 engines (5.3L and 6.2L), although pleasant, are overshadowed by the chronic fragility of their hydraulic lifters (DFM system) and a recent major recall on the 6.2L. The 2021 and 2022 models also suffered teething problems with an avalanche of electronic recalls.

Buying advice: If you must buy a Yukon, favor models from 2023 onwards (stabilized software) and opt without hesitation for the excellent 3.0L Duramax Diesel engine, which offers the best reliability in the lineup and fuel consumption cut in half compared to the V8s. If you insist on the V8, demand an impeccable maintenance history with very frequent oil changes.
