Summary

The 11th generation of the Ford F-150 (code name P415) marked a major turning point for the model with the introduction of an all-new hydroformed chassis, which was much more rigid, and a cabin whose finish quality took a spectacular leap forward compared to the previous generation. It introduced the 5.4L Triton V8 engine with 3 valves per cylinder (3V). Although it was a huge commercial success, this generation is now infamous for the catastrophic reliability problems of its flagship engine (the 5.4L V8), contrasting with the robustness of its chassis and its other engines.

Overall reliability rating: Average to Good (depending on engine). Data from Consumer Reports and the ADAC highlight a great disparity: the 4.6L V8 is very reliable, while the 5.4L V8 requires extreme vigilance.

Strengths 5

  • Extremely rigid and robust chassis for its time
  • Cabin quality and soundproofing significantly improved
  • Exemplary reliability of the 4.6L V8 engine
  • Towing and payload capacities
  • Massive availability of spare parts (in the USA)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 5.4L V8 (Cam phasers, spark plugs, timing system)
  • Obsolete and slow 4-speed automatic gearbox (4R75E)
  • Very high fuel consumption on all engines
  • Heavy curb weight penalizing performance
  • Size unsuited to European infrastructure (for imported models)

Check before buying

  • On the 5.4L V8: Listen carefully to the engine when hot at idle. A ticking or clacking noise indicates failed cam phasers.
  • Spark plug history (5.4L V8): Demand the invoice for the last replacement. If they have never been done, plan a substantial budget for extraction.
  • 4x4 system (IWE): Check the engagement of the front axle. The vacuum solenoids (IWE) are fragile and can cause a grinding noise in the front hubs.
  • Exhaust manifolds (4.6L and 5.4L V8): Listen for cold leaks (ticking noise that disappears when hot), a sign of broken studs.
  • Automatic transmission: Check the color of the oil (must be red, not brown/black) and the absence of jerks when shifting into overdrive.

Official recalls

Although the F-150 was not officially sold in France (Rappel Conso data concerns other Ford models), the American NHTSA issued several critical recalls for this generation:

  • February 2011 (NHTSA 11V107000): Risk of inadvertent deployment of the driver's airbag due to chafing of the clockspring cable (2004-2006 Models).
  • May 2008 (NHTSA 08V208000): Swelling of the brake booster vacuum hose on the 5.4L V8, leading to a loss of braking assistance (2005-2006 Models).
  • August 2007 (NHTSA 07V336000): Internal leak of the cruise control deactivation switch that could cause a short circuit and a fire (an infamous problem at Ford).
  • June 2007 & April 2004 (NHTSA 07V278000 / 04V200000): Deformation of the fuel tank at the weld, which could lead to fuel leaks.
  • November 2005 (NHTSA 05V520000): Wiper motor assembled without grease, leading to failure under heavy rain.

What sets it apart 6

Reliability

  • Proven 4.6L V8 Reliability — The 4.6L Triton V8 engine is renowned for its robustness and exceptional longevity, offering peace of mind and lasting performance over many hundreds of thousands of miles.

Safety

  • Ultra-Robust Chassis — Featuring an exemplary rigid hydroformed chassis, the F-150 provides a solid and secure foundation, ensuring excellent road holding and unwavering durability.

Comfort

  • Enhanced and Quiet Cabin — Enjoy a spacious interior with significantly improved finish quality and refined sound insulation, transforming every journey into a pleasant and serene experience.

Practicality

  • Impressive Utility Capabilities — With its top-tier towing and payload capacities, the F-150 is the ideal companion for the most demanding tasks, whether professional or recreational.

Economy

  • Simplified Maintenance & Parts Availability — The widespread popularity of the F-150 ensures excellent availability of spare parts, simplifying maintenance and contributing to the longevity of your vehicle.

Performance

  • Iconic V8 Sound — Be captivated by the powerful and characteristic sound of its V8 engine, an authentic signature that enhances the driving pleasure of this legendary pickup.

Alternatives

  • Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500 (GMT800 / GMT900): Vortec V8 engines (5.3L) generally more reliable than the Ford 5.4L, but inferior interior finish.
  • Ram 1500 (DR/DH Generation): High-performance 5.7L HEMI V8 engine, but frequent transmission and rust problems.
  • Toyota Tundra (1st and 2nd generation): The absolute benchmark for reliability (4.7L and 5.7L V8), but gargantuan fuel consumption and very high used prices.
  • Nissan Titan (1st generation): Powerful 5.6L V8, but rear axle and exhaust manifold problems.

Verdict

Buying an 11th generation Ford F-150 (2004-2008) is a double-edged sword. If you opt for the 4.6L V8, you get an extremely robust pickup, well-finished for its time and capable of enduring hundreds of thousands of kilometers without flinching. On the other hand, the widespread 5.4L Triton 3V V8 is to be avoided unless you have formal proof (invoices) that the complete timing system (chains, guides, tensioners, camshaft phasers/cam phasers), the oil pump, and the spark plugs have been recently replaced. Data from J.D. Power and the ADAC confirm that this model sits in the segment average, but this average hides the great disparity in reliability between its engines.
