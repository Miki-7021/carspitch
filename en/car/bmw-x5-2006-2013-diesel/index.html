Summary

BMW X5 (E70) : The Benchmark Dynamics SUV

The second-generation BMW X5 (E70) marked a significant leap over its predecessor (E53). It introduced a double-wishbone front suspension, vastly improving its already class-leading handling, and grew in size to accommodate an optional third row of seats. It also debuted the iDrive system as standard and the electronic 'joystick' gear selector.

A major Life Cycle Impulse (LCI / facelift) occurred in 2010, bringing updated styling, an 8-speed ZF automatic transmission (replacing the 6-speed), and a complete overhaul of the engine lineup. While the E70 remains a phenomenal driving machine with a high-quality interior, it is a complex, heavy vehicle. Maintenance costs are premium, and reliability varies drastically depending on the chosen engine. The diesel variants (M57 and N57) are the most popular globally due to their blend of torque and fuel economy, but they require meticulous maintenance to avoid catastrophic failures.

Strengths 6

  • Class-leading handling and driving dynamics for an SUV
  • Excellent ZF 6-speed and 8-speed automatic transmissions
  • High-quality interior materials and solid build quality
  • M57 diesel engine is a legendary, durable workhorse
  • Spacious cabin with optional (though cramped) 3rd-row seating
  • xDrive system is highly effective in poor weather

Weaknesses 6

  • High cost of ownership, maintenance, and parts
  • V8 engines (N62 and especially N63) are financial nightmares
  • N57 diesel timing chain replacement requires engine removal
  • Stiff ride quality, exacerbated by factory run-flat tires
  • Water ingress issues can destroy expensive electronic modules
  • Heavy weight accelerates wear on brakes and suspension components

Check before buying

  • Trunk Water Ingress: Check the side compartments in the trunk. Clogged panoramic roof drains cause water to pool here, frying the EHC (air suspension), PDC, and parking brake modules.
  • Air Suspension: Rear air springs frequently leak. Listen for the compressor running constantly or the rear end sagging after sitting overnight.
  • Transfer Case (xDrive): Jerking or stuttering under acceleration usually means the plastic gear in the transfer case actuator motor is stripped, or the fluid is degraded.
  • Electronic Parking Brake (EMF): The module gears can jam or the module can fail entirely (often due to water ingress).
  • Front Suspension: Lower control arm bushings (thrust arms) wear out quickly due to the vehicle's weight, causing a clunking noise or steering wheel shimmy under braking.
  • Diesel DPF: Ensure the vehicle reaches operating temperature (90°C). If thermostats are broken, the car runs cold and will never regenerate the DPF, leading to a clogged filter.

Official recalls

Official Manufacturer Recalls

  • EGR Cooler (N57 Diesels, 2018+): Massive global recall. The EGR cooler can leak glycol coolant into the intake manifold, mixing with soot and creating a severe fire hazard.
  • Takata Airbags (2016+): Replacement of driver and/or passenger front airbag inflators due to risk of explosion and shrapnel.
  • Front Driveshaft (2014): The front universal joint on the driveshaft can fail, causing loss of power and potential damage to the engine block. Applies to certain xDrive models.
  • Fuel Filter Heater (2011): An electrical short in the fuel filter heater could cause a battery drain or a vehicle fire, even when the ignition is off.
  • Brake Vacuum Pump (V8 models, 2010): Oil could leak into the brake vacuum pump, resulting in a loss of power braking assist.

Verdict

Final Verdict

The BMW X5 E70 is a brilliant luxury SUV that demands respect and a healthy maintenance budget. If you are looking for a diesel, the pre-LCI 3.0d or 35d with the M57 engine is the safest bet. Despite being older, the M57 is fundamentally more robust than the newer N57, provided you address the swirl flaps and thermostats.

If you opt for an LCI diesel (N57), you must budget for a potential timing chain replacement and ensure the EGR recall has been performed. For petrol buyers, the naturally aspirated 3.0si (N52) is the most reliable choice. Absolutely avoid the 50i (N63 V8) unless you have unlimited funds for repairs. Always buy an E70 based on its service history, not its mileage or price tag. A cheap X5 is the most expensive car you will ever own.
