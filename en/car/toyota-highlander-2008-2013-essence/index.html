Toyota Highlander

II (XU40) · 2008 – 2013

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The second-generation Toyota Highlander (code name XU40, also called Kluger in some markets) is a 3-row family SUV (up to 7 seats) designed primarily for North America, Australia, and certain Asian/Eastern European markets. Not officially sold in France at the time (the model only arrived there in its 4th generation), it can be found on the import market. Based on the Camry platform (K platform), it prioritizes ride comfort, interior space, and bulletproof reliability over driving dynamics. It received a facelift in late 2010 for the 2011 model year, bringing a modernized front fascia and, most importantly, a new V6 engine for the hybrid version. It is one of the most reliable vehicles in its segment, acclaimed by Consumer Reports and J.D. Power for its excellent total cost of ownership (TCO).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall reliability (powertrain and electronics)
  • Extremely durable hybrid system (Synergy Drive) with an excellent TCO
  • Generous interior space and modularity (stowable 2nd-row center seat 'Center Stow')
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing worthy of a Lexus
  • Very high resale value on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Clumsy road handling, heavily assisted steering lacking feel
  • Quality of some interior plastics (hard and prone to scratching)
  • 2.7L 4-cylinder engine a bit weak under load (heavy vehicle)
  • Limited access and space in the 3rd row (reserved for children)
  • Rare model in Western Europe (specific body parts on order)

Check before buying

  • VVT-i oil line (on 2008-2009 V6): ensure it is made of metal and not rubber.
  • Water pump (V6): look for traces of dried pink coolant around the pulley.
  • Steering column: a 'clunk' felt in the steering wheel at low speeds indicates wear on the intermediate steering shaft (known issue, not dangerous but annoying).
  • Hybrid Battery: perform a health check (Hybrid Health Check) at Toyota or via an OBD app (e.g., Dr. Prius) to check the voltage delta of the NiMH blocks.
  • Recall history: it is imperative to check that the recalls related to the accelerator and the window switch have been carried out.

Alternatives

* **North America / Asia**: Honda Pilot (more spacious but V6 with VCM sometimes problematic), Ford Explorer (more technological but much lower reliability), Mazda CX-9 (more dynamic but thirsty 3.7L Ford V6 engine), Nissan Pathfinder. * **Europe**: Volvo XC90 I (more premium, available in diesel), Hyundai Santa Fe / Kia Sorento (good diesel alternatives), Lexus RX (same technical base, 5 seats, superior finish).

Verdict

The second-generation Toyota Highlander (2008-2013) is an exceptional choice for anyone looking for an ultra-reliable family SUV, capable of reaching very high mileages with minimal issues. If you buy an early V6 model (2008-2009), it is imperative to check that the VVT-i oil line has been replaced with the metal version. The **king choice is the facelifted Hybrid version (2011-2013)** with the chain-driven 3.5L V6: it offers incomparable driving smoothness, controlled fuel consumption for its size, and bulletproof reliability. Buy with your eyes closed if the maintenance history is clear and the safety recalls (pedal, window switch) have been carried out.
