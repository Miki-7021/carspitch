Summary

The Volkswagen Arteon (internal code 3H7) is a five-door coupe sedan, launched in 2017 to succeed the Passat CC. Based on the Volkswagen Group's modular MQB platform, it positions itself as a more upscale and stylish model than the Passat, with a distinctive design, frameless doors, and a practical tailgate. It is often perceived as a more accessible alternative to premium German coupe sedans.

In 2020, the Arteon received a mid-cycle refresh (facelift) bringing minor aesthetic changes (bumpers, light signature), an infotainment update, new driving aids, and the introduction of plug-in hybrid powertrains (eHybrid) as well as a sporty R version. A Shooting Brake variant was also launched at this time, offering increased cargo volume without sacrificing style.

Overall, the Arteon is appreciated for its comfort, generous interior space (especially in the rear seats and trunk), serious build quality, and driving pleasure. Its reliability is in the good average for the segment, with proven powertrains, but some points of attention, particularly regarding diesel emission control systems and the increasing complexity of hybrid versions.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history : up-to-date logbook, invoices, especially for DSG gearbox oil changes and timing belt replacements (TDI).
  • AdBlue system (TDI) : Check for absence of warning lights, AdBlue consumption, and history of NOx sensor or pump replacements.
  • Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF - TDI) : Ensure the vehicle has had sufficient road use for regenerations. Suspicious smoke or loss of power may indicate a clogged DPF.
  • DSG gearbox : Test gear changes when cold and hot. Look for jerks, slipping, or abnormal noises. Oil change every 60,000 km is crucial.
  • Engine (TSI) : Check oil level and absence of abnormal noises (knocking, turbo whistling).
  • Hybrid system (eHybrid) : Check actual electric range, proper functioning of thermal/electric transitions, and absence of warning lights related to the battery or hybrid system.
  • Onboard electronics : Test infotainment, driving aids (Front Assist, Lane Assist), parking sensors.
  • General condition : Tire and brake wear (heavy vehicle), wheel condition (large sizes sensitive to curbs), frameless door operation (windows).
  • Manufacturer recalls : Check if all recalls have been performed (via VIN at a dealership).

Official recalls

Official recalls for the Volkswagen Arteon (3H7) have mainly concerned software updates and issues with fastenings or sensors. Here is a non-exhaustive list of the most significant recalls :

  • 2018 (KBA) : Engine control unit software issue on some 2.0 TDI, potentially leading to reduced power or irregular operation.
  • 2019 (KBA) : Risk of rear spoiler detachment on Arteon R versions due to insufficient fastening.
  • 2019 (KBA) : Potentially defective panoramic sunroof fastening, risking detachment in the event of an accident.
  • 2020 (KBA, Rappel Conso) : Defective eCall emergency call system software, potentially preventing correct transmission of vehicle position in the event of an accident.
  • 2020-2021 (KBA) : Wheel fastening issue (loose wheel nuts) on some vehicles, requiring inspection and retightening.
  • 2021 (KBA) : Possible fuel leak at the fuel filter on some 2.0 TDI, posing a fire risk.
  • 2022 (KBA) : Software issue for the Front Assist emergency braking system, potentially leading to incorrect obstacle detection or unintended braking.
  • 2023 (KBA) : Engine hood fastening issue, risking unexpected opening while driving.

It is imperative to check the history of recalls performed on a specific vehicle via its chassis number (VIN) with a Volkswagen dealership.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Arteon is an excellent choice for those seeking a stately, comfortable, and stylish sedan, without opting for a more expensive premium brand. Its general reliability is good, relying on proven technical foundations from the VW group.

For high-mileage drivers and highway journeys, the 2.0 TDI is an excellent companion, offering exemplary fuel consumption and generous torque. However, particular vigilance is required regarding the AdBlue system and the DPF, which can be a source of problems if maintenance is neglected or if usage is predominantly urban. Prioritize models with a clear service history and regular long-distance trips.

The 2.0 TSI models offer more dynamic performance and are generally reliable, but monitoring oil consumption and rigorous DSG gearbox maintenance are essential. The R version is a real pleasure for performance enthusiasts.

The eHybrid is interesting for urban and suburban use, provided it can be recharged frequently to maximize electric range and minimize consumption. Its technical complexity requires particular attention to the software update history.

Buying advice : Negotiate the price, especially for diesel models which may be harder to resell. Ensure all manufacturer recalls have been performed and that the service history is complete and transparent. A thorough test drive is essential to check the proper functioning of the DSG gearbox and the absence of abnormal noises or warning lights. With these precautions, the Arteon remains a highly recommendable car.
