Summary

The first-generation Mazda CX-5 (chassis code KE) marked a major turning point for the Japanese brand. Launched in 2012, it was the first model to fully embody the 'Kodo – Soul of Motion' design philosophy and integrate all 'Skyactiv' technologies (engines, transmissions, chassis, bodywork). Positioned in the highly competitive compact SUV segment, it stood out for its dynamic road handling, controlled consumption, and bold design.

A facelift occurred in late 2014 (2015 models), bringing aesthetic improvements (headlights, grille, taillights), better sound insulation, slightly revised interior materials, and the integration of new driving assistance technologies.

Overall, the CX-5 KE is a pleasant vehicle to drive and well-equipped. However, its reliability is highly dependent on the chosen engine, with the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engine having experienced recurring problems that tarnish its overall record.

Strengths 6

  • Kodo design still current and elegant
  • Dynamic and precise road handling
  • Very reliable and efficient Skyactiv-G gasoline engines
  • Decent cabin space and boot volume
  • Good equipment level (depending on trim)
  • Controlled fuel consumption (all engine types)

Weaknesses 5

  • Problematic reliability of the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engine (oil dilution, clogging, timing chain)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on models before the 2015 facelift
  • Quality of some interior plastics (before facelift)
  • Outdated multimedia system on early models
  • Firm suspension for some passengers

Check before buying

  • For the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel: Complete maintenance history (invoices for frequent oil changes), oil level check (no overfill), careful listening to the engine (chain noises, knocking), DPF status (no warning light), braking efficiency (vacuum pump).
  • For all engine types: Condition of tires and brakes, air conditioning operation, condition of tailgate struts, absence of engine or other warning lights on the dashboard, operation of onboard electronics.
  • Check body panel alignment and absence of repair traces following an accident.

Official recalls

Official Recalls Mazda CX-5 I (KE)

  • 2012-2014 (2.2 Skyactiv-D Engines): Vacuum pump issue.
  • Subject: Possible failure of the braking system's vacuum pump, which could lead to reduced braking assistance and an increased braking distance.
  • Affected population: Vehicles equipped with the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engine produced between 2012 and 2014. (European and Japanese recall, similar to an NHTSA recall for other Mazda models in the USA).
  • 2012-2016 (2.2 Skyactiv-D Engines): Oil dilution and clogging issue.
  • Subject: Risk of engine oil dilution by fuel (injector seal leaks) and/or excessive engine clogging, which could lead to premature wear of the engine, turbo, or even engine failure. Software updates and parts replacements (injector seals, timing chain on early models) have been carried out as part of service campaigns or specific market recalls.
  • Affected population: All vehicles equipped with the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engine of this generation. It is crucial to check if the corrective actions have been applied.
  • 2012-2017 (All engine types): Tailgate struts.
  • Subject: The tailgate struts may lose effectiveness, making opening and holding the tailgate difficult, or even dangerous.
  • Affected population: All first-generation CX-5s.
  • 2014-2016 (Certain markets, to be checked): Fuel pump control module.
  • Subject: Possible failure of the fuel pump control module, which could lead to engine stalling or inability to start.
  • Affected population: Certain models, primarily in the North American market, but it is important to check if the CX-5 KE is specifically affected in other markets via the chassis number.

Verdict

The first-generation Mazda CX-5 is a compact SUV that appeals with its timeless design, driving pleasure, and Skyactiv-G gasoline engines of exemplary reliability. If you are looking for a versatile and reliable vehicle, the gasoline versions (2.0L or 2.5L) are an excellent choice and deserve a "BUY" without hesitation, provided regular maintenance.

However, the picture is much less rosy for the 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel versions. Despite attractive performance and consumption, recurring problems with oil dilution, clogging, and timing chain issues on early models make them a risky choice. If you are considering a diesel, it is imperative to target a post-facelift model (from 2015 onwards) and meticulously check the maintenance history, recalls performed, and absence of symptoms. Even in this case, very rigorous maintenance and appropriate use (long journeys) are essential. For these versions, the verdict is rather "NEGOTIATE" with strong reservations, or even "AVOID" for older or poorly maintained models.

In summary, prioritize gasoline for peace of mind. If diesel is your only option, be extremely vigilant and prepare for more demanding maintenance.
