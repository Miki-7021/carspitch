Summary

The facelifted first-generation MG HS (and its plug-in hybrid variant MG EHS) is a compact family SUV that marked the strong return of the Sino-British brand (SAIC Motor group) to the international stage. This 2021 facelift brought a more aggressive front end, inspired by the MG5 sedan, and a technological update. Positioned as a very well-equipped 'budget' alternative to the segment leaders (Peugeot 3008, Hyundai Tucson), it appeals with its roominess and its 7-year manufacturer warranty (in Europe).

**⚠️ EXPERT NOTE REGARDING DIESEL:** Although the request targets a diesel engine, **the MG HS was never designed or marketed with a diesel engine**, in any global market. The SAIC group made the strategic choice to focus exclusively on gasoline (T-GDI) and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) for this model. This sheet therefore exhaustively details the engines that actually exist for this vehicle.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio in the segment
  • Generous roominess and rear seat space
  • Well-crafted interior finish (soft-touch materials, sport seats)
  • Reassuring manufacturer warranty (7 years or 150,000 km in Europe)
  • Appreciable suspension comfort on long journeys

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of a diesel engine (penalizing for very high-mileage drivers)
  • Slow, unergonomic infotainment system prone to bugs
  • Fuel consumption of gasoline engines (1.5T and 2.0T) above the competition
  • Clumsy road handling, steering lacking feedback
  • Gearbox management (DCT or EDU) sometimes erratic

Check before buying

  • Software update history (crucial for the multimedia system and hybrid management)
  • Smoothness of the automatic gearbox during low-speed maneuvers
  • Front tire wear (the high torque of the PHEV version puts strain on the front axle)
  • Operation of the charging flap and T2 cable on the EHS version
  • Body panel alignment (quality control sometimes uneven from the factory)

Verdict

The facelifted MG HS (and EHS) is a pragmatic choice par excellence. It does not offer the dynamism of a Peugeot 3008 nor the energy efficiency of a Toyota RAV4, but it compensates with an aggressive price, abundant equipment, and a long-term warranty. **The absence of a diesel engine** forces high-mileage drivers to turn to the PHEV version (EHS), which is only relevant if charged daily. On the used market, it is a vehicle to buy with full awareness: aim for a model whose multimedia system has been updated and scrupulously check the maintenance history to retain the benefit of the 7-year warranty.
