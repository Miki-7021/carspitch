Summary

The third-generation Hyundai Tucson (codename TL) marks a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer. Replacing the ix35 in Europe, it reclaims the Tucson nameplate globally. This model allowed the brand to make a spectacular leap in terms of design, perceived quality, and road handling, bringing it closer to European standards. Based on the same platform as its cousin, the Kia Sportage IV, it offers excellent cabin space and rich equipment. The mid-cycle refresh (Phase 2) that occurred in mid-2018 modernized the front fascia, improved the quality of interior plastics, and, most importantly, revised the mechanical offering (replacement of the 1.7 CRDi by the 1.6 CRDi Smartstream, introduction of 48V mild-hybridization). Overall, it's a very well-designed SUV, whose mechanical reliability (especially in diesel) is among the best in its category, although the DCT-7 dual-clutch transmission needs to be monitored.

Strengths 6

  • Overall reliability of diesel engines (very durable CRDi blocks)
  • Generous cabin space and boot volume (513 liters in conventional thermal version)
  • Ergonomics of controls and intuitive infotainment system
  • Sound and safe road handling
  • 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer's warranty (guarantee of good follow-up if the vehicle has been serviced within the network)
  • Significantly improved sound insulation compared to the ix35

Weaknesses 5

  • DCT-7 dual-clutch gearbox (on 1.7 CRDi and 1.6 T-GDI) prone to premature wear and jerks
  • Quality of certain interior plastics on Phase 1 (before 2018)
  • High fuel consumption of gasoline engines (especially the 1.6 T-GDI)
  • Significant vehicle weight which penalizes dynamism and consumption
  • Fickle AdBlue system on the 1.6 CRDi (Phase 2)

Check before buying

  • DCT-7 gearbox behavior: no juddering or hesitation should be felt during starts or low-speed maneuvers.
  • Maintenance history: crucial to ensure that annual services have been performed, a prerequisite for the 5-year warranty.
  • Dashboard warning lights: particularly the engine or AdBlue light on 1.6 CRDi (Phase 2) versions.
  • Operation of the multimedia system and rearview camera (some black screen bugs reported).
  • Condition of the DPF and EGR valve on diesels (2.0 CRDi and 1.7 CRDi) if the vehicle has been driven extensively in the city (unstable idle, loss of power).

Official recalls

  • September 2020 (ABS/ESP Module - HECU): Major global recall. A short circuit in the hydraulic electronic control unit (HECU) of the braking system can cause a fire in the engine compartment, even when stationary. Affects models produced between 2015 and 2020. Fuse or module replacement.
  • June 2016 (Hood Latch): The secondary hood latch may not engage correctly, risking opening while driving. Affects models produced between May 2015 and March 2016.
  • December 2018 (2.0 CRDi Engine): Possible leak at the brake vacuum pump, leading to a loss of braking assistance. Affects a small series of 2015-2016 models.
  • Theta II Engines (North America only): Multiple recalls for engine inspection and replacement due to connecting rod bearing failures (risk of fire and stalling).

Verdict

The Hyundai Tucson III is an excellent choice on the used car market, particularly for high-mileage drivers looking for a reliable diesel SUV. The CRDi blocks (1.7, 1.6, and 2.0) are among the most robust diesel engines of their generation, often surpassing their European competitors in terms of engine block longevity.

The expert's choice: The 2.0 CRDi (136 or 185 hp) paired with the conventional automatic gearbox (6-speed or 8-speed automatic) is the pinnacle of reliability for this model. It is indestructible and does not suffer from the issues of the dual-clutch gearbox.

If you opt for the 1.7 CRDi or the 1.6 CRDi, opt for a manual gearbox. If you absolutely insist on the automatic gearbox (DCT-7), ensure that the clutch has been replaced or that it operates with perfect fluidity, both cold and hot. Avoid gasoline versions imported from North America (2.0 and 2.4 GDI) due to their serious design flaws.
