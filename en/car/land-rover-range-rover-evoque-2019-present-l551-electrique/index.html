Summary

The second-generation Land Rover Range Rover Evoque (L551) was introduced in 2019. Built on the new Premium Transverse Architecture (PTA), it was designed from the ground up to accommodate electrified powertrains, including Mild-Hybrid (MHEV) and Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV) systems. While it retains the highly successful, fashion-forward silhouette of its predecessor (L538), it features a completely overhauled interior with dual touchscreens, improved material quality, and enhanced refinement. Despite Land Rover's efforts to improve build quality, independent data shows that the L551 still struggles significantly with reliability, particularly concerning electronics, software, and suspension components.

Strengths 5

  • Stunning, timeless exterior design that retains strong residual desirability.
  • High-quality, luxurious interior materials and finish.
  • Genuine off-road capability with Terrain Response 2 and impressive wading depth (600mm).
  • Pivi Pro infotainment system (2021+) is a massive improvement in usability and stability.
  • Refined ride quality and excellent cabin noise insulation.

Weaknesses 6

  • Poor overall reliability: What Car? rating of 2/5 (34% of owners reported breakdowns over 12 months).
  • High defect rate at technical inspections: TÜV reports a 20% defect rate at 3 years (below average).
  • Rampant electronic and software glitches (blanking screens, camera failures).
  • Suspension components are prone to premature wear.
  • Cramped rear seating and a relatively small boot compared to segment rivals.
  • High running costs, expensive parts, and premium dealership labor rates.

Check before buying

  • Electronics & Infotainment: Thoroughly test all screens, cameras, and digital dials. Ensure the center console does not go blank on startup (known NHTSA recall issue).
  • 48V MHEV System: Verify that the vehicle has received the critical recall for the 48V electrical overload/MOSFET issue to prevent fire risks.
  • Suspension: Listen for knocking or clunking noises over speed bumps, indicating worn bushes or dampers (a common TÜV failure point).
  • Diesel Emissions Systems: On D-badged models, check the service history for frequent oil changes and ensure no DPF warning lights are present.
  • Water Ingress: Check the rearview camera functionality and inspect the gear shifter area for moisture (both subject to official recalls).

Official recalls

The L551 Evoque has been subject to numerous official recalls across different markets.

Safety & Airbags:

  • Passenger Airbag Tearing: The passenger airbag may tear during deployment due to improper folding during assembly (NHTSA 25V454000, Rappel Conso 2026-04-13).
  • Inflator Degradation: Ammonium nitrate inflator may rupture due to propellant degradation (Rappel Conso 2026-05-06).
  • Seatbelts: Incorrect specification of the 2nd-row seatbelt retractor (Rappel Conso 2021-01-15); locking mechanism failure (Rappel Conso 2021-06-25).

Electrical & Fire Risks:

  • 48V MHEV Overload: An electrical overload in the 48V system can cause MOSFET failure, leading to a fire risk (Rappel Conso 2021-05-07).
  • DC/DC Converter: Issues with the propulsion system's DC/DC converter (NHTSA 26V248000).
  • Battery Ground Stud: Insufficient contact of the battery ground stud with the chassis, causing lighting failures (Rappel Conso 2021-05-07).
  • Steering Column Wiring: Harness routing lacks sufficient clearance to the metal column, risking chafing and short circuits (Rappel Conso 2021-05-07).

Software & Sensors:

  • Blanking Touchscreen: Center console touchscreen may go blank at startup (NHTSA 25V016000).
  • Camera Failures: Water ingress in the rearview camera causing image failure (NHTSA 25V645000); Near Field Sensing Module issues (NHTSA 24V678000).
  • Gear Shifter: Moisture entering the gear position indicator (NHTSA 25V705000).

Other:

  • Fuel Hose: Defective support wire mesh in the fuel return hose (Rappel Conso 2021-09-24).
  • Climate Seats: Laminated bonded seat material fails burn rate requirements (Rappel Conso 2021-01-15).

Verdict

The Land Rover Range Rover Evoque (L551) remains one of the most desirable premium compact SUVs on the market, offering unmatched style, a luxurious cabin, and genuine off-road pedigree. However, its reliability record is objectively poor. With a 20% defect rate at the 3-year mark according to TÜV, a dismal 2/5 reliability score from What Car? (34% breakdown rate), and a long list of serious recalls (including fire risks and airbag failures), ownership can be a frustrating and expensive experience. While ADAC notes that the L551 is an improvement over the older L538—particularly regarding the Aisin gearbox and the newer Pivi Pro system—electrical and software gremlins remain rampant. We strongly advise against buying an Evoque out of warranty. If you must have one, ensure it has a flawless, fully documented service history, that all recalls have been performed, and negotiate a comprehensive extended warranty.
