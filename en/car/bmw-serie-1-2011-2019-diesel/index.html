Summary

The BMW 1 Series F20 (5-door) and F21 (3-door) represents the second generation of the Bavarian compact. It is historically significant because it is the last rear-wheel drive 1 Series, offering unrivaled driving pleasure in its category. Its career is split into two distinct phases: Phase 1 (2011-2015) with a divisive design and improvable engines (N generation), and Phase 2 (LCI, 2015-2019) which corrects the aesthetic issues and introduces the new modular engine family (B generation), significantly more reliable. Although this sheet focuses on diesel engines, very popular on this model for high-mileage drivers, the entire range is covered. Overall score: 7.5/10 (highly dependent on the year and engine).

Strengths 5

  • Unique rear-wheel drive architecture in the segment (50/50 weight distribution).
  • Excellent ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox (smooth and responsive).
  • Remarkable reliability of B-generation engines (Phase 2 from 2015).
  • Ergonomics of the iDrive infotainment system (the best of its generation).
  • Extremely low fuel consumption of diesel engines (B47/B37).

Weaknesses 5

  • Mediocre rear passenger space and boot volume (penalized by the transmission tunnel).
  • Disastrous reliability of N47 (diesel) and N13 (petrol) engines in Phase 1.
  • Interior finish of early models (2011-2014) unworthy of the BMW badge.
  • High maintenance cost in the official network.
  • Average sound insulation on 3-cylinder diesel versions (B37).

Check before buying

  • Timing chain history: Crucial on N47 diesels (116d, 118d, 120d before 2015). Demand invoices.
  • EGR cooler (Diesels): Check if the vehicle has undergone the manufacturer's recall (proven fire risk).
  • Steering rack: Clunking or stiff spots during stationary maneuvers (recurrent problem on F20).
  • Gearbox: Demand proof of ZF8 automatic gearbox oil change if mileage exceeds 100,000 km.
  • Water ingress: Check for moisture in the rear lights and under the boot floor.
  • Tire wear: Pronounced asymmetrical wear indicates a geometry misalignment (sensitive on this chassis).

Official recalls

  • EGR Cooler (2018, 2019, 2022): Massive recall on 4 and 6-cylinder diesel engines (including B47 and N47). Coolant leak in the EGR module can create incandescent soot and cause an intake manifold fire.
  • Takata Airbags (2020+): Replacement of the driver airbag gas generator (risk of metallic fragment projection).
  • Steering box (2019): Replacement of the fixing screws for the electric power steering box which can corrode and break.
  • Fuel filter heater (2014): On diesel models produced until 2011/2012, risk of short circuit in the diesel heater (fire risk).

  • Unique Driving Pleasure with Rear-Wheel Drive — Enjoy unparalleled driving pleasure thanks to its unique rear-wheel drive architecture in the segment, offering perfect weight distribution (50/50) and authentic sporty sensations.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Audi A3 (8V) (superior finish, FWD/Quattro), Mercedes A-Class (W176) (attractive design, Renault engines on entry-level), Volkswagen Golf VII (more homogeneous and spacious), Volvo V40.
  • Asia / Japan: Lexus CT200h (ultra-reliable but less dynamic hybrid), Mazda 3.
  • Emerging markets: Often competed by local three-box sedans, or the Mercedes A-Class sedan.

Verdict

The BMW 1 Series F20/F21 is a two-faced car. Phase 1 (2011-2015) should be approached with extreme caution: N47 diesel and N13 petrol engines are plagued by costly timing chain problems. Unless there's a clear history with the chain already replaced, it's better to AVOID them. In contrast, Phase 2 (LCI, from mid-2015) transforms the car. The introduction of modular B37/B47 (diesel) and B38/B48/B58 (petrol) engines makes it one of the most reliable and desirable compacts on the market, enhanced by its unique rear-wheel drive architecture. For a high-mileage driver, a 118d or 120d Phase 2 (B47 engine) coupled with the ZF8 automatic gearbox is a masterful choice, provided that the EGR recall has been carried out.
