Summary

The fourth-generation Audi A6, codenamed C7 (Typ 4G), marked a technological turning point for the four-ring brand. Launched in 2011 and facelifted in late 2014 (Phase 2), it is based on the modular MLB platform, incorporating a significant amount of aluminum to reduce its weight. Positioned in the highly competitive premium executive car segment (E-Segment), it stands out for its exceptional build quality, sound insulation, and technological content.

Although the European market was dominated by diesel engines (TDI), petrol units (TFSI/FSI) enjoyed strong international success and are regaining popularity in the used car market due to traffic restrictions. The reliability of this generation is generally good, but it is heavily dependent on the production year (post-facelift models from 2014 are significantly more reliable) and the type of gearbox (the Multitronic continuously variable transmission in early front-wheel-drive models is to be avoided, while the 8-speed Tiptronic is a model of robustness).

Strengths 5

  • Benchmark build and finish quality (ages exceptionally well)
  • Sound insulation and ride comfort (especially with air suspension)
  • Enjoyment and robustness of the 3.0 TFSI supercharged V6
  • Efficiency of the Quattro (Torsen) system on V6 and V8 versions
  • Intuitive MMI infotainment system, still relevant (Phase 2)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 TFSI engines before 2014 (excessive oil consumption)
  • Multitronic automatic gearbox (on Phase 1 FWD models) fragile and unpleasant
  • Very high maintenance and spare parts cost (especially S6/RS6)
  • Significant bulk (nearly 5 meters) and turning radius
  • Standard suspension (without adaptive damping) a bit firm at low speeds

Check before buying

  • Clear service history for automatic gearbox oil changes (S tronic every 60k km, Tiptronic recommended at 80k-100k km).
  • Metallic rattling noise on cold start (sign of timing chain tensioner wear on V6/V8).
  • Functionality of the retractable MMI screen (plastic gears in the mechanism tend to break).
  • Condition of the air suspension bellows (Adaptive Air Suspension): check that the car does not sag after prolonged parking.
  • On S6/RS6 (4.0 TFSI): demand proof of turbo oil screen replacement (otherwise imminent engine/turbo failure risk).

Official recalls

  • - **Takata Airbags (Various years)**: Replacement of driver/passenger frontal airbag inflators. - **Coolant Pump (2011-2017 - 2.0 TFSI)**: Risk of overheating and short-circuit of the auxiliary water pump, potentially leading to fire. Software update or replacement. - **Fuel Rail (2012-2013 - 3.0 TFSI)**: Risk of fuel leakage from the fuel rails, potentially causing a petrol smell or fire. - **Turbo Oil Screen (2013-2017 - 4.0 TFSI)**: Recall/Service action (depending on markets) to replace the oil screen filter which clogs and destroys the turbochargers. - **Auxiliary Heater (2011-2015)**: Risk of overheating of the electric auxiliary heater (PTC) connector, which can melt or cause a fire.

Verdict

The Audi A6 C7 is a masterful executive car that offers high-level performance, but it requires an informed buyer. If you are targeting a petrol engine, **at all costs avoid the 2.0 TFSI models from before 2014** (Phase 1) as well as models equipped with the Multitronic gearbox.

The **top petrol choice is the 3.0 TFSI (supercharged V6)**: it offers remarkable reliability, V8-worthy enjoyment, and pairs perfectly with the S tronic or Tiptronic gearbox. For a more reasonable budget, a Phase 2 2.0 TFSI (after late 2014, EA888 Gen 3 engine) is an excellent compromise. Plan for a maintenance budget commensurate with the badge, particularly for gearbox oil changes and running gear.
