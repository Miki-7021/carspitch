Summary

The first-generation Hyundai IONIQ 5 (code NE) revolutionized the electric vehicle market upon its release thanks to its neo-retro design and its E-GMP platform with 800V architecture, allowing for ultra-fast charging. The facelift (mid-cycle update), gradually introduced between 2023 and 2025 depending on the markets, corrects the main ergonomic flaw of the initial model by adding a rear wiper. It also brings higher capacity batteries (going from 77.4 kWh to 84 kWh for the Long Range version, and from 58 to 63 kWh for the entry-level), structural reinforcements to reduce vibrations, a recalibrated suspension, and additional physical buttons in the cabin. This period also marks the introduction of the ultra-sporty IONIQ 5 N (650 hp) version, praised by critics. Although generally very reliable, the model remains marred by recurring problems related to the ICCU (Integrated Charging Control Unit) affecting the 12V battery.

Strengths 6

  • 800V architecture allowing a 10 to 80% charge in 18 minutes
  • Gigantic interior space (3-meter wheelbase) and flat floor
  • Correction of the main ergonomic flaw: addition of the rear wiper on the facelift
  • Very practical V2L (Vehicle-to-Load) functionality
  • Improved suspension comfort on 2024/2025 versions
  • N version offering an unprecedented and playful driving experience for an EV

Weaknesses 6

  • Endemic ICCU (Integrated Charging Control Unit) problem on the E-GMP platform
  • Frequent 12V battery failures (often linked to the ICCU)
  • Imperfect aerodynamics penalizing highway range at 130 km/h
  • Large turning radius (especially on AWD versions)
  • Imposing size (width) complicating urban parking
  • Maintenance cost related to the specific coolant (low conductivity)

Check before buying

  • Manufacturer recall status: DEMAND proof of the ICCU update or replacement.
  • 12V battery condition: if the vehicle has suffered deep discharges, it must be replaced.
  • Tire wear: instant torque and weight (over 2 tons) wear out the rubber quickly, especially on AWDs.
  • Coolant: check the level and replacement history of the low-conductivity coolant (specific to high-voltage batteries).
  • Suspension noises: listen for any clunking at low speeds (although the facelift improved this point, recalls exist on the mountings).

Verdict

The Hyundai IONIQ 5 Facelift (2023-2025) brilliantly corrects the early flaws of the first iteration, notably by adding the essential rear wiper and optimizing range and comfort. It is one of the most accomplished electric vehicles on the market thanks to its ultra-fast 800V charging and its royal interior space. The N version is an engineering masterpiece for enthusiasts. However, overall reliability is tarnished by the recurring ICCU (charge and 12V battery management) problem. A recent used or new purchase is highly recommended, on the *sine qua non* condition of verifying that all recall campaigns (especially software and hardware concerning the ICCU) have been scrupulously applied by the Hyundai network.
