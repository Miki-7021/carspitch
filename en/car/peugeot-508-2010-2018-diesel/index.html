Summary

The **first-generation Peugeot 508 (Type 8D for the sedan, 8E for the SW estate, and RXH)** had the heavy task of simultaneously replacing the 407 and 607. Launched in late 2010, it marked a significant move upmarket for the lion brand with a refined finish, advanced sound insulation, and imperial road handling, true to Peugeot's reputation. The mid-life facelift (Phase 2) in 2014 modernized the front end (verticalized grille, Full LED headlights) and updated the engines to meet the Euro 6 standard (BlueHDi generation). Designed as a grand tourer, it particularly shines with its diesel engines, its historical core range. While the 2.0 HDi engines before 2015 are benchmarks for reliability, the introduction of SCR (AdBlue) depollution systems on BlueHDi engines and the issues with THP petrol engines (PSA/BMW design) slightly tarnish the overall assessment.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional road handling (comfort/road holding compromise)
  • Remarkable reliability of 2.0 HDi engines (Euro 5 generation: 140 and 163 hp)
  • Finish quality and material aging in the cabin
  • Very refined sound insulation, worthy of the upper segment
  • Generous boot volume on the SW (estate) version
  • Decoupled front axle on GT versions (2.2 HDi and 2.0 BlueHDi 180) offering diabolical precision

Weaknesses 6

  • Massive failures of AdBlue tanks (SCR system) on BlueHDi engines
  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 THP 156 hp petrol engine (timing chain)
  • Catastrophic driving experience of the BMP6 automated manual gearbox (slowness, jerks)
  • Multimedia system (RT6 / SMEG) slow, dated, and prone to bugs (black screens)
  • Average rear passenger space for its size (4.79 m)
  • Lack of practical storage compartments in the front

Check before buying

  • **AdBlue system (BlueHDi):** Check for the absence of 'UREA' or 'Engine fault' warning lights. Ask if the tank has already been replaced (invoice of 1000 to 1500€ otherwise).
  • **Timing chain history (THP 156):** Demand invoices proving the replacement of the complete timing chain kit.
  • **Flywheel (1.6 HDi 112/115):** Test the clutch. Slippage or vibrations at idle indicate a worn dual-mass flywheel.
  • **Onboard electronics:** Test the GPS, Bluetooth, power windows, and keyless entry (door handle sensors are prone to moisture).
  • **Running gear:** On GT versions, check for wear of the complex front axle's silentblocks, which are more expensive to replace than the standard pseudo-MacPherson.

Official recalls

  • **November 2011:** Recall on 2.0 HDi for a risk of starter motor wiring harness chafing.
  • **December 2012:** Replacement of the vacuum pump on 2.0 HDi engines (risk of loss of braking assistance).
  • **October 2015:** Diesel pre-heater wiring harness on 2.0 HDi (risk of overheating and melting of the diesel filter cover).
  • **2016 - 2019 (Silent campaigns):** Replacement of AdBlue tank caps on BlueHDi to prevent tank depressurization.
  • **February 2020:** Recall (code JZR) concerning the particulate filter (DPF) on certain BlueHDi models (risk of emission non-compliance).

Verdict

The Peugeot 508 I is an excellent grand tourer that is best appreciated on major roads. **If you are looking for a reliable and economical diesel, the 2.0 HDi 140 or 163 hp (Euro 5, before 2015) is the absolute best buy.** It offers superior driving pleasure to the 1.6 HDi while avoiding the costly AdBlue problems of later BlueHDi generations. The BlueHDi versions (120, 150, 180) are excellent to drive, especially when paired with the EAT6 gearbox, but require a contingency budget for the SCR system. On the petrol side, avoid the THP 156 and opt for the THP 165, which is much more refined. Finally, avoid the BMP6/ETG6 automated manual gearbox at all costs, as its outdated driving experience spoils the car's dynamic qualities.
