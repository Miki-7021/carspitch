Summary

The second-generation Mercedes-Benz GLC (internal code name **X254** for the SUV and **C254** for the Coupé, often mistakenly referred to as W254) is based on the MRA2 platform, shared with the C-Class W206. Launched in late 2022 (2023 model year), it marks a major technological breakthrough: **100% of its powertrains are electrified**, ranging from 48V mild hybrid (MHEV) to plug-in hybrid (PHEV) equipped with a huge 31.2 kWh battery offering over 100 km of electric range. Positioned as the brand's global best-seller, it offers an ultra-modern cabin dominated by the MBUX 2.0 system. Although this report focuses on gasoline engines (M254 family), the overall electronic complexity and the significant weight of the PHEV versions (over 2.3 tonnes) are the main reliability challenges for this still very recent generation.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional electric range of PHEV versions (often > 100 km in real conditions)
  • Suspension comfort (especially with the AIRMATIC air suspension option)
  • Advanced onboard technology (MBUX 2.0, Digital Light headlights)
  • Very high level of sound insulation
  • Smoothness of the 9G-Tronic gearbox coupled with the 48V ISG
  • Significantly improved finish quality compared to the previous generation

Weaknesses 6

  • Excessive weight of plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions exceeding 2.3 tonnes
  • Accelerated wear of tires and brake pads on PHEVs
  • All-touch ergonomics (capacitive steering wheel controls) sometimes frustrating
  • Early electronic bugs (black screens, capricious driving aids)
  • Prohibitive purchase prices and option costs
  • Reduced boot volume on PHEV versions (470L vs 620L for MHEV)

Check before buying

  • History of software updates (MBUX and energy management control units)
  • Tire wear (especially on the rear-wheel steering axle if equipped, and on PHEVs)
  • Smooth operation of electric/thermal transition on PHEV models
  • Absence of parasitic noises in the interior trim on degraded roads
  • Verification of recalls concerning the fuel pump and 80A fuses

Verdict

⚖️ Expert's Verdict

The Mercedes GLC X254 is a technological tour de force, particularly impressive in its plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions which redefine the segment's electric range standards. The gasoline powertrains (M254), whether MHEV or PHEV, are mechanically mature and benefit from a much better integrated 2nd generation mild hybridization.

However, this technological extravagance comes at a price: a ponderous weight for PHEVs that compromises agility and accelerates wear of consumables, as well as inevitable early electronic bugs on such a complex network architecture.

**Buying Advice:** If you don't have the possibility to recharge daily, the **GLC 300 4MATIC (Gasoline MHEV)** is the most balanced choice, offering power, smoothness, and contained weight. If you are a daily electric driving enthusiast, the **GLC 300 e** is fantastic, but demand a model for which all recalls and software updates have been performed. In any case, an extended warranty is highly recommended given the cost of electronic components.
