Skoda Scala

I (NW2) (2018–2025) · 2018 – 2025

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The Skoda Scala (Generation I, type NW2) was unveiled at the end of 2018 to replace the Rapid Spaceback. Based on the MQB A0 platform (stretched to its maximum capacity, shared with the VW Polo and Skoda Kamiq), it offers dimensions close to a C-segment compact (VW Golf) with B-segment underpinnings. It is an ultra-rational model, praised for its record rear passenger space and giant boot (467 liters). A restyling (facelift) occurred at the end of 2023 (marketed in 2024), bringing aesthetic touches, optional Matrix LED headlights, and a technological update.

**Diesel Focus (1.6 TDI)**: Although demand for diesel has sharply declined in this segment, Skoda offered the 1.6 TDI (EA288) 115 hp engine at launch. This engine had a very short career on the Scala (removed from the catalog at the end of 2020) due to Euro 6d anti-pollution standards and the disinterest in diesel. However, it remains an excellent choice on the used market for high-mileage drivers, offering remarkable fuel efficiency on the highway.

Strengths 5

  • Rear spaciousness worthy of the segment above (excellent legroom)
  • Record boot volume for the category (467 liters)
  • General ergonomics and presence of physical controls for air conditioning
  • Extremely low fuel consumption of the 1.6 TDI engine on road/highway
  • Very competitive price/performance/equipment ratio on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Very short career of the diesel engine (difficult to find on the recent used market)
  • Frequent electronic bugs on the MIB3 infotainment system (black screens, reboots)
  • DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox still sensitive to wear in 100% urban use (dry clutches)
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speed (wind and road noise)
  • Hard plastics omnipresent in the lower part of the cabin

Check before buying

  • Multimedia system: test all functions, check if the screen freezes or restarts, and ensure that the SOS module (e-Call) does not report any errors on the dashboard.
  • DSG7 gearbox: during the test drive, check for the absence of stuttering, vibrations, or metallic noises when shifting from 1st to 2nd gear.
  • On the 1.6 TDI: check the service history, look for coolant leaks (water pump), and ensure the vehicle has not been used exclusively in the city (DPF/EGR risk).
  • On the 1.5 TSI (manual gearbox): test a cold start to check for the absence of pronounced jerks in first gear.

Alternatives

The Scala is positioned between segment B (versatile city cars) and segment C (compact cars):

* **Europe**: Volkswagen Golf (more expensive, better finish), Seat Leon (more dynamic), Peugeot 308 (better chassis, but problematic PureTech engines), Kia Ceed / Hyundai i30 (long warranties, good CRDi diesel alternatives), Toyota Corolla (hybrid, unbeatable in the city). * **Asia / Oceania**: Mazda 3 (premium finish, naturally aspirated engines), Toyota Corolla Hatchback. * **North America**: N/A (model not sold).

Verdict

The Skoda Scala is one of the most pragmatic and intelligent choices on the used car market. It offers the space of a compact car for the price of a city car.

**Regarding the 1.6 TDI**: It is a robust and extremely frugal engine, perfect for sales representatives or families making regular long journeys. However, its purchase is only justified if you drive more than 20,000 km per year, mostly outside urban areas, to avoid clogging the DPF and EGR valve.

For 80% of buyers, the **1.0 TSI 110/115 hp** will be a more sensible choice, easier to find, less subject to traffic restrictions (LEZ) and just as reliable. Be careful with models equipped with the DSG7 gearbox: opt for a car with a clear history and avoid those that have been used exclusively in the city.
