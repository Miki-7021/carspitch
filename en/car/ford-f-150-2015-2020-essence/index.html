Summary

The 13th generation of the Ford F-150 (codename P552) marked a true revolution in the full-size pickup segment by adopting a military-grade aluminum alloy body, mounted on a high-strength steel frame. This innovation allowed a weight saving of up to 300 kg, improving payload, towing, and fuel consumption. Facelifted in 2018 (new grilles, headlights, and the widespread use of the 10-speed automatic gearbox), it remains the best-selling vehicle in North America. While the aluminum body solved the rust problems of the exterior panels, this generation experienced temperamental beginnings mechanically and electronically, before stabilizing. Overall rating: 7.5/10.

Strengths 5

  • Rust-proof aluminum body (exterior panels)
  • Class-leading towing and payload capacities
  • Gigantic interior space (SuperCrew cab) and flat rear floor
  • Responsive and intuitive SYNC 3 infotainment system (from 2016)
  • Excellent passive safety (5-star NHTSA)

Weaknesses 5

  • Below-average overall reliability early in its run (2015-2016) according to ADAC/Consumer Reports
  • 10-speed automatic gearbox (10R80) often harsh and hesitant
  • Recurring Cam Phasers issues on the 3.5L EcoBoost
  • Marked excessive oil consumption on the 5.0L V8 from 2018 to 2020
  • Size unsuited to European infrastructure (parking, narrow roads)

Check before buying

  • Listen carefully to the 3.5L EcoBoost engine during a cold start (after sitting overnight): a rattling/ticking noise lasting 2 to 5 seconds indicates failed Cam Phasers (very expensive repair).
  • Check the oil consumption history on the 5.0L V8s (2018-2020) and the presence of excessive soot at the exhaust.
  • Test the 10R80 gearbox both cold and hot: violent jerks between 1st, 3rd, and 5th gears are signs of wear on the valve body or the CDF clutch drum.
  • Inspect the lower oil pan on the 2.7L EcoBoost (frequent leaks on pre-2018 plastic pans).
  • Check the operation of the panoramic sunroof (the plastic tracks tend to break).

Performance

  • Exceptional Towing and Payload Capacities — The Ford F-150 stands out with its class-leading towing and payload capacities, allowing you to transport and haul the heaviest loads with ease and confidence.

comfort

  • Spacious and Versatile Cabin — The SuperCrew cab offers a gigantic interior space and a flat rear floor, ensuring optimal comfort for all passengers and remarkable versatility for transporting bulky items.
  • Stable and Comfortable Handling — Despite its size, the F-150 offers stable and predictable handling. Its suspension is finely tuned to absorb road irregularities, ensuring appreciable driving comfort and refined dynamic behavior.

reliability

Alternatives

- North America / Europe Import: Ram 1500 (leader in ride comfort, more luxurious interior, reliable but thirsty Hemi V8), Chevrolet Silverado 1500 / GMC Sierra 1500 (very reliable pushrod V8s, but dated interior before 2022), Toyota Tundra (previous generation: legendary reliability of the 5.7L V8, but technology and fuel consumption from another era).

Verdict

The 13th generation Ford F-150 is a technologically advanced pickup, whose aluminum body is a major asset against corrosion. However, its mechanical reliability is uneven. Data from ADAC and J.D. Power (VDS 2023: average with ~185 PP100, behind the Ram 1500) confirm a difficult start (2015-2016) before an improvement (2017-2020). The choice of engine is crucial: avoid the 3.5L EcoBoost without a clear history of the chains/phasers, and be wary of the 2018-2020 5.0L V8s (oil consumption). The naturally aspirated V6 versions or the 2.7L EcoBoost (post-2018) are the most pragmatic choices. The 6-speed gearbox of the early years is significantly more reliable than the temperamental 10-speed gearbox introduced later.
