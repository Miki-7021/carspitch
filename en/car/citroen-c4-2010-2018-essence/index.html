Citroën C4

II (B7) · 2010 – 2018

All Citroën models

Summary

The second-generation Citroën C4 (code name B7) marked a turning point compared to its predecessor: it abandoned the atypical design (fixed-hub steering wheel, 3-door coupé body) for a much more consensual approach, aiming to directly compete with the Volkswagen Golf.

It stands out for its very high level of ride comfort, careful soundproofing, significantly improved finish quality, and a generous boot (408 liters). A facelift occurred in early 2015, bringing new light signatures (3D lights), a 7-inch touchscreen simplifying the center console, and the introduction of 3-cylinder PureTech petrol engines as well as BlueHDi diesels.

However, its used car career is heavily marred by the disastrous reliability of most of its petrol engines (Prince VTi/THP family, then PureTech) and by AdBlue problems on recent diesels.

Strengths 6

  • Very high level of suspension comfort (typical Citroën)
  • Careful soundproofing, very quiet on the highway
  • Generous boot volume (408 liters, among the best in its category at the time)
  • Significant improvement in material and finish quality compared to the C4 I
  • Remarkable fuel efficiency of diesel engines (HDi and e-HDi)
  • Excellent EAT6 automatic gearbox (available after the 2015 facelift)

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of petrol engines (VTi, THP, PureTech)
  • Slow BMP6 / ETG6 automated manual gearbox, causing jerks and premature clutch wear
  • Recurrent and costly AdBlue tank problem on BlueHDi models
  • Average rear passenger space for its size
  • Slow and unintuitive multimedia system (SMEG) ergonomics
  • Clumsy road behavior, focused on comfort at the expense of dynamism

Check before buying

  • On PureTech: Visual condition of the timing belt via the oil filler opening (cracks, fraying).
  • On VTi / THP: Oil consumption history and invoices for timing chain kit replacement.
  • On BlueHDi: Presence of invoices concerning AdBlue tank replacement or UREA warning light on the dashboard.
  • On HDi / e-HDi: Traces of black soot around the injectors (seal leaks) and turbo whistling.
  • Gearbox: On BMP6/ETG6, check for absence of juddering when starting on a hill. On manual gearbox, test the flywheel (clunking sound when switching off the ignition on HDi 110/115).
  • Electronics: Proper functioning of the touchscreen, GPS, and automatic air conditioning.

Official recalls

  • November 2020 / December 2022 (PureTech) : Massive recall (code JZR/HFC) to check the width of the timing belt and clean the oil pump/vacuum pump strainer if the belt has disintegrated.
  • February 2016 (Bonnet) : Risk of improper bonnet latch fastening which could lead to it opening while driving. Fastening reinforcement.
  • October 2013 (Electrical) : Possible rubbing of the starter harness against the EGR valve on 2.0 HDi engines, risk of short circuit.
  • January 2014 (Braking) : Replacement of front brake caliper fixing screws on a small series produced in 2013.
  • 2018 (DPF) : On some BlueHDi, risk of non-compliance with emissions requiring an ECU update or DPF replacement.

Verdict

The Citroën C4 II is an excellent compact for those who prioritize absolute comfort and cargo space. However, if you are looking for a petrol engine, caution is advised. VTi and THP are to be avoided due to their design flaws (chain, oil). The 1.2 PureTech offers good driving pleasure but requires an impeccable service history (belt changed, frequent oil changes) otherwise engine failure may occur.

Paradoxically, this car is best appreciated and bought with peace of mind in diesel. The 2.0 HDi 150 is indestructible, and the 1.6 e-HDi 115 is an excellent compromise. BlueHDi versions are very pleasant but require a budget set aside for the capricious AdBlue system. Avoid the BMP6/ETG6 gearbox and opt for a facelifted version (post-2015) with a manual or EAT6 gearbox.
