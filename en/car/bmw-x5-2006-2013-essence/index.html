BMW X5

E70 · 2006 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The second-generation BMW X5 (E70) marked a major evolution compared to its predecessor (E53). More imposing, more luxurious, and equipped with advanced onboard technology (iDrive as standard, Adaptive Drive suspension, Active Steering), it also introduced the option of a third row of seats. The facelift (LCI), introduced in spring 2010, brought profound mechanical changes: the abandonment of naturally aspirated engines in favor of turbocharged units (N55 and N63 for gasoline) and the replacement of the 6-speed automatic gearbox with the excellent ZF 8-speed. Although its dynamic qualities are undeniable, the X5 E70 is a heavy and complex vehicle, which translates into high maintenance costs and very uneven reliability depending on the chosen engine. The V8 gasoline versions are particularly feared for their expensive breakdowns.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling for an SUV of this weight
  • Significantly improved finish and assembly quality compared to the E53
  • Fantastic ZF 8-speed automatic gearbox (on LCI models post-2010)
  • Reliability of 6-cylinder gasoline (N52) and diesel (M57) engines
  • Very intuitive and modern iDrive CIC system (from late 2009)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of V8 gasoline engines (N62 and especially N63)
  • Very high weight (often > 2.2 tons) leading to rapid wear of suspension components and brakes
  • Capricious electronics (FRM module, electric parking brake)
  • Frequent water ingress (panoramic sunroof, taillights) destroying control units
  • Cost of spare parts and labor in the BMW network

Check before buying

  • xDrive transfer case (ATC400/700): jerking during acceleration often due to wear of the plastic gear in the actuator (a €20 part but frightening symptoms).
  • Rear air suspension: vehicle sagging when stopped (porous air springs to be replaced).
  • Panoramic sunroof: check for proper operation and especially the absence of moisture in the side compartments of the trunk (where the control units are located).
  • FRM Module (Footwell Module): manages lighting and windows. It easily 'bricks' during a voltage drop or battery change.
  • Suspension components: wear of front thrust arm bushings (braking vibrations) and asymmetrical tire wear (significant negative camber at the rear).
  • Electric parking brake (EMF): the module located under the trunk gets water ingress or the cables seize, blocking the brake.

Verdict

The BMW X5 E70 is an SUV with a dual personality. If equipped with an inline 6-cylinder engine (the 3.0si/30i gasoline or the 30d/35d M57 diesel), it proves to be a robust, powerful, and relatively reliable road companion, provided rigorous maintenance is followed. In contrast, the V8 gasoline versions (4.8i and especially 50i) are true financial black holes, afflicted with major design flaws (N63) that will ruin the ownership experience. Absolutely prioritize a facelifted model (LCI, post-2010) to benefit from the ZF8 gearbox and iDrive CIC, targeting the xDrive35i for gasoline enthusiasts, or the xDrive30d for high-mileage drivers. Avoid V8s without a pristine service history and recent engine overhaul invoices.
