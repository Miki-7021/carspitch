Toyota Auris

I (E150) · 2006 – 2012

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Toyota models

Summary

The first-generation Toyota Auris (E150) had the challenging task of succeeding the iconic Corolla in the European market (although it retained the Corolla name in other markets). Designed specifically to appeal to Europeans, it is based on the MC platform. While its design was often considered consensual or even bland, it excels in interior space and ergonomics (raised gear lever).

However, its reliability record is very mixed. Historically renowned for its indestructible gasoline engines, Toyota stumbled with this generation's large-displacement diesel engines (AD series), which suffered from serious design flaws. Conversely, the introduction of the hybrid version (HSD) in 2010 during the facelift laid the groundwork for exceptional reliability. The 2010 facelift also improved the perceived quality of the interior plastics and revised the suspension settings.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional reliability of gasoline and hybrid (HSD) versions
  • Generous interior space and flat rear floor
  • Ergonomics of the driving position (gear lever falling readily to hand)
  • Robustness of the small 1.4 D-4D diesel engine (especially without DPF)
  • Suspension comfort (improved after the 2010 facelift)

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of 2.0 and 2.2 D-4D diesels (head gaskets)
  • Slow, jerky, and unreliable MMT automated manual gearbox
  • Very disappointing interior plastic quality before 2010 (easily scratched)
  • Reduced boot space on the hybrid version (battery under the floor)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (road noise)

Check before buying

  • For 2.0 and 2.2 diesels: clear history required, check if the engine was replaced under warranty by Toyota (support campaign up to 7 years / 180,000 km, now expired).
  • For the 1.4 D-4D: condition of the EGR valve, turbo whistling, and gearbox operation if it's an MMT (jerks = actuator or clutch failed).
  • For 1.33 gasoline engines: check oil level with the dipstick (risk of excessive consumption due to piston rings).
  • For the hybrid: demand 'Hybrid Health Check' certificates to ensure battery warranty.
  • Steering column: clunking noises when steering at low speeds (worn steering universal joint).

What sets it apart 6

Reliability

  • 1.4 D-4D Diesel Engine: Proven Robustness — The 1.4 D-4D engine is renowned for its robustness and durable design, including a timing chain, ensuring remarkable longevity and peace of mind on the road.

Practicality

  • Spacious and Versatile Interior — Enjoy generous cabin space for all passengers and a flat rear floor, offering superior comfort and great ease of loading for your daily commutes or getaways.

Safety

  • Optimal Safety Certified by Euro NCAP — The Toyota Auris I achieved the maximum rating in Euro NCAP tests, with high scores for adult (89%) and child (82%) protection, ensuring reassuring safety for all occupants.
  • Solid and Controlled Braking — Equipped with ABS and EBD as standard, the Auris I offers solid and reliable braking performance, ensuring effective and controlled stops for increased safety in all circumstances.

Handling

  • Stable and Reassuring Road Handling — Its dynamic behavior focuses on safety and comfort, with predictable and stable road holding. The light steering facilitates maneuvers and ensures a serene driving experience.

Comfort

  • Enhanced Suspension Comfort — Appreciate noticeable suspension comfort, particularly improved after the 2010 facelift, which effectively absorbs road irregularities for more pleasant journeys.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Volkswagen Golf VI (higher trim, TDI diesels made reliable with Common Rail), Peugeot 308 I (excellent road handling, robust HDi diesels), Honda Civic VIII (bold design, very reliable 2.2 i-CTDi engine), Mazda 3 (dynamic and reliable).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Fit/Jazz, Nissan Tiida, Subaru Impreza hatchback.
  • North America: Model not sold (replaced by the Toyota Matrix and Corolla sedan).

Verdict

Buying a used Toyota Auris I requires great vigilance regarding engine choice. If you absolutely want a diesel, only the 1.4 D-4D (90 hp) is recommendable, preferably with a manual gearbox. It is robust, economical, and avoids the flaws of its larger siblings. The 2.0 D-4D and 2.2 D-CAT must be avoided, as the risk of engine failure (head gasket/porous block) is far too high and repairs are prohibitively expensive.

However, the true strength of the Auris lies in its gasoline engines (VVT-i) and especially in its Hybrid (HSD) version, which appeared in 2010. If your budget allows, the Auris HSD is an excellent choice for its bulletproof reliability and negligible maintenance costs.
