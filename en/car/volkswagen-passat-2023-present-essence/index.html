Summary

Volkswagen Passat B9 (Typ 32)

The ninth generation of the Volkswagen Passat (B9) marks a historic turning point for the model: in Europe, it is now **exclusively available as an estate (SW / Variant)**, the sedan having been sacrificed on the altar of SUVs and aerodynamics (although it survives in China under the names Magotan and Passat Pro). Developed jointly with the Skoda Superb (and assembled in the same Bratislava factory), it is based on the **MQB Evo** platform.

This generation corrects the ergonomic errors of the late B8/Golf 8 era: the return of real physical buttons on the steering wheel, finally backlit touch sliders for climate control, and the introduction of the much smoother MIB4 infotainment system (12.9 to 15-inch screen). Technically, it introduces the **DCC Pro** adaptive suspension (dual-valve dampers) and new-generation PHEV powertrains capable of exceeding 100 km in 100% electric mode. Although the model is very recent (lack of long-term hindsight), it relies on evolutions of proven engines (EA211 evo2, EA888 gen4, EA288 evo).

Strengths 5

  • Giant interior space and boot volume (690 to 1920 liters)
  • Exceptional ride comfort with the DCC Pro option
  • Electric range of PHEV versions (19.7 kWh net battery) and DC fast charging
  • Corrected ergonomics (real buttons on the steering wheel, responsive MIB4)
  • Refined aerodynamics (Cd of 0.25) benefiting sound insulation and fuel consumption

Weaknesses 5

  • Disappearance of the sedan body style in Europe
  • Significantly increased weight, especially on eHybrid versions (nearly 1.9 tons)
  • DQ200 dry-clutch DSG7 gearbox still present on the 1.5 eTSI 150hp
  • Prices have become very elitist (near-premium positioning)
  • Exterior design deemed too consensual or similar to the ID range

Check before buying

  • Infotainment software version (demand the latest update at the dealership to avoid black screen bugs or CarPlay/Android Auto connection issues).
  • DSG gearbox behavior at low speeds (potential jerks on the DQ200 of the 1.5 eTSI).
  • Body panel alignment and wind noise (some adjustments needed on the very first models from late 2023).
  • Condition of front tires on eHybrid versions (instant torque and weight wear out the rubber quickly).

Verdict

The Volkswagen Passat B9 is the culmination of VW's thermal lineage. By focusing solely on the estate in Europe, it embraces its role as a large family touring car and fleet vehicle.

**For a petrol engine purchase**, the **1.5 eTSI 150 hp** is the most rational choice: it is extremely frugal thanks to micro-hybridization and aerodynamics, and the EA211 evo2 engine is now very reliable. The only downside: the DQ200 dry-clutch DSG7 gearbox, which requires gentle handling in the city to last. If the budget allows, the **2.0 TSI 204 hp** offers a high level of driving pleasure with a much more robust wet-clutch DSG gearbox (DQ381), ideal for those who drive with heavy loads or in the mountains.

The **eHybrid (PHEV)** versions are technologically impressive (100 km of real-world range), but their technical complexity (thermal engine + electric motor + large battery + complex onboard charger) advises caution for a very long-term purchase outside of warranty.

**Verdict:** An excellent choice for high-mileage drivers seeking supreme comfort, provided that initial software updates have been performed.
