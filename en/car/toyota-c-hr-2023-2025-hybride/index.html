Toyota C-HR

II (AX30) · 2023 – 2025

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

Summary

The **second-generation Toyota C-HR (AX30)**, launched in late 2023, marks a spectacular evolution of the Japanese brand's compact coupe SUV. Designed and produced in Europe (Turkey) specifically for the European market, it completely abandons 100% internal combustion engines to focus exclusively on Toyota's 5th generation hybrid system (HEV) and, for the first time, a plug-in hybrid (PHEV) variant. Based on the optimized TNGA-C platform, it corrects some flaws of the first generation (infotainment, sound insulation) while retaining its polarizing and dynamic design. **Overall reliability rating: Excellent**, supported by Toyota's technological maturity in hybridization.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall reliability (ADAC breakdown rate: 1.1/1000 veh/year, What Car?: 5/5)
  • Formidable efficiency of the 5th generation hybrid system (real-world consumption often below 5L/100km)
  • Significantly improved finish and assembly quality compared to the previous generation
  • Infotainment system (Toyota Smart Connect) finally modern and responsive
  • Dynamic road handling thanks to the TNGA-C platform

Weaknesses 4

  • Rear passenger space still cramped (small window area, feeling of claustrophobia)
  • Average boot volume (388L for 1.8L, 364L for 2.0L, and only 310L for PHEV)
  • Significantly higher purchase price compared to the first generation
  • Driver assistance systems (ADAS) sometimes intrusive (mandatory overspeed audible alerts at each start-up)

Check before buying

  • Charge status and health of the 12V battery (recurrent issue on Toyota hybrids driven infrequently)
  • Check recall history (especially the high-pressure fuel pump and cameras)
  • Dashboard or plastic noises in cold weather (a few isolated cases reported)
  • Software updates (OTA) for the multimedia system and instrument cluster

Verdict

**BUY.** The second-generation Toyota C-HR (AX30) is an extremely safe choice on the recent used or new car market. Reliability data is excellent (What Car? gives it 5/5 with only 9% minor breakdowns reported, and ADAC notes a very low breakdown rate of 1.1). The 1.8 HEV engine is the most rational and proven choice for daily use. The 2.0 HEV offers better driving pleasure, while the PHEV targets a niche with easy access to charging. Just make sure that early recalls (fuel pump, cameras, software) have been carried out at the dealership.
