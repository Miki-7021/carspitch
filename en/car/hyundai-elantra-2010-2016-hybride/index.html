Summary

The fifth-generation Hyundai Elantra (code MD, or UD depending on the factory) marked a radical turning point for the brand with its *Fluidic Sculpture* design, offering careful aerodynamics and generous equipment for the compact sedan category.

Focus on the Hybrid: Although demand targets the hybrid, it is crucial to specify that on this MD generation (2010-2016), the hybrid powertrain was not marketed globally. It was limited to the South Korean market under the name Hyundai Avante LPI Hybrid (using a rare combination of Liquefied Petroleum Gas - LPG - and a 15 kW electric motor with a lithium-ion polymer battery). In the North American and European markets, Hyundai preferred to focus its hybrid efforts on the Sonata, leaving the Elantra with conventional combustion engines (Gamma and Nu families).

Overall, the Elantra V is a very reliable sedan, although tarnished by several recall campaigns concerning accessories and the ABS/ESC module.

Strengths 5

  • 'Fluidic Sculpture' design that has aged very well
  • Excellent original manufacturer warranty (5 years/unlimited mileage in Europe, 10 years/100k miles in the USA)
  • Overall reliability of the naturally aspirated engines (1.6 MPI and 1.8 Nu)
  • Smooth and durable 6-speed automatic gearbox (acceptable according to ADAC)
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume for the category

Weaknesses 5

  • Hybrid powertrain almost impossible to find outside South Korea
  • Fire risk linked to the ABS/HECU module (imperatively requires checking the recall)
  • Hard interior plastics that scratch easily
  • Torsion beam rear suspension sometimes jittery on degraded roads
  • Soundproofing could be improved at high speeds (road noise)

Check before buying

  • ABS/HECU module: Check if the recall for the short-circuit/fire risk has been carried out (installation of a new fuse or replacement of the module).
  • Steering column (EPS): Turn the steering wheel from left to right while stationary. A clunking noise indicates a worn steering flexible coupling.
  • Brake pedal: Check that the brake lights turn off properly when the pedal is released (wear of the stopper pad, subject of multiple recalls).
  • 1.8 Nu engine when cold: Listen carefully to the engine during a cold start. A persistent metallic clacking can indicate piston wear (piston slap).
  • Hybrid system (if imported): Check the state of health (SOH) of the high-voltage battery and the validity date of the LPG tank.

Verdict

The Hyundai Elantra V (MD) is a compact sedan with a bold design and generally very satisfactory mechanical reliability. Regarding the hybrid powertrain, it is anecdotal on this generation (reserved for Korea in the form of LPG/Electric); if you are absolutely looking for a hybrid compact from those years, a Toyota Auris/Corolla or a Honda Civic IMA will be a much more realistic and easy-to-maintain choice.

On the other hand, if you opt for a combustion version (1.6 MPI or 1.8 Nu), the Elantra is an excellent purchase, often valued lower than its Japanese rivals. Absolute prerequisite before purchase: scrupulously check the recall history, particularly the one concerning the ABS/HECU module (fire risk), which affected a large part of the production.
