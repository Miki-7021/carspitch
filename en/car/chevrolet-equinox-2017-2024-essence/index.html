Chevrolet Equinox

III · 2017 – 2024

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The third-generation Chevrolet Equinox (based on General Motors' D2XX platform) marks a major break from its predecessors. More compact, nearly 180 kg lighter, and equipped with an entirely turbocharged engine lineup, it has established itself as one of the best-selling compact SUVs in North America. It received a facelift presented in 2020 but marketed for the 2022 model year due to the pandemic, bringing cosmetic tweaks (front fascia, LED headlights) and a new RS trim. Overall, this generation shines for its reliability, praised by J.D. Power and Consumer Reports, with a very extensive service network in North America.

Strengths 6

  • Above-average overall reliability (Consumer Reports Recommended since 2018)
  • Excellent J.D. Power scores (IQS 2022-2023) in the compact SUV category
  • Very robust and proven 1.5L Turbo engine
  • Intuitive and responsive infotainment system (Apple CarPlay / Android Auto as standard)
  • Generous rear roominess and flat floor
  • Very extensive after-sales network (especially in Canada and the United States)

Weaknesses 5

  • Quality of some interior plastics falls short of the competition (Mazda CX-5, Honda CR-V)
  • High-performance 2.0L Turbo engine but discontinued too early (2020)
  • 6-speed automatic gearbox (on the 1.5L) somewhat dated and slow
  • Trunk volume slightly smaller than segment leaders
  • Diesel version (1.6L) complex to maintain and rare

Check before buying

  • Recall history: the vehicle has been subject to several campaigns (seats, brakes, fuel pump).
  • Gearbox behavior: on the 2.0L (9-speed auto), look out for cold jerks; on the 1.5L (6-speed auto), check the smoothness of shifts.
  • Braking system: check for seizing of the rear calipers (2019 recall for incorrect lubricant).
  • On-board electronics: ensure the proper functioning of the occupant classification system (passenger seat) and the touchscreen.

Verdict

The third-generation Chevrolet Equinox is an extremely rational and safe choice on the used market. Although it is not the most luxurious or dynamic in its category, it compensates with remarkable mechanical reliability, particularly with the proven 1.5L Turbo engine. Data from J.D. Power and ADAC confirm its robustness over time. The number of recalls may seem high, but they are highly targeted and handled by a dense GM network.

**Buying advice**: Favor a post-2019 1.5L Turbo model (to avoid the early teething problems of the 2018 models) with a clear maintenance history and confirmation that recalls (especially brakes and fuel pump) have been performed. The 2.0L Turbo is an excellent find for those who need to tow, while the 1.6L Diesel should be avoided due to its rarity and potential maintenance costs related to emissions control.
