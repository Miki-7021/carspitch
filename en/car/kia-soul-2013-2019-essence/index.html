Summary

The second-generation Kia Soul, internally codenamed PS, was introduced in 2013, building on the distinctive, boxy styling of its predecessor while refining its aesthetics and improving its driving dynamics. Positioned as a compact crossover or urban MPV, the Soul II aimed to offer a unique blend of practicality, style, and value. It retained its quirky charm but matured significantly in terms of interior quality, ride comfort, and available technology.

A facelift arrived for the 2017 model year (late 2016 in some markets), bringing revised front and rear bumpers, updated lighting signatures (including optional bi-xenon headlights), new wheel designs, and an upgraded infotainment system. Crucially, the facelift also introduced a more powerful 1.6 T-GDI engine option in some markets, paired with a 7-speed dual-clutch transmission.

Overall, the Kia Soul II generally offers good reliability, particularly with its naturally aspirated petrol engines. The electric variant (Soul EV) was also a significant offering, providing a practical and affordable entry into the EV market. While some specific engine variants and transmissions require closer attention, the Soul II remains a solid choice for those seeking a distinctive and practical urban vehicle.

Strengths 6

  • Distinctive and practical design with good interior space for its class.
  • Excellent warranty coverage (new), which often translates to well-maintained used examples.
  • Good value for money, especially on the used market.
  • Available as a full EV (Soul EV) offering a practical electric option.
  • Naturally aspirated petrol engines (1.6 GDI, 2.0 MPI) are generally reliable.
  • User-friendly infotainment system and good standard features.

Weaknesses 6

  • Ride can be firm on some models, especially with larger wheels.
  • Fuel economy of the 1.6 GDI can be average compared to some rivals.
  • Road and wind noise can be noticeable at highway speeds.
  • Interior materials, while improved, can still feel a bit hard plastic on lower trims.
  • 1.6 T-GDI engine and its associated DCT can have reliability concerns.
  • Limited boot space compared to some traditional compact SUVs.

Check before buying

  • **Service History:** Crucial for all models, especially for GDI/T-GDI engines (carbon cleaning) and diesels (DPF/EGR).
  • **Engine Noises:** Listen for unusual ticking or knocking, especially on 1.6 GDI (potential carbon buildup or GDI pump noise).
  • **DCT Operation (1.6 T-GDI):** Check for smooth shifts, no hesitation or jerking, especially at low speeds. Test in stop-and-go traffic.
  • **DPF Warning Light (1.6 CRDi):** Indicates potential DPF issues. Check for excessive black smoke during acceleration.
  • **EV Battery Health:** Request a battery State of Health (SOH) report if possible. Check charging port for damage.
  • **Suspension Components:** Listen for clunks or squeaks over bumps, indicating worn bushings or shock absorbers.
  • **Electrical System:** Test all lights, infotainment, power windows, and air conditioning.

Verdict

The Kia Soul II (PS) is a compelling choice for buyers seeking a distinctive, practical, and generally reliable compact vehicle. My 20 years of experience tell me that the **2.0 MPI (G4NC)** engine, primarily found in North America and some Asian markets, is the absolute sweet spot for long-term reliability and peace of mind. It's a simple, robust engine with minimal known issues, making it an **ACHETER** recommendation without hesitation.

The **1.6 GDI (G4FD)** is also a good option, but be prepared for potential carbon buildup maintenance. With a diligent service history, it's a **NÉGOCIER** to **ACHETER** depending on price and condition. The **1.6 CRDi (D4FB)** is excellent for high-mileage drivers, but requires proper DPF care; it's a **NÉGOCIER** if you primarily do short urban trips.

The **1.6 T-GDI (G4FJ)**, while offering spirited performance, comes with the added complexity of direct injection and a dual-clutch transmission. These are known areas of concern across many brands. If you're considering this variant, a thorough inspection of the DCT and engine is paramount, and a full service history is non-negotiable. This is a **NÉGOCIER** at best, and potentially an **ÉVITER** if the service history is patchy or if you're not prepared for potentially higher maintenance costs.

The **Soul EV (EM11)** is a strong contender for urban dwellers. Its reliability is generally good, but battery degradation is a natural factor to consider on older units. A pre-purchase inspection focusing on battery health is crucial. It's an **ACHETER** for those with suitable charging access and range requirements.

Overall, the Soul II offers a lot of character and utility. Choose your engine wisely based on your driving habits and maintenance tolerance, and you'll likely be very happy with this quirky Korean.
