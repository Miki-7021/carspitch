Summary

Mazda2 II (DE Generation): The Weight Loss Cure

Launched in 2007, the second generation Mazda2 (known as Demio in Japan) marked a complete break from its mini-MPV-like predecessor. Based on the B3 platform jointly developed with Ford (shared with the Fiesta Mk6), it stands out for a drastic weight loss (nearly 100 kg less), offering dynamic, agile, and very pleasant road handling.

**Diesel Focus:** Although Mazda is renowned for the reliability of its gasoline engines, the brand had to rely on partnerships for its diesel powertrains in Europe. The 1.4 and 1.6 MZ-CD blocks under the hood of this Mazda2 are actually PSA/Ford-sourced engines (the famous 1.4 HDi/TDCi and 1.6 HDi/TDCi). While these engines offer excellent fuel efficiency, they also bring their well-known chronic issues (injector seals, turbo, EGR valve), which strongly contrasts with the legendary reliability of the gasoline versions (MZR).

A facelift occurred in late 2010, bringing minor aesthetic changes, improved sound insulation, and an engine update to meet the Euro 5 standard.

  • Excellent chassis: agile, precise, and very fun handling
  • Featherlight (under a ton for some versions) promoting fuel efficiency and dynamism
  • Exceptional reliability of gasoline engines (MZR)
  • Manual gearbox with perfect feel (typical Mazda)
  • Generally low maintenance cost (excluding major diesel breakdowns)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very inconsistent reliability of diesel engines (PSA/Ford origin)
  • Poor sound insulation, especially on Phase 1 models (wind and road noise)
  • Quite firm suspension comfort, jittery at low speeds
  • Modest boot volume (250 liters) and high loading sill
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches)

Check before buying

  • On diesels (MZ-CD): Lift the engine cover and inspect the base of the injectors. Any trace of black paste (carbon build-up) or exhaust gas smell in the cabin indicates leaking injector seals.
  • On the 1.6 MZ-CD: Listen carefully for turbo whistling. A loud whistle or siren-like noise indicates premature shaft wear, often due to lubrication issues.
  • Diesel service history: Demand invoices proving regular oil changes (ideally every 10,000 to 15,000 km maximum) with the recommended oil (Low SAPS for DPF).
  • Front axle: Listen for clunking noises on degraded roads (stabilizer bar links or shock absorber top mounts often at fault).
  • Steering: Check for any stiff spots or abnormal noises when turning at a standstill (occasional issues with the steering rack or electric power steering EPS).

Verdict

The second-generation Mazda2 is an excellent city car, praised for its lively chassis and lightness. **However, if you are targeting a Diesel model (1.4 or 1.6 MZ-CD), extreme caution is advised.** These engines, borrowed from PSA/Ford, suffer from chronic flaws (injector seals, turbo, clogging) that can turn a good deal into a financial pitfall.

**Our advice:** If you drive less than 20,000 km per year, avoid diesel and buy a gasoline version (1.3 or 1.5 MZR) with your eyes closed. If you absolutely must opt for diesel (high mileage driver), demand a clear service history, check for injector leaks, and prioritize the 1.4 MZ-CD, which is slightly less prone to turbo failures than the 1.6, or the 1.6 in its 95hp (Euro 5, 8-valve) version that appeared late in its production run.
