Summary

The Volkswagen Passat B8 (codename 3G) is based on the modular MQB platform. Launched in late 2014, it marked a significant upgrade compared to the B7, with a finish bordering on the premium segment, benchmark roominess, and a cutting-edge technological arsenal. The 2019 facelift brought technological updates (MIB3 infotainment, Travel Assist semi-autonomous driving) and the introduction of the 1.5 TSI evo engine. Note: the sedan version was withdrawn from the European catalog at the end of 2021, leaving the wagon (SW/Variant) alone until the arrival of the B9. Overall, the B8 is an extremely accomplished road car, whose mechanical reliability (especially on the EA211 and EA888 Gen 3 petrol engines) has largely corrected the shortcomings of previous generations.

Strengths 6

  • Finish and assembly quality worthy of the premium segment
  • Exceptional roominess (especially in SW wagon) and trunk volume
  • Reliability of TSI petrol engines (significant improvement over the B7)
  • Sound insulation and ride comfort on long journeys
  • Ergonomics of controls (especially before the MIB3 facelift)
  • Superb and safe road handling

Weaknesses 5

  • Fragile dry-clutch DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox on smaller engines (1.4 TSI, 1.5 TSI, 1.6 TDI)
  • Frequent electronic bugs on the MIB3 infotainment system (post-2019)
  • Cost of replacing Matrix LED headlights out of warranty
  • Capricious AdBlue system on TDI versions
  • Suspension sometimes jittery at low speeds with 18/19-inch wheels (without DCC)

Check before buying

  • DSG gearbox service history (oil change every 60,000 km for wet-clutch gearboxes)
  • Behavior of the DSG7 DQ200 gearbox during the test drive (juddering on startup, metallic noises on rough roads)
  • Traces of coolant around the water housing/thermostat (especially on 1.8 and 2.0 TSI)
  • Smooth operation of the touchscreen and GPS (intermittent restarts on MIB3)
  • Wear of front wishbone bushings (frequent creaking over speed bumps around 80,000 km)
  • On GTE versions: charging history and absence of warning lights related to the hybrid system

Official recalls

  • Panoramic sunroof (2016-2017): Risk of water ingress or frame cracking. Frame replacement or reinforcement.
  • Rear wheel bearings (2019): Manufacturing defect in the bearing housing potentially leading to loss of control (2018-2019 models).
  • Brake booster (2020): Risk of vacuum leak at the brake booster, extending braking distance.
  • GTE High Voltage Battery (2022): Risk of short circuit or fire due to insulation defect in the HV battery. Installation of an additional insulating mat.
  • Seat belts (2017): Replacement of defective seat belt pretensioner igniter.

Verdict

The Volkswagen Passat B8 is an excellent rational choice. With petrol engines, it shines with the rediscovered reliability of its TSI units (EA211 and EA888 Gen 3), erasing the nightmares of timing chains from previous generations. The 1.4 TSI 150 hp or 1.5 TSI evo represent the ideal compromise for 80% of drivers. The main point of vigilance remains the transmission: opt for a manual gearbox on smaller engines for complete peace of mind, as the DSG7 (DQ200) remains the group's Achilles' heel. If you choose a 2.0 TSI or a 2.0 TDI, the wet-clutch DSG gearboxes are robust, provided they have been meticulously serviced. An excellent road car, to buy with confidence with a clear history.
