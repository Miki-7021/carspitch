Toyota Sienna

IV (XL40) · 2021 – 2025

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

All Toyota models

Summary

The fourth-generation Toyota Sienna (XL40) marks a radical break in the model's history. Abandoning the traditional V6 engine, it adopts exclusively a hybrid powertrain (THS II system) based on the TNGA-K modular platform, shared with the RAV4, Highlander, and Camry. This bold technical choice allows it to achieve record efficiency for the minivan segment, with an average fuel consumption hovering around 6.5 L/100 km. Available in FWD or with an electric all-wheel drive (AWD-e) transmission, it stands out for its gigantic interior space, smooth operation, and exceptional overall reliability. Although primarily intended for the North American and Asian markets, it remains an absolute benchmark for large families seeking peace of mind.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable fuel consumption for a minivan of over 2 tons (around 6.5L/100km).
  • Imperial reliability of the Toyota hybrid system (series-parallel) and the 2.5L TNGA engine.
  • AWD-e all-wheel drive without a mechanical driveshaft (fewer moving parts).
  • Gigantic interior space and modularity (available in 7 or 8 seats).
  • Very well-designed and durable power sliding doors and tailgate.

Weaknesses 4

  • Combustion engine (4-cylinder) noisy during hard acceleration (rubber-band effect of the e-CVT transmission).
  • Second-row seats are not removable (they fold but cannot be taken out to load very large objects).
  • Not officially marketed in Europe (specific body parts requiring importation).
  • Quality of some interior plastics lags behind the competition (Honda Odyssey).

Check before buying

  • Cleanliness of the ventilation grille and the hybrid battery filter (located inside the cabin).
  • Smooth operation of the infotainment system (some sluggishness or bugs reported on 2021 model years).
  • Condition of the brake discs (risk of corrosion due to underutilization linked to regenerative braking).
  • History of software updates (especially for the ESC module).

Official recalls

  • [2022-04-13] NHTSA (22V239000) - Stability Control (ESC): Software update of the control module (Skid Control ECU) which might not activate the VSC system upon startup (affects 2021-2022 models).
  • [2021-11-17] NHTSA (21V889000) - Seat belts: Replacement of an incorrectly installed seat belt webbing guide on the outboard 2nd-row seats (affects 8-seat 2022 models).
  • [2025-12-17] NHTSA (25V877000) - Accessibility equipment: Affects vehicles modified by Vantage Mobility (VM) for wheelchair access. The QRT-Deluxe/Max retractors may fail to lock.
  • [2024-08-22] Rappel Conso (Global Toyota/Lexus TNGA) - Cameras: Risk of camera housings disintegrating under stress (incorrect welding) allowing water infiltration.
  • [2024-06-26] Rappel Conso (Global Toyota/Lexus TNGA) - Immobilizer: Immobilizer device design insufficiently robust, allowing the vehicle to remain in "Ready On" mode.

Alternatives

  • North America: Honda Odyssey (V6 only, excellent modularity but no hybrid or AWD), Chrysler Pacifica (PHEV available but very hit-or-miss reliability), Kia Carnival (V6 or recent hybrid, very well finished).
  • Europe / Asia: Volkswagen Multivan T7 (PHEV available, European format), Hyundai Staria (futuristic design, diesel or hybrid), Lexus LM (ultra-luxury version of the Toyota minivan).

Verdict

The 4th generation Toyota Sienna is undeniably the most rational and reliable minivan in its segment. Toyota's gamble to impose 100% hybridization is a total success: it offers the fuel consumption of a compact sedan in a vehicle capable of transporting 8 people and their luggage. Reliability is spot on, supported by the excellent reputation of the 2.5L TNGA-K powertrain (shared with the RAV4). If one accepts the inability to remove the second-row seats and the engine noise under hard acceleration, it is a highly recommended purchase, offering unparalleled peace of mind over the long term.
