Chevrolet Captiva

I facelift (C140) · 2011 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 66/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Chevrolet Captiva benefited from a major facelift in 2011 (codename C140), marking a clear evolution compared to phase 1 (C100). This family SUV, a technical cousin of the Opel Antara and developed by GM Korea, stood out with a much more aggressive front end, a modernized interior, and, above all, a complete overhaul of its engines and gearboxes. The aging 2.0 diesel block was replaced by an all-new 2.2 VCDi (developed with VM Motori), available in 163 hp and 184 hp, offering much better drivability. Available in 5 or 7 seats, the Captiva C140 positioned itself as an affordable and status-enhancing alternative to the segment leaders. Although its price/equipment ratio is very attractive on the used market, its high weight (nearly 2 tons) penalizes fuel consumption and prematurely wears out the running gear. Chevrolet's withdrawal from the European market in 2015 caused its value to drop, making it an excellent financial deal, provided you are vigilant about the maintenance history.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable price/roominess ratio on the used market
  • Practical 7-seat configuration (seats fold flat into the floor)
  • High-performance and torquey 2.2 VCDi engine (especially the 184 hp)
  • Very rich standard equipment (LTZ trims)
  • Soundproofing significantly improved compared to phase 1

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption (1.9-ton weight)
  • Slow and energy-consuming 6-speed automatic gearbox
  • Very temperamental FAP reliability in urban use
  • Chevrolet network disappeared in Europe (maintenance to be done at Opel or independents)
  • Sluggish road handling, significant body roll

Check before buying

  • Oil level on the 2.2 VCDi: if the level is above the maximum, there is dilution by diesel fuel (danger to the engine).
  • Automatic gearbox oil change history (must have been done every 60,000 km).
  • Clunking noises on the front axle (links, shock absorber mounts, silent blocks).
  • All-wheel drive (AWD) operation and absence of leaks under the rear axle.
  • FAP/Engine warning light on the dashboard (road test essential to verify the absence of limp mode).

Verdict

The Chevrolet Captiva C140 (2011-2015) is the typical smart buy choice for large families on a tight budget. The switch to the 2.2 VCDi engine transformed the driving experience compared to older models. However, it is not a vehicle to buy with your eyes closed. Its substantial weight leads to overconsumption of fuel and wear parts (brakes, tires, suspension). The 2.2 diesel engine is mechanically robust (chain), but its emission control peripherals (FAP, EGR) do not tolerate the city. Purchase is recommended **only** if you drive mostly on main roads/highways, if you find an example with a crystal-clear maintenance history (frequent engine oil changes, automatic gearbox oil change performed), and if you negotiate the price taking into account the disappearance of the Chevrolet network in Europe.
