Summary

The second-generation Hyundai i30 (GD code) was launched in 2012, succeeding the first i30 and sharing its platform with the second-generation Kia Cee'd. Positioned in the C-segment, it aimed to be a direct competitor to European benchmarks such as the Volkswagen Golf, Ford Focus, or Opel Astra. Available in 5-door hatchback and wagon (CW) body styles, it was praised for its more mature and European design, generous equipment, and a 5-year warranty, a significant selling point at the time.

A minor facelift occurred in 2015, bringing minor aesthetic changes (grille, lights), new wheels, and the introduction of the sporty i30 Turbo version (1.6 T-GDI) as well as the option of a dual-clutch automatic transmission (DCT) on certain engines. Overall, the i30 GD is a reliable car, offering good value for money and appreciable comfort for daily use. Its reliability is one of its major assets, particularly with naturally aspirated gasoline engines.

Strengths 6

  • General reliability, especially of MPI gasoline and CRDi diesel engines
  • Attractive value for money on the used market
  • Generous equipment for the segment
  • Decent suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • 5-year manufacturer's warranty (transferable on used cars)
  • Modern and well-finished exterior and interior design

Weaknesses 5

  • MPI gasoline engines a bit underpowered on the highway
  • Valve fouling on GDI engines (direct injection)
  • Power steering sometimes considered too light or lacking feel
  • MPI gasoline engine consumption slightly higher than direct competitors
  • Diesel DPF sensitive to short urban trips

Check before buying

  • Complete service history, especially for GDI and CRDi
  • Presence of abnormal engine noises (knocking, timing chain)
  • DPF operation on diesels (warning light, regenerations)
  • Condition of suspensions and bushings (noises on degraded roads)
  • Operation of air conditioning and electrical equipment
  • Tire and brake wear (wear indicator, warped discs)

Official Recalls Hyundai i30 GD (Europe) * 2013 (December): Brake pressure sensor issue. The brake master cylinder pressure sensor may be defective, leading to ABS/ESP malfunction. Affected population: Vehicles produced between 2012 and 2013. * 2014 (June): Electric power steering (MDPS) issue. A software defect may lead to a loss of steering assistance. Affected population: Certain vehicles produced in 2013. * 2015 (March): Crankshaft position sensor issue. A sensor defect may lead to starting difficulties or engine stalling. Affected population: Certain vehicles equipped with 1.6 GDI and 1.6 MPI engines produced between 2012 and 2014. * 2016 (February): Engine wiring harness issue. A wiring harness may be improperly positioned and rub against other components, risking a short circuit. Affected population: Certain vehicles equipped with the 1.6 CRDi engine produced between 2012 and 2015.

Official Recalls Hyundai Elantra GT (GD - North America) * 2013 (April): Brake light switch. The switch may become defective, preventing the brake lights from illuminating, cruise control disengagement, or vehicle starting. Affected population: Numerous Hyundai/Kia models, including the Elantra GT, produced between 2007 and 2011 (extension for certain models until 2013). * 2017 (November): Engine Control Module (ECM) wiring harness issue. A harness connector may be damaged by moisture, leading to misfires or loss of power. Affected population: Certain vehicles equipped with the 2.0 GDI engine produced between 2013 and 2017.

Verdict

The second-generation Hyundai i30 is an excellent choice on the used car market, offering very satisfactory overall reliability and generous equipment for its price.

For predominantly urban and suburban use, the 1.4 MPI and 1.6 MPI are the most recommended: their mechanical simplicity guarantees bulletproof reliability and reduced maintenance costs. Their lack of power will be their only drawback on the highway.

For more versatile use (road, highway), the 1.6 GDI is a good performance/consumption compromise, but vigilance will be required regarding the maintenance history and preventive decarbonization should be considered at high mileage. The 1.6 CRDi are also very good choices for high-mileage drivers, provided they are not confined to short urban trips to preserve the DPF.

The 1.6 T-GDI (i30 Turbo) version is reserved for sporty driving enthusiasts, aware of the increased maintenance requirements of a direct-injection turbo engine.

In summary: The i30 GD is a car to BUY without hesitation, prioritizing well-maintained MPI gasoline or CRDi diesel engines. GDI versions require a more thorough check of the engine's history and condition. It is a serious and reliable alternative to European compacts, often more expensive to buy and maintain.
