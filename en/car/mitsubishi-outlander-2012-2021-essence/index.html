Summary

The third-generation Mitsubishi Outlander (chassis code GF/GG/ZJ/ZK/ZL depending on market and year) was launched in 2012, succeeding an already well-established model. Positioned as a versatile family SUV, it was offered in 5 or 7-seater configurations. Its initial, more understated design was significantly refreshed in 2015 with the adoption of the 'Dynamic Shield' grille, giving it a more modern and aggressive look. A second minor facelift occurred in 2018.

The Outlander III is particularly recognized for its pioneering introduction of the plug-in hybrid (PHEV) version in 2013, which quickly dominated sales in this segment in Europe. Overall, it's a vehicle that prioritizes robustness and practicality over luxury or sportiness. Its reliability is generally good, especially for the naturally aspirated gasoline engines, but there are points of attention, particularly regarding CVT transmissions and the complex systems of the PHEV versions.

Strengths 6

  • General reliability of naturally aspirated gasoline engines
  • Versatility and spaciousness (7-seater option)
  • Effective 4x4 capabilities (S-AWC system on some versions)
  • Pioneer and leader in the plug-in hybrid SUV (PHEV) segment
  • Suspension comfort on long journeys
  • Good value for money on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Somewhat austere interior design and sometimes dated materials (before 2015 facelift)
  • Modest performance of 2.0L/2.4L gasoline engines with CVT
  • High fuel consumption of V6 gasoline and diesel versions in urban areas
  • CVT noise during acceleration
  • Complexity of the PHEV system (requires specific maintenance and regular charging to be efficient)

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: Essential, especially for the CVT (fluid changes) and PHEV (software updates, battery maintenance).
  • CVT Transmission: Test for smoothness, absence of jerks or abnormal noises during acceleration. A recent fluid change is a plus.
  • PHEV System: Check the displayed electric range (compare with new range), the condition of the 12V battery, proper charging function (cables included). Ask for the software update history.
  • Gasoline Engines: Check oil level and absence of blue smoke from the exhaust (oil consumption).
  • Diesel Engines: Check for absence of excessive smoke, proper DPF function (no warning light on), turbo condition.
  • Running Gear: Suspension noises, tire wear (indicating poor alignment or worn parts).
  • Electronics: Functionality of infotainment, driving aids, power windows.

Official recalls

Official Recalls (Examples, non-exhaustive list)

  • 2013-2014 (PHEV): Traction battery or onboard charger overheating issues, potentially leading to fire risk. Corrected by software update and/or component replacement. (Rappel Conso, NHTSA)
  • 2014-2015 (PHEV): Hybrid system management software issue potentially leading to power loss or vehicle shutdown. (Rappel Conso, NHTSA)
  • 2015-2016 (All models): Potential issue with the electric parking brake (EPB) possibly not disengaging or engaging correctly. (NHTSA)
  • 2016-2017 (All models): Fuel pump relay issue potentially leading to engine stalling. (NHTSA)
  • 2018-2019 (PHEV): Corrosion issue on the charging cable connector, potentially leading to fire risk during charging. (Rappel Conso, NHTSA)
  • 2019-2020 (All models): Low-pressure fuel pump issue potentially leading to engine stalling. (NHTSA)

*It is imperative to check with a Mitsubishi dealership, using the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN), whether the vehicle in question has benefited from all recall campaigns.*

Verdict

The Mitsubishi Outlander III is a wise choice for anyone looking for a robust and reliable family SUV, without frills.

  • For tight budgets and peace of mind: The 2.0L and 2.4L MIVEC gasoline versions are excellent choices, provided that the CVT has been properly maintained. They are perfect for versatile family use.
  • For high-mileage drivers or towing: The 3.0L V6 is an exceptionally reliable and powerful engine, but its fuel consumption must be considered. The 2.2L DI-D may be suitable, but requires rigorous maintenance and highway use to avoid DPF/EGR issues.
  • For eco-conscious drivers with charging access: The Outlander PHEV is a very relevant option. It is a complex vehicle but surprisingly reliable for its technology. It is crucial to check the maintenance history and software updates. It is ideal if you can charge it daily to maximize its efficiency.

In summary, the Outlander III is a vehicle to BUY if the maintenance history is clear, especially for the CVT and PHEV. It is a faithful companion that will not break the bank in maintenance if you choose the right engine for your use.
