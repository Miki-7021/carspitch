Summary

The third-generation Kia Rio (internal code UB) marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer. Designed under the direction of Peter Schreyer, it abandoned the insipid look of its predecessors for a dynamic and European design. Launched in 2011 and facelifted in early 2015, it stood out for its generous interior space, rich equipment for its category, and the famous 7-year manufacturer's warranty. Although this report focuses on diesel (CRDi) engines, which were very popular in Europe at the time for their fuel efficiency, the Rio was offered with a multitude of gasoline engines worldwide. Overall, it is a very reliable model, but diesel versions require a suitable driving profile to avoid costly breakdowns related to emission control.

Strengths 5

  • Exterior design still current
  • Rear passenger space and trunk volume (288 L)
  • Excellent overall reliability, especially for gasoline models
  • Generous standard equipment (often with rearview camera, GPS on high trims)
  • Timing chain on most engines (reduces maintenance costs)

Weaknesses 5

  • 1.1 CRDi engine rough, noisy, and vibrating (3 cylinders)
  • Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) very temperamental on diesels in urban use
  • Firm suspension, degraded comfort on bumpy roads (especially with 17-inch wheels)
  • Hard interior plastics and very prone to scratches
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway

Check before buying

  • DPF History (Diesels): Check if the engine light comes on or if the idle is unstable (sign of aborted regenerations).
  • Steering column: Listen for a knocking sound when turning the steering wheel while stationary (star-shaped flex disc of the electric power steering needs replacement, inexpensive part but labor-intensive).
  • Clutch: Test the biting point, particularly on the 1.4 CRDi where premature wear (before 80,000 km) has been reported.
  • Paint and bodywork: The paint (especially red and black) is thin and prone to stone chips on the hood.
  • Onboard electronics: Check the proper functioning of the touchscreen and Bluetooth, which can sometimes glitch (requires a firmware update).

What sets it apart 7

Design

  • Modern and timeless exterior design — The Kia Rio III stands out with its dynamic and European look, designed to remain attractive over the years.

practicality

  • Generous interior space and appreciable boot volume — Enjoy comfortable space for rear passengers and a 288-liter boot, ideal for daily needs and getaways.

equipment

  • Rich and technological standard equipment — The Rio III offers generous equipment for its category, often including a rearview camera and a navigation system on higher trims, for enhanced comfort and connectivity.

safety

  • High-level passive and active safety — With a 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, including 92% for adult protection and 84% for children, the Kia Rio III ensures optimal protection for all its occupants.

braking

  • Effective and progressive braking — The Kia Rio III's brakes are renowned for their effectiveness, progressiveness, and good stopping distances, offering a reassuring pedal feel and a high-performance ABS system.

road_holding

  • Stable road holding and precise steering — Enjoy a serene drive thanks to stable and predictable road holding, light and precise steering, and good stability on the highway, with well-controlled body roll.

economy

  • Economical and reliable CRDi diesel engines — The Kia Rio III's CRDi diesel engines are appreciated for their low fuel consumption and timing chain design, contributing to reduced long-term maintenance costs.

Alternatives

  • Europe : Peugeot 208 I (1.4/1.6 HDi), Renault Clio IV (1.5 dCi), Volkswagen Polo V (1.2/1.6 TDI), Hyundai i20 II (technical cousin with the same CRDi engines).

Verdict

The Kia Rio III is an excellent versatile city car on the used market. If you are targeting a diesel (CRDi), caution is advised: avoid models that have been used exclusively in the city. The 1.4 CRDi 90 is clearly preferable to the 1.1 CRDi 75, both for driving pleasure (4 cylinders) and versatility. Ensure that the service history is clear and that the vehicle has regularly driven on highways to preserve its DPF and EGR valve. However, unless you drive more than 15,000 km/year on extra-urban journeys, gasoline engines (1.25 or 1.4 CVVT) remain the most rational and reliable choice for this model.
