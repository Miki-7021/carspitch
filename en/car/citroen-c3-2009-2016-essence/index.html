Summary

The second-generation Citroën C3 (code name SC) marked a stylistic break from its predecessor while retaining its comfort-oriented philosophy. Presented at the Frankfurt Motor Show in 2009, it stands out with its panoramic 'Zenith' windscreen (depending on trim) offering exceptional brightness. Based on PSA's PF1 platform (shared with the Peugeot 207/208), it prioritizes suspension suppleness over pure dynamism. A facelift occurred in spring 2013, incorporating LED daytime running lights into the front bumper and new 3-cylinder PureTech engines.

**Overall reliability score:** The C3 II is a car with two faces. While the diesel engines (HDi) and older petrol units (TU) show correct to good reliability (despite some known defects), the modern petrol units (VTi 'Prince' and PureTech) heavily weigh down the balance with major design flaws (timing chain, wet belt, excessive oil consumption). Purchasing a petrol version therefore requires extreme vigilance and an impeccable service history.

Check before buying

  • On PureTech: Condition of the timing belt via the oil filler cap (cracks, swelling) and recall history.
  • On VTi: Metallic knocking noise on cold start (timing chain) and signs of oil leaks.
  • On HDi: Presence of black carbon deposits around injectors and exhaust gas smell in the cabin.
  • On BlueHDi: AdBlue system operation (no warning lights illuminated on the dashboard).
  • Running gear: Clunking noises over bumps (shock absorber top mounts, anti-roll bar links).
  • Electronics: Proper functioning of air conditioning, multimedia system, and power windows.

Verdict

Purchasing a petrol-powered Citroën C3 II is a minefield. The older 1.1i and 1.4i engines (until 2012) are slow and thirsty but mechanically robust. In contrast, the VTi and PureTech units, which make up the majority of the post-2012 offerings, suffer from serious design flaws (chain or belt, oil consumption). If you are looking for a petrol C3 II, prioritize an older 1.4i 73hp model or demand a PureTech model whose engine has been replaced or whose timing belt/chain has just been done with a strainer cleaning. Paradoxically, the diesel versions (especially the HDi 90) are much better choices for reliability, provided you drive enough to prevent DPF clogging.
