Honda Accord

VI (CG/CH) · 1997 – 2002

NEGOTIATE Reliability 78/100 — bon

Summary

The 6th generation Honda Accord (chassis CG/CH/CF/CL) is a pivotal model, characterized by an extensive regionalization strategy: Honda designed three distinct vehicles for Europe, North America, and Japan. The European version (CG/CH), manufactured in Swindon, UK, stands out for its dynamic road handling and excellent reliability of its gasoline engines (VTEC).

However, as Honda did not have its own diesel engine at the time (the famous 2.2 i-CTDi would only arrive in the next generation), the brand turned to Rover. The **2.0 i-TD (or 2.0 TDI)** engine is therefore a Rover L-Series block. While it offered an economical alternative for high-mileage drivers of the era, it stands out for its lack of refinement (noise, vibrations) and peripheral reliability inferior to Honda's usual standards. Today, this generation is sought after for its gasoline versions (especially the legendary Type R), while the diesel is aimed at small budgets looking for a robust utility vehicle, provided maintenance is monitored.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional reliability of gasoline engines (F and H Series)
  • Sharp road handling (double wishbone front suspension on European models)
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume
  • Assembly quality from the Swindon plant (correct aging of plastics)
  • Type R (CH1) version has become a true collector's item

Weaknesses 5

  • Noisy, rough, and less reliable diesel engine (Rover) than the rest of the car
  • Declining availability of spare parts (especially for the Rover 2.0 i-TD engine and bodywork)
  • Disastrous automatic transmissions on North American V6 models
  • Average sound insulation on the highway (road noise)
  • Susceptibility to corrosion on the rear wheel arches (especially in cold/humid regions)

Check before buying

  • On the 2.0 i-TD: Clear history of the replacement of BOTH timing belts.
  • On the 2.0 i-TD: Cold start (check glow plugs and absence of diesel leaks on the Bosch pump).
  • Corrosion: Thoroughly inspect the rear fenders (bumper junction) and sills.
  • Power steering: Frequent leaks on the steering rack or power steering pump (a known issue for Hondas of this era).
  • Rear brake caliper: Tendency to seize (handbrake remains stuck).

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Peugeot 406 (diesel benchmark with the 2.0 HDi), Volkswagen Passat B5 (very powerful but noisy 1.9 TDI), Toyota Avensis (superior overall diesel reliability), Nissan Primera. - **North America:** Toyota Camry (the eternal rival, more reliable V6), Nissan Altima, Ford Taurus. - **Japan:** Toyota Corona/Camry, Nissan Bluebird, Subaru Legacy.

Verdict

The 6th generation Honda Accord is a sedan with two faces. If you opt for a gasoline engine (1.8i or 2.0i), you are buying one of the most reliable sedans of its decade, capable of exceeding 300,000 km with basic maintenance.

However, the **2.0 i-TD diesel** requires much more caution. This Rover engine is not bad in itself (the block is solid), but its peripherals (EGR, mass air flow sensor, injection pump) and complex maintenance (double timing belt) often negate the economic advantage at the pump, especially given the increasing rarity of parts. Only buy at a very low price, with an impeccable service history. If you are looking for a reliable diesel from this era, a Peugeot 406 HDi or a VW Passat TDI will be better choices. If you want a Honda, get it in gasoline.
