Summary

Lexus UX I Facelift (AYX10)

The Lexus UX (Urban Explorer) is the entry-level compact SUV from the Japanese premium manufacturer, based on the TNGA-C platform. The 2022 facelift corrected the model's main flaw: its infotainment system. The old touchpad was replaced by an intuitive 8- or 12.3-inch touchscreen (Lexus Link / Link Pro). The chassis also benefited from additional spot welds to increase rigidity, and the driving aids (Lexus Safety System+ 2.5) were optimized.

⚠️ EXPERT CLARIFICATION REGARDING DIESEL: It is imperative to specify that the Lexus UX has NEVER been produced with a diesel engine. Lexus definitively abandoned diesel in 2013 (end of production for the IS 200d/220d). Searching for a diesel Lexus UX on the used market is therefore pointless. The lineup revolves exclusively around gasoline engines (in certain markets), self-charging hybrids (HEV), and 100% electric powertrains (BEV). This sheet will therefore focus on the actual engines that build the reputation and exceptional reliability of this model.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall reliability (mechanical and electronic)
  • Very low real-world fuel consumption on hybrid versions (often under 5.5 L/100 km)
  • Quality of finish and assembly (top-tier interior materials)
  • Suspension comfort and soundproofing (especially post-2022)
  • New touchscreen infotainment finally on par with premium competitors
  • Lexus Relax warranty (up to 10 years or 160,000 km with network maintenance)

Weaknesses 5

  • Ridiculous trunk volume for the category (320 L on 250h 2WD, 283 L on AWD)
  • Very restricted rear roominess (legroom and headroom)
  • Engine revving effect (rubber-band effect) during hard acceleration (e-CVT gearbox)
  • UX 300e penalized by its CHAdeMO fast charging port (a disappearing standard in Europe)
  • Total absence of a diesel engine (for high-mileage highway drivers)

Check before buying

  • Condition of the 12V (accessory) battery: tends to discharge if the vehicle remains stationary for several weeks.
  • History of hybrid health checks (necessary to extend the traction battery warranty).
  • Tire wear on FWD versions, particularly on the very torquey UX 300e.
  • Verification of the application of software recalls (notably for the rearview camera and immobilizer).

Official recalls

Official manufacturer recalls (Sources: Rappel Conso)

  • [2025-11-07] Parking assist (PVM): Due to a software issue, the parking assist control unit that operates with the panoramic view monitor (PVM) may malfunction. Risk of injury. (Affects multiple Toyota/Lexus models including the UX).
  • [2025-09-19] Air conditioning compressor (UX 300e): The electric compressor may fail, causing the HVAC system to enter a fail-safe mode that suspends heating/defogging operation. Risk of reduced visibility/injury.
  • [2025-01-09] Wiring harness (UX 300h): The primary wiring harness wires that supply the motor generator electronic control unit (MG ECU) are improperly routed. Risk of chafing, short circuit, and loss of power.
  • [2024-08-22] Camera housings: The camera housings can separate under stress due to improper welding, allowing water ingress and failure of the rearview image.
  • [2024-06-26] Immobilizer: The design of the accessory immobilizer is not robust enough. It allows the vehicle to be in "Ready ON" mode even if no valid key is detected under certain specific conditions.

Alternatives

Competing alternatives

  • Europe: Volvo XC40 (very roomy, mild-hybrid and electric versions), BMW X2 (more dynamic, diesel offering available), Audi Q2 (rigorous finish, end of career), Mercedes GLA (technological, plug-in hybrid available).
  • Japan / Asia: Toyota C-HR (technical cousin, less premium), Honda Vezel/HR-V (very spacious, efficient e:HEV hybrid).
  • North America: Acura ADX (newcomer), BMW X1, Audi Q3.
  • Emerging markets: Lexus NX (often preferred for its superior space for a moderate extra cost).

Verdict

Expert verdict

The Lexus UX I Facelift (2022+) is one of the most reliable premium compact SUVs on the global market. The facelift erased its biggest flaw (the obsolete multimedia system) to make it an extremely accomplished vehicle.

Regarding diesel: Forget this idea. Lexus made the visionary choice of all-hybrid over 10 years ago. If you are a sales representative driving 50,000 km on the highway per year, the UX is not for you (turn to a diesel BMW X1).

On the other hand, for urban, suburban, and classic highway use, the UX 250h or the new 300h are absolute best buys. Their maintenance costs are negligible, the fuel consumption is stunning, and peace of mind is total thanks to the Lexus Relax warranty. The UX 300e (electric) should be reserved for local use due to its CHAdeMO fast charging being unsuitable for long European trips. The only real compromise to accept with the UX remains its tiny trunk and cramped rear seats.
