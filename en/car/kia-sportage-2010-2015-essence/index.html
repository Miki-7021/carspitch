Kia Sportage

III (SL) · 2010 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

All Kia models

Summary

The third-generation Kia Sportage (code name SL) marked a radical turning point for the Korean brand. Designed by Peter Schreyer, it abandoned the rustic off-roader look to adopt a dynamic and urban compact SUV design, sharing its platform with the Hyundai ix35. This model propelled Kia's sales in Europe and North America thanks to its aggressive price/equipment ratio and its famous 7-year warranty (now expired). Restyled in early 2014 (modified grille, LED taillights, improved sound insulation), it offers healthy road handling, albeit a bit firm. While diesel engines (CRDi) dominated the European market, petrol engines (GDI, MPI) largely equipped the rest of the world and are seeing renewed interest in the used car market due to traffic restrictions. Be careful, however: the reliability of petrol engines varies from excellent (1.6 GDI) to catastrophic (Theta II 2.0 and 2.4 family).

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 and 2.4 petrol engines (Theta II) on the global market
  • Quality of some interior plastics (scratch-prone, hard)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on pre-facelift models (2014)
  • Very poor rear visibility (thick C-pillars, small rear window)
  • Firm suspension, especially with 18-inch wheels
  • Fragility of the AWD system (splines wear out, pump fails)

Check before buying

  • Oil level on ALL petrol engines (risk of excessive consumption and breakdown).
  • AWD system operation: turn the steering wheel fully at low speed, check for absence of jerks or metallic noises.
  • Suspension noises: stabilizer bar links and bushings wear out quickly (clunking on degraded roads).
  • Panoramic sunroof: check for absence of creaks, smooth sliding, and absence of water leaks.
  • Cabin wear: steering wheel leather tends to peel quickly, and faux leather seats crack.

What sets it apart 7

Design

  • Modern and Timeless Design — Its bold and dynamic design, penned by Peter Schreyer, has aged remarkably well, giving it a consistently contemporary and distinctive look on the road.

comfort

  • Generous and Comfortable Equipment — Enjoy a rich standard equipment package, often including popular features like a rearview camera or heated seats, for optimal daily comfort.

reliability

  • Proven Reliability of the 1.6 GDI Engine — The 135 hp 1.6 GDI petrol engine is praised for its robustness, chain distribution, and affordable maintenance, offering peace of mind for your urban and suburban journeys.

safety

  • Optimal and Award-Winning Safety — With 5 stars in Euro NCAP crash tests and the IIHS Top Safety Pick distinction, the Sportage III ensures a high level of protection for all occupants.

road_handling

  • Serene and Precise Road Handling — Its chassis offers stable and secure road holding, with good body roll control and precise steering, guaranteeing predictable and enjoyable driving.

practicality

  • Everyday Space and Practicality — Benefit from good interior space and a generous 465-liter boot, ideal for families and activities requiring ample room.

braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — The braking system is renowned for its power and progressive feel, allowing for quick and stable stops, with competitive distances for its category.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Nissan Qashqai (the pioneer, more comfortable but less reliable in petrol), Volkswagen Tiguan (better finished, TSI to watch), Ford Kuga (excellent chassis), Hyundai ix35 (its technical twin).
  • North America: Honda CR-V (reliability benchmark), Toyota RAV4 (indestructible), Mazda CX-5 (excellent handling and reliable Skyactiv engines).
  • Asia / Emerging Markets: Honda HR-V, Mitsubishi ASX, Subaru Forester.

Verdict

The Kia Sportage III is an SUV with a striking design that remains very attractive on the used market. If you are looking for a petrol model in Europe, the 1.6 GDI 135 hp is the only truly recommendable choice: it is reliable, economical, and sufficient for daily use, although a bit underpowered when loaded. Absolutely avoid the 2.0 and 2.4 petrol versions (Theta II), very common in North America and Switzerland/Germany, due to a major design flaw in the short block (connecting rod bearings). If you drive a lot, the 1.7 CRDi and 2.0 CRDi diesels are excellent, very robust road companions. Be careful with 4x4 (AWD) versions, whose coupling system requires rigorous maintenance to avoid costly breakdowns. Opt for a restyled model (post-2014) with 2-wheel drive (2WD) for maximum peace of mind.
