Summary

The third-generation Audi A8 (code name D4 / Typ 4H) represents the pinnacle of luxury and technology for the brand with the four rings during this period. Based on the *Audi Space Frame* (ASF) aluminum structure, it manages to keep its weight down despite its imposing size. Equipped as standard with Quattro AWD (except on the rare hybrid version) and the excellent 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox (Tiptronic), it offers an exceptional compromise between supreme comfort and dynamism. A major facelift took place at the end of 2013 (Model Year 2014), introducing notably Matrix LED headlights, dynamic turn signals, and revised engines to meet the Euro 6 standard. On the used market, the diesel versions (3.0 TDI and 4.2 TDI) are highly praised for their massive torque and range, but require rigorous maintenance. Overall rating: 8/10.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional interior finish quality, remarkable aging of materials
  • Top-tier soundproofing (especially with acoustic glazing)
  • Smooth, responsive, and very reliable 8-speed ZF automatic gearbox
  • Efficiency and driving pleasure of the 3.0 TDI V6 engine
  • Imperial traction thanks to the standard Quattro system
  • Comfort of the adaptive air suspension

Weaknesses 5

  • Supercar-level maintenance and repair costs on the V8/W12
  • Imposing size (5.14m in short version, 5.27m in L version) difficult in the city
  • Rapid wear of tires and brakes (weight often exceeding 2 tons)
  • MMI infotainment system that has aged technologically (before the facelift)
  • Very complex mechanical accessibility (especially on the 4.2 TDI and W12)

Check before buying

  • Air suspension: check for sagging after prolonged parking, listen to the compressor (if it runs too often, there is a leak).
  • Metallic noises on cold start (1 to 3 seconds): sign of fatigue in the timing chain tensioners (very expensive on V6 and V8).
  • Retractable MMI screen: check that the opening/closing mechanism is smooth and noiseless (fragile plastic gears).
  • Water leaks: check the front floor and the trunk (clogged windshield cowl or sunroof drains, which can fry ECUs).
  • Maintenance history: avoid any model without a clear logbook or detailed invoices. The A8 does not tolerate approximations.

Safety Recalls (Rappel Conso & NHTSA)

Engine & Fuel: - March 2022 (NHTSA 22V178000) - 4.0 TFSI (2013-2017): The turbocharger oil supply screen can become clogged, leading to bearing wear from lack of lubrication and turbo failure. - August 2021 (Rappel Conso) - 3.0 TDI / 4.2 TDI: Engine control software update following Dieselgate (unauthorized software). - January 2019 & June 2018 (NHTSA 19V057000 / 18V430000) - A8/S8 (2013-2016): The high-pressure pump fuel supply line can become porous and leak. - January 2019 (NHTSA 19V035000) - A8 (2015-2018): Risk of fuel leak at the left and right fuel rails. - August 2016 (NHTSA 16V619000) - A8 (2010-2013): Possible leak from a coolant valve.

Chassis & Suspension: - September 2022 (Rappel Conso): Alteration of the rear axle toe/camber values leading to uneven and dangerous tire wear. - November 2021 (Rappel Conso): The nuts of the rear axle control arm eccentric bolt can break due to corrosion. - May 2020 (Rappel Conso): Defective rear axle suspension forks, risk of contact between the tire and the linkage.

Airbags & Electronics: - June 2020 & June 2022 (Rappel Conso): Replacement of defective Takata NADI driver airbag gas generators. - November 2021 (Rappel Conso): Defective crash sensors in the front doors for airbag deployment.

Other: - November 2025 [sic - 2024/2025] (Rappel Conso): Risk of overheating of the high-voltage battery cell modules (concerns recent hybrid/PHEV versions, potentially retroactive). - July 2022 (Rappel Conso): Deformation of the radiator tank seal (fire risk). - July 2020 (Rappel Conso): Manufacturing defect of an automatic gearbox oil line, risk of leaking onto the wheels. - July 2020 (NHTSA 20V397000): The engine compartment seal can detach and touch hot components. - September 2013 & June 2016 (NHTSA): Fixing issues with the sunroof glass which can detach.

Verdict

The Audi A8 D4 is a formidable traveling machine, particularly brilliant in its diesel versions. The 3.0 TDI V6 is the rational choice: it offers more than adequate performance, controlled consumption, and very decent reliability if maintenance has been followed. The 4.2 TDI is a monster of driving pleasure, but its maintenance costs in the event of a glitch (especially the timing chain) can ruin its owner. On the petrol side, the 3.0 TFSI is the most well-rounded. The 4.0 TFSI (A8 and S8) is magical but imperatively requires that the turbo screen issue has been resolved. Avoid the 2.0 TFSI Hybrid version, whose mechanical (EA888) and electrical reliability is hazardous. In all cases, plan for a substantial maintenance budget: wear parts (brakes, tires, suspension arms) and electronics are expensive.
