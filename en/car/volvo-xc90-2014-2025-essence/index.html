Summary

The second-generation Volvo XC90 (code name L, based on the SPA platform) marked the spectacular rebirth of the Swedish brand under the Geely era. Launched in late 2014, it introduced Volvo's new design language (« Thor's Hammer » headlights), a minimalist interface centered around the vertical Sensus touchscreen, and a bold mechanical philosophy: the complete abandonment of 5, 6, and 8-cylinder engines in favor of exclusive 2.0L 4-cylinder blocks (VEA / Drive-E family), supercharged and/or electrified.

Positioning & Evolution: The quintessential premium family SUV (available in 5 or 7 seats), it focuses entirely on active/passive safety, ride comfort, and a refined design. - 2015-2017: Early years marked by teething issues (electronics, slow Sensus interface). - 2019: Minor aesthetic facelift and introduction of 48V mild-hybrid powertrains (« B » badges replacing « T » and « D »). - 2022: Replacement of the Sensus system with Android Automotive-based infotainment (integrated Google). - 2024/2025: Second major facelift announced to extend its career alongside its 100% electric equivalent, the EX90.

Expert's overall note: An exceptional vehicle in terms of comfort and safety, but whose technical complexity (especially on T6 and T8 versions) requires rigorous monitoring. The T5 and B5 petrol versions are the most rational choices for long-term reliability.

Strengths 5

  • Timeless design and elegant interior (premium materials, open-pore wood).
  • Top-tier active and passive safety (IntelliSafe, Pilot Assist).
  • Exceptional seat comfort (among the best in the automotive industry).
  • Bowers & Wilkins audio system (option) with masterful acoustic quality.
  • Remarkable spaciousness, including in the 3rd-row seats (for people up to 1m70).

Weaknesses 5

  • Unremarkable 4-cylinder engine sound, unworthy of an SUV at this price.
  • Very high weight (up to 2.3 tons in T8) leading to rapid brake and tire wear.
  • Capricious electronic and software reliability on 2015 to 2017 model years.
  • Air suspension (option) expensive to repair in case of compressor failure or airbag leaks.
  • Extreme mechanical complexity of T6 and T8 versions out of warranty.

Check before buying

  • Water Ingress: Check for absence of moisture under the front carpets. Sunroof drains can clog, causing water to overflow and potentially short-circuit the Central Electronic Module (CEM).
  • Electronics (Sensus): On pre-2018 models, test the responsiveness of the central screen at startup. Crashes sometimes require extensive updates or module replacement.
  • Air Suspension: Verify that the vehicle raises and lowers without abnormal compressor noise and that it does not sag after a night of parking.
  • Brake Wear: Discs and pads wear out very quickly (sometimes less than 40,000 km). Check the lip of the front discs.
  • T8 PHEV (ERAD): Listen for any whistling or metallic noise coming from the rear axle in 100% electric mode (sign of imminent electric motor failure).

Verdict

The Volvo XC90 II is a masterpiece of design and safety, but it paid the price for its technological innovation in its early years. Absolutely avoid the 2015 to 2017 model years, which are too prone to electronic bugs and early-life issues with the Drive-E engines. From 2019 onwards, reliability takes a significant leap forward. If you're looking for a petrol engine, the T5 (or B5) is the most sensible choice: sufficiently powerful, mechanically simpler than the T6, and significantly less financially risky than the complex T8 plug-in hybrid out of warranty. Meticulous maintenance (shortened oil change intervals, sunroof drain cleaning) is the key to serene ownership.
