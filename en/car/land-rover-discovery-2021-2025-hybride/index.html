Summary

The fifth-generation Land Rover Discovery (L462) received a major facelift in 2021. This facelift marks a revolution under the hood with the abandonment of the old V6 engines in favor of the new family of Ingenium inline 6-cylinder engines (gasoline and diesel), all paired with a 48V mild hybrid system (MHEV). The cabin receives the new Pivi Pro infotainment system, correcting the exasperating sluggishness of the old system. Although its off-road capabilities (Terrain Response 2) and family comfort (up to 7 real seats) remain exceptional, the reliability record is heavily marred by persistent electronic problems and peripheral design flaws.

Overall reliability rating: Poor.

Strengths 5

  • Imperial ride comfort (standard air suspension)
  • Real off-road capabilities (unrivaled Terrain Response 2 system)
  • Excellent roominess with 7 truly usable seats
  • New L6 Ingenium engines much smoother than the old V6s
  • Pivi Pro multimedia system finally responsive, modern, and ergonomic

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous overall reliability (What Car?: 2/5, 38% of breakdowns reported over 12 months)
  • Alarming 3-year TÜV defect rate (25%, well below average)
  • Excessive weight (over 2.4 tonnes) penalizing agility, brakes, and fuel consumption
  • Recurring electronic bugs (infotainment, sensors, 48V network)
  • After-sales network with prohibitive rates and often overwhelmed by complex breakdowns

Check before buying

  • Complete and traceable recall history (extremely numerous on this phase)
  • Air suspension operation (smooth raising/lowering, absence of excessive compressor noise or sagging when parked)
  • Absence of oil leaks under the engine (common issue with the oil filter housing and valve cover)
  • Electrical system stability (no error messages on the dashboard related to the 48V network or battery)
  • Pronounced wear of tires, brake discs, and pads (heavily stressed by the weight)

Verdict

The facelifted Land Rover Discovery V (2021+) brings a welcome mechanical modernization. Its new mild-hybrid (MHEV) inline 6-cylinder engines erase the fatal flaws of the old V6s (crankshaft failures), while offering imperial driving pleasure. However, the factual data is damning: with a 3-year TÜV defect rate of 25%, a What Car? score of 2/5 (38% annual breakdowns), and a dizzying list of official recalls (fire risks, oil leaks, 48V failures), reliability remains the Achilles' heel of this model.

Buying advice: Buying an L462 Discovery is a matter of passion. It is imperative to acquire this vehicle only with an active manufacturer's warranty or a comprehensive extended warranty. Avoid models whose maintenance and recall history is not 100% crystal clear.
