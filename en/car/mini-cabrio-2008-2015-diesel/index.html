Summary

The second-generation MINI Cabriolet (codename **R57**) succeeds the R52, adopting the R56 sedan's platform. It stands out with the integration of active roll-over bars (invisible when retracted), a soft top that opens in 15 seconds (with a sunroof function), and the introduction of the 'Always Open Timer' gadget.

**Positioning & Evolution:** Initially launched with 'Prince' petrol engines (co-developed with PSA), the R57 received a facelift (LCI) in summer 2010. It was at this point that it introduced, for the first time on the convertible, **diesel** engines (Cooper D, then Cooper SD in 2011), equipped with BMW engines from the **N47** family.

**Expert's overall rating:** Although very endearing, dynamic, and fuel-efficient in its diesel versions, the R57 suffers from capricious mechanical reliability. The N47 diesel engines and turbocharged petrol engines require meticulous monitoring, making a used purchase risky without a clear service history.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional driving pleasure (karting feel preserved despite the extra weight)
  • Extremely low fuel consumption on diesel versions (Cooper D and SD)
  • Abundant torque from the Cooper SD (305 Nm) ideal for acceleration
  • Improved finish quality compared to the R52 generation
  • Very versatile soft top with sunroof function (up to 120 km/h)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of N47 (Diesel) and N14 (Petrol) engines if poorly maintained
  • Agricultural noise of diesel engines, not in keeping with the convertible spirit
  • Almost zero rear visibility with the soft top closed and very poor with the soft top open (folded like an accordion)
  • Ridiculous boot volume (125 to 170 liters) and difficult access
  • Very firm suspension, spartan comfort on degraded roads

Check before buying

  • Timing chain history (replacement invoice required for N47 diesels and N14 petrols)
  • Condition of the Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) on diesels: avoid models that have only been driven in the city
  • Smooth operation of the electric soft top (no jerks, no forcing noise from the hydraulic motor)
  • Traces of moisture under the floor mats (FRM module very sensitive to water, located at foot level)
  • Clutch release bearing noise or vibrations at idle (worn dual-mass flywheel on Cooper SD)

Official recalls

  • - **Additional water pump (2012):** On Cooper S/JCW models, risk of overheating of the turbo water pump's circuit board, potentially leading to fire.
  • - **Temperature sensor (2012):** Replacement of the coolant temperature sensor which could leak by capillarity to the ECU.
  • - **FRM Module (Footwell Module):** Not a strict safety recall, but many warranty claims (PUMA) for corruption of this unit managing lighting and windows, often following a drop in battery voltage.
  • - **Chain tensioner (Internal campaigns):** Replacement of the timing chain tensioner on certain batches of N47 and Prince engines, often addressed during dealership services.

Verdict

Buying a MINI Cabriolet R57 with a **diesel engine (Cooper D or SD)** is a purely rational choice for those who drive a lot, but it somewhat contradicts the hedonistic philosophy of a convertible (rough sound, vibrations).

**The expert's verdict:** If you opt for a diesel, caution is advised. The BMW N47 engine is notoriously famous for its timing chain failures. **Never** buy this model without a complete service history and, ideally, an invoice proving that the timing chain kit has already been replaced with the reinforced version. If you drive less than 15,000 km/year, prefer a petrol version (Cooper 122 hp or Cooper S N18 post-2010), which is much more suited to the model's spirit.
