Toyota Camry

VIII (XV70) · 2017 – 2024

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

All Toyota models

Summary

The eighth-generation Toyota Camry (XV70) marks a major turning point for the model. Based on the modular TNGA-K platform, it sheds its image as a placid sedan to offer much more dynamic road handling, a lowered center of gravity, and increased structural rigidity. While it marked the great return of the Camry to Western Europe (exclusively with a hybrid powertrain to replace the Avensis), it remains an absolute best-seller in North America, Asia, and the Middle East, where it is predominantly sold with 100% gasoline engines (naturally aspirated and V6). A facelift occurred in late 2020/early 2021, bringing minor aesthetic touches, a floating infotainment screen, and the standard integration of Apple CarPlay / Android Auto (which was sorely lacking in the early model years). It is one of the most reliable sedans in the world.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall mechanical reliability (engines and transmissions)
  • Extremely low fuel consumption on the Hybrid version (THS II)
  • Ride comfort and rear passenger space
  • Transformed road handling thanks to the TNGA platform
  • Very high resale value in all markets
  • Powerful and indestructible V6 engine (in relevant markets)

Weaknesses 6

  • Infotainment system (Entune 3.0) dated, slow, and graphically outdated
  • Absence of Apple CarPlay / Android Auto on 2018 models (added later)
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speeds (road and wind noise)
  • Thin water-based paint, very susceptible to stone chips
  • Intrusive trunk hinges that reduce usable volume
  • Imposing size (nearly 4.90 m) not well-suited for European city centers

Check before buying

  • Recall history: imperatively check if the low-pressure fuel pump has been replaced.
  • 8-speed automatic transmission (on 2.5 and V6): ensure absence of jerks or hesitations at low speeds (requires a software update).
  • Battery cooling filter (Hybrid): check its cleanliness under the rear seat.
  • Coolant Bypass Valve on the 2.5L engine: check for leaks or error codes.
  • Condition of the bodywork and front bumper: the paint chips easily from projections.

Official recalls

  • January 2020 / November 2020 (Fuel pump) : Massive global recall (Denso supplier). The low-pressure fuel pump can fail, causing the engine to stall while driving. Affects many 2018-2019 models.
  • November 2021 (Brake assist) : On some V6 models, premature wear of the vacuum pump can reduce brake assist.
  • April 2018 (Engine) : Very rare (North America). Oversized pistons may have been mistakenly installed in some 2.5L (A25A-FKS) engines, leading to vibrations and loss of power. Complete engine replacement.
  • August 2019 (Seatbelts / Airbags) : Calibration issue with the front passenger seat Occupant Classification Sensor (OCS), which could prevent airbag deployment.

Verdict

The Toyota Camry XV70 is the epitome of a rational, worry-free purchase. If you are looking for a gasoline or hybrid sedan capable of covering hundreds of thousands of kilometers with minimal maintenance, this is the number one choice. The gasoline engines (2.5L and V6) sold outside Europe are monuments of reliability, although the 8-speed automatic transmission in early model years sometimes requires a software update to be perfectly smooth. The hybrid version, only available in Europe, is a masterpiece of efficiency. Its only real flaws lie in an outdated multimedia system and sound insulation that is not on par with German premium standards. Verdict: A highly recommended purchase; prioritize post-2020 models to benefit from standard Apple CarPlay/Android Auto and avoid the early issues of the 8-speed automatic transmission.
