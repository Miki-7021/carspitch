Mazda 2

· 2014 – Présent (génération DJ/DL)

ACHETER Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The Mazda 2 (DJ generation for the hatchback, DL for the sedan) was introduced in 2014, bringing Mazda's 'Kodo' design language and 'Skyactiv' technology to the highly competitive B-segment (supermini). Unlike most of its European rivals that downsized to small-capacity turbocharged 3-cylinder engines, Mazda took a 'right-sizing' approach, equipping the Mazda 2 with naturally aspirated 4-cylinder engines. This strategy has paid massive dividends in terms of long-term reliability. The car is praised for its engaging driving dynamics, precise manual gearbox, and an interior quality that punches above its class.

*Note: In Europe, since 2022, Mazda also sells the 'Mazda 2 Hybrid', which is a rebadged Toyota Yaris (XP210). This reliability sheet focuses exclusively on the Mazda-developed DJ/DL generation, which continues to be sold alongside the Hybrid model.*

Strengths 5

  • Outstanding reliability of petrol engines (Skyactiv-G)
  • Engaging driving dynamics and precise steering
  • Excellent, slick-shifting manual gearbox
  • High-quality interior materials for the B-segment
  • Real-world fuel economy is excellent without needing a turbo

Weaknesses 5

  • Cramped rear seats compared to rivals (Polo, Jazz)
  • Small boot capacity (280 liters)
  • Firm ride quality on pre-2019 models
  • Infotainment screen (MZD Connect) prone to 'ghost touch' issues
  • Diesel engine (Skyactiv-D) reliability is problematic

Reliability by engine

EngineReliability Assessment
**1.3 / 1.5 Skyactiv-G (Petrol)****Excellent.** These naturally aspirated 4-cylinder engines are among the most reliable in the segment. The absence of a turbocharger and complex emissions equipment (like GPF in early models) means fewer points of failure. The timing chain is robust and designed to last the life of the vehicle. Carbon build-up on intake valves (common in direct injection) is minimal compared to rivals.
**1.5 e-Skyactiv G (Mild-Hybrid)****Very Good.** Introduced to lower CO2 emissions, this adds a 24V mild-hybrid system. The core engine remains bulletproof. The integrated starter-generator (ISG) and small lithium-ion battery have proven reliable so far, with no widespread failure reports.
**1.5 Skyactiv-D (Diesel)****Average to Poor.** This engine suffers from typical modern diesel issues, especially if driven primarily in urban environments. DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) clogging is common. More critically, interrupted DPF regenerations lead to fuel mixing with engine oil (oil dilution), which raises the oil level and degrades lubrication, potentially causing severe engine wear. It was also subject to a high-pressure fuel pipe recall.

Check before buying

  • **MZD Connect Screen:** Check for 'ghost touches' (the screen acting as if it's being tapped on its own) or delamination of the display. This is a known issue on 2014-2018 models.
  • **Power Folding Mirrors:** The internal plastic gears in the wing mirrors are prone to stripping, causing the mirrors to fail to fold or unfold completely. Listen for a continuous whirring motor sound.
  • **Oil Level (Diesel only):** Check the dipstick on the 1.5 Skyactiv-D. If the oil level is above the 'X' mark, it indicates severe oil dilution with diesel fuel. The oil must be changed immediately.
  • **Suspension Noise:** Listen for knocking or clunking sounds from the front suspension over speed bumps, which usually indicates worn top strut mounts or anti-roll bar bushes.

Alternatives

**Toyota Yaris:** The benchmark for hybrid efficiency and reliability, though less engaging to drive. **Honda Jazz (Fit):** Unbeatable for interior space and practicality, highly reliable. **Ford Fiesta:** The closest rival in terms of driving dynamics, but the 1.0 EcoBoost engine has a worse reliability record (especially wet belt issues). **Volkswagen Polo:** Offers more interior space and a more refined, 'big car' feel, but DSG gearbox and TSI engine reliability can be hit-or-miss in older models.

Verdict

The Mazda 2 (DJ/DL) is one of the best-kept secrets in the used car market. By avoiding the industry trend of tiny turbocharged engines, Mazda created a supermini that is exceptionally reliable, efficient, and fun to drive. If you choose any of the Skyactiv-G petrol engines, you are buying one of the most dependable cars in its class. However, the 1.5 Skyactiv-D diesel should be strictly avoided due to DPF and oil dilution issues. While it lacks the rear-seat space of a Honda Jazz or VW Polo, its premium feel and bulletproof petrol powertrains make it a highly recommended purchase.
