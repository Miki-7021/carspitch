Summary

The first-generation Skoda Yeti (phase 1, type 5L) is a compact SUV with a cubic and atypical design, based on the robust Volkswagen Group PQ35 platform (shared with the VW Tiguan I and Golf VI). Appreciated for its clever spaciousness thanks to the independent and removable *VarioFlex* rear seat system, it offers rare versatility in the segment. Dynamically, it stands out for its precise road handling, although a bit firm. This report particularly focuses on diesel engines (TDI), which represent the majority of sales in Europe and prove to be the most relevant choices for this vehicle, especially since the petrol engines of this period (EA111 and EA888 Gen2 generation) experienced major early defects. The Yeti benefited from a facelift in late 2013, losing its characteristic round headlights in favor of a more conventional design.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable reliability of the 2.0 TDI engine (noted by ADAC and TÜV)
  • Exceptional modularity (independent, sliding, and removable VarioFlex rear seats)
  • Very sound and safe road handling (Golf VI platform)
  • Build quality and aging of interior materials
  • Excellent traction of 4x4 versions (Haldex 4)
  • Compact size (4.22 m) ideal for parking while offering good interior space

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.8 TSI (EA888 Gen2) and chain issues on 1.2/1.4 TSI (EA111)
  • Capricious and expensive-to-repair DSG7 gearbox (DQ200)
  • Limited boot volume (405 liters) when rear seats are pushed back to the maximum
  • Quite firm suspension comfort, especially with 17-inch wheels
  • Norman wardrobe aerodynamics generating wind noise on the highway
  • EGR valve clogging on short trips (1.6 TDI and 2.0 TDI)

Check before buying

  • Haldex system maintenance history (oil change every 60,000 km) on 4x4 versions.
  • DSG6 gearbox oil change history (every 60,000 km).
  • For TDIs: ask if the 'Dieselgate' update has been done and test the vehicle at low revs to detect jerking (sign of a worn EGR).
  • For TSIs (EA111): chain noise on cold start.
  • For the 1.8 TSI: check oil level and ask if there is oil consumption between oil changes (avoid if > 0.5L/1000 km).
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel on 2.0 TDI (vibrations at idle, knocking when engine is switched off).

Alternatives

  • Europe : Volkswagen Tiguan I (technical cousin, more expensive, less modular), Nissan Qashqai I (best-seller, less precise on the road), Peugeot 3008 I (excellent handling, no mechanical 4x4), Dacia Duster I (more rustic, excellent value for money).
  • Japan / Asia : Suzuki SX4 S-Cross (good compact 4x4 alternative), Mitsubishi ASX.
  • North America : The Skoda Yeti was not marketed in this market (the VW Tiguan ensured the group's presence there).

Verdict

The Skoda Yeti I (2009-2013) is an excellent compact SUV, particularly recommendable with the 2.0 TDI (110, 140 or 170 hp) engine. These diesel engines, combined with a manual gearbox or the robust DSG6, offer above-average reliability (confirmed by ADAC and TÜV), controlled consumption, and undeniable driving pleasure. The Haldex 4x4 transmission is a major asset for mountain dwellers, provided it has been rigorously maintained. However, the petrol engines of this period (before the switch to EA211 belt-driven engines in late 2013/2014) should be approached with extreme caution, the 1.8 TSI even being one to avoid due to its design flaws (excessive oil consumption). If you are looking for a practical, spacious, and robust vehicle, a 2.0 TDI Yeti with a clear service history is a very smart purchase.
