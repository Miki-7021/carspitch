Peugeot 208

I · 2012 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 52/100 — faible

Summary

The first-generation Peugeot 208 (codename A9) marked a major break for the lion brand. Replacing the 207, it introduced the famous 'i-Cockpit' (small steering wheel, raised instrument cluster, central touchscreen) and benefited from a drastic weight reduction (up to -110 kg compared to the 207), greatly improving agility and fuel consumption. Restyled in 2015 (new 3D lights, wider grille, multimedia update), it was a huge commercial success. However, its reliability record is heavily marred by major design flaws in its petrol engines (Prince and PureTech families) and its diesel emissions control systems (BlueHDi).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (typical Peugeot comfort/road holding compromise)
  • Contained weight promoting agility and fuel efficiency
  • Modern interior presentation and improved finish (especially post-2015)
  • e-HDi diesel engines (pre-2015) extremely economical and reliable
  • GTi versions (200/208 hp) very powerful and communicative

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of PureTech petrol engines (wet belt)
  • Random reliability of VTi/THP petrol engines (timing chain)
  • AdBlue tank scandal on BlueHDi diesels
  • i-Cockpit ergonomics: the steering wheel obstructs the instrument cluster for some body types
  • Multimedia system (SMEG) slow, prone to bugs and spontaneous reboots
  • 5-speed manual gearbox (BVM5) notchy and with vague shifting

Check before buying

  • PureTech Petrol: Open the oil filler cap and visually inspect the belt (cracks, fraying). Demand strict maintenance invoices.
  • VTi/THP Petrol: Cold start with the hood open to check for any metallic rattling (slack chain).
  • BlueHDi Diesel: Check for the absence of 'Antipollution fault' or 'UREA' warning lights. Ask if the AdBlue tank has been changed.
  • Electronics: Test the touchscreen (responsiveness, Bluetooth, GPS) and air conditioning.
  • Running gear: Listen for clunking noises on degraded roads (shock absorber mounts, front wishbones).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2020 / December 2022 (PureTech)**: Massive recall (code JZR/HFC) to inspect or replace the timing belt, vacuum pump, and clean the oil strainer following belt degradation.
  • - **May 2016 (1.6 THP)**: Risk of fuel leak on the high-pressure pipe (replacement).
  • - **February 2014 (Running gear)**: Replacement of front suspension wishbone fixing screws (risk of breakage).
  • - **October 2019 (BlueHDi)**: Engine control unit update to correct a DPF emissions fault.
  • - **2016 (Electronics)**: Reprogramming of the intelligent service unit (BSI) following lighting or wiper malfunctions.

Verdict

The Peugeot 208 I is an excellent car on paper, ruined by disastrous engineering choices for its engines. If you are looking for a **petrol** model, caution is advised: avoid 1.2 PureTech engines whose belt has not been recently changed and whose maintenance history is not clear. The older 1.4/1.6 VTi and THP also require great vigilance (chain). Paradoxically, the safest purchases for this generation are the diesels from before 2015 (1.4 HDi and 1.6 e-HDi). If you opt for a BlueHDi, make sure the AdBlue tank has already been replaced with the new reference.
