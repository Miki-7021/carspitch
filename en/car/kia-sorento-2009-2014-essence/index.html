Summary

The second-generation Kia Sorento (code name XM) marks a radical turning point for the model. Abandoning the ladder frame chassis of the first generation for a unibody structure (shared with the Hyundai Santa Fe), it loses pure off-road capabilities but gains in road handling, comfort, and interior space. Offered in 5 or 7-seat configurations, it positions itself as a versatile family SUV. A significant restyling (facelift) occurred in late 2012 (2013 model year), bringing structural modifications to the chassis, an updated interior, and the introduction of direct injection (GDI) on 4-cylinder gasoline engines. Although the European market favored diesel engines (CRDi), the Sorento was widely distributed with gasoline engines in North America, Asia, and the Middle East. Overall note: An excellent family SUV in diesel, but whose 4-cylinder gasoline versions (Theta II) are marred by serious reliability issues.

Strengths 5

  • Generous interior space and availability of 7 true seats
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Safe road handling (transition to unibody chassis)
  • Excellent reliability of diesel engines (2.2 CRDi)
  • Smoothness of the 6-speed automatic gearbox

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Theta II)
  • Gargantuan fuel consumption of the 3.5 V6 gasoline engine
  • Fragility of the AWD system (viscous coupling/splines)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (easily scratched)
  • Slightly firm suspension at low speeds (especially with 18/19-inch wheels)

Check before buying

  • Metallic noises or knocking in the engine (2.4L Gasoline): early sign of connecting rod bearing failure.
  • AWD transmission operation: turn the steering wheel fully at low speed, check for absence of jerks or dull noises (wear of the pump or differential splines).
  • Maintenance history: crucial for gasoline engines. Oil change intervals > 15,000 km are prohibitive for the 2.4L.
  • Rearview camera: the lens tends to fog up with moisture and become blurry or inoperative.
  • Brake light switch: if defective, it can prevent starting (push button) or unlocking the automatic gear lever.

Official recalls

- Theta II Engine (2017/2020): Massive recall (primarily North America) for 2.4 MPI and GDI. Premature wear of connecting rod bearings leading to stalling, engine failure, or fire. Installation of KSDS software or engine block replacement.,- ABS/HECU Module (2020): Risk of short circuit in the anti-lock braking module that can cause a fire in the engine compartment, even with the ignition off. Replacement of fuses or the module.,- Brake Light Switch (2013): Replacement of the defective switch affecting brake lights, cruise control, and ESC.,- Transmission (2015): On some AWD models, the right transmission output shaft may crack, leading to a loss of traction.,- Corrosion (2013): In cold regions (salt), application of an anti-corrosion treatment on the front subframe and suspension arms.

Verdict

The Kia Sorento II (XM) is a two-faced vehicle. If you are looking for a gasoline model (2.4 MPI or GDI), extreme caution is advised: engines from the Theta II family are plagued by major design flaws (connecting rod bearings) that can lead to engine destruction. The purchase of a used 2.4L should only be considered if the engine has already been replaced by Kia or if it benefits from a certified extended warranty. The 3.5 V6 is much more reliable but its running cost (fuel) is prohibitive in Europe. Paradoxically, although your search targets gasoline, the Sorento II is a top buy with the diesel engine (2.2 CRDi), where it proves to be a tireless and extremely reliable workhorse.
