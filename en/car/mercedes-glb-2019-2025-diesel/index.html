Summary

The Mercedes-Benz GLB (X247) is a compact family SUV based on the MFA2 platform (shared with the A-Class and GLA). Its positioning is unique in the premium segment: it offers a cubic silhouette maximizing interior space and provides up to 7 seats (optional), placing it as a more compact alternative to the GLC and more practical than the GLA.

Launched in late 2019, it received a facelift in 2023, introducing mild hybridization (MHEV 48V) on gasoline engines, an update to the MBUX infotainment system, and minor aesthetic tweaks. Although demand for gasoline is increasing, diesel engines (OM654q) remain the preferred choice for this family-oriented and road-trip vehicle, offering generous torque and controlled consumption given the vehicle's weight and aerodynamics.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional spaciousness for its size (4.63 m) and a very rare 7-seater option in this premium segment.
  • Particularly successful diesel engines (OM654q): economical, torquey, and quiet.
  • Advanced onboard technology (highly responsive MBUX dual screen).
  • Suspension comfort, especially with the optional adaptive damping.
  • Smooth and responsive 8G-DCT gearbox, well-calibrated with the diesel's torque.

Weaknesses 5

  • Aerodynamics (cubic profile) generating wind noise on the highway above 120 km/h.
  • Quality of some plastics in the lower part of the cabin, unworthy of the Mercedes badge.
  • 3rd-row seats (7-seater option) strictly reserved for children or for occasional use (max 1.68m according to Mercedes).
  • Very high prices, both for purchase and maintenance within the official network.
  • Absence of a plug-in hybrid (PHEV) powertrain.

Check before buying

  • AdBlue System (Diesel): Check for the absence of an engine warning light related to the anti-pollution system (frequent crystallization if the vehicle has been driven too much in the city).
  • Gearbox (DCT): Ensure that gear changes are smooth, without jerks when cold. Demand the gearbox oil change history.
  • Electronics (MBUX): Test all functionalities of the central screen (possible display bugs, often resolved by OTA updates).
  • Tire wear: The vehicle's weight (especially with 4MATIC and 7 seats) can lead to premature wear of the front running gear.
  • Water ingress: Check the trunk floor and panoramic sunroof seals (some cases of clogged drains).

Alternatives

In Europe: - BMW X1 / X2: Superior finish, better dynamics, but no 7-seater option. - Audi Q3: Very well-rounded, excellent finish, but less spacious. - Volkswagen Tiguan Allspace: The direct mainstream alternative, offers 7 seats, cheaper but less premium. - Land Rover Discovery Sport: Offers 7 seats and true off-road capabilities, but overall reliability significantly lower than the GLB.

In North America: - Lexus NX: Unbeatable reliability, but smaller trunk volume and no 3rd row. - Acura RDX: Excellent price/equipment ratio, dynamic, but 5-seater only.

In Asia / Emerging Markets: - Volvo XC40: Very safe, strong design, but limited rear space.

Verdict

The Mercedes GLB (X247) is undoubtedly the most pragmatic and intelligent compact SUV in the star's lineup. By maximizing interior space on a reduced footprint, it perfectly meets the needs of families.

Diesel Focus: The OM654q engine is a resounding success. It erases the flaws of the previous generation (noise, roughness) while offering remarkable fuel efficiency for a vehicle of this size. The 200 d (150 hp) version represents the ideal compromise: torquey enough to power the loaded vehicle, without the extra cost of the 220 d version.

Buying Advice: Prioritize a 200 d model with a clear service history (especially for the DCT gearbox). Avoid diesel if your use is strictly urban (to preserve the DPF and AdBlue); in this case, the GLB 200 gasoline (M282) will be a more judicious choice. The 2023 facelift brings a real technological advantage, but 2021-2022 models currently offer the best value for money on the used market.
