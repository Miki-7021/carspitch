Summary

The fourth-generation Mercedes-Benz A-Class (W177 for the compact hatchback, V177 for the sedan, Z177 for the Chinese long-wheelbase version) marks a major technological turning point for the star brand. Based on the MFA2 platform, it introduced the MBUX dual-screen infotainment system, redefining premium segment standards. While the visual presentation is spectacular, the first model years (2018-2019) suffered from early defects (electronic bugs, cabin rattles). The late 2022 facelift corrected most of these shortcomings, simplified the engine range (widespread adoption of 48V mild-hybridization on petrol engines), and improved perceived quality. Mechanically, the petrol engines (partially developed with the Renault-Nissan Alliance for the 1.3L) are generally reliable, although the 2.0L has experienced some specific weaknesses.

Strengths 5

  • MBUX system still at the top of its class (responsiveness, voice control)
  • Very successful, economical, and reliable 1.3L petrol engine (M282)
  • Dynamic and safe road handling
  • Very appealing and aerodynamic exterior design
  • Excellent electric range on the A 250 e plug-in hybrid version

Weaknesses 5

  • Disappointing interior assembly quality on 2018-2019 models (parasitic noises)
  • Dual-clutch gearbox sometimes harsh at low speeds
  • Poor rear visibility
  • Very high purchase prices and maintenance costs within the Mercedes network
  • Costly cylinder head issue on early 2.0L petrol engines (A 220 / A 250)

Check before buying

  • Complete history of software updates (MBUX and engine/gearbox control units).
  • Absence of engine warning light on A 220 and A 250 (risk of defective cylinder head).
  • Smoothness of the DCT gearbox during very low-speed maneuvers and in reverse.
  • Wear of front tires, particularly on the A 250 e (significant electric torque).
  • Operation of the charging flap and cable on plug-in hybrid versions.

Verdict

The Mercedes A-Class W177 is a highly technological compact car that has managed to make its predecessors' dynamic shortcomings forgotten. If you are targeting a petrol engine, the 1.3L unit (A 180, A 200) is the most rational and reliable choice, the result of a successful collaboration with Renault. However, caution is advised for 2.0L versions (A 220, A 250) from before 2021 due to proven weaknesses in the cylinder head. Models produced from 2020 onwards, and especially the facelifted versions (late 2022), are largely preferable as they have been purged of most of their electronic bugs and assembly defects. Purchase is recommended, provided a clear service history exclusive to the manufacturer's network is demanded.
