KGM Korando

III (C200) · 2010 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 76/100 — bon

Summary

The third-generation Korando (code name C200) marks a historic turning point for the South Korean brand (sold under the name **SsangYong** at the time, today **KGM**). Designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro, it abandons the ladder chassis for a modern monocoque structure, positioning it against European and Asian compact SUVs. It benefited from two major facelifts: in 2013 (significant improvement in interior quality and soundproofing) and in 2017 (new front end). Although shunned in Western Europe in favor of diesels, the 2.0 e-XGi petrol engine was a great success in Eastern European, Asian, and South American markets thanks to its simple and robust design. It is a rational SUV, offering an excellent price-to-roominess ratio and very good towing capabilities, but penalized by a weak brand image and high depreciation.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent overall mechanical reliability on the petrol version (2.0 e-XGi)
  • Generous rear roominess (flat floor) and trunk volume
  • High towing capacity (up to 2000 kg depending on version)
  • Unbeatable price-to-equipment ratio on the used market
  • Very smooth and reliable Aisin automatic gearbox (on petrol and 2.2 diesel)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very high fuel consumption on the 2.0 petrol (often > 9L/100km)
  • Petrol engine lacking low-rev torque (197 Nm) compared to current standards
  • Very basic interior plastic quality on pre-2013 models (scratch easily)
  • DSI automatic gearbox on early 2.0 diesels (slow, jerking, fragility)
  • Sparse dealer network and difficult resale (high depreciation)

Check before buying

  • On petrol versions: condition of the exhaust (possible surface corrosion) and oil change history.
  • On 2.0 diesel automatic versions: demand the DSI gearbox oil change history. Test gear shifts cold and hot (no jerking should be felt).
  • Running gear: premature wear of the anti-roll bar silent blocks (thumping noises over bumps).
  • Electronics: operation of the multimedia/GPS system (frequent bugs on early generations) and parking sensors.
  • Underbody: check for absence of impacts or rust if the vehicle was used off-road or in the mountains (salt).

Verdict

The SsangYong/KGM Korando III is an extremely rational used choice for those looking for a spacious SUV without breaking the bank. **The 2.0 e-XGi petrol version is the hidden gem of the range in terms of reliability**: its simple architecture (no turbo, indirect injection, timing chain) shields it from the costly breakdowns that strike modern downsized engines. Its only real flaw is its fuel appetite. If you opt for a diesel, avoid the 2.0 models associated with the DSI automatic gearbox and favor the post-2015 2.2 e-XDi, which is much more refined. Buy it with full knowledge of the facts: you will keep it until the end, as its resale will be difficult.
