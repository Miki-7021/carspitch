Opel Vectra

C (2002–2008) · 2002 – 2008

NEGOTIATE Reliability 65/100 — moyen

Summary

The third generation of the Opel Vectra, known by the code C, was launched in 2002, succeeding the Vectra B. It shared its Epsilon platform with other GM models such as the Saab 9-3 and the Chevrolet Malibu. Offered as a 4-door sedan, 5-door sedan (GTS), and wagon (Caravan), it targeted the family car segment, competing with models like the Volkswagen Passat, Ford Mondeo, and Peugeot 406/407. A major facelift occurred in 2005, bringing aesthetic modifications (front fascia, lights) and technical improvements, notably the introduction of new diesel and petrol engines. The Vectra C also served as the basis for the Signum model, a more luxurious and spacious variant. Overall, the Vectra C offers a good price/performance ratio on the used car market, but its reliability is mixed, with recurring weaknesses on certain engines and electronics.

Strengths 6

  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially on highways)
  • Generous spaciousness, especially in wagon version (Caravan)
  • Often comprehensive standard equipment
  • Good road holding and safe road behavior
  • Very attractive price/performance ratio on the used car market
  • 1.9 CDTI (Fiat) engines generally reliable and powerful

Weaknesses 5

  • Electronic reliability (CIM module, ABS/ESP, power windows)
  • Recurring problems on certain engines (Z22YH, Y20/22DTH, Y30DT)
  • Somewhat austere interior finish and materials aging poorly in places
  • Large turning radius
  • Less prestigious brand image than some competitors

Check before buying

  • **Electronics:** Check the proper functioning of all equipment (air conditioning, cruise control, power windows, car radio, dashboard warning lights). The CIM module (steering column) is a known weakness.
  • **Diesel Engines:** For 2.0/2.2 DTI, ensure the VP44 injection pump has been replaced or that there are no symptoms (difficult hot start, loss of power). For 1.9 CDTI, check for abnormal noises from the dual-mass flywheel, proper alternator function, and the condition of the intake manifold (swirl flaps on the 150hp). For 3.0 V6 CDTI, check the cylinder head gasket history and absence of overheating.
  • **Petrol Engines:** For the 2.2 Z22SE, check the timing chain history. For the 2.2 Z22YH, **absolutely avoid** unless there is proof of recent HP pump replacement. For others, ignition coils and EGR valve.
  • **Suspension and Steering:** Clunking noises from the front (ball joints, anti-roll bar links), play in the steering.
  • **Braking:** Wear of discs and pads, ABS/ESP function (warning lights).
  • **Bodywork:** General condition, signs of impact, corrosion (less common than on older Opels, but to be checked).

Verdict

The Opel Vectra C is a spacious and comfortable family car, offering an excellent price/equipment ratio on the used car market. However, its reliability is uneven and highly dependent on the chosen engine and maintenance history. The 1.8L (Z18XER) and 2.0 Turbo (Z20NET) petrol versions are good choices. For diesels, the **1.9 CDTI (120 or 150 hp)** is the most recommendable, provided that known weaknesses are checked (dual-mass flywheel, alternator, swirl flaps on the 150hp). The older 2.0/2.2 DTI and the 3.0 V6 CDTI (especially the Y30DT) should be approached with great caution due to costly problems. The 2.2 Direct (Z22YH) is to be avoided. A thorough inspection of the electronics and a complete service history are crucial. If you find a well-maintained example with a reliable engine, the Vectra C can be an excellent deal for road and family use.
