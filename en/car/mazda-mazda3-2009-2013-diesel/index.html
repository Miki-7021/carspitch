Mazda Mazda3

II (BL) · 2009 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The second-generation Mazda3 (code BL) had the difficult task of succeeding a very successful first iteration (BK). It reuses the platform (shared with the Ford Focus II) but significantly improves rigidity, aerodynamics, and perceived quality. While the petrol engines shine with almost irreproachable reliability, the picture is much more contrasted on the diesel side (MZ-CD and MZR-CD), which accounted for the bulk of sales in Europe. The model benefited from a facelift in late 2011, bringing minor aesthetic touches, better sound insulation, and, above all, a major evolution of the 1.6 diesel block (from 109 to 115 hp) which transformed its reliability. Overall rating: excellent for petrol, very uneven for diesel.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis: comfort/handling compromise among the best in its class
  • Absolute reliability of petrol engines (MZR)
  • Precise and pleasant manual gearbox control
  • Very rich standard equipment (especially in Sport or Dynamique trim)
  • Corrosion resistance significantly improved compared to the previous generation (BK)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 109 hp diesel (turbo failure) and very erratic reliability of the 2.2 diesel (chain, oil strainer)
  • Imperfect sound insulation (road and wind noise on the highway)
  • Hard interior plastics and very prone to scratches
  • Fragile paint (prone to stone chips on the front fascia)
  • Average rear passenger space and boot volume (340L) for its size (4.46m)

Check before buying

  • On 1.6 MZ-CD 109hp: Presence of black tar around the injectors ('black death') and turbo whistling.
  • On 2.2 MZR-CD: Oil level (if it's above the 'X' on the dipstick, there's fuel dilution). Flashing DPF light = stretched timing chain.
  • On all models: Wear of anti-roll bar bushings (clunking noises on degraded roads).
  • On all models: Functioning of the tire pressure monitoring system (fragile TPMS sensors at the valves).
  • On models imported from hot regions (North America/Australia): Deformation or melting of the dashboard cowl.

Verdict

If you are looking for a Mazda3 BL, the golden rule is simple: **prioritize petrol**. The 1.6 and 2.0 MZR engines are models of reliability that will take you very far without issues. If your annual mileage requires a **diesel**, avoid the 1.6 MZ-CD 109 hp (before 2011) and be extremely vigilant with the 2.2 MZR-CD (clear service history mandatory, check of the oil strainer and chain). The only rational and recommendable diesel choice is the **1.6 MZ-CD 115 hp** (appeared late 2010), which is much more robust and refined.
