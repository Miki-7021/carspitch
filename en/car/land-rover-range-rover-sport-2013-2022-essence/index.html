Land Rover Range Rover Sport

II (L494) · 2013 – 2022

NEGOTIATE Reliability 42/100 — faible

Summary

The second-generation Land Rover Range Rover Sport (L494) marks a major technical turning point compared to its predecessor (L320) by adopting an all-aluminum architecture, allowing it to shed up to 420 kg. Positioned as the ultimate dynamic and luxurious SUV, it offers exceptional on-road and off-road performance. It received a facelift in late 2017 (2018 models) introducing new headlights, the dual touchscreen (Touch Pro Duo), and the P400e plug-in hybrid version. However, despite its very high-end positioning, the L494 suffers from a particularly bleak reliability record. Data from the TÜV Report (22.5% failure rate at 3 years), ADAC (5.8 breakdowns/1000 vehicles), and What Car? (score of 2/5, 36% breakdown rate) confirm that this model requires a colossal maintenance budget and an extended warranty to be considered with peace of mind.

Strengths 4

  • Exceptional road handling and outstanding off-road capabilities
  • Finish quality, interior luxury, and soundproofing
  • Performance and sound of the V8 Supercharged petrol engines
  • Aluminum chassis offering better driving dynamics than the L320 generation

Weaknesses 5

  • Alarming TÜV Report defect rate (22.5% at 3 years), one of the worst in the premium segment
  • Disastrous reliability according to What Car? (36% reported breakdowns, score 2/5)
  • Very fragile air suspension (EAS) and extremely expensive to repair (ADAC)
  • Temperamental onboard electronics (black screens, infotainment bugs)
  • Exorbitant maintenance, insurance, and spare parts costs

Check before buying

  • Crystal-clear maintenance history exclusively from the Land Rover network or a specialist
  • Operation of the air suspension (raise/lower the vehicle, listen to the compressor)
  • Signs of coolant leaks in the center of the V on V6 and V8 petrol engines
  • Condition of the front suspension wishbones and steering (rapid wear reported by the TÜV Report)
  • For PHEVs: battery state of health (SOH) and application of fire recalls

Verdict

The Range Rover Sport L494 is a paradoxical vehicle: it is one of the most desirable, luxurious, and high-performing SUVs on the market, but it is also one of the least reliable. The statistics from ADAC, the TÜV Report, and What Car? are definitive. If you are targeting a **petrol** engine, avoid the early 5.0 SC V8s (pre-2015) due to timing chain issues, and favor the recent inline 6-cylinders (P360/P400) or a post-2016 V8 with a meticulous maintenance history. The PHEV model (P400e) should be approached with extreme caution given the battery recalls. **Golden rule: never buy this vehicle without a comprehensive and valid mechanical breakdown warranty.**
