Summary

The Espace I (codename J11) is a historic vehicle: it is the pioneer of the minivan segment in Europe. Designed and produced by Matra for Renault, it stands out for its innovative design: bodywork made of fiberglass and polyester panels bonded to a galvanized steel chassis. This architecture makes it impervious to rust on its exterior panels and relatively light. Launched in 1984, it had very timid commercial beginnings before becoming a huge success. It benefited from a facelift (Phase 2) in 1988, modernizing its front end (slanted headlights, integrated grille) and improving interior finish quality. Today, it is a collector's vehicle (youngtimer) prized for its revolutionary modularity (swiveling, removable seats) and its immense glass surface.

Strengths 6

  • Composite bodywork (impervious to external corrosion)
  • Hot-dip galvanized chassis (excellent rust resistance for its time)
  • Exceptionally robust 2.1 Turbo D (J8S) engine
  • Revolutionary interior modularity (swiveling front seats, removable rear seats)
  • Exceptional peripheral visibility (thin pillars)
  • Suspension comfort and road handling (close to a sedan)

Weaknesses 6

  • Very capricious electrical harness and connectors (a common issue with 80s Renault/Matra vehicles)
  • Aging interior plastics (cracking, discoloration, breakage)
  • Headliner fabric and door trims that peel off
  • Sensitivity to crosswinds
  • Very average sound insulation, especially on the 2.1 Turbo D
  • Availability of specific spare parts (bodywork, fittings, Quadra parts) becoming critical

Check before buying

  • Condition of the cooling system on the 2.1 Turbo D (traces of leaks, swollen radiator, 'mayonnaise' in the oil)
  • Functioning of all electrical accessories (windows, ventilation, dashboard, central locking)
  • Condition of the fuse board (glove compartment): traces of overheating or DIY repairs
  • Cracks or delamination of the fiberglass (especially at attachment points and bumpers)
  • Corrosion of front/rear subframes and suspension wishbones (the chassis is galvanized, but not the running gear)
  • Presence and condition of the rear seats (often lost or damaged over time by owners)

Official recalls

Due to the vehicle's age (pre-1990), modern databases (Rappel Conso, etc.) do not list official recalls from that era. Historically, campaigns of that time (often managed during revisions) concerned: - 1984-1985 : Adjustments to the steering rack and front axle anchorages on the very first models. - 1985-1986 : Replacement or reinforcement of certain electrical connectors (service board) prone to overheating. - 1988 (Quadra) : Inspection of the composite driveshaft.

What sets it apart 6

Reliability

  • Unrivaled Body Durability — Benefit from a composite body and galvanized chassis, offering exceptional corrosion resistance for extended longevity.
  • Legendary Robust Diesel Engine — The 2.1 Turbo D (J8S) engine is renowned for its reliability, capable of exceeding 400,000 km with proper maintenance.

Practicality

  • Revolutionary Interior Modularity — Enjoy unparalleled adaptability with pivoting front seats and removable rear seats, allowing for infinitely customizable configurations.

Comfort

  • Exceptional Driving Comfort — Experience smooth suspension comfort and stable road handling, offering a driving experience comparable to a sedan.

Safety

  • Optimal Peripheral Visibility — Drive with confidence thanks to slim pillars and a large glass area, ensuring an unobstructed view of the road and enhanced safety.

Economy

  • Controlled Fuel Consumption — The 2.1 Turbo D engine offers remarkable efficiency for its category, making this vehicle ideal for long journeys and economical use.

Alternatives

  • North America / Import : Chrysler Voyager / Dodge Caravan (the direct competitor, which arrived later in Europe), Plymouth Voyager.
  • Japan / Asia : Nissan Prairie (smaller but with a similar concept of sliding doors without a central pillar), Mitsubishi Space Wagon (Chariot).
  • Europe : Upon its release in 1984, the Espace had no direct competition in the European passenger vehicle market.

Verdict

Buying a Renault Espace I today is a matter of collecting (Youngtimer) rather than buying a classic used vehicle. The 2.1 Turbo D (J8S) engine is the sensible choice for high-mileage drivers or those who want the most indestructible mechanics in the range, provided the cooling system has been maintained. It's an agricultural but tireless engine. Absolutely prioritize a Phase 2 (1988-1991), which is better finished and electrically more reliable. The biggest challenge will not be the mechanics (engine parts are easily found), but the preservation of the interior and tracking down electrical failures. Avoid models with fractured fiberglass bodywork or ruined interiors, as specific parts are unobtainable.
