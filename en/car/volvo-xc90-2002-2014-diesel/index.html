Volvo XC90

I (C/N) · 2002 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 76/100 — bon

Summary

The first-generation Volvo XC90 (P2 platform) is a historic premium SUV for the Swedish brand. Launched in 2002, it redefined family safety standards with its 7 true seats, its anti-rollover system (ROPS), and its exceptional interior space. Facelifted in 2006 (headlights, taillights, bumpers, arrival of the 185hp D5 and the V8), it enjoyed a career of rare longevity (12 years in Europe, until 2016 in China as the XC Classic). While its road handling is sluggish compared to a BMW X5, it excels in comfort and durability. The diesel engine (the famous 5-cylinder D5) accounted for the vast majority of sales in Europe and remains the most relevant choice, although it is important to distinguish between the different generations of this engine (Euro 3, Euro 4, Euro 5) which present distinct reliability profiles.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional passive and active safety for its era
  • Giant interior space and 7 true seats retractable into the floor
  • Seat comfort (among the best in the automotive industry)
  • Exceptional longevity of the 5-cylinder D5 engine block (Euro 3 particularly)
  • Quality of the audio system (Premium Sound Dynaudio/Alpine)
  • Bodywork corrosion resistance

Weaknesses 6

  • Very high weight (over 2 tons) leading to rapid wear of running gear (bushings, ball joints, tires, brakes)
  • Turning radius worthy of a truck
  • GM automatic gearbox on the T6 extremely fragile
  • AWD system (Haldex and angle gear) requiring rigorous maintenance to avoid costly failure
  • Interior plastics (center console, buttons) that peel over time
  • Very expensive self-leveling rear shock absorbers (Nivomat) to replace

Check before buying

  • AWD system operation: test on slippery ground or gravel. A worn angle gear sleeve or a faulty Haldex pump turns the XC90 into a simple FWD vehicle.
  • History of timing belt AND accessory belt replacement on D5 engines.
  • Condition of front wishbone bushings, anti-roll bar links, and engine mounts (heavily stressed by weight).
  • Water ingress: check for dampness under the front carpets (clogged windshield cowl drains) which can burn out the CEM (Central Electronic Module).
  • Dashboard (DIM): check that all displays and gauges are working (frequent dry solders on 2002-2006 models).
  • Automatic gearbox: demand oil change (flush) invoices. Jerks when hot indicate a tired valve body (especially on early TF-80SC 2006-2007 models).

Official recalls

  • - **2006** : Replacement of steering ball joints (premature wear potentially leading to loss of control). - **2008** : Cooling fan module (risk of short circuit and fire). - **2009** : Possible leak on the fuel injection rail (diesel engines). - **2010** : Seat belt attachment issue on the third row of seats. - **2020** : Massive recall (2006-2014 models) for front seat belts (steel cable can weaken over time).

  • Exceptional Space and Versatility — Enjoy a generous interior with 7 true seats that fold flat into the floor, offering unparalleled flexibility for large families or transporting bulky items.

Alternatives

- **Europe** : BMW X5 (E53/E70 - more dynamic but less spacious), Mercedes-Benz ML-Class (W163/W164), Audi Q7 (4L - similar size, V6 TDI), Volkswagen Touareg. - **North America / Asia** : Lexus RX (more reliable but 5 seats only), Acura MDX, Honda Pilot.

Verdict

The first-generation Volvo XC90 is a formidable family tool, designed to cover miles safely. For a used car, the engine choice is crucial. **The D5 diesel is the best buy**, but you have to choose your camp: the 163hp D5 (2002-2006) offers absolute mechanical reliability at the cost of modest performance and an unfavorable Crit'Air sticker. The 200hp D5 (2010-2014) is the best modernity/driving pleasure compromise. Absolutely avoid the T6 petrol engine and its GM gearbox. Buying an XC90 requires a substantial budget for the maintenance of the running gear (heavy vehicle) and the AWD transmission. Prioritize a model with a clear history, up-to-date gearbox and Haldex oil changes, rather than low mileage.
