Summary

The third-generation Kia Sorento (UM) marks a spectacular move upmarket for the Korean manufacturer. Gone is the rustic off-roader positioning; this Sorento asserts itself as a large family SUV (up to 7 seats) focused on comfort, soundproofing, and technology. Sharing its platform with the Hyundai Santa Fe, it benefited from a late 2017 facelift (2018 model year) bringing slight aesthetic touches, new driving aids, and, above all, an excellent 8-speed automatic gearbox replacing the old 6-speed unit. On the European market, it was almost exclusively distributed with a diesel engine (2.2 CRDi), a unit renowned for its great robustness.

Overall generation rating: An excellent choice for large families looking for an affordable and reliable alternative to German premium SUVs, provided you prioritize the diesel and absolutely avoid the 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Theta II) sold in other markets.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable reliability of the 2.2 CRDi diesel engine
  • Giant interior space and true modularity (usable 7 seats)
  • Soundproofing and ride comfort worthy of the upper segment
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Excellent towing capacity (up to 2,500 kg in diesel)
  • 7-year manufacturer's warranty (often still active on later models)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very high weight (nearly 2 tons) penalizing agility and braking
  • High fuel consumption in the city, even with diesel
  • Disastrous reliability of 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Theta II) sold outside Europe
  • Multimedia system of the early phases (2015-2017) a bit dated
  • Fragility of the AWD coupler if not maintained

Check before buying

  • All-Wheel Drive (AWD): Check for the absence of jerks during tight turns at low speed. Failure of the Haldex (Dynamax) coupler pump or wear of the splines is common if maintenance has been neglected.
  • DPF and EGR (Diesel): Ensure the vehicle has not been used exclusively in the city. An unstable idle or acceleration hesitations can indicate clogging.
  • Maintenance history: Demand frequent oil changes (ideally every 15,000 km). The 30,000 km intervals are too long for the longevity of the turbo and chain.
  • Suspension noises: Due to its weight, the front wishbone bushings and anti-roll bar links wear prematurely (clunking noises on degraded roads).
  • Oil level (Gasoline): On imported models (2.4 GDI / 2.0 T-GDI), avoid any vehicle showing a low oil level or engine knocking.

Official recalls

  • Tow Hitch Harness (2022): Massive recall (2016-2022 models) for a risk of short circuit and fire at the tow hitch harness module, even with the ignition off. Fuse removal at the dealership pending repair.
  • Theta II Engines (2017-2021): Multiple recalls in North America for the 2.4 GDI and 2.0 T-GDI concerning premature connecting rod bearing wear (risk of failure and fire). KSDS software installation or engine replacement.
  • High-Pressure Fuel Line (2018): On 3.3 V6 engines, risk of fuel leak due to improper tightening at the factory.
  • Seatbelt Latch (2015): Replacement of front seatbelt buckles that might not latch correctly.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Skoda Kodiaq (more dynamic, efficient TDI engines), Peugeot 5008 (more agile but without 4x4 transmission), Hyundai Santa Fe (its direct technical cousin), Nissan X-Trail.
  • North America: Toyota Highlander (benchmark for V6/Hybrid reliability), Honda Pilot (very spacious), Ford Explorer.
  • Asia / Oceania: Mazda CX-8 (premium finish, sensitive diesel), Toyota Fortuner (ladder frame, more rustic but indestructible).

Verdict

The Kia Sorento III is an exceptional family SUV provided you choose the right engine. In Europe, where the 2.2 CRDi reigns supreme, it is a highly recommended purchase (Verdict: BUY). This diesel engine is one of the most reliable of its generation, capable of handling very high mileages if maintenance is followed. Prioritize facelifted versions (post-2018) equipped with the 8-speed automatic gearbox, which is smoother and lowers highway fuel consumption. However, if you reside in North America or are looking for an imported model equipped with the 2.4 GDI or 2.0 T-GDI 4-cylinder gasoline engines, the verdict radically reverses (Verdict: AVOID) due to major engine failure risks.
