Vauxhall Corsa

F · 2019 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 55/100 — moyen

Summary

The **Vauxhall Corsa F** (marketed as Opel in continental Europe) marks a radical turning point in the model's history. As the first generation developed under the PSA era (now Stellantis), it is based on the modular CMP platform, thus sharing its underpinnings with the Peugeot 208 II. This paradigm shift gives it transformed road handling, significant weight reduction (up to 108 kg less than the E generation), and a much more dynamic design. A facelift occurred in 2023, introducing the 'Vauxhall Vizor' grille and new hybrid powertrains.

**Focus on the Diesel (1.5 Turbo D):** Although the market is increasingly shunning diesel, the 102 hp 1.5 Turbo D engine (from PSA's BlueHDi / DV5 family) was offered for high-mileage drivers. It stands out for its exceptional fuel efficiency and generous torque (250 Nm), ideal for highway driving. However, it inherits the chronic weaknesses of this Stellantis engine, requiring increased vigilance when buying used.

Strengths 5

  • Extremely low fuel consumption on the 1.5 Turbo D
  • Dynamic road handling and rigorous chassis (CMP platform)
  • Successful and modern exterior design (especially post-2023 facelift)
  • Comprehensive technological equipment (Matrix LED headlights rare in this category)
  • Very smooth EAT8 automatic gearbox (8-speed automatic) on internal combustion versions

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 1.2 petrol engines (wet belt) before 2023
  • Erratic reliability of the 1.5 Diesel (AdBlue and camshaft chain)
  • Very average rear passenger space and narrow rear access
  • Boot volume in the lower average (and very reduced on the electric version)
  • Firm suspension, particularly on GS Line / Ultimate trims

Check before buying

  • On the 1.5 Diesel: Metallic rattling noise on cold start (slack camshaft chain).
  • On the 1.5 Diesel: Presence of 'Antipollution Fault' or 'AdBlue' warning lights on the dashboard (pump/tank faulty).
  • On the 1.5 Diesel: History of the previous owner's journeys (avoid if primarily used in the city).
  • On 1.2 Petrol: Visual condition of the timing belt via the oil cap (cracks, swelling).
  • On all versions: Functionality of the touchscreen and reversing camera (frequent bugs).

Official recalls

  • - **2020 (Steering):** Risk of loosening steering tie rods on models manufactured in 2019.
  • - **2020 (Rear Axle):** Welding defect on the rear axle potentially leading to loss of control (late 2019 production).
  • - **2021 (Fuel - Diesel):** Possible leak at the high-pressure fuel pump on the 1.5 Diesel, risk of fire.
  • - **2022 (Braking - Petrol):** Massive recall (shared with Peugeot/Citroën) concerning the degradation of the timing belt clogging the vacuum pump, leading to a loss of braking assistance.
  • - **2023 (Software - Diesel):** Engine control unit update to correct NOx emissions (AdBlue management).

Verdict

The Vauxhall/Opel Corsa F is an attractive city car, well-designed and pleasant to drive. However, the choice of engine is a real minefield.

**Regarding the 1.5 Turbo D:** This is a fantastic engine for high-mileage drivers due to its exceptional fuel efficiency. Nevertheless, it demands absolute vigilance. Only buy this diesel if you drive more than 20,000 km/year, primarily on highways. When purchasing, demand a clear service history, check if the AdBlue tank has already been replaced, and listen carefully for any camshaft chain issues during a cold start (prioritize a model where the 8mm chain kit has already been installed).

If you drive little, avoid the diesel and older 1.2 petrol engines with belts. Opt for the new 1.2 Hybrid (chain-driven) versions or the Corsa Electric, which are much more reassuring.
