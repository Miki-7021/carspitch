Summary

The Mercedes GLE V167 generation, launched in 2018, represents the fourth iteration of the star brand's mid-size luxury SUV (or second if the GLE is considered the direct successor to the ML). It follows in the footsteps of large premium SUVs, offering a blend of luxury, advanced technology, comfort, and on-road and off-road capabilities.

Compared to its predecessor (W166), the V167 gains in interior space, particularly with the seven-seat option, and integrates the latest generation MBUX infotainment system. Its design is more fluid and modern. A significant facelift occurred in 2023, bringing aesthetic (headlights, bumpers) and technical evolutions (all engines became mild-hybrid, improved PHEVs, second-generation MBUX).

The GLE V167 is a complex and sophisticated vehicle. Its general reliability is good for a vehicle in this category, but its technological complexity can lead to high maintenance and repair costs in case of problems. The diesel engines, particularly targeted by this report, are pillars of the range in Europe, offering an excellent compromise between power, torque, and consumption for a vehicle of this size.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with Airmatic suspension)
  • Quality of finish and interior materials
  • Advanced onboard technology (MBUX, driving aids)
  • Range of powerful and efficient engines (especially the 6-cylinder diesels)
  • Generous interior space and 7-seat option
  • On-road and off-road capabilities (with Off-Road package)

Weaknesses 6

  • Technological complexity potentially leading to high maintenance costs
  • High vehicle weight (impact on consumption, brake/tire wear)
  • Reliability of the 48V system (ISG/battery) on Mild-Hybrid versions (pre-facelift)
  • Potential problems related to the AdBlue system on diesels
  • Some MBUX software bugs (often fixed by updates)
  • High purchase price and options

Check before buying

  • Complete and up-to-date service history (Mercedes network preferred)
  • AdBlue system operation (warning lights, abnormal consumption)
  • 48V system status (warning lights, abnormal starter-generator noises)
  • Absence of leaks in the air suspension (Airmatic)
  • Functioning of all electronic equipment and the MBUX system
  • Brake and tire wear (heavy and powerful vehicle)
  • Condition of the high-voltage battery and charging system on PHEVs
  • Presence of unperformed manufacturer recalls

Verdict

The Mercedes GLE V167 is a highly accomplished luxury SUV, offering a high level of comfort and cutting-edge technology. Its reliability is generally good for a vehicle in this category, but its technical complexity, particularly with the 48V Mild-Hybrid systems and PHEVs, can be a source of costly breakdowns if maintenance is not impeccable.

**For diesel engines (OM654 and OM656):** They represent an excellent choice for their torque, fuel efficiency, and durability. Problems are mainly related to anti-pollution systems (AdBlue, DPF, EGR), common to all modern diesels. Predominantly highway use and regular maintenance are essential to prevent these issues. The 6-cylinder versions (350 d, 400 d, 450 d) are particularly recommended for their driving pleasure.

**Buying advice:** * **BUY** if the service history is complete and performed at a dealership, and if manufacturer recalls have been carried out. Prioritize post-facelift models (2023+) for the improvements made to the 48V and PHEV systems, or the 6-cylinder diesels which are safe bets. * **NEGOTIATE** if the vehicle is a pre-facelift model equipped with the 48V system (M256, M177) and the seller cannot prove software updates or specific system checks. Also negotiate if the service history is incomplete or if recalls are pending. * **AVOID** vehicles without a clear history, with warning lights on (especially AdBlue or 48V), or showing signs of electronic or Airmatic suspension malfunction. PHEVs without a high-voltage battery warranty can be risky.

In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a Mercedes specialist is highly recommended.
