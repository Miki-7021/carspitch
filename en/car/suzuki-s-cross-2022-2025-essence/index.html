Suzuki S-Cross

II · 2022 – 2025

BUY Reliability 89/100 — excellent

All Suzuki models

Summary

The second-generation Suzuki S-Cross (often technically considered a very heavy facelift of the first generation) was launched in late 2021 for the 2022 model year. It abandons its curves for a much more cubic, statutory, and assertive design, bringing it visually closer to a true SUV rather than a raised crossover.

Faithful to the Suzuki philosophy, it shines with its pragmatism: an aggressive price-to-equipment ratio, first-rate mechanical reliability, and the availability of an excellent AWD system (AllGrip), which is rare in this segment. In Europe, it skips diesel and focuses exclusively on electrified gasoline engines (48V mild-hybrid and 140V self-charging hybrid). While it falls short with a somewhat dated interior presentation (hard plastics) and soundproofing that could be improved, it remains a formidable rational choice for buyers seeking peace of mind.

Overall design rating: 7.5/10 (A no-frills vehicle, technically proven, but lacking in refinement).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall mechanical reliability (chain-driven engines, mastered technologies)
  • Very effective AllGrip AWD system (with driving modes)
  • Very low real-world fuel consumption, particularly on the 1.4 MHEV
  • Unbeatable price-to-equipment ratio (360° camera, heated seats often standard)
  • Decent roominess and trunk volume (430L) for its size (4.30m)

Weaknesses 5

  • Drivability of the AGS automated gearbox on the 1.5 HEV version (slowness, jerks)
  • Quality of interior materials (omnipresence of hard, scratch-sensitive plastics)
  • Light soundproofing on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • Somewhat slow infotainment system with a dated interface
  • Reduced trunk volume on the 1.5 HEV version (battery under the floor)

Check before buying

  • AGS gearbox behavior (1.5 HEV): during the test drive, check for the absence of excessive shuddering when pulling away.
  • Tire wear: a few cases of imperfect factory alignment causing asymmetrical wear.
  • Multimedia system operation: test the Apple CarPlay / Android Auto connection, which can sometimes disconnect (requires a dealership update).
  • Underbody: on AllGrip versions, check for the absence of impacts under the vehicle (off-road use by the previous owner).

Official recalls

Official safety recalls (Rappel Conso / RAPEX)

[2023-02-22] Braking (Vacuum pump): Affects Swift, Baleno, Vitara, and S-Cross models. *Reason*: Due to the swelling of the valve in the vacuum pump, the negative pressure of the brake boosters can be temporarily blocked. Consequently, braking assistance may be reduced, increasing the stopping distance and the risk of an accident. *Fix*: Inspection and replacement of the valve or vacuum pump at the dealership.

Verdict

The Suzuki S-Cross II is the archetype of a pragmatic purchase. If you are looking for a vehicle with an avant-garde design or soft-touch plastics, look elsewhere. On the other hand, if you prioritize long-term reliability, reduced maintenance costs, and a true 4x4 transmission, it is an exceptional choice.

Buying advice: Absolutely favor the 1.4 Boosterjet MHEV (129 hp) engine. It offers excellent drivability, very reasonable fuel consumption, and flawless reliability. The 1.5 Dualjet HEV (115 hp) version is to be avoided unless you drive exclusively in the city and have an extremely smooth driving style, due to an outdated AGS automated gearbox that ruins the overall drivability.
