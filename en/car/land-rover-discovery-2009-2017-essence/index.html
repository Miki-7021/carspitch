Summary

The **Land Rover Discovery IV (L319 facelift generation)** is not an entirely new model, but a profound and highly successful evolution of the Discovery III. It retains the extremely heavy Integrated Body Frame chassis (over 2.5 tonnes), but significantly modernizes the cabin, electronics, and engines to elevate itself to the level of luxury SUVs. While it offers unparalleled off-road capabilities, regal comfort, and an ultra-practical cubic interior space (up to 7 real seats), **its reliability record is historically disastrous**. Maintenance and repair costs are prohibitive, weighed down by a capricious air suspension, fragile electronics, and engines (both petrol and diesel) prone to major failures. Overall reliability rating: Poor.

Strengths 5

  • Outstanding off-road capabilities (Terrain Response, low-range gearbox)
  • Exceptional ride comfort (air suspension)
  • Cubic interior space, 7 real seats usable by adults
  • Excellent towing capacity (up to 3,500 kg)
  • All-around visibility ("Command Driving Position")

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous overall reliability (TÜV Report 3-year defect rate: 25%, among the worst on the market)
  • Excessive weight (2.6 tonnes) leading to premature wear of brakes and running gear
  • Air suspension very expensive to maintain (#1 breakdown according to ADAC)
  • Risk of engine failure on diesels (crankshaft) and overheating on the petrol V6
  • Capricious electronics (EPB parking brake, sensors, battery drain)
  • Prohibitive maintenance, insurance, and fuel costs

Check before buying

  • Clear maintenance history and invoices (steer clear of vehicles without a strict service history)
  • Air suspension operation (raise/lower the vehicle, listen to the compressor, check for sagging overnight)
  • Metallic noises on cold start on petrol engines (timing chain)
  • Traces of coolant leak in the center of the engine (V) on the 3.0 V6 Supercharged
  • Clunking noises in the front axle over speed bumps (lower control arm bushings)
  • Electric parking brake (EPB) operation: must not squeak or bind

Verdict

The Land Rover Discovery IV is a paradoxical vehicle: it is one of the most versatile, comfortable, and capable SUVs in the world, but it is also **one of the least reliable of its generation**. The data is damning: a TÜV Report 3-year defect rate of 25% and a What Car? score of 2/5 (38% annual breakdown rate).

If you are targeting a **petrol engine**, the 5.0 V8 and the 3.0 V6 Supercharged offer exceptional drivability but demand absolute vigilance regarding the timing (chains) and cooling (plastic pipes on the V6 SC). Overheating on these aluminum blocks is fatal.

**Verdict:** To be bought only with full knowledge of the facts, out of passion, with a meticulous maintenance history (frequent oil changes, suspension and control arm invoices), and keeping an annual repair budget of several thousand euros. For a rational purchase, turn to a Toyota Land Cruiser or a Lexus GX.
