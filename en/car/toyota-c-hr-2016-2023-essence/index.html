Toyota C-HR

I (AX10) · 2016 – 2023

BUY Reliability 91/100 — excellent

All Toyota models

Summary

The first-generation Toyota C-HR (Coupé High-Rider) (AX10) marked a major stylistic break for the Japanese manufacturer. Based on the excellent modular TNGA-C platform (shared with the Corolla and Prius), it offers dynamic road handling, far removed from the placid image of the brand's older hybrid models. Although European demand has massively shifted towards hybrid powertrains (HEV), the C-HR was also sold with 100% gasoline engines (turbocharged in Europe/Asia, naturally aspirated in North America) that deserve attention. The late 2019 facelift (MY2020) brought minor aesthetic tweaks, a multimedia system finally compatible with Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, and the introduction of the powerful 2.0L 184 hp hybrid unit. Overall, the C-HR is a model of formidable reliability, true to Toyota's reputation.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall reliability, especially for hybrids
  • Bold design that ages very well
  • TNGA chassis offering an excellent comfort/dynamism compromise
  • Very high resale value on the used car market
  • Assembly quality and dashboard materials
  • Very low real-world fuel consumption (Hybrids)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic rear visibility (reversing camera essential)
  • Feeling of confinement in the rear seats (small windows, high handles)
  • Average boot volume (377L), reduced to 358L on the 2.0 Hybrid (12V battery in the boot)
  • Dated and slow infotainment system before the 2019 facelift
  • Highway sound insulation (wind and road noise)
  • Engine revving (motorboating) effect during strong accelerations (CVT/e-CVT)

Check before buying

  • 12V Battery: Undersized, it tends to drain if the car remains stationary for more than 2 weeks.
  • Windshield: Prone to impacts and cracks (costly replacement due to the Toyota Safety Sense camera).
  • CVT Gearbox (US 2.0L 2018-2019 Models): Listen for any whistling or groaning during acceleration.
  • Hybrid battery ventilation filter: Check that it is not obstructed by pet hair or dust.
  • Tires: Sometimes asymmetrical wear at the rear on early models (geometry adjustment).

Official recalls

Major Manufacturer Recalls

  • November 2020 (Fuel Pump): Replacement of the low-pressure fuel pump (Denso supplier) whose impeller could deform, leading to engine stalling. Concerns models produced between 2017 and 2019 (mainly 1.2T and 2.0L naturally aspirated).
  • November 2017 (Parking Brake): ECU software update. The electronic parking brake (EPB) might not engage or disengage correctly.
  • October 2018 (Rear Hubs): The bolts for the rear axle hub bearings might not have been tightened to the specified factory torque, risking damage to the brakes or causing the wheel to detach (very rare).
  • February 2021 (Pre-collision Radar): The radar sensor for the pre-collision system (PCS) might not have been initialized correctly, deactivating automatic emergency braking.

What sets it apart 7

Reliability

  • Legendary Toyota Reliability — The Toyota C-HR petrol benefits from Toyota's reputation for excellence in reliability, with robust and proven petrol engines, ensuring lasting peace of mind. The 1.2 Turbo engine is particularly renowned for its robustness.

Safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — Designed to protect its occupants, the C-HR achieved the maximum rating in Euro NCAP tests, demonstrating its robust structure and advanced safety systems.
  • Efficient and Stable Braking — The C-HR ensures remarkable braking performance, with short distances and great stability, enhancing the vehicle's active safety in all situations.

Performance

  • Dynamic and Precise Driving — Thanks to its TNGA platform, the C-HR offers agile road handling and excellent grip, with controlled body roll and precise steering, for daily driving pleasure.

Comfort

  • Bold and Timeless Design — Its distinctive and avant-garde coupé-SUV style gives it a unique presence on the road, a design that continues to appeal and age well.
  • Interior Quality and Comfort — The C-HR's interior stands out for its impeccable assembly quality and pleasant-to-touch materials on the dashboard, offering a welcoming and comfortable environment.

Economy

  • High Resale Value — The Toyota C-HR maintains a very high resale value on the used car market, making it an excellent long-term investment and minimizing depreciation.

Verdict

The first-generation Toyota C-HR is a highly recommendable purchase on the used car market. If you're looking for a purely gasoline version, the 1.2 Turbo (Europe/Asia) is an excellent choice, reliable and flexible, ideal for those who do a lot of highway driving or towing (things the 1.8 hybrid does less well). In North America, prioritize 2.0L models from 2020 onwards to avoid the weaknesses of the first K313 CVT gearbox. However, the very essence of the C-HR lies in its hybrid powertrains (1.8 and 2.0), which offer almost unrivaled reliability on the market, reduced maintenance costs (no clutch, no timing belt, minimal brake wear), and unbeatable fuel consumption in urban environments. The ideal choice remains a post-2019 2.0 Hybrid model for the best compromise of technology/power/reliability.
