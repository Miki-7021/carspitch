Nissan Frontier

III (D41) · 2022 – 2025

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The **third-generation Nissan Frontier (D41)** marks a major breakthrough for the North American market after 17 years of loyal service from the previous generation (D40). Designed specifically for North America, it rests on a heavily revised ladder frame chassis and adopts a muscular design inspired by the legendary Nissan Hardbody. Contrary to the current trend of *downsizing* and forced induction, the Frontier D41 bets on old-school reliability by offering a single engine option: a large naturally aspirated 3.8-liter V6 paired with a 9-speed automatic gearbox. Although it is slightly less radical in pure off-roading than a Toyota Tacoma TRD Pro, it stands out as one of the most reliable and comfortable mid-size pickups in its class, backed by excellent ratings from J.D. Power and Consumer Reports.

Strengths 5

  • Above-average mechanical reliability (proven naturally aspirated V6)
  • Smooth and responsive 9-speed automatic gearbox
  • Excellent ride comfort and soundproofing for a pickup
  • Simple and ergonomic infotainment system
  • Excellent reliability ratings (J.D. Power IQS, Consumer Reports)

Weaknesses 5

  • High fuel consumption (no hybridization or effective Stop&Start before 2025)
  • Heavy hydraulic steering at low speeds
  • Limited rear legroom (Crew Cab)
  • Off-road capabilities (suspension travel) slightly behind the Tacoma
  • Numerous minor software and assembly recalls in the early years

Check before buying

  • Effective engagement of the 'P' (Park) position on the gearbox (risk of rollaway).
  • Operation of the rearview camera (frequent black screen bugs).
  • Condition of the front lower suspension ball joints (recall on 2024 models).
  • Presence and integrity of the towing hitch (recall on 2022 Long Bed models).
  • Alignment and welds of the door strikers (2025-2026 models).

Alternatives

* **North America:** Toyota Tacoma (the benchmark, but transition to a more complex 4-cylinder turbo hybrid on the new generation), Chevrolet Colorado / GMC Canyon (more modern, excellent off-road, but temperamental electronic reliability), Ford Ranger, Honda Ridgeline (unibody chassis, superior comfort but less utilitarian). * **Europe / Rest of the world:** The Frontier D41 is not sold. Alternatives are the Ford Ranger, Toyota Hilux or Nissan Navara (D23).

Verdict

The Nissan Frontier D41 is a true 'modern dinosaur', and that is exactly what makes its strength. By keeping a large naturally aspirated V6 (VQ38DD) where the competition is moving to complex 4-cylinder turbos, Nissan offers a pickup with formidable mechanical reliability, praised by J.D. Power and Consumer Reports. While the early production years (2022) suffered several recalls (notably on the gearbox and software bugs), the mechanical foundation is indestructible. It is a highly recommended purchase for those who prioritize longevity, mechanical simplicity, and ride comfort, at the expense of modern fuel consumption.
