Summary

The second-generation DS4, launched in 2021, marks the return of DS Automobiles to the premium compact sedan segment, with a decidedly distinctive approach. Based on the Stellantis group's EMP2 V3 platform (shared with the Peugeot 308 III and Opel Astra L), it positions itself as a chic and technological alternative to German benchmarks. Its bold design, halfway between a sedan and a crossover, and its refined interior focused on comfort and innovation (multiple screens, DS Smart Touch) are its major assets. This generation places particular emphasis on electrification, notably with the E-Tense 225 plug-in hybrid powertrain, which represents a significant portion of the brand's sales and technological offering. The DS4 II targets customers seeking exclusivity, comfort, and a certain avant-garde style, even if it means sacrificing a bit of practicality. Overall reliability is currently being evaluated, as the model is relatively new, but the powertrains are proven within the Stellantis group.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and supporting invoices
  • Condition of the timing belt on the PureTech 1.2 (visual inspection)
  • Functioning of the AdBlue system on the BlueHDi (warning lights, error messages)
  • Battery health and actual range on the E-Tense (dealer diagnostic)
  • Verification that all manufacturer recalls have been carried out
  • Functioning of all electronic equipment and screens

Verdict

The second-generation DS4 is a vehicle that appeals with its style, comfort, and technology. However, its young age on the market and manufacturer recalls, particularly for the E-Tense version, warrant caution.

If you are attracted by its premium positioning and originality, opt for a model with an impeccable service history and for which all recalls have been carried out.

For the E-Tense 225 powertrain, it's an excellent choice if you can recharge frequently and your use is primarily urban or suburban. It is imperative to check the battery's condition and its recall history.

The BlueHDi 130 remains relevant for high-mileage drivers, provided the AdBlue system is monitored. The PureTech 130 is a more economical option, but vigilance regarding the timing belt is essential.

In summary, the DS4 II is a car to consider for its unique character, but a price negotiation is justified given initial feedback and fierce competition in this segment. A thorough test drive and meticulous inspection are essential.
