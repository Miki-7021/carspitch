Summary

The second-generation Mazda CX-5 (KF code) was launched in 2017, succeeding an already highly acclaimed model. It follows Mazda's Kodo design philosophy, offering a more refined and dynamic aesthetic than its predecessor. Positioned in the highly competitive compact SUV segment, it stands out for its driving pleasure, perceived build quality, and unique technological approach with Skyactiv engines.

The CX-5 KF received a significant facelift for the 2022 model year (late 2021), bringing aesthetic updates (bumpers, light signature), interior improvements (new infotainment screen, center console), and chassis optimizations for even better comfort and road holding.

Overall, the CX-5 KF is recognized for its above-average reliability, particularly for its naturally aspirated gasoline engines. The diesels, while having a more complex start on the previous generation, have been significantly improved and offer good robustness with rigorous maintenance.

Weaknesses 5

  • Trunk volume slightly smaller than some competitors
  • Infotainment system (pre-facelift) a bit dated
  • Fuel consumption of naturally aspirated gasoline engines can be high in dynamic driving
  • 2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel engines sensitive to short trips and neglected maintenance
  • No hybrid or plug-in hybrid (PHEV) option on this generation

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices, especially for diesels (oil changes, DPF)
  • Engine oil level on diesels (check for excessive dilution)
  • DPF condition on diesels (warning lights, regeneration history)
  • Operation of infotainment and driver assistance systems
  • Tire and brake wear (the CX-5 is a relatively heavy vehicle)
  • Absence of abnormal noises on startup or acceleration (timing chain on diesel, though rare on KF)

Verdict

The second-generation Mazda CX-5 is an excellent choice in the used car market, offering a rare blend of driving pleasure, perceived quality, and reliability.

**For versatile or urban/road use**, the **Skyactiv-G gasoline engines (2.0 and 2.5)** are to be preferred. They are exemplarily robust, suffer from no known major defects, and offer great peace of mind. Their fuel consumption is reasonable for naturally aspirated engines, but don't expect miracles in the city.

**For high-mileage drivers primarily undertaking long highway journeys**, the **2.2 Skyactiv-D diesel** can be an interesting option for its fuel efficiency and torque. However, it requires increased vigilance regarding maintenance: very regular oil changes with the specific oil and ensuring the vehicle is used on routes that allow for complete DPF regenerations. An impeccable service history is non-negotiable for this engine.

The 2022 facelift brought notable improvements in terms of infotainment and comfort, making post-2021 models slightly more desirable, but earlier versions remain very good buys. Overall, the CX-5 is a vehicle to **BUY** with confidence if maintenance has been followed, especially for gasoline versions.
