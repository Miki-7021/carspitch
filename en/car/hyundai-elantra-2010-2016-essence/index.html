Summary

The fifth-generation Hyundai Elantra (code name MD, or Avante in some Asian markets) marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer. Introducing the "Fluidic Sculpture" design language, it abandoned the bland look of its predecessors for a bold and aerodynamic design. Positioned in the highly competitive compact sedan segment, it stood out with an excellent price-to-equipment ratio, generous interior space, and a reassuring manufacturer warranty. While its road handling lags behind European benchmarks (electric power steering deemed artificial, damping leaves room for improvement), it established itself as a rational and economical choice, particularly in North America and Asia. In Western Europe, its distribution remained limited in the face of the supremacy of the i30 (hatchback version) and local compact sedans.

Strengths 5

  • "Fluidic Sculpture" design that has aged well
  • Very competitive price-to-equipment ratio
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume
  • Smooth and reliable 6-speed automatic gearbox
  • Long initial manufacturer warranty (5 years/unlimited mileage in Europe, 10 years/100k miles in the USA)

Weaknesses 5

  • Vague electric power steering (EPS) lacking feedback
  • Soundproofing leaves room for improvement (road and wind noise on the highway)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (prone to scratching)
  • Rear damping sometimes bouncy on rough roads
  • Risk of premature wear/failure on the 1.8L Nu engine (North America)

Check before buying

  • Cold engine noise (1.8L Nu): A persistent metallic ticking indicates a piston problem (scuffing). Walk away if the noise does not disappear when warm.
  • ABS/HECU module: Check if the recall for the ABS module fire risk has been performed (do not park indoors before correction).
  • Steering (Clunk): A clunking noise in the steering column when turning at a standstill indicates wear of the flexible coupling (rubber star piece). Inexpensive part but requires labor.
  • Brake lights: Check that the brake lights turn off properly when the pedal is released (wear of the pedal switch stopper).
  • Maintenance history: Hyundai engines of this generation tolerate extended oil change intervals very poorly.

Official recalls

The MD generation has been the subject of numerous recalls, particularly in North America (NHTSA) and a few in Europe (Rappel Conso):

  • [2024-11-04] Transmission software (France/Europe): The software does not activate the fail-safe mode during a fault, which can lead to sudden deceleration.
  • [2023-09-22] ABS/HECU module (NHTSA 23V651000): Risk of short circuit and fire in the ABS module (2011-2015 models). It is advised to park outside until repaired (fuse/module replacement).
  • [2026-05-15] Takata airbags (NHTSA 26V307000): Risk of driver airbag inflator rupture upon deployment (2015-2016 models).
  • [2024-02-23] Trunk latch (NHTSA 24V146000): Possible damage preventing opening from the inside (2015-2016 models).
  • [2016-08-02 & 2017-11-30] Brake light switch (NHTSA 16V574000 / 17V769000): Deterioration of the brake pedal stopper, leaving the brake lights permanently on.
  • [2015-12-23] ESC sensor (NHTSA 15V871000): Malfunction of the electronic stability control sensor that can unexpectedly brake a wheel (2011-2012 models).
  • [2015-10-07] Suspension springs (NHTSA 15V629000): Risk of front coil spring fracture (2011 models).
  • [2013-04-01] Side airbags (NHTSA 13V115000): Possible displacement of the mounting bracket during an impact (2011-2013 models).

Verdict

The Hyundai Elantra V (MD) is a rational compact sedan that offers an excellent price-to-quality ratio on the used market. Its design has aged well and its interior is spacious. However, its reliability record is mixed. While the 1.6 MPI versions (Europe/Asia) are robust, the 1.8L Nu engine (very common in North America) suffers from a known design flaw (cylinder scratching) that can lead to engine death. Furthermore, the significant number of recalls (notably the ABS module fire risk) requires a rigorous check of the history. It is a recommendable purchase provided you favor a model with a clear history, avoid 1.8L engines that tick when cold, and ensure that all safety recalls have been performed.
