Summary

The second-generation Citroën C5 (internal codes RD for the sedan, TD for the Tourer) was launched in 2008, marking a turning point for the brand in the executive car segment. Abandoning the bold style of its predecessor, it adopted a more classic and stately line, inspired by German sedans, while retaining Citroën's DNA with exceptional suspension comfort. It was available as a sedan and a wagon (Tourer).

Its main technical characteristic lay in the choice between a classic metallic suspension (firmer) and the famous Hydractive III+ hydropneumatic suspension (available on higher trims and more powerful engines), offering unparalleled ride comfort and serene road holding.

The C5 II underwent a minor facelift in 2010 (phase 2) with LED lights and new engines, followed by a second in 2012 (phase 3) with technological improvements and the arrival of BlueHDi engines. It was praised for its sound insulation, the quality of its interior materials, and its overall comfort. Its reliability is generally good, especially with the most common diesel engines, provided rigorous maintenance is performed.

Strengths 7

  • Exceptional suspension comfort (Hydractive III+)
  • Remarkable sound insulation
  • Quality of finish and interior materials
  • Generous interior space (especially in the Tourer)
  • Very safe road holding and serene road behavior
  • Very attractive price/performance ratio on the used market
  • Very reliable and powerful 2.0 HDi engines

Weaknesses 5

  • High weight penalizing fuel consumption and performance of smaller engines
  • Large turning radius
  • Limited rear visibility on the sedan
  • Potentially high maintenance cost for V6 diesel engines and Hydractive suspension in case of breakdown
  • Imperfect reliability of 1.6 HDi and 1.6 THP

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices: crucial for diesels and Hydractive suspension.
  • Hydractive III+ Suspension: check for leaks, proper functioning of modes (Sport/Comfort), vehicle raising/lowering, condition of spheres (no knocking).
  • Dual-mass flywheel: listen for noises at idle, when starting/stopping the engine, and during gear changes (especially on 1.6 HDi and 2.0 HDi).
  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter): check for warning lights, regeneration history, and the condition of the Eolys additive tank (if applicable).
  • Turbos: listen for abnormal whistling, check for absence of blue smoke from the exhaust (especially on 1.6 HDi and V6 HDi).
  • Injectors: check for leaks, excessive knocking, or abnormal smoke.
  • EGR valve: check for absence of engine warning light, power loss, or black smoke.
  • Automatic gearbox: check for smooth gear changes, absence of jerks or slipping.
  • Electronics: test all equipment (GPS, air conditioning, electric windows, electric parking brake).

Official recalls

Official recalls for the Citroën C5 II (2008-2017) primarily concerned safety and engine management elements:

  • 2008-2009 (Several campaigns): Problems related to the braking system (assistance, master cylinder) potentially leading to a loss of effectiveness. Concerns vehicles produced between 2008 and early 2009.
  • 2009 (May): Risk of fuel leak at the return hose, potentially leading to a fire risk. Concerns vehicles produced during a specific period in 2008.
  • 2010 (February): Loss of steering assistance due to a defect in the power steering pump. Concerns vehicles produced in 2009.
  • 2011 (June): Risk of fire due to engine wiring rubbing against a metal part. Concerns vehicles produced between 2009 and 2010.
  • 2012 (March): Steering column mounting problem potentially leading to excessive play. Concerns vehicles produced in 2011.
  • 2013 (November): Risk of fuel leak at the return hose, similar to the 2009 recall, but concerning another production series.
  • 2014 (April): Malfunction of the electric parking brake, potentially leading to unintended release or blockage. Concerns vehicles produced between 2012 and 2013.
  • 2015 (September): Engine hood fastening problem, risking opening while driving. Concerns vehicles produced in 2014.
  • 2016 (March): Software update for BlueHDi engines to optimize the depollution system (AdBlue) and avoid error messages or power limitations. Concerns vehicles equipped with these engines.

It is imperative to verify that all recalls concerning the targeted vehicle have been carried out via the VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) at a Citroën dealership.

Verdict

The Citroën C5 II is an excellent executive car, offering first-class comfort and sound insulation, especially with the Hydractive III+ suspension. It's a car that ages well and offers an unbeatable quality/price ratio on the used market.

For a worry-free purchase, prioritize the 2.0 HDi engines (140, 150, or 163 hp), which are the most reliable, versatile, and economical to use. They represent the best compromise for this car. The 2.2 HDi 204 is also an excellent option for those seeking more power.

Be more cautious with the 1.6 HDi: it can be interesting for predominantly urban/suburban use if maintenance has been impeccable and you are prepared to maintain it rigorously. The 1.6 THP petrol versions should be considered with caution and a clear service history.

The V6 HDi (2.7 and 3.0) engines offer exceptional driving pleasure but can incur very high maintenance costs in case of problems. They are reserved for enthusiasts with a substantial budget for preventive and curative maintenance.

It is imperative to check the service history, especially for vehicles equipped with Hydractive suspension and diesel engines. A thorough road test is essential to ensure the proper functioning of all systems. If these conditions are met, the C5 II is a wise choice for anyone looking for a comfortable and stately car at an affordable price.
