BMW X1

E84 · 2009 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The **BMW X1 (E84)** is the Bavarian brand's first premium compact SUV. Based on the 3 Series Touring (E91) platform, it offers exceptional road handling, closer to a raised wagon than a true SUV. It received a facelift (LCI) in July 2012, bringing interior finish improvements and generalizing the 4-cylinder turbo engines (N20) to replace the naturally aspirated units. Although dynamic and offering excellent inline 6-cylinders in its early years, this model is marred by major reliability issues with its timing chains (N47 diesel and N20 petrol engines).

Strengths 5

  • Dynamic road handling (3 Series E91 chassis)
  • Excellent driving position, low for an SUV
  • Availability of the fabulous inline 6-cylinder engines (N52/N55) in its early years
  • Very communicative hydraulic steering (on sDrive versions and early xDrive)
  • Ergonomics of controls and intuitive iDrive (CIC) system

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of timing chains (N47 diesel and N20 petrol pre-2015)
  • Extreme sensitivity of the xDrive transfer case to tire wear differences
  • Quality of some interior plastics (lower part) unworthy of premium before the facelift
  • Limited rear passenger space and boot volume
  • Very firm suspension, especially with the Sport chassis option or Runflat tires

Check before buying

  • Cicada-like noise or metallic scraping sound when cold from the passenger compartment (sign of a faulty timing chain on N47/N20)
  • Jerks or hesitations during strong accelerations or when turning at low speed (worn xDrive transfer case)
  • Oil traces around the valve cover and oil filter housing (common issue on BMW petrol engines)
  • Operation of all lights and electric windows (if faulty: FRM module burned out)
  • Automatic gearbox fluid change history (ZF 6 or 8-speed): to be done every 80,000 to 100,000 km maximum

Official recalls

  • - **Takata Airbags (2016-2019)**: Replacement of front airbag gas generators (driver and passenger) at risk of exploding.
  • - **B+ Battery Cable (2013)**: Risk of corrosion at the fuse box connection, potentially leading to a complete loss of electrical power while driving.
  • - **VANOS Bolts (2014)**: On 6-cylinder engines (N52/N55), risk of variable timing system bolt breakage, leading to power loss or engine damage.
  • - **Brake Assist (2013)**: On N20 engines (20i/28i), lack of vacuum pump lubrication can reduce braking assistance.

Verdict

The BMW X1 E84 is a double-edged sword. If you're looking for driving pleasure, it's the best in its class thanks to its rear-wheel drive/xDrive architecture derived from the 3 Series. **However, buying a 4-cylinder petrol (20i, 28i) or diesel (N47) version without a recent invoice for timing chain replacement is an extremely risky gamble.**

**The expert's choice:** Actively look for an **xDrive25i or xDrive28i (pre-2011)** model equipped with the naturally aspirated 6-cylinder (N52). This is by far the most reliable and refined engine in the range. If you opt for a 20i (N20), demand a 2015 model or budget €1500 to €2000 to make the timing chain reliable upon purchase. Avoid xDrive models where the 4 tires are not of the same brand and wear.
