Audi Q5

I (8R) · 2008 – 2017

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Audi models

Summary

The first-generation Audi Q5 (Type 8R) is based on the MLB platform (shared with the A4 B8). Launched in 2008, it quickly established itself as one of the best-selling premium SUVs worldwide thanks to its exemplary finish, comfort, and high-performance Quattro system. A significant restyling (Facelift) took place at the end of 2012 (model year 2013), bringing aesthetic touches, an infotainment (MMI) update, and, most importantly, major mechanical evolutions.

Gasoline Focus (TFSI / FSI): The reliability of gasoline engines is split into two eras. Models before 2013 (EA888 Gen 2 engines) are notoriously known for their excessive oil consumption and timing chain failures. Post-restyling versions (Gen 3) corrected most of these defects, making the gasoline Q5 much more recommendable on the used market.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional interior finish quality and material aging
  • Very high level of cabin sound insulation
  • Efficiency and safety of the Quattro all-wheel drive system (Torsen)
  • Enjoyment of V6 engines (3.0 TFSI and 3.0 TDI) and ZF8 gearbox (depending on markets)
  • Timeless design that ages very well

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 TFSI engines from 2008 to 2012 (oil, chain)
  • Fragility of the S tronic 7 gearbox (mechatronics, clutches) if poorly maintained
  • High weight (often > 1.8 tons) penalizing gasoline consumption
  • Very high maintenance and spare parts cost within the Audi network
  • Average rear passenger space and trunk volume for its size

Check before buying

  • Oil consumption history (2.0 TFSI 2008-2012): Demand an invoice proving the replacement of piston rings/pistons by Audi.
  • Timing (All chain-driven engines): Listen carefully for metallic rattling noises when cold (worn tensioners). Verifiable via VCDS diagnostic tool (phase deviation).
  • S tronic gearbox: Check the smoothness of gear changes (especially 1st to 2nd and reverse). No jerks should be felt.
  • Water ingress: Check for dampness under floor mats and in the trunk (clogged panoramic roof drains).
  • Running gear: Premature wear of front suspension arm bushings (dull thuds over bumps).

Official recalls

  • Airbags (2016-2021): Massive recall related to defective Takata gas generators (risk of metallic fragment projection).
  • Auxiliary heater (2017): Risk of overheating and fire of the electrical connections of the auxiliary heater (PTC) on certain models.
  • Coolant pump (2017): On 2.0 TFSI engines, risk of auxiliary water pump blockage by debris, potentially leading to local overheating or a short circuit.
  • Emissions (2016): Software update (and sometimes installation of a flow stabilizer) on 2.0 TDI EA189 engines following Dieselgate.
  • Fuel pump flange (2017): Risk of cracking and fuel leakage at the pump flange (mainly gasoline models).

What sets it apart 6

safety

  • Recognized Top-Tier Safety — The Audi Q5 achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores for adult (89%) and child (82%) protection, confirming its status as a safe family vehicle.

braking

  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — Experience exceptional braking performance, with short stopping distances, typically between 36 and 39 meters from 100 km/h, offering maximum peace of mind in all circumstances.

handling

  • Control and Dynamism with Quattro — The permanent Quattro all-wheel-drive system ensures exemplary road holding, stable and secure, with precise steering and a perfect balance between comfort and agility, even in the most demanding conditions.

comfort

  • A Cocoon of Silence and Refinement — The Audi Q5's cabin offers a very high level of soundproofing, transforming every journey into a serene and relaxing experience, complemented by exceptional interior finish quality.

quality

  • Timeless Finish Quality — The interior of the Audi Q5 stands out with high-quality materials and impeccable assembly, ensuring remarkable aging and a design that retains all its appeal over the years.

Performance

  • High-Performance and Reliable Petrol Engines (post-2013) — Post-facelift petrol versions (from 2013 onwards), especially the 2.0 TFSI Gen 3 and the 3.0 TFSI V6, offer superior driving pleasure, top-tier performance, and increased robustness for dynamic and serene journeys.

Alternatives

  • Europe: BMW X3 (F25 - prioritize gasoline engines post-2013), Mercedes-Benz GLK or GLC, Volvo XC60 I (very reliable with 5-cylinder engines), Porsche Macan (shares the same platform but with sportier settings).
  • North America: Lexus RX 350 (absolute benchmark for gasoline reliability), Acura RDX, BMW X3 (xDrive28i/35i).
  • Asia / Japan: Lexus NX, Infiniti QX50.

Verdict

The first-generation Audi Q5 is a vehicle with two faces. If you are looking for a gasoline engine, the golden rule is to ABSOLUTELY AVOID 2.0 TFSI models from 2008 to 2012, unless the engine has been completely rebuilt (with an invoice of €5000+ to prove it).

However, restyled versions (from 2013 onwards) equipped with the 2.0 TFSI Gen 3 or 3.0 TFSI are excellent choices. They offer undeniable premium driving pleasure. Make sure the S tronic gearbox has been serviced every 60,000 km. For maximum peace of mind with gasoline, the 3.0 TFSI (supercharged V6) coupled with the Tiptronic 8 gearbox (import or specific markets) is the most robust combination.
