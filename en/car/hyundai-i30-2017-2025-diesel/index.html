Summary

The third-generation Hyundai i30, known by the internal code PD, was launched in 2017 and quickly consolidated Hyundai's position in the C-segment in Europe. Designed and developed in Europe, it positions itself as a serious alternative to segment benchmarks such as the Volkswagen Golf, Ford Focus, or Peugeot 308. It is available in several body styles: 5-door hatchback, wagon, and the more stylish Fastback.

A first major facelift occurred in 2020, bringing aesthetic changes (front fascia, lights), technological improvements (new infotainment screen, driving aids), and the introduction of 48V mild-hybrid (MHEV) powertrains. A second facelift was presented in 2024, further refining the design and equipment.

The i30 PD is generally perceived as a reliable, well-equipped car offering good value for money, with a generous manufacturer's warranty (5 years unlimited mileage in Europe). Its versatility and comfort make it a relevant choice for a wide range of users.

Strengths 7

  • 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer's warranty (Europe).
  • Good value for money and generous equipment.
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially after facelift).
  • Solid build quality and improving finish.
  • Generally reliable gasoline and diesel engines.
  • N version for sports enthusiasts.
  • Versatility of body styles (hatchback, wagon, Fastback).

Weaknesses 5

  • Somewhat austere interior design on early versions.
  • Hard plastics on the lower parts of the cabin.
  • Fuel consumption of turbo gasoline versions in dynamic driving.
  • Infotainment system of early versions a bit dated (before 2020).
  • The 1.6 CRDi is not suitable for urban low-mileage drivers (risk of DPF/EGR clogging).

Check before buying

  • **Complete service history and invoices:** Essential for warranty and longevity.
  • **DPF and EGR valve condition (Diesel):** Check for absence of engine warning lights, regeneration history, and vehicle usage (avoid models with extensive city driving).
  • **Abnormal engine/gearbox noises:** Especially on 1.6 CRDi (dual-mass flywheel) and i30 N (clutch).
  • **MHEV system operation (if equipped):** Check for absence of warning lights related to the 48V battery or starter-generator.
  • **Tire and brake wear:** Indicator of general maintenance and driving style.
  • **Infotainment and driving aids operation:** Test all functionalities.
  • **Bodywork and underbody:** Check for absence of impacts or corrosion.

Verdict

The third-generation Hyundai i30 is an excellent choice on the used car market. It offers solid reliability, generous equipment, and appreciable comfort, all often at a more competitive price than its European rivals. The 5-year warranty (if still active) is a major asset.

**For gasoline engines:** The 1.4 MPI / 1.5 DPi are safe bets for their robustness and simplicity, ideal for urban and suburban use. The 1.0 T-GDI and 1.4/1.5 T-GDI offer a good performance/consumption compromise for more versatile use. N versions are demanding sports cars, to be reserved for enthusiasts with a substantial maintenance budget.

**For the diesel engine (1.6 CRDi):** This is an excellent engine for **high-mileage drivers** and those who regularly make long journeys. Its fuel efficiency and torque are its main assets. However, it is **strongly discouraged for predominantly urban use or for low-mileage drivers**, due to the risks of DPF and EGR valve clogging, which can lead to significant repair costs. If you opt for a diesel, ensure the vehicle's maintenance history and previous use.

**Buying advice:** Prioritize a model with a clear maintenance history, ideally serviced at a Hyundai dealership to fully benefit from the warranty. Facelifted versions (from 2020) bring notable technological and aesthetic improvements. The i30 is a smart and reliable purchase, provided you choose the engine suitable for your use.
