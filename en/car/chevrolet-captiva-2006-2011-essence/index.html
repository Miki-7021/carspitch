Chevrolet Captiva

I (C100) · 2006 – 2011

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Chevrolet Captiva (C100) marked General Motors' offensive in the family SUV segment in Europe and worldwide. Developed by GM Daewoo in South Korea, it shares its platform (Theta) with the Opel Antara. Offered in 5 or 7-seater versions, it stood out for its very aggressive price/spaciousness ratio and its American adventurer look. While its spacious cabin and generous equipment are appealing, it suffers from a basic interior finish (hard plastics), sluggish road handling, and a high weight (nearly 1.8 tons) which heavily penalizes fuel consumption, particularly for the gasoline engines covered in this report. A major facelift (C140) would occur in 2011.

Strengths 5

  • Excellent spaciousness and modularity (very practical 7-seater version)
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Suspension comfort on long journeys
  • Overall reliability of the 2.4 gasoline engine (excluding wear parts)
  • Robust SUV look that ages quite well

Weaknesses 5

  • Excessive fuel consumption on gasoline engines (2.4 and V6)
  • Very high weight (1.8t) penalizing performance and wear of brakes/tires
  • Low-end interior plastic quality and assembly (rattles)
  • Sluggish road handling, significant body roll
  • Disastrous reliability of the DPF/EGR system on diesel versions (VCDi)

Check before buying

  • Metallic noise on startup for the 3.2L V6 (symptom of a loose timing chain).
  • Condition of the steering rack (frequent power steering fluid leaks).
  • Running gear: premature wear of front wishbone bushings, stabilizer bar links, and rear shock absorbers (especially on 7-seater versions with self-leveling suspension).
  • Dashboard warning lights: ABS/ESP sensors are temperamental and often light up without serious mechanical reason (loose connections).
  • Operation of the driveshaft and rear axle on AWD versions (growling noises if the oil is too old).

Verdict

The Chevrolet Captiva C100 is a rational choice only if you need 7 seats at a ridiculously low price. For gasoline engines, the choice is a difficult compromise: the 2.4L is reliable but anemic and very thirsty, while the 3.2L V6 offers good driving pleasure but suffers from astronomical fuel consumption and a sword of Damocles regarding its timing chain. If you opt for a gasoline version, prioritize a 2.4L with a clear service history and plan for a significant fuel budget (or a conversion to E85 bioethanol if local legislation allows). Avoid diesels if you drive in the city.
