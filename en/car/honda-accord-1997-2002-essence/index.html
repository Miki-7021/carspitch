Honda Accord

VI (CG/CH) · 1997 – 2002

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The 6th generation Honda Accord (CG/CH chassis in Europe, CF/CL in Japan) is a textbook case in automotive history: Honda developed three distinct versions (Europe, North America, Japan) to adapt to the specificities of each market. In Europe, it was produced in Swindon (United Kingdom) and distinguished itself by its incisive road handling (double wishbone suspension) and the legendary reliability of its VTEC gasoline engines (F and H series). The Type R (CH1) version has become an icon for purists. While the gasoline engines with manual gearboxes are absolute benchmarks in terms of longevity, this generation is marred by the chronic fragility of automatic gearboxes (especially in North America) and by the use of an unconvincing Rover diesel engine for the European market.

Strengths 6

  • Stratospheric reliability of gasoline engines (F and H Series)
  • Excellent road handling (double wishbone suspension front/rear)
  • Ergonomics of controls and driving position
  • Type R (CH1) version with exceptional mechanical temperament
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume
  • Aging of interior materials (plastics and velour)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic automatic transmissions (especially on North American V6 versions)
  • Diesel engine (Rover) unworthy of Honda's reliability standards
  • Imperfect sound insulation (road and wind noise on the highway)
  • Susceptibility to corrosion (rear wheel arches, engine subframe)
  • Large turning radius
  • Varnished paint sometimes fragile (Milano Red that fades)

Check before buying

  • Corrosion: meticulously inspect the rear fenders (under the rubber seal) and the front subframe.
  • PGM-FI relay: if the car refuses to start when hot (no fuel pump noise), the relay solders are cracked.
  • Automatic transmission: avoid any vehicle showing jerks, slipping, or black/burnt-smelling oil.
  • EGR valve: an unstable idle or a check engine light (code P0401) indicates clogged passages.
  • Power steering: check for leaks on the rack and pump (common issue).
  • Type R: check that the VTEC engages cleanly without excessive blue smoke from the exhaust.

Handling

  • Dynamic and Precise Road Handling — Thanks to its sophisticated double-wishbone suspension at the front and rear, the Accord offers sharp road holding and remarkable driving pleasure.

Performance

  • Exhilarating Performance (Type R Version) — The Type R (CH1) version stands out with its sporty 2.2L DOHC VTEC engine, delivering lively acceleration and an exciting driving experience.

Practicality

  • Generous Cabin Space and Daily Comfort — The Accord offers a spacious interior for passengers and a generous boot volume, ideal for family trips or long journeys.

Comfort

  • Intuitive Ergonomics and Durable Materials — The control layout is designed for ease of use, and the interior materials (plastics, velour) show excellent resistance to aging.

Economy

  • Controlled Maintenance and Durability — The inherent reliability of Honda's petrol engines ensures reasonable maintenance costs and long-term peace of mind.

Alternatives

- **Europe** : Toyota Avensis (equivalent reliability, less dynamic), Nissan Primera (excellent chassis), VW Passat B5 (better finish, powerful TDI diesels but fragile running gear), Peugeot 406 (benchmark for comfort/road holding). - **North America** : Toyota Camry (the absolute benchmark for automatic transmission reliability), Nissan Altima. - **Japan** : Toyota Corona/Premio, Subaru Legacy (all-wheel drive).

Verdict

The 6th generation Honda Accord is a car with two faces. If you opt for a gasoline engine (1.8i, 2.0i, 2.3i or the incredible 2.2 Type R) coupled with a manual gearbox, you are buying one of the most reliable and indestructible sedans of its era, capable of exceeding 400,000 km with basic maintenance. On the other hand, automatic transmission versions (particularly the American V6) are financial headaches, and the Rover diesel is to be absolutely avoided. Prioritize a healthy European model (CG8/CG9) in terms of bodywork, and you will have a perfect youngtimer 'daily'.
