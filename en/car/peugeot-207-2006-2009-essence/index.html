Peugeot 207

I (WA/WC) · 2006 – 2009

NÉGOCIER Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The Peugeot 207 (Generation I, internal code WA for 3-door, WC for 5-door) was introduced in 2006 as the successor to the wildly successful 206. It marked a significant shift for Peugeot: it was much heavier, larger, and focused heavily on passive safety and interior quality.

However, this era (2006-2009) is infamous for the introduction of the 'Prince' petrol engines (VTi and THP), co-developed with BMW. While these engines offered excellent performance and efficiency on paper, they became a reliability nightmare, severely tarnishing the 207's reputation. The older, carry-over engines (TU/ET series) remain the safest petrol choices.

*Note: The '207' sold in Mercosur and China (often called 207 Compact) was actually a heavily facelifted 206. This sheet focuses on the true European A7 platform 207.*

Strengths 5

  • Excellent chassis dynamics and road holding (typical Peugeot DNA)
  • High passive safety standards (5-star Euro NCAP)
  • Interior material quality was a massive step up from the 206
  • TU and ET series petrol engines are virtually bulletproof
  • Very comfortable front seats and good driving position

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of the Prince engines (VTi and THP)
  • Heavy weight blunts performance and increases fuel consumption on base engines
  • Electric Power Steering (EPS) failures are common and expensive
  • Cramped rear legroom compared to rivals (Clio III)
  • Notchy and vague 5-speed manual gearboxes (MA5 type)

Check before buying

  • Cold start sound on VTi/THP engines (any rattling means timing chain replacement is imminent)
  • Service history: Oil changes must be frequent (max 10k miles/15k km) for both Prince petrols and HDi diesels
  • Smell of exhaust fumes in the cabin on HDi models (indicates leaking injector seals)
  • Heavy or inconsistent steering feel (indicates failing EPS rack/motor)
  • Check the boot floor and rear lights for water ingress
  • Operation of the anti-pollution system (Depollution System Fault warning on dash is very common)

Alternatives

- **Europe**: Renault Clio III (much more reliable petrol engines, especially the 1.2 TCe and 1.4/1.6 16v), Ford Fiesta Mk6/Mk7 (better driving dynamics), VW Polo Mk4/Mk5 (better perceived quality, but avoid early TSI engines). - **Japan/Asia**: Honda Fit/Jazz (vastly superior reliability and space), Toyota Yaris. - **Emerging Markets**: Fiat Punto, Chevrolet Onix/Agile (where the 207 Compact was sold).

Verdict

The Peugeot 207 Phase 1 is a car of extremes. If you buy a model with the older TU/ET petrol engines (1.4 8v, 1.4 16v, 1.6 16v), you are getting a safe, comfortable, and highly reliable supermini that represents excellent value for money today.

However, if you are looking at the 1.4 VTi, 1.6 VTi, or 1.6 THP, the verdict changes to a strict **AVOID** unless the car comes with a massive folder of receipts proving the timing chain, HPFP, and valve stem seals have already been replaced. The financial risk associated with the Prince engines in this era far outweighs the car's value. For diesels, the 1.4 HDi is a solid workhorse, while the 1.6 HDi requires meticulous maintenance history to be a safe buy.
