MINI Hatch

· 2014 – présent (F56, LCI à partir de 2018)

ACHETER Reliability 85/100 — excellent

Summary

The third generation of the modern MINI Hatch (F56 for the 3-door, F55 for the 5-door, and F57 for the Convertible) marks a massive leap forward in terms of reliability and build quality compared to its predecessor (R56). Built on BMW's UKL1 platform, it introduced the modular B-series engines (B38 3-cylinder and B48 4-cylinder), finally leaving behind the problematic Prince engines. The F56 is larger, more refined, and features a significantly upgraded interior while retaining the brand's signature 'go-kart' handling. It received two major updates (LCI in 2018 and LCI 2 in 2021) which modernized its technology and aesthetics.

Strengths 5

  • Massive reliability improvement over the R56 generation (thanks to BMW B-series engines)
  • Excellent driving dynamics ('go-kart' feel) and precise steering
  • High-quality, premium interior materials
  • Strong residual values
  • Excellent automatic transmissions (both the early 6-speed Aisin and the later 7-speed DCT)

Weaknesses 4

  • Firm ride quality, especially on larger wheels (17/18 inch) and run-flat tires
  • Limited rear legroom and small boot space (211 liters for the 3-door)
  • Expensive optional extras and high purchase price
  • Poor rear visibility

Check before buying

  • Upper right engine mount: Visually inspect for torn rubber or leaked black fluid (very common on B38/B48 engines around 40,000 - 60,000 miles).
  • Clutch feel on 2014-2015 manual 1.5T models: Heavy or noisy clutch could indicate the dreaded crankshaft thrust bearing issue.
  • EGR Cooler on Diesels: Verify via a BMW/MINI dealer if the EGR recall has been performed.
  • Front suspension: Listen for clunks over speed bumps indicating worn control arm bushings or anti-roll bar links.
  • Service History: Ensure oil changes were done on time (ideally every 10,000 miles / 15,000 km rather than the long-life 18,000 miles / 30,000 km interval) to protect the turbocharger.

Verdict

The F56 generation is the car that finally made the modern MINI a reliable purchase. By adopting BMW's modular B-series engines, the catastrophic timing chain and oil consumption issues of the past are gone. While early 2014-2015 manual 1.5T models should be approached with caution due to crankshaft issues, anything from 2016 onwards is a highly recommended buy. The Cooper S (B48) offers the best balance of performance and reliability. Always check the upper engine mount, as it is the only consistent weak point across the petrol range.
