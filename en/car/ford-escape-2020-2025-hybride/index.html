Summary

The fourth-generation Ford Escape (known as the Ford Kuga in Europe) marks a turning point for the model with a more urban design, akin to a raised sedan, and a strong push towards electrification. Based on the C2 platform, it focuses on its hybrid (HEV) and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) powertrains built around a 2.5L Atkinson cycle engine. A facelift occurred in 2023, bringing a modernized front end and the SYNC 4 infotainment system.

However, despite excellent driving dynamics, well-thought-out interior space (sliding rear bench seat), and remarkable fuel efficiency in hybrid form, the reliability record of this generation is catastrophic, particularly for the 2020-2022 model years. With over 40 NHTSA recalls in the United States and multiple alerts in Europe (Rappel Conso), including fire risks, power losses, and software failures, it is a model that requires extreme vigilance on the used market. Consumer Reports and J.D. Power also rank it well below the average for its segment.

Strengths 4

  • Excellent rear interior space thanks to the sliding bench seat

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous overall reliability on 2020-2022 model years (over 40 NHTSA recalls)
  • Documented fire risks (1.5L injectors, PHEV high-voltage battery)
  • Disappointing interior plastic quality on lower trims
  • Multiple electronic bugs (rearview camera, dashboard, driving aids)
  • Fragile 1.5L EcoBoost engine (wet timing belt, injectors)

Check before buying

  • Enter the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) on the Ford website to verify that ALL recalls have been performed.
  • On PHEV/HEV: state of health of the high-voltage battery and absence of error messages related to the hybrid system.
  • On 1.5L EcoBoost: scrupulous maintenance history, check if the injectors have been replaced following the recall.
  • Operation of the central screen (SYNC 3) and rearview camera (frequent image inversions).
  • Absence of jerking from the 8-speed automatic gearbox (on 1.5L and 2.0L EcoBoost versions).

Official recalls

The Gen 4 Ford Escape / Kuga accumulates an exceptionally high number of recalls (41 NHTSA, 16 Rappel Conso). Here are the most critical:

  • Fire risk - Battery (2021 / 2024): On PHEV/HEV (2020-2024), internal short circuit of high-voltage battery cells or venting of hot gases. Replacement of cells or the pack is necessary.
  • Fire risk - Injectors (2022-2025): On 1.5L EcoBoost (2020-2023), cracking of fuel injectors leaking onto hot engine surfaces. Multiple recalls for replacement and addition of detection software.
  • Power loss - HPCM (2024-2025): On HEV/PHEV (2020-2024), the hybrid control module software can force the vehicle to shift into neutral while driving.
  • Engine - Oil leak (2025): On 2.5L HEV/PHEV (2023-2025), manufacturing defect of the cylinder head (plugs) leading to an oil leak.
  • Electronics - Camera and Screens (2024-2026): Inversion of the rearview camera image or dashboard screen (IPC) failing to illuminate on startup.
  • Braking (2021 / 2025): Assembly defect of the brake booster (2020) and poorly manufactured rear brake pads (2020).
  • Steering (2020): Electronic Steering Column Lock (ESCL) that can activate unexpectedly while driving.

Verdict

The 4th generation Ford Escape (Kuga) is a vehicle full of intrinsic qualities (comfort, space, hybrid efficiency), but its reliability record is one of the worst in its category, particularly for the years 2020 to 2022. The hybrid powertrains (2.5L), although brilliant in use, have been plagued by serious software problems (loss of traction) and battery defects (fire risks). The 1.5L EcoBoost engine is to be avoided due to its defective injectors and fragile timing belt.

Buying advice: Absolutely avoid the 2020-2022 models. If you are drawn to the efficiency of the PHEV, target a facelifted model (2023 or newer) featuring the SYNC 4 system, and demand a comprehensive extended warranty. Do not finalize any purchase without verifying that all of the (numerous) recalls have been performed by an official dealership.
