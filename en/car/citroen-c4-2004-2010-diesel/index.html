Citroën C4

I (LC) · 2004 – 2010

All Citroën models

Summary

The first-generation Citroën C4 (internal code LC for the 5-door sedan, LA for the 3-door Coupé) succeeded the Xsara in 2004. It marked a major stylistic and technological break for the chevron brand. Featuring a fixed-hub steering wheel (grouping controls) and digital central instrumentation, it stood out for its originality. Based on PSA's PF2 platform (shared with the Peugeot 307), it offers an excellent comfort/road holding compromise. A restyling (facelift) occurred in late 2008, bringing slight aesthetic touches and, most importantly, the introduction of the 'Prince' petrol engines (VTi and THP) co-developed with BMW. While the C4 I was a commercial success, its reliability is very heterogeneous, particularly for the diesel (HDi) engines, which accounted for the vast majority of sales in Europe.

Strengths 6

  • Excellent suspension comfort (typical Citroën)
  • Safe road holding and dynamic chassis
  • Original exterior design, especially in the 3-door Coupé version
  • Innovative ergonomics (fixed-hub steering wheel, digital head-up display)
  • Reliability of petrol engines before 2008 (1.4i, 1.6i, 2.0i) and the 1.6 HDi 90
  • Rich equipment for its time (directional xenon headlights, AFIL on high trim levels)

Weaknesses 6

  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.6 HDi 110 (turbo, injector seals, flywheel)
  • Reliability of 'Prince' petrol engines post-2008 (VTi and THP)
  • Rear tailgate wiring harness that breaks over time
  • Peeling interior plastics (steering wheel, door handles, center console)
  • Frequent electronic bugs (inoperative cruise control, parking sensors)
  • Noisy or leaking rear shock absorbers before 80,000 km on early models

Check before buying

  • On 1.6 HDi 110: Presence of black tar around the injectors (seal leaks) and turbo whistling.
  • On all diesels: Idling tremors or clunking when changing gears (worn dual-mass flywheel).
  • On all diesels: FAP maintenance history (Eolys refill) and absence of 'Anti-pollution system faulty' warning light.
  • Electronics: Test the cruise control/speed limiter (often faulty due to the brake pedal switch or steering wheel buttons).
  • Tailgate: Check that the rear wiper, defrost, and trunk opening work (harness cut in the sheath).
  • Air conditioning: Check that cold air comes out of both sides (the mixing flaps of the dual-zone heating tend to break).

Official recalls

  • 2006 (Braking): Recall of 2.0 HDi models for replacement of the brake assist vacuum pump valve.
  • 2007 (ABS/ESP): Reprogramming of the ABS/ESP hydraulic unit due to risk of malfunction.
  • 2008 (Engine): On 1.6 HDi, inspection and possible replacement of the air flow meter (risk of stalling).
  • 2010 (Electrical): Replacement of the electrical harness for the rear tailgate hinge (risk of short circuit or loss of functions).
  • 2011 (Lighting): Replacement of the steering column control module (COM2000) due to intermittent headlight extinctions.

Alternatives

Europe

  • Peugeot 307 / 308 I: Technical cousins, sharing the same engines (and the same problems on the 1.6 HDi 110), but with a more classic presentation.
  • Renault Mégane II (Phase 2) / Mégane III: The 1.5 dCi (after 2006) is more reliable than the 1.6 HDi 110. Excellent comfort.
  • Volkswagen Golf V / VI: Superior finish, 1.9 TDI engines (noisy but robust) and 2.0 TDI (beware of cylinder heads on early 2.0 TDI 140 BKD models).
  • Ford Focus II: Shares the 1.6 TDCi engine (identical to the 1.6 HDi) and the 2.0 TDCi. Excellent chassis.

South America / Asia

  • Chevrolet Vectra / Cruze: Very popular local alternatives.
  • Honda Civic (8th gen): Significantly superior mechanical reliability, indestructible i-VTEC petrol engines.

Verdict

The first-generation Citroën C4 is a car full of road qualities, but its reliability record is heavily tarnished by certain engines. If you're looking for a diesel, steer clear of the 1.6 HDi 110 (DV6TED4) unless the turbo, injector seals, and flywheel have been recently replaced with supporting invoices. Without hesitation, opt for the modest but indestructible 1.6 HDi 90, or the powerful and robust 2.0 HDi 136/140 for long journeys. For petrol engines, the older blocks (1.4i, 1.6i, 2.0i) from before 2008 are excellent choices, very reliable and inexpensive to maintain, unlike the post-2008 VTi and THP engines which are true problem magnets (timing chain). Buying a C4 I therefore requires a surgical selection of the engine and a meticulous check of the onboard electronics.
