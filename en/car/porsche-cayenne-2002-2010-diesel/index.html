Porsche Cayenne

I (955/957) · 2002 – 2010

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

All Porsche models

Summary

The first-generation Porsche Cayenne (Type 955 from 2002 to 2006, then facelift Type 957 from 2007 to 2010) is the model that saved Porsche from bankruptcy. Developed jointly with the Volkswagen Touareg and the Audi Q7 (PL71 platform), it shocked purists but enjoyed immense commercial success. The Diesel engine, the main focus of this report, was introduced very late, in 2009, on the phase 2 (957). It is the 3.0 V6 TDI block of Volkswagen/Audi origin. While the Cayenne I offers exceptional on-road and off-road performance, its overall reliability record is poor, weighed down by major design flaws on the petrol V8s (cooling tubes) and a fragile air suspension. Paradoxically, the late-career Diesel model is one of the most rational and reliable choices of this generation.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling for an SUV of this weight
  • Real off-road capabilities (transfer case with low range, differential locks)
  • Interior finish quality and material aging
  • Inherent reliability of the 3.0 V6 Diesel block (compared to the petrol V8s)
  • Towing capacity (up to 3.5 tons)

Weaknesses 7

  • Catastrophic TÜV Report record: 18.5% defect rate at 3 years (among the worst in the premium SUV segment)
  • High ADAC breakdown rate (4.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year for Gen I)
  • Highly problematic air suspension: frequent leaks from the air springs and tired compressors beyond 80,000 km
  • Plastic cooling tubes on the 4.5L V8s (possible engine failure)
  • Cylinder scratches (Bore scoring) on the 4.8L V8s
  • Temperamental electronics (wiring harnesses, sensors, ECUs)
  • "Porsche" maintenance costs applied to VW-sourced engines (Diesel and VR6)

Check before buying

  • Crystal-clear maintenance history: avoid models without a service book or invoices
  • Air suspension: test all ride heights, listen to the compressor (must not run continuously)
  • 4.5L V8: Demand proof of replacement of the cooling tubes with aluminum models
  • 4.8L V8: Listen to the engine when cold (knocking) and check oil consumption (risk of cylinder scratches)
  • 3.0 V6 Diesel: Check for the absence of a check engine light (DPF/EGR) and inspect the engine V for oil leaks
  • Transmission: Check for the absence of jerks during gear changes and test the shift into low range
  • Driveshaft bearing: Vibrations or thumping noises under the center armrest under acceleration

What sets it apart 6

reliability

  • Proven Diesel Powertrain — The 3.0 V6 Diesel engine, introduced late in the model's lifecycle, is renowned for its robustness and inherent reliability, offering significant peace of mind on the road.

Performance

  • Unrivaled Driving Dynamics — Experience remarkable agility and road holding, worthy of a sports car, thanks to advanced systems like PASM and PDCC that ensure exemplary stability and steering precision.
  • Generous Engine Torque — The 3.0 V6 Diesel engine delivers a generous torque of 550 Nm, providing vigorous acceleration and reasonable fuel consumption for an SUV in its class, ideal for long journeys.

safety

  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — The Cayenne offers excellent, powerful, and consistent braking, often superior to its class standards, ensuring very short stopping distances for optimal safety.

practicality

  • Master of All Terrains — With its genuine off-road capabilities, including a low-range transfer case and differential locks, the Cayenne is ready to tackle the most demanding adventures.
  • Refined Interior and Towing Power — Enjoy impeccable interior finish quality and the ability to tow up to 3.5 tonnes, combining luxury and utility for all your needs.

Alternatives

  • Europe: BMW X5 (E53/E70 - dynamic benchmark, robust diesels), Mercedes-Benz ML-Class (W164 - comfort, reliable V6 CDIs), Volkswagen Touareg (shares the same platform and the same V6 TDI, cheaper to buy and maintain), Range Rover Sport (L320 - prestige, but equally hit-or-miss reliability).
  • North America: Lexus RX (absolute benchmark for reliability), Acura MDX, BMW X5.

Verdict

The first-generation Porsche Cayenne (955/957) is a double-edged sword. While its dynamic performance remains impressive, its reliability record is objectively poor, confirmed by ADAC and TÜV Report statistics (18.5% major defects). The phase 1 (955) petrol V8s are to be avoided unless the cooling tubes have been replaced with aluminum. The phase 2 (957) V8s suffer from fatal cylinder scratches.

The case of the Diesel (3.0 V6 TDI 240 hp, 2009-2010): It is paradoxically the safest choice of this generation. Arriving at the end of the model's life, it benefits from a more reliable platform and a proven VW/Audi engine. It will nevertheless be necessary to monitor the anti-pollution peripherals (DPF, EGR) and the air suspension (frequent leaks after 80,000 km). To be purchased only with a complete maintenance history and a substantial reserve budget for electronic and pneumatic failures.
