Summary

The 13th generation Ford F-150 (P552) marks a historic turning point in the automotive industry with the adoption of a fully military-grade aluminum alloy body, allowing a weight saving of up to 340 kg compared to the previous generation. This bold choice improved payload, towing capacity, and fuel consumption. Facelifted in 2018, it saw the introduction of a 10-speed automatic gearbox (developed with GM) across most of the range and the highly anticipated arrival of a 3.0L Power Stroke Diesel V6 engine.

According to data from *Consumer Reports* and the *ADAC*, the reliability of this aluminum generation was rated below average for the years 2015-2016, before rising to average for the 2017-2020 period. The *J.D. Power VDS 2023* (Vehicle Dependability Study) places the F-150 in the average of its segment (~185 PP100), behind the Ram 1500 (leader) and neck-and-neck with the Chevrolet Silverado / GMC Sierra.

Strengths 5

  • Aluminum body: total immunity to rust on exterior panels.
  • 5.0L V8 Coyote engine (especially 2015-2017) recognized as the most reliable option.
  • 6-speed automatic gearbox (2015-2017) extremely robust.
  • Towing and payload capacities at the top of the category.
  • Very attractive fuel consumption on the 3.0L Power Stroke Diesel on the highway.

Weaknesses 5

  • 10-speed automatic gearbox (2017-2020): sometimes erratic gear shifts, whining noises, valve body issues.
  • 3.5L EcoBoost engine: chronic fragility of the camshaft adjusters (cam phasers) and exhaust manifolds.
  • 5.0L V8 engine (2018-2020): abnormal excessive oil consumption on many models.
  • 3.0L Diesel engine: higher maintenance cost, sensitivity of the CP4 injection pump, unsuitable for short trips.
  • Imposing size making European urban use very complex.

Check before buying

  • Cold start: listen for any metallic chain noise or rattling (especially on EcoBoost engines).
  • 10-speed gearbox behavior: test shifts from 1st to 3rd and reverse (jerks).
  • Oil level: crucial on the 5.0L V8 from years 2018 to 2020.
  • Recall history: strictly verify if the brake master cylinder recall has been performed (risk of loss of braking).
  • On the 3.0L Diesel: check the DPF regeneration history and the absence of leaks around the EGR system.

Official recalls

The F-150 has been the subject of numerous safety recalls (NHTSA in the USA and Rappel Conso in Europe for imported models). Here are the most critical:

  • Braking (Master cylinder) [2020-06-08 / 2022-03-11 / 2025-04-11]: Massive recalls (NHTSA 20V332000, 22V150000, 25V236000) concerning 2014-2018 models equipped with the 3.5L EcoBoost. The brake master cylinder can leak into the brake booster, resulting in a sudden loss of braking power.
  • Fire risk (Engine block heater) [2018-12-18 / 2019-04-08 / 2020-02-21]: Water and contaminants can infiltrate the engine block heater cable, causing a short circuit and a fire (NHTSA 18V894000, 19V278000).
  • Fire risk (Seatbelt pretensioners) [2018-08-31]: Upon deployment, the front seatbelt pretensioners can generate sparks and ignite the B-pillar insulation (NHTSA 18V568000).
  • Transmission (Unexpected downshift) [2024-04-14 / 2026-04-14]: Loss of signal between the position sensor and the control module, causing a sudden downshift into 1st gear (NHTSA 26V237000).
  • Tailgate [2021-12-16]: The tailgate latches can open unexpectedly while driving (NHTSA 21V983000).
  • Takata Airbags [Rappel Conso France 2021-05-04 / 2019-11-01]: Although primarily targeting other Ford models (Ranger, Mustang), some imported F-150s may be affected by the defective ammonium nitrate propellant.

Verdict

The 13th generation Ford F-150 is a revolutionary pickup thanks to its aluminum body, but its mechanical reliability is uneven.

Regarding the 3.0L Power Stroke Diesel: It is an excellent choice for those who regularly tow over long distances. It offers generous torque and controlled fuel consumption. However, like any modern diesel, it is to be absolutely avoided if you mainly drive in the city or take short trips, otherwise you risk clogging the DPF and the EGR valve. The Bosch CP4 high-pressure pump also requires quality fuel.

For the other engines: The safest and most reliable choice (confirmed by the ADAC) remains the 5.0L V8 from the years 2015 to 2017, coupled with the indestructible 6-speed automatic gearbox. The 2018+ models with the 10-speed gearbox and the EcoBoost engines (especially the 3.5L) require a rigorous inspection (timing noises, transmission jerks) and a larger maintenance budget. Strictly verify that the braking-related recalls have been performed.
