Volvo V60

I (Y20) · 2010 – 2018

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The **first-generation Volvo V60 (Y20)** is a shooting brake ('Sportswagon') based on the S60 sedan. Built on the Ford EUCD platform (shared with the XC60 and Ford Mondeo), it prioritizes style and safety over cargo volume.

Its career is marked by a major mechanical transition: launched with engines from the Ford era (4-cylinder EcoBoost) and historic Volvo blocks (5 and 6-cylinder), it inaugurated in 2014 the new 100% Volvo **VEA (Drive-E)** 4-cylinder engine architecture. A facelift occurred in mid-2013, modernizing the front end and infotainment. Although this sheet focuses on **petrol** engines, the V60 was massively sold as a diesel in Europe, including a plug-in hybrid version (D6 Twin Engine).

Strengths 6

  • Passive and active safety at the highest level (City Safety standard)
  • Exceptional seat comfort (among the best on the market)
  • Build quality and aging of interior materials
  • Sound and driving pleasure of 5 and 6-cylinder engines
  • Timeless shooting brake design
  • Excellent Aisin 8-speed automatic gearbox (Geartronic 8) on VEA engines

Weaknesses 6

  • Ridiculous trunk volume for an estate (430 liters, less than a VW Golf SW)
  • Very high weight (often > 1600 kg, up to 2 tons for PHEV) penalizing agility and consumption
  • Powershift automatic gearbox (on 1.6 and 2.0 Ford) slow and very unreliable
  • Chronic oil overconsumption on early 4-cylinder VEA engines (2014-2016)
  • Aging and unergonomic Sensus infotainment system before 2014
  • Very large turning radius

Check before buying

  • Automatic gearbox fluid change history (especially Powershift MPS6, every 60,000 km)
  • Oil level on VEA petrol engines (T3, T4, T5, T6) produced between 2014 and 2016
  • Air conditioning operation (particularly on the D6 PHEV, related to battery cooling)
  • Condition of the accessory belt on 5-cylinder engines
  • Door locks (central locking motors tend to burn out)
  • Wear of front suspension bushings (the vehicle's weight quickly fatigues them)

Verdict

The first-generation Volvo V60 is an atypical estate: you buy it for its style, supreme comfort, and safety, not for moving house. For **petrol** engines, the choice of powertrain is crucial. Versions equipped with the 6-cylinder (T6) or 5-cylinder (T5 US) are reliability powerhouses, though thirsty. The original Ford 4-cylinders (1.6 T3/T4) are decent with a manual gearbox but to be avoided with the Powershift automatic gearbox. As for the 100% Volvo VEA engines (T3 to T6 post-2014), they offer the best performance/consumption compromise, but **it is imperative to avoid unrepaired 2014-2016 models** (segmentation issue) and prioritize 2017-2018 model years.
