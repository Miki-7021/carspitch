E90 · 2005 – 2012

Summary

The BMW 3 Series E90 (sedan), E91 (wagon/Touring), E92 (coupe), and E93 (convertible) marked a stylistic and technological break from the previous generation (E46). Launched in 2005, it received a major facelift (LCI - Life Cycle Impulse) in late 2008, correcting many early flaws and modernizing its appearance. Mechanically, this generation underwent a major transition: the shift from indirect to direct injection for gasoline engines, and the introduction of aluminum diesel engines (N family) replacing cast iron blocks (M family). While its road handling remains the absolute benchmark in its category, the E90's reliability varies greatly depending on the chosen engine. Diesels, very popular in Europe, are divided into two eras: the robust but imperfect M47/M57, and the efficient but fragile N47/N57 (infamous timing chain problems).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (rear-wheel drive, 50/50 weight distribution)
  • Interior finish quality (especially on post-2008 LCI versions)
  • Enjoyment and reliability of the M57 6-cylinder diesel (330d/335d phase 1)
  • iDrive CIC infotainment system (from late 2008) very modern for its time
  • Perfect driving position

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous timing chain reliability on the N47 diesel (2.0d post-2007)
  • Costly direct injection problems on N43, N53, and N54 gasoline engines
  • Very firm comfort with original Runflat tires and M Sport suspension
  • Average rear passenger space and trunk volume for the category
  • Sometimes capricious electronics (FRM module, ELV steering column lock)

Check before buying

  • Timing chain noise when cold and at 1500 rpm (especially on N47 and N57)
  • Oil change history (LongLife intervals of 30,000 km are destructive for chains and turbos)
  • Operation of all lights and power windows (symptom of a faulty FRM module)
  • ABS/DSC warning light on (DSC hydraulic unit often faulty on 4-cylinders)
  • Water ingress: panoramic sunroof (E91), retractable roof seals (E93), and floor (door vapor barrier detached)
  • Exhaust gas smell in the cabin (cracked manifold on M57)

Official recalls

  • Takata Airbags (Multiple years): Replacement of driver and passenger airbag inflators (risk of metal fragment projection).
  • Blower motor wiring (2017/2019): Risk of overheating and fire at the cabin fan connector. Affects almost the entire production.
  • Positive battery cable (2013): The B+ cable connecting the battery to the fuse box can degrade, leading to a total loss of electrical power while driving.
  • PCV valve heater (2017/2022): Risk of short circuit and fire in the crankcase ventilation system (primarily affects 6-cylinder gasoline engines in North America).
  • N47 Timing Chain (Technical campaign, not a safety recall): Partial coverage by BMW depending on age and mileage for tensioner/chain replacement (often difficult to obtain today).

Alternatives

  • Japan / Asia: Lexus IS, Toyota Mark X.

Verdict

The BMW 3 Series E90 is a car with two faces. It's a formidable driving machine, boasting a brilliant chassis, but it demands a surgical selection of its engine. For a diesel purchase, absolutely prioritize the 6-cylinder M57 generation (325d, 330d, 335d from before late 2008) which, once their intake flaps are removed, are indestructible. Avoid the N47 4-cylinders (318d, 320d from 2007 to 2012) unless you are provided with the timing chain replacement invoice (often over €2000). For gasoline, the naturally aspirated indirect injection 6-cylinder (N52) is the choice for peace of mind. Buying an E90 today requires a significant maintenance budget and a rigorous inspection of its history.
