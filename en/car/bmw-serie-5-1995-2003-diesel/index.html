Summary

The BMW 5 Series E39 is widely considered by purists and experts as the pinnacle of the 5 Series, combining exceptional build quality, timeless design (by Joji Nagashima), and benchmark road handling. Launched in 1995 and facelifted (LCI - Life Cycle Impulse) in September 2000, it introduced aluminum running gear and advanced onboard electronics for its time.

Although this report focuses on diesel engines (which made the model a success in Europe with the introduction of the legendary M57 6-cylinder), the E39 also excelled with its naturally aspirated petrol engines. Today, it is a collector's vehicle (youngtimer) or a robust 'daily', provided it receives rigorous maintenance. The mechanical reliability of the blocks is excellent, but peripherals (cooling, running gear, electronics) show their age.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional build and interior finish quality (ages very well)
  • High-level sound insulation and ride comfort
  • Intrinsic reliability of 6-cylinder engine blocks (M57 diesel and M52/M54 petrol)
  • Imperial road handling (aluminum running gear)
  • Superior corrosion resistance compared to its direct competitor (Mercedes W210)

Weaknesses 5

  • Undersized and fragile cooling system (plastics baked by heat)
  • GM automatic gearbox on the 530d unable to handle torque long-term
  • Aging electronics: disappearing instrument cluster pixels (MID), Bosch 5.7 ABS/DSC module sensitive to heat
  • Complex and costly running gear to overhaul (thrust arm bushings, suspension arms)
  • Battery discharge problem (often caused by the 'hedgehog' blower resistor / FSU)

Check before buying

  • History of swirl flaps removal on M47 and M57.
  • Automatic gearbox behavior (jerks, slipping, converter lock-up) and proof of fluid changes.
  • Condition of the cooling system (white/blue leak traces around the expansion tank and thermostat housing).
  • Steering wheel vibrations when braking around 80-100 km/h (sign of worn thrust arm bushings).
  • Functionality of all electrical accessories (windows, dashboard pixels, ABS/DSC module with no warning light).
  • Presence of rust (though rare, check jacking points under the sills, the tailgate on Touring versions, and the fuel filler flap).

Official recalls

  • Airbags (Takata) (Multiple years): Replacement of driver and passenger airbag gas generators due to the risk of metallic fragment projection.
  • Cooling Fan (2001): Risk of short circuit in the auxiliary fan resistor potentially leading to fire.
  • Seatbelt Pretensioners (1997): Inadvertent deployment or failure of the pyrotechnic system.
  • Brake Pedal (1999): On some models, a brake pedal retaining clip could detach.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Mercedes-Benz E-Class (W210) (excellent CDI diesels but major rust problems), Audi A6 (C5) (superb finish, but fragile 2.5 V6 TDI and complex front axle), Peugeot 607 (comfortable but less refined diesels).

Verdict

The BMW 5 Series E39 is a masterpiece of 90s engineering, but today it requires enthusiast maintenance. In diesel, the 530d with a manual gearbox is the Holy Grail: modern performance, reasonable consumption, and an indestructible M57 block (once the swirl flaps are removed). The 525d is an excellent alternative, often less abused. You should AVOID the 520d (VP44 pump too temperamental) and be wary of 530d models with automatic gearboxes, as the GM transmission is a ticking time bomb if it hasn't been meticulously serviced. Plan a budget to overhaul the cooling system and running gear, and you'll have one of the best sedans ever built.
