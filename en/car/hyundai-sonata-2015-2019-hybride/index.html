Summary

The seventh-generation Hyundai Sonata (codename LF) marks a turning point towards greater maturity compared to the previous generation (YF). Its design is more conservative, but it gains considerably in structural rigidity, soundproofing, and finish quality. The Hybrid (HEV) and Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV) version, which is the focus of particular attention here, is a major strategic choice: it uses the 2.0L engine from the *Nu* family, thus avoiding the catastrophic setbacks of the *Theta II* family (2.4L and 2.0T) that tarnished the model's reputation in North America. Comfortable, spacious, and equipped with a true 6-speed automatic gearbox (unlike Toyota's e-CVTs), the Sonata Hybrid is an excellent cruiser, although its image is clouded by the massive recalls affecting the other engines in the lineup.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of the Hybrid version (robust 2.0L Nu engine)
  • Driving pleasure of the hybrid thanks to the true 6-speed automatic gearbox
  • Excellent roominess and generous trunk volume
  • Soundproofing and ride comfort significantly improved compared to the YF generation
  • Long-term manufacturer warranty (5 years/unlimited mileage or 10 years/100k miles depending on markets)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of non-hybrid petrol engines (Theta II 2.4 and 2.0T)
  • Dual-clutch gearbox (DCT) on the 1.6T and 1.7 CRDi lacking smoothness and longevity
  • Electric power steering (MDPS) sometimes vague or requiring corrections
  • Quality of some interior plastics that scratch easily
  • Significant depreciation due to the bad reputation of conventional combustion engines

Check before buying

  • On Hybrid versions: battery state of health (SOH), smooth operation of combustion/electric transitions.
  • On 2.4 and 2.0T versions: clear history, verification of the KSDS (Knock Sensor) update installation, oil consumption.
  • Operation of the panoramic sunroof (risk of jamming or wind noise, subject to a recall).
  • Gearbox behavior (jerking on the DCT, slipping on the 6-speed automatic).
  • Operation of all electronic equipment (turn signals, dashboard warning lights - see recalls).

Official recalls

The Sonata LF has been the subject of numerous recalls, mainly in the United States (NHTSA). Here are the most notable:

  • [24/09/2021] Turn signals (SJB): A software defect in the Smart Junction Box can misinterpret signals, preventing the turn signals from working (Affects 2015-2017 Sonata and 2016-2017 Hybrids).
  • [06/08/2021] Trunk latch: The latch can become damaged, preventing the trunk from being opened from the inside (Affects 2017-2018 Sonata and 2016-2018 Hybrids).
  • [05/06/2017] Parking brake warning light: The warning light may intermittently fail to illuminate (2015-2016 Models).
  • [30/12/2016 & 24/08/2016 & 21/04/2016] Front airbags: Multiple recalls concerning defective inflator modules or inadequate deployment on the driver's side (2015-2017 Models).
  • [11/10/2016] Panoramic sunroof: The wind deflector can detach while driving (Affects 2015-2016 Sonata and 2015-2016 Hybrids).
  • [20/10/2015] ABS warning light: Malfunction of the ABS warning light display on the dashboard (2015-2016 Models).
  • [30/06/2015] Seat belt: The front passenger seat belt latch may not lock properly (2015 Models).
  • [09/12/2014] Driveshaft (1.6T): Possible installation of an incorrect left halfshaft (2015 1.6L Models).
  • [24/06/2014] Brake calipers: Risk of fracture of one or both front brake calipers due to a manufacturing error (2015 Models).

Verdict

The Hyundai Sonata (LF) is a car with two faces. If you opt for the conventional combustion versions (2.4 GDI or 2.0T), the risk of engine failure (Theta II) is far too high to justify the purchase, despite the warranty extensions. On the other hand, the Hybrid (HEV) or Plug-in (PHEV) version is highly recommendable. Equipped with the 2.0 Nu engine, it escapes the flaws of its combustion siblings and offers excellent reliability, controlled fuel consumption, and great comfort. It is an excellent alternative, often cheaper on the used market, to a Toyota Camry or a Honda Accord, provided you carefully check the history of electronic and bodywork recalls.
