Citroën C2

I (JM) · 2003 – 2009

NEGOTIATE Reliability 78/100 — bon

All Citroën models

Summary

The Citroën C2 (JM generation) succeeded the 3-door version of the Saxo, positioning itself as a dynamic and modular city car, exclusively available in 3 doors (the C3 I covered the 5-door offering). Based on PSA's PF1 platform, it stands out with its two-part opening tailgate (practical in the city) and its two independent and sliding rear seats (depending on trim level). While it lost some of the radical character of the Saxo VTS, the C2 VTS managed to appeal with its playful chassis. The model underwent two minor facelifts (2005 with the integration of CAN multiplexing and 2008 with a new grille).

*Historical note: The 'Citroën C2' sold in China (2006-2013) has nothing to do with the European model; it is a re-bodied Peugeot 206.*

Strengths 5

  • Petrol engines (TU) with great mechanical robustness
  • Dynamic and fun road handling (especially VTR/VTS)
  • Excellent rear modularity (independent sliding seats)
  • Two-part tailgate very practical in tight parking
  • Negligible maintenance cost for small petrol engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Sensodrive automated gearbox: slow, causes jerks, and unreliable
  • Basic interior finish and hard plastics that age poorly
  • Ridiculous boot volume if rear seats are pushed back
  • Very firm suspension comfort (especially on VTR/VTS)
  • Annoying electronic problems (multiplexing, COM2000)

Check before buying

  • Sensodrive gearbox operation: avoid if jerks on startup or excessive slowness.
  • Oil traces on the left side of the engine (timing/alternator side) on 1.1i and 1.4i (cylinder head gasket).
  • 'Pschitt' noise or exhaust smell in the cabin on the 1.4 HDi (leaking injector seals).
  • Operation of all steering wheel controls (indicators, headlights, wipers) to test the COM2000.
  • Condition of front shock absorber springs (corrosion or sagging).

Official recalls

  • December 2004: Risk of front suspension spring breakage (installation of a retaining cup). Concerns models produced until September 2004.
  • February 2005: Risk of short-circuit on the starter wiring harness (rubbing against the EGR valve on HDi).
  • September 2006: Reprogramming of the ABS/ESP ECU following a calibration fault.
  • May 2008: Replacement of the handbrake mechanism (risk of unintended release).

What sets it apart 6

reliability

  • Highly Robust Petrol Engines — The C2's petrol (TU) engines are renowned for their exceptional mechanical reliability, ensuring remarkable longevity and daily peace of mind.

practicality

  • Ingenious Modularity and Easy Access — Benefit from excellent rear modularity thanks to independent sliding seats and a two-part tailgate, ideal for city loading and tight parking spaces.

handling

  • Agile and Fun Driving Dynamics — Its dynamic chassis and short wheelbase give the C2 remarkable agility, offering engaging handling and genuine driving pleasure, especially in urban environments.

safety

  • Proven Passive Safety — The Citroën C2 achieved a 4-star rating in Euro NCAP tests for adult occupant protection, demonstrating a design focused on the safety of its occupants.

braking

  • Stable and Effective Braking — The C2's braking system performs well for its class, offering stable and effective deceleration, particularly with ABS available on equipped versions.

economy

  • Low Maintenance Costs — The C2's petrol engines stand out for their particularly low maintenance costs, making this vehicle an economical and wise choice for everyday use.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Renault Twingo II (more spacious, excellent in petrol), Peugeot 206+ (more versatile), Ford Ka II, Suzuki Swift II (very reliable in petrol), Fiat 500.
  • China (local C2): Honda Fit, Volkswagen Polo, Toyota Yaris.

Verdict

The Citroën C2 is an excellent small used city car, provided you target the right versions. The petrol engines (1.1i, 1.4i, and 1.6i 125hp) are true mechanical 'tanks', capable of exceeding 200,000 km with basic maintenance. However, you must absolutely avoid the Sensodrive automated gearbox, a source of costly breakdowns that often exceed the vehicle's market value today. The VTS 125hp version is an affordable future collector. The diesels, although economical, are less relevant today given the age of the vehicle and the risk of breakdowns (especially the 1.6 HDi 110).
