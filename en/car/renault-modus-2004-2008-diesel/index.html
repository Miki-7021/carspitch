Summary

The first-generation Renault Modus (Phase 1, project code JP0) is a mini-MPV based on the Renault-Nissan Alliance B platform (shared with the Clio III and Nissan Note). Designed to offer maximum interior space in an ultra-compact format (3.79 m), it stands out for its innovative modularity, notably its "Triptic" sliding rear bench seat and its dual-opening tailgate (optional). Very comfortable and safe (5 Euro NCAP stars), it nevertheless suffered from annoying design flaws (replacing front bulbs required removing the bumper) and very erratic reliability on its early diesel engines, permanently tarnishing its reputation.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional modularity (sliding and folding Triptic rear bench seat)
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (for the category)
  • Passive safety (5 Euro NCAP stars, rare at the time for this size)
  • Peripheral visibility and raised driving position
  • Very low fuel consumption of 1.5 dCi engines (when they work)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of early 1.5 dCi (Delphi pump)
  • Nightmarish mechanical accessibility (bumper removal required to change a headlight bulb)
  • Ridiculous trunk volume when the bench seat is moved all the way back
  • Frequent electronic bugs (power windows, stalks, UCH)
  • Slow, jerky, and unreliable Quickshift automated gearbox

Check before buying

  • Injection system (1.5 dCi): Demand a clear service history. The presence of metal filings in the fuel filter condemns the engine (repair cost > €2000).
  • Windshield scuttle: Water drains get clogged with leaves. Water overflows into the cabin and fries the UCH (Central Habitacle Unit) electronic control unit.
  • Steering column: Clunking noises or sudden loss of electric power assistance (requires column replacement).
  • Electric power windows: Cables break or motors burn out frequently.
  • Headlights: Check that all bulbs are working, as labor for replacement is very expensive.

Official recalls

  • December 2006: Steering column. Risk of loss of electric power steering assistance (ECU reprogramming or column replacement).
  • February 2007: Front seat belts. Risk of buckle not locking.
  • October 2005: Rear bench seat. Problem with locking of the Triptic bench seat slides.
  • April 2008: Quickshift automated gearbox. ECU reprogramming to avoid untimely gear changes or gearbox blockages.

What sets it apart 7

Practicality

  • Smart Modularity — Enjoy exceptional modularity thanks to its sliding and folding Triptic rear bench seat, offering remarkable adaptability for your transport and loading needs.
  • Compact and Spacious Design — Designed as a minispace, the Modus maximizes interior space in an ultra-compact format, offering surprising habitability and great ease of parking in the city.
  • Easy Boot Access — The double-opening tailgate (depending on version) greatly facilitates loading and unloading, even in the tightest spaces, adding to the vehicle's daily practicality.

Comfort

  • Optimized Driving Comfort — The Modus offers superior suspension comfort and sound insulation for its category, ensuring pleasant and serene journeys, even over long distances.

Safety

  • High-Level Passive Safety — Benefit from optimal protection for all occupants, as the Modus achieved an impressive score in European safety tests, rare for a vehicle of its size at the time.
  • Panoramic Visibility and Elevated Driving Position — The elevated driving position and clear peripheral visibility contribute to safer and more pleasant driving, facilitating maneuvers and environmental awareness.

Economy

  • Controlled Fuel Consumption — The 1.5 dCi diesel engines are renowned for their very low consumption, allowing you to make significant savings on every journey and reduce your fuel budget.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Opel Meriva A (more spacious but less comfortable), Fiat Idea / Lancia Musa (good interior space, reliable JTD engines), Peugeot 1007 (heavy and capricious sliding doors), Ford Fusion (very robust, but rustic).
  • Japan/Asia: Honda Jazz I/II (exceptional reliability, unbeatable modularity with "Magic Seats"), Nissan Note I (Modus' technical cousin, longer wheelbase, more trunk space).

Verdict

The first-generation Renault Modus is a vehicle full of good ideas, ideal for city driving and short trips thanks to its modularity and comfort. However, buying a diesel version (1.5 dCi) produced between 2004 and 2008 requires extreme caution. The 65, 70, and 80 hp versions equipped with Delphi injection are veritable financial time bombs (metal filings issue). If you absolutely want a diesel, prioritize the 1.5 dCi 105 hp (Siemens injection) or look for a petrol model (1.2 16v or 1.4 16v), which are infinitely more reliable and economical in the long term. Negotiate the price firmly, checking that maintenance (timing belt, oil changes) is up to date and that the onboard electronics are working perfectly.
