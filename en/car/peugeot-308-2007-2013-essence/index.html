Peugeot 308

I (4A/C) · 2007 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

Summary

The **first-generation Peugeot 308 (T7)** succeeded the 307, reusing its platform but correcting its finish flaws. Launched in 2007, it stands out with imperial road handling, typical of Peugeot, and a very high-quality interior for its time. However, it suffers from a high weight which penalizes entry-level engines.

The **facelift (Phase 2) occurred in spring 2011**, bringing a refined front end with LED daytime running lights, a slight weight reduction, and most importantly, a major engine update (transition to Euro 5 standards, introduction of 8-valve e-HDi diesels, and minor corrections to Prince petrol engines).

*Expert's note: Although the request is for petrol, it is crucial to note that this generation was marked by serious failures of the "Prince" petrol engines (VTi and THP) co-developed with BMW. Purchasing a petrol version requires extreme vigilance.*

Strengths 5

  • Benchmark road handling (comfort/road holding compromise)
  • Interior finish quality significantly improved compared to the 307
  • Generous cabin space and boot volume
  • Remarkable reliability of 2.0 HDi and 1.6 HDi 8v engines (post-2011)
  • Careful sound insulation

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of VTi and THP petrol engines (before 2011/2012)
  • High weight (approx. 1.3 to 1.4 tonnes) penalizing smaller engines
  • Frequent turbo failure on the 1.6 HDi 110 (16v)
  • Suspensions a bit firm at low speeds (especially with 17/18-inch wheels)
  • Premature wear of front brake discs

Check before buying

  • Petrol VTi/THP: Listen to the engine when cold (metallic rattling sound = stretched chain).
  • Petrol VTi/THP: Check oil consumption history and High-Pressure pump invoices.
  • Diesel 1.6 HDi 16v: Lift the engine cover and look for traces of black tar around the injectors.
  • Electronics: Test all controls (windows, AC, radio) and check for absence of error messages (defective anti-pollution system).
  • Running gear: Check rear tire wear (slight tendency for faceted wear) and front wishbone silentblocks.

Official recalls

  • - **November 2010**: Risk of loss of braking assistance (defective vacuum pump valve on 1.6 THP and 1.6 VTi). - **February 2011**: Non-compliant exterior lighting harness (risk of short circuit). - **September 2012**: Improper tightening of the battery ground cable potentially leading to engine cut-offs. - **January 2014**: Replacement of the BSM (Engine Control Unit) following untimely stalls or inability to start. - **December 2015**: Possible fuel leak on the diesel filter (2.0 HDi engines).

Verdict

Purchasing a first-generation Peugeot 308 with a **petrol** engine is a minefield. The VTi and THP engines (Prince family) have accumulated serious design flaws (timing chain, oil consumption, HP pump). If you absolutely insist on petrol, prioritize a post-2012 model with a clear service history proving the timing chain replacement, or look for the rare 1.6 BioFlex (TU5) version. Paradoxically, this car shines with its diesels: a 2.0 HDi or a 1.6 HDi 8v (from 2011 onwards) offers excellent reliability and perfectly suits the 308's heavy, road-oriented chassis.
