Summary

The second-generation Kia Rio (JB code) marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer, abandoning the small estate car look of the first generation to adopt a classic versatile city car format (available in 5-door hatchback and 4-door sedan depending on the market). Sharing its platform with the Hyundai Accent (MC), it stands out with a very aggressive price/equipment ratio and generous cabin space. The model benefited from a facelift in late 2009, bringing aesthetic touches ('Tiger Nose' grille, new bumpers) and a slight improvement to the interior. Although its road handling and sound insulation were behind European benchmarks of the time (Clio III, Peugeot 207), it compensates with very satisfactory overall mechanical reliability. The 1.5 CRDi diesel version, particularly targeted here, surprises with its very high power (110 hp) for the segment at the time, offering excellent acceleration.

Strengths 5

  • Very powerful (110 hp) and willing 1.5 CRDi engine
  • Decent cabin space and boot volume (270L) for its time
  • Overall mechanical reliability (proven engines)
  • Timing chain on the diesel engine
  • Very attractive price/equipment ratio on the used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Poor sound insulation (wind and road noise, noisy diesel engine when cold)
  • Interior plastic quality (hard and prone to scratches)
  • Firm and jittery suspension at low speeds
  • Vague and uncommunicative electric power steering (EPS)
  • Fragile paintwork (prone to stone chips)

Check before buying

  • Service book: check the regularity of oil changes on the 1.5 CRDi (vital for the turbo).
  • EGR valve (1.5 CRDi): check for absence of jerking during acceleration and black smoke from the exhaust.
  • Steering: listen for a 'clunk' when turning the steering wheel at a standstill (wear of the star-shaped flexible coupling in the electric steering column, a very common problem with Hyundai/Kia).
  • Running gear: premature wear of anti-roll bar drop links (dull noises over bumps).
  • Electrics: check the proper functioning of the central locking and car radio.

Official recalls

  • Brake light switch (2013): Massive recall (global campaign) concerning the replacement of a defective brake light switch. The lights might not illuminate when braking, or the cruise control might not deactivate. Affects models produced between 2006 and 2011.
  • Power steering (2008): On some models, risk of loss of electric power steering assistance requiring reprogramming or replacement of the EPS module.

Verdict

The Kia Rio II (JB) is a pragmatic and economical choice on the used market. If you target the 1.5 CRDi, you'll get a surprisingly lively city car capable of venturing onto the motorway without hesitation thanks to its 110 hp. However, this diesel engine, although equipped with a robust timing chain, requires a clear service history. Avoid examples that have been used exclusively in the city (risk of clogged EGR and worn turbo). If the history is unclear, prefer to negotiate the price down to anticipate an intake cleaning or peripheral replacement. For purely urban use, you should definitely opt for the 1.4 or 1.6 petrol versions, which are certainly less torquey but indestructible if the belt is up to date.
