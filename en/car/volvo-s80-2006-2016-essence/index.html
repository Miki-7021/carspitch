Summary

The second-generation Volvo S80 (code name AS) is based on the EUCD platform developed during the Ford era (shared with the Mondeo, S-Max, and Land Rover Freelander 2). As the Swedish brand's flagship, it stands out with exceptional ride comfort, some of the best seats in the automotive industry, and a pioneering level of passive and active safety (introduction of BLIS, City Safety, adaptive cruise control).

It underwent several evolutions: a minor facelift in 2009, a technological and interior update in 2011 (Sensus), and a more significant facelift in 2013 (new bumpers, digital gauges) coinciding with the arrival of the new generation of in-house 4-cylinder “VEA Drive-E” engines. Although European demand heavily shifted towards diesel (D5), the S80 offers a fascinating range of petrol engines, from the modest Ford 4-cylinder to the majestic Yamaha V8, including the legendary 5-cylinders and transverse inline 6-cylinders.

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous Powershift (Ford) dual-clutch automatic gearbox on 4-cylinders
  • High weight leading to rapid wear of suspension bushings and brakes
  • Very large turning radius (difficult in urban maneuvers)
  • High fuel consumption of petrol engines (V8, T6, 3.2)
  • Less developed network of independent specialists than for German cars
  • Sluggish road behavior, focused on comfort rather than dynamism

Check before buying

  • Automatic gearbox oil change history (Aisin TF-80SC or TG-81SC): must be done every 60k-80k km. If never done beyond 150k km, risk of jerking.
  • Whistling noise at idle on petrol engines (2.5T, 3.2, T6): indicates a perforated PCV (crankcase ventilation) diaphragm. If the noise stops when removing the oil dipstick, it's confirmed.
  • Bearing noise on the passenger side on 3.2 and T6 (READ Unit).
  • Air conditioning operation (the compressor tends to wear out around 150,000 km).
  • Water ingress into the cabin (clogged windshield scuttle) potentially damaging the Central Electronic Module (CEM).
  • Wear of front suspension arm bushings and shock absorbers (heavy vehicle).

What sets it apart 6

Comfort

  • Exceptional Travel Comfort — Enjoy imperial ride comfort and top-notch sound insulation, complemented by seats among the most ergonomic and comfortable on the market, ideal for long journeys.

Reliability

  • Remarkably Reliable Petrol Engines — The 5 and 6-cylinder petrol engines (2.5T, 3.2, T6) as well as the majestic Yamaha V8 are renowned for their robustness and exceptional longevity, guaranteeing years of serenity.

Verdict

The Volvo S80 II is an underrated luxury sedan that offers exceptional comfort and safety for its used car price. With petrol engines, it's a brilliant choice provided you target the right mechanics. The 5-cylinder (2.5T), 6-cylinder (3.2, T6), and V8 engines are monuments of reliability if maintenance is followed, especially the Aisin automatic gearbox oil change. However, you must absolutely avoid the Ford 4-cylinder engines (1.6T, 2.0T) associated with the Powershift gearbox, whose reliability is catastrophic. Drive-E versions (post-2014) are good modern alternatives, provided you check the oil consumption of the early model years.
