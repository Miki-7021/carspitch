Toyota Highlander

IV (XU70) · 2020 – 2025

BUY Reliability 93/100 — excellent

All Toyota models

Summary

The fourth-generation Toyota Highlander (XU70) is a large 3-row family SUV (up to 7 or 8 seats) based on the TNGA-K modular platform. Designed primarily for the North American market but sold globally (including in Western Europe for the first time as a hybrid version), it stands out for its supreme comfort, build quality, and very high level of reliability. In 2023, the legendary naturally aspirated 3.5L V6 was replaced by a torquier and more efficient 2.4L Turbo 4-cylinder, although less refined in terms of sound. The 2.5L hybrid version remains the most popular choice for its unbeatable running costs in this weight category.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall reliability (engines and transmissions, ranked above average by J.D. Power)
  • Fuel consumption of the Hybrid version (unmatched for a 7-seat SUV of this size)
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing (especially on high trim levels)
  • Very high resale value
  • Improved multimedia system from 2023 onwards (12.3-inch screens)

Weaknesses 4

  • Very restricted third-row space (reserved for young children)
  • Limited trunk volume when all 7/8 seats are deployed
  • Sound and roughness of the new 2.4 Turbo engine compared to the old V6
  • Dated and slow infotainment system on 2020-2022 models

Check before buying

  • Lower front bumper attachment (subject to a recall, can easily detach during minor impacts)
  • Second-row seatback locking (ongoing recall)
  • Condition of the 12V battery on hybrid versions (tendency to discharge if the vehicle remains stationary for a long time)
  • Software update history (PVM cameras, digital instrument clusters)

Official recalls

Several recalls have been issued, the majority concerning peripheral or software elements, with no impact on fundamental mechanical reliability: - Seats and Seatbelts: Recalls in Nov. 2024 and March 2026 (2nd-row seatbacks may not lock properly); Dec. 2025 (incorrect anchorage angle of the 3rd-row center seatbelt). - Electronics and Software: Recalls in Sept./Dec. 2025 (memory deterioration of the instrument cluster); Oct./Nov. 2025 (software bug in the PVM panoramic camera). - Bodywork: Recalls in Oct. 2023 and April 2024 (the lower front bumper can detach following a minor impact, causing the engine skid plate to fall off). - Steering: Recalls in July 2023 and Jan. 2025 (potential defect on the steering column). - Airbags: Recalls in Oct. 2020 (seat trim blocking the side airbag) and Dec. 2023 (defective occupant classification sensor). - Engine (3.5L V6): Recall in March 2020 (ECU programming error that could cut off the fuel supply). - Tires: Recalls in June 2024 (20-inch accessory tires with insufficient load index installed by some distributors).

safety

  • Advanced Active and Passive Safety — Designed for maximum occupant protection, the Highlander achieved a top 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores for adult (89%) and child (87%) occupant protection. It is also a regular recipient of the IIHS "Top Safety Pick+" award.

braking

  • Confident and Effective Braking — Despite its size, the Highlander offers remarkable braking performance for its class, ensuring short and controlled stops. Independent tests have shown a stopping capability from 60 to 0 mph in just 126 feet, guaranteeing optimal safety in all circumstances.

comfort

  • Superior Ride Comfort and Sound Insulation — The Highlander prioritizes passenger well-being with sovereign ride comfort and high-level sound insulation, transforming every journey into a serene experience. Its stable and predictable handling complements this sense of tranquility.

practicality

  • Spaciousness and Family Versatility — Ideal for large families, this large SUV offers up to 7 or 8 seats, built on the modern TNGA-K platform. Its spacious and modular interior adapts to all your needs, making every trip a comfortable adventure for everyone.

Performance

  • Powerful and Proven Gasoline Engines — Enjoy the power and smoothness of Toyota's gasoline engines. The former 3.5L V6 offers linear power of 295 hp and a noble sound, while the new 2.4L Turbo delivers generous torque of 265 hp from low RPMs, for dynamic and responsive driving.

Verdict

The fourth-generation Toyota Highlander is one of the most reliable and rational 3-row family SUVs on the market, regularly praised by Consumer Reports and the ADAC. If you are looking for a classic gasoline engine, the old 3.5L V6 (2020-2022) is a monument of smoothness and reliability, although quite thirsty. The new 2.4 Turbo (2023+) gets the job done with more torque but less refinement. However, the real star of the lineup is the 2.5L Hybrid version: it offers foolproof reliability (proven HSD system) and fuel consumption worthy of a compact sedan, making it the absolute best choice for this vehicle. Recalls are numerous but mainly concern assembly adjustments or software updates, confirming the model's mechanical excellence.
