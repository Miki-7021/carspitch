IV (R52) · 2013 – 2021

All Nissan models

Summary

The fourth-generation Nissan Pathfinder (R52) marks a radical break in the model's history. Abandoning the ladder frame chassis of its predecessor (R51) for a unibody structure (D platform shared with the Murano and Altima), it transitions from a hardcore off-roader to a large 7-seater family crossover. This change in philosophy prioritizes comfort, interior space, and fuel consumption at the expense of off-road capabilities. While its V6 engine is renowned as indestructible, this generation saw its reputation heavily tarnished by the catastrophic failures of its JATCO continuously variable transmission (CVT) on 2013-2016 models, which led to class-action lawsuits in North America. The 2017 facelift brought major fixes to the gearbox and direct injection to the V6, making late-model versions significantly more recommendable.

Strengths 5

  • Legendary V6 engine (VQ series) for its robustness and longevity
  • Generous interior space with 7 truly usable seats
  • Very easy access to the third row (EZ Flex system)
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing (especially post-2017)
  • Effective Intelligent 4WD system for winter conditions

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the CVT gearbox on 2013-2016 models (class-action lawsuits, frequent replacements)
  • Clumsy handling and vague steering before the 2017 facelift
  • Dated and slow infotainment system
  • High fuel consumption in urban environments
  • Unconvincing hybrid version with questionable reliability

Check before buying

  • CVT behavior: During the test drive, simulate slow stop/starts and hard accelerations. The slightest shuddering (judder), hesitation, or metallic belt noise should make you walk away.
  • Gearbox maintenance history: Demand invoices proving the CVT oil change every 40,000 km.
  • CVT fluid leaks: Check the condition of the transmission cooling hoses (subject to recall).
  • ABS actuator: Check for brake fluid leaks near the ABS pump (fire risk, NHTSA recall).
  • Brake and tire wear: The heavy weight of the vehicle leads to rapid wear of front axle consumables.

What sets it apart 10

Reliability

  • V6 Engine Known for Longevity — The 3.5L V6 is described as legendary for its robustness and longevity, giving buyers confidence in the engine's ability to cover immense mileage over years of family use.
  • Improved CVT from 2017 Onward — The 2017 restyling brought major fixes to the CVT, with a new software and valve body, lifting transmission reliability back to average and making late models far more recommendable.

Practicality

  • Seven Genuinely Usable Seats — The interior offers generous space with seven real, usable seats, making it practical for larger families who need to carry passengers without sacrificing everyday comfort.
  • Easy Third-Row Access System — The EZ Flex system provides very easy access to the third row, so passengers can get in and out without struggle, which simplifies daily family transport.

Comfort

  • Comfortable and Quiet Ride — Ride comfort and sound insulation are highlighted, especially on post-2017 models, giving occupants a calmer and more relaxed experience on longer journeys.

Performance

  • Direct Injection on Later V6 — The post-2017 V6 gained direct injection, bringing increased power, better responsiveness and controlled consumption, which benefits buyers seeking a more efficient driving experience.

Handling

  • Intelligent 4WD for Winter Conditions — The Intelligent 4WD system is described as effective for winter conditions, helping drivers maintain traction on slippery roads and adding confidence during cold-season family trips.
  • Smoother Road Behavior After Restyling — The 2017 update improved road behavior, addressing the clumsy handling and vague steering of earlier models so the vehicle feels more composed and easier to place on the road.

Economy

  • Simple and Affordable 2.5 L4 — The 2.5 L4 offers mechanical simplicity and low maintenance costs, making it a practical choice for buyers who prioritize inexpensive upkeep for urban and flat-road use.
  • Lower Urban Consumption with Hybrid — The 2.5 hybrid version slightly reduces urban fuel consumption, offering a modest economy benefit for buyers who mostly drive in city and peri-urban conditions.

Verdict

The 4th generation Nissan Pathfinder (R52) is a vehicle with two faces. Its 3.5L V6 engine is a marvel of reliability and smoothness, capable of handling immense mileages. Unfortunately, this brilliant engine was mated to a dramatically fragile JATCO CVT transmission between 2013 and 2016. The premature failures of this gearbox are extremely well documented (Consumer Reports, J.D. Power, class-action lawsuits).

Our advice: Absolutely avoid 2013 to 2016 models, unless the transmission has been replaced very recently by Nissan with a supporting invoice. If you are looking for an R52 Pathfinder, you must target the facelifted models from 2017 onwards. The CVT gearbox was made much more reliable, the engine gained direct injection, and the handling was improved. For total peace of mind, the next generation (Gen V R53, 2022+), which replaced the CVT with an excellent 9-speed automatic gearbox, remains the ultimate choice.
