Toyota Yaris Cross

I (MXPJ) · 2021 – 2025

BUY Reliability 90/100 — excellent

All Toyota models

Summary

The Toyota Yaris Cross (first generation, codename MXPJ/MXPB for the TNGA version) is a B-segment urban SUV based on the TNGA-B platform, shared with the Yaris IV. Launched in 2021, it quickly established itself as a bestseller thanks to its adventurous design, compactness (4.18 m), and the efficiency of its powertrains. Although the European market overwhelmingly favors the hybrid (HSD) version, the Yaris Cross is also offered with a 100% naturally aspirated gasoline engine (M15A-FKS), which stands out for its mechanical simplicity and unparalleled reliability. A slight restyling (facelift) and a technological update (new multimedia system, digital gauges, revised sound insulation) occurred in early 2024, accompanied by the introduction of a more powerful hybrid version (130 hp). Overall design rating: Excellent.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional mechanical reliability, particularly with the 1.5 naturally aspirated gasoline engine (no turbo, no complex DPF).
  • D-4S dual injection on the 1.5 gasoline engine prevents valve fouling (a common problem on pure direct injection engines).
  • Very controlled fuel consumption, even on the 100% gasoline version (around 5.5 to 6.0 L/100 km in mixed use).
  • Availability of all-wheel drive (AWD-i), rare in this segment.
  • Excellent resale value on the used car market.
  • Toyota Relax warranty (up to 10 years or 160,000 km if serviced within the network).

Weaknesses 6

  • Imperfect sound insulation on pre-2024 models (wind and road noise on highways).
  • 1.5 gasoline engine (M15A-FKS) noisy during strong accelerations (typical of 3-cylinders).
  • Very average rear passenger space and narrow rear access (limited door opening).
  • Quality of some interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches).
  • Multimedia system on 2021-2023 versions slow and dated (corrected in 2024).
  • Recurring weakness of the 12V auxiliary battery on hybrid versions with short, infrequent trips.

Check before buying

  • Condition of the 12V battery (especially on used models with low mileage).
  • Maintenance history: verify that the oil used is indeed 0W-16 or 0W-20 (crucial for the timing chain and VVT-i).
  • Multimedia system operation (Apple CarPlay / Android Auto connection bugs on early models).
  • Condition of plastics in the trunk and rear seats.
  • On AWD-i versions: regular tire wear on the rear axle.

Official recalls

  • November 2021 (Code 21SMD-104): Reprogramming of the millimeter-wave radar sensor for the Pre-Collision System (PCS) which might not detect certain obstacles.
  • May 2022 (Code 22SMD-039): Inspection and modification of the central rear seat belt anchor, risk of the strap being cut by a metal frame in the event of a severe impact.
  • February 2023: Software update for the Data Communication Module (DCM) affecting the eCall emergency call system.
  • December 2023: Reprogramming of the hybrid control module (on HSD versions) following isolated cases of loss of motive power.

What sets it apart 7

reliability

  • Unrivaled Mechanical Reliability — The Yaris Cross's 1.5 VVT-i atmospheric petrol engine is renowned for its robustness and intelligent design, including D-4S dual injection, ensuring exceptional longevity and peace of mind.

economy

  • Remarkable Fuel Efficiency — Enjoy very controlled fuel consumption for a petrol SUV, allowing you to make significant savings on a daily basis.
  • Exceptional Resale Value — The Toyota Yaris Cross maintains excellent value on the used car market, reinforced by the Toyota Relax warranty, ensuring the longevity of your investment.

safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — The Toyota Yaris Cross achieved the maximum rating from Euro NCAP, demonstrating its robust design and advanced safety systems to protect all occupants.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Renault Captur (the 1.3 TCe is reliable, but the gasoline Yaris Cross is more frugal), Peugeot 2008 (to avoid in 1.2 PureTech, prefer the electric or the new mild-hybrid), Ford Puma (1.0 EcoBoost reliable but wet belt in the early years), Honda HR-V (hybrid only, very reliable).
  • Japan / Asia: Honda Vezel (HR-V), Nissan Kicks (e-Power), Mazda CX-3.
  • North America: The Yaris Cross is not sold there, the direct alternative from Toyota is the Corolla Cross.
  • Emerging Markets: Hyundai Creta, Kia Seltos, Honda WR-V.

Verdict

The Toyota Yaris Cross is a pragmatic and extremely reassuring choice. While the hybrid version captures all the attention, the 100% gasoline version (1.5 VVT-i 125 hp) is a true gem of reliability for those who wish to keep their vehicle for the very long term. Devoid of a turbocharger and equipped with intelligent dual-mode injection, this M15A-FKS engine is immune to the costly breakdowns that plague its European competitors (PureTech, older TSI). Although it can be a bit noisy under load and the interior lacks refinement, its negligible running costs and its manufacturer's warranty, extendable to 10 years, make it one of the smartest purchases in the B-SUV segment.
