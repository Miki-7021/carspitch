Toyota RAV4

IV (XA40) · 2012 – 2018

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

All Toyota models

Summary

The fourth-generation Toyota RAV4 (XA40) marks a major turning point in the model's history. Gone is the small urban 4x4 with the spare wheel on the tailgate: the XA40 becomes more refined and transforms into a spacious family SUV, prioritizing boot volume and interior space. Restyled in late 2015 (Phase 2), it introduced the hybrid powertrain that made it a success.

However, in the European market, the first part of its career was largely dominated by diesel (D-4D) powertrains. Toyota initially used its own blocks (1AD-FTV and 2AD-FHV) before forming a partnership with BMW for the facelift, integrating a 2.0-liter block of Bavarian origin (1WW/2WW). Although the RAV4 is intrinsically robust, these diesel versions represent the model's Achilles' heel in terms of reliability, particularly in urban use.

Strengths 5

  • Giant rear spaciousness and boot volume (547 liters)
  • Exceptional reliability of petrol and hybrid versions
  • Suspension comfort and smooth driving
  • Assembly quality (no parasitic noises despite hard plastics)
  • Excellent resale value (especially the hybrid)

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of diesel engines highly dependent on usage (clogging)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the motorway (wind and road noise)
  • Infotainment system (Toyota Touch 2) slow and dated
  • Perceived quality of some interior plastics (scratch-prone)
  • Manual gearbox sometimes notchy on diesels

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history (Diesels): Demand invoices proving regular oil changes and the use of the correct oil specification.
  • DPF and EGR (Diesels): Check for absence of engine warning light, acceleration jerks, or black smoke from the exhaust.
  • Timing chain noise (2.0 D-4D 143hp): Start the vehicle cold with the bonnet open to listen for a chain noise (rattling).
  • Coolant level (2.0 D-4D 143hp): An unexplained drop often indicates an EGR cooler leak.
  • Hybrid Health Check: For HSD versions, demand the annual Toyota inspection certificate to guarantee the battery.

Official recalls

  • Seat belts (August 2016): Risk of rear seat belt severance by the metal seat frame in the event of a severe frontal collision. Installation of resin protectors (2012-2016 Models).
  • Wipers (October 2015): Possible water infiltration into the front wiper motor, leading to its blockage.
  • DC-DC Converter (July 2020): On hybrid versions, risk of overheating of a converter transistor which could lead to a loss of power.
  • Takata Airbags: Some early production models were recalled for replacement of the passenger airbag inflator.

What sets it apart 7

Practicality

  • Exceptional Interior Space and Boot Volume — The RAV4 stands out with its generous rear passenger space and vast boot volume, offering optimal comfort for all passengers and large loading capacity.

Comfort

  • Suspension Comfort and Smooth Driving — Enjoy a serene and pleasant driving experience, characterized by excellent suspension comfort that absorbs road irregularities and overall smooth controls.

Safety

  • High-Level Passive Safety — The Toyota RAV4 achieved the maximum rating in Euro NCAP tests, ensuring robust protection for adult and child occupants in the event of a collision.

Braking

  • Efficient and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from a high-performance braking system, ensuring short stopping distances and great vehicle control, for increased daily safety.

Handling

  • Safe and Stable Road Handling — The RAV4 offers balanced and predictable road handling, providing a feeling of safety and stability, ideal for family trips and long distances.

Economy

  • Diesel Engines with Controlled Consumption — The RAV4's diesel engines are designed to offer optimized fuel consumption, making your journeys more economical, especially on long distances.

Performance

  • Generous Torque for Dynamic Driving — Diesel powertrains provide appreciable engine torque, ensuring strong acceleration and suitable towing capacity, whether for daily use or leisure.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Honda CR-V IV (its 1.6 i-DTEC diesel engine is much more reliable), Volkswagen Tiguan (robust 2.0 TDI but more expensive), Mazda CX-5 (2.2 Skyactiv-D to avoid, same clogging issues), Nissan X-Trail.
  • North America: Honda CR-V (1.5T or 2.4L), Subaru Forester, Ford Escape.
  • Asia / Emerging Markets: Nissan X-Trail, Hyundai Tucson, Kia Sportage.

Verdict

The Toyota RAV4 (XA40) is an excellent family SUV, but its reliability record is split in two. If you opt for a petrol or hybrid version, it's a 'no-brainer' purchase that will take you beyond 300,000 km without a hitch.

On the other hand, diesel versions require great caution. Whether it's the Toyota blocks (124/150 hp) or the BMW block (143 hp), they all suffer from modern diesel ailments (DPF, EGR, chain for the BMW). If you drive less than 20,000 km per year or mostly in the city, avoid diesel. If you are a high-mileage driver (motorway/expressways), the 2.0 D-4D 124 hp or 143 hp can be considered, provided you have a clear maintenance history and negotiate the price, as these versions depreciate significantly compared to the hybrid.
