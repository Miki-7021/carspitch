Summary

The **third-generation Hyundai Tucson (TL)** marked a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer, replacing the ix35 with a much more stately design, a significantly improved finish, and Europeanized road behavior. Launched in 2015, it benefited from a **facelift in 2018**, bringing a new light signature, a redesigned dashboard with an 8-inch floating screen, and the introduction of mild-hybrid diesel engines (MHEV 48V).

Positioned in the ultra-competitive compact SUV (C-SUV) segment, it established itself thanks to its unbeatable price/equipment ratio and its 5-year unlimited mileage manufacturer's warranty. While European gasoline engines (Gamma family) are generally very reliable, North American versions (Nu and Theta II families) have suffered from major problems. The DCT-7 dual-clutch transmission remains the weak point of this generation in terms of driving pleasure and longevity.

Strengths 6

  • Exterior design that ages very well
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume (513 liters)
  • Overall reliability of European gasoline engines (1.6 GDI/T-GDI)
  • Very rich standard equipment (especially Executive/Creative trims)
  • Ergonomics of physical controls (climate control separate from screen)
  • Significantly improved sound insulation compared to the ix35

Weaknesses 5

  • Capricious DCT-7 dual-clutch automatic gearbox (juddering, wear)
  • Fuel consumption of gasoline engines (the 1.6 T-GDI easily exceeds 8.5 L/100km)
  • Quality of interior plastics on pre-facelift models (2015-2018)
  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 and 2.4 gasoline engines in North America
  • High vehicle weight which penalizes dynamism and consumption

Check before buying

  • DCT-7 gearbox behavior: no jerking or juddering should be felt when starting or in reverse.
  • Recall history: imperatively check if the ABS/ESP module (HECU) recall has been performed (fire risk).
  • Service book: the 5-year warranty required strict maintenance within the network. An incomplete service book is a reason for negotiation.
  • Oil level (especially on imported or North American 2.0/2.4 versions).
  • Multimedia system operation (slowness or touchscreen bugs on 2015-2017 models).

Alternatives

Verdict

The Hyundai Tucson III is an excellent used car choice on the European market, provided you target the right version. The 1.6 GDI and 1.6 T-GDI gasoline engines are mechanically very robust. However, the DCT-7 automatic gearbox requires absolute vigilance during the test drive: at the slightest judder, walk away or negotiate clutch replacement. Opt for a manual gearbox version for complete peace of mind, or choose the excellent diesels (1.6 or 2.0 CRDi) if you drive a lot.

**Warning:** For buyers in North America, the 2.0 and 2.4 gasoline versions are to be strictly avoided unless the engine has already been replaced under warranty by Hyundai and the KSDS update is certified.
