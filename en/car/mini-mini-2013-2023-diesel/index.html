Summary

The third-generation MINI (F56 for the 3-door, F55 for the 5-door introduced in 2014) marks a major turning point in the model's history. Based on BMW's modular UKL platform, it definitively abandons powertrains shared with PSA (Prince gasoline engines and DV/N47 diesels) in favor of BMW's modular "B-Series" engine family (B37/B47 for diesel, B38/B48 for gasoline). This technical change is accompanied by a spectacular leap in reliability and finish quality. The model underwent two facelifts (LCI in 2018 with the arrival of Union Jack taillights and DKG dual-clutch gearboxes, then LCI 2 in 2021). Although demand for diesel significantly dropped towards the end of its career (gradual withdrawal from the catalog starting in 2019), the One D, Cooper D, and Cooper SD versions are among the most robust diesel city cars of their era, provided their road-going purpose is respected.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability dramatically improved compared to the R56 generation
  • Extremely robust B37 and B47 diesel engines (reliable timing chain)
  • Interior finish quality worthy of the premium segment
  • Driving pleasure preserved (go-kart feel)
  • Excellent automatic gearboxes (Aisin torque converter and DKG7)

Weaknesses 5

  • Problematic EGR system on diesels (cooler)
  • Undersized upper right engine mount (UKL platform common issue)
  • Very firm comfort, especially with wheels > 16 inches and Runflat tires
  • Limited rear passenger space and boot volume (even in the F55 5-door)
  • Cupping wear on rear tires (road noise)

Check before buying

  • Coolant level (Diesels): a drop often indicates an EGR cooler leak.
  • DPF history (Diesels): check ash level with diagnostic tool if the vehicle has been driven a lot in the city.
  • Upper right engine mount: open the hood, check the condition of the large silentblock at the top left (passenger side). If it is sagging or leaking, it needs to be replaced (approx. 250-300€).
  • Front wishbone bushings: tendency to crack around 80,000 km (clunking noises over bumps).
  • iDrive central screen: some cases of intermittent restarts or peeling screen (anti-reflective coating).

Official recalls

- EGR Cooler (2018, 2019, 2022): Major global recall for B37/B47 diesel engines (4 and 3 cylinders). Risk of coolant leakage into the EGR module, potentially creating flammable deposits. Replacement of the cooler and cleaning/replacement of the intake manifold.,- Crankshaft (2015): On 1.5L gasoline engines (B38) with manual gearbox produced between mid-2014 and mid-2015. Premature wear of the crankshaft thrust bearing. Replacement of the bearing or the complete engine.,- Spare Wheel (2014): On some models, the fixing nut for the spare wheel under the chassis could loosen.,- Airbags (2020): Some batches affected by defective airbag modules (independent of the main Takata scandal, but linked to specific suppliers).

Verdict

The MINI F55/F56 is the generation of redemption. By abandoning shared mechanics for 100% BMW engines, it becomes one of the most reliable premium city cars on the market. Regarding the diesel versions (One D, Cooper D, Cooper SD), the mechanical record is excellent: the engine block itself is indestructible. However, like any modern diesel, the emission control peripherals (EGR, DPF) require appropriate use. Buy a diesel only if you drive more than 20,000 km per year with a majority of highway driving. Absolutely ensure that the EGR cooler recall campaign has been carried out at a BMW/MINI dealership. If you drive in the city, avoid diesel and opt for the excellent B38/B48 gasoline engines.
