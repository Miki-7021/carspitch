Toyota Tundra

III (XK70) · 2021 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Toyota models

Summary

The third-generation Toyota Tundra (codename XK70) marks a radical departure in the history of Toyota's full-size pickup. Abandoning the legendary but thirsty naturally aspirated V8 (UR-FE) of the previous generation, it adopts the new TNGA-F global architecture (shared with the Land Cruiser 300 and the Sequoia) and switches exclusively to a 3.4L twin-turbo V6 engine (V35A-FTS), available in a classic combustion or hybrid version (i-FORCE MAX).

While the Tundra XK70 gains enormously in ride comfort (multi-link rear suspension with coil springs), onboard technology, and efficiency, its debut was marred by teething problems unusual for Toyota, culminating in a massive recall for engine failures linked to machining debris on the 2022 to 2024 model years. The overall reliability rating suffers temporarily as a result, although Toyota's after-sales service covers these major defects.

Strengths 5

  • Rigid TNGA-F chassis and multi-link rear suspension offering exceptional ride comfort for the category
  • Massive torque from the i-FORCE MAX hybrid version (790 Nm)
  • Interior finish quality significantly improved (especially on Platinum, 1794, and Capstone trims)
  • Highly responsive modern 14-inch infotainment system
  • Excellent historical resale value (although Gen III still needs to prove itself in the long term)

Weaknesses 5

  • Critical engine recall (machining debris) tarnishing the reliability reputation of the 2022-2024 models
  • Disappearance of the legendary and indestructible 5.7L V8
  • Payload and towing capacity slightly behind American competitors (F-150, Silverado)
  • Fuel consumption of the hybrid version remains high in real-world conditions (hybridization here serves performance, not economy)
  • Frequent electronic bugs on the 2022 model year (cameras, screens)

Check before buying

  • Recall history (CRUCIAL): Check via the VIN if the V35A engine is affected by the machining debris recall and if the block has been replaced.
  • Rear axle nuts: Ensure the tightening recall has been performed on 2022 models to prevent the axle shaft from detaching.
  • Fuel line: Check for rubbing against the metal brake lines (2022-2023 models).
  • Onboard electronics: Test the display of the rearview camera (PVM) and the digital dashboard, prone to software blackouts.
  • Water leaks: Check for moisture in the reversing lights (ongoing recall) and the sealing of the exterior camera housings.

Official recalls

Generation III (XK70) has been the subject of numerous early-life recalls (NHTSA / Rappel Conso data):

  • Engine (Machining debris) - [2024/2025/2026]: Massive recalls (e.g., 24V381000, 25V767000, 26V320000) on 2022-2024 Tundras equipped with the V35A engine. Machining debris can contaminate the engine and cause main bearing failure, leading to a stall or engine failure. Fix: Engine block replacement.
  • Transmission (Unexpected movement) - [Feb 2024]: (24V125000) On 2022-2024 models, the vehicle may move unexpectedly when in neutral. Software update required.
  • Fuel (Leak risk) - [Aug 2023]: (23V566000) On 2022-2023 models, a plastic fuel tube can rub against metal brake lines and become damaged, creating a fire risk.
  • Rear axle (Loosening) - [Jun 2022]: (22V445000) The flange nuts on the axle shaft sub-assemblies can loosen over time, risking axle separation.
  • Electronics / Cameras - [2022 to 2026]: Multiple recalls (e.g., 26V038000, 25V657000, 22V501000) regarding software errors preventing the display of the rearview camera (PVM) or the dashboard (23V111000).
  • Braking (Skid Control ECU) - [Sep 2022]: (22V661000) The skid control ECU can falsely detect an overcurrent, affecting braking assistance.
  • Lighting - [May 2025]: (25V322000) Moisture can penetrate the reversing lights and cause them to fail.
  • Tires - [Oct 2024]: (24V767000) Possible damage to the inner sidewall of the passenger-side front and rear tires.

Verdict

The third-generation Toyota Tundra (2021-2025) is an inherently excellent pickup, offering superior ride comfort, cutting-edge technology, and highly capable twin-turbo V6 engines. However, it does not (yet) benefit from the bulletproof reliability of its V8-powered predecessor. The major recall concerning machining debris in the V35A engine block (which can lead to outright failure) on the 2022 to 2024 model years dictates the utmost caution when buying used. If you are targeting a model from these years, demand proof that the recall has been performed (and the engine replaced if necessary). Models produced from late 2024 / 2025 are corrected at the factory and represent a much safer purchase.
