I phase 2 (BA/DA) · 1999 – 2002

Summary

The Renault Mégane I phase 2 (launched in spring 1999) marks a major evolution for the diamond-badged compact car. Aesthetically, it adopts a more expressive front end, new lights, and a modified grille. Technically, it takes a leap forward in safety (ABS and 4 airbags as standard) and thoroughly renews its engine range. It is with this generation that Renault introduces its 16-valve gasoline engines (1.4, 1.6) and, above all, makes its diesel transition: from the rustic atmospheric 1.9 D to the modern common rail 1.9 dCi (appeared in 2000), via the direct injection 1.9 dTi. Today, it is a very low-cost used vehicle (a future youngtimer for coupes/cabriolets), whose viability depends on its maintenance history, particularly for the diesel engines targeted here.

**Overall generation rating: 7/10** (Excellent value for money on the used market, marred by recurring electrical issues).

Strengths 6

  • Excellent suspension comfort, typical of Renaults of this era.
  • Safe and secure road handling (ABS and 4 airbags standard on phase 2).
  • Today's maintenance and insurance costs are negligible.
  • Massive availability of spare parts (new and from scrapyards).
  • Generous boot volume (especially on Classic/4-door and Break/Estate versions).
  • Very low fuel consumption on dTi and dCi engines.

Weaknesses 5

  • Chronic electrical problems (immobilizer, power windows, central locking).
  • Poor sound insulation on diesels (especially 1.9 D and 1.9 dTi).
  • Poor quality interior plastics that age badly (sticky, scratches).
  • Fragility of the rear axle (torsion bars that sag or break).
  • Random reliability of the first-generation 1.9 dCi.

Check before buying

  • **Immobilizer:** Check that the red light on the dashboard turns off with all keys. UCH (Cabin Control Unit) or key failures are common and immobilize the car.
  • **Timing belt (dTi):** Demand the invoice proving the recent replacement of the timing belt AND the accessory belt with a freewheel pulley.
  • **Rear axle:** Look at the car from the side on a flat surface. If the rear appears saggy or if the wheels have excessive negative camber (Gordini style), the rear axle is dead.
  • **EGR valve and Turbo (dCi):** Test drive the car under load. Jerks, loss of power, or thick black smoke indicate a clogged EGR valve or a tired turbo.
  • **Electrical equipment:** Absolutely test all power windows, ventilation (heater resistor often burns out), and central locking.

Verdict

The Renault Mégane I phase 2 is today a sensible choice for ultra-tight budgets. If you are looking for a diesel, the **1.9 dTi (98 hp)** represents the best compromise between performance, fuel consumption, and reliability, **on the strict condition** that the accessory belt problem has been definitively resolved by fitting a freewheel pulley. The 1.9 D is indestructible but anemic, best reserved for short trips. The 1.9 dCi, although more modern and pleasant, is too temperamental (EGR, injection) to be recommended blindly at this mileage/age. Beware of immobilizer failures which often send these cars to the scrapyard prematurely.
