Summary

The Toyota Camry V (XV30), produced from 2001 to 2006, is widely regarded as one of the most reliable and comfortable mid-size sedans ever built. Designed with a focus on refinement, cabin space, and ride comfort, it earned a reputation as a 'refrigerator on wheels'—meaning it is incredibly dependable, albeit unexciting to drive. It was offered exclusively with petrol engines globally, as Toyota did not introduce a hybrid version until the subsequent XV40 generation, and diesels were not part of the Camry's target market strategy. A mid-cycle facelift was introduced in 2004, bringing updated headlights, taillights, a revised grille, new gauges, and a 5-speed automatic transmission for the 2.4L engine. Overall, it remains a highly sought-after used car for buyers prioritizing longevity and low running costs.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional long-term durability (many reach 300,000+ miles with basic care)
  • Incredibly comfortable and soft ride quality
  • Spacious cabin and large trunk
  • Whisper-quiet interior, especially with V6 engines
  • High availability of cheap spare parts globally

Weaknesses 5

  • Bland, unengaging driving dynamics (heavy body roll)
  • Severe engine block thread issue on early 2.4L (2AZ-FE) engines
  • Oil sludge risk on 3.0L V6 (1MZ-FE) if poorly maintained
  • Spongy brake pedal feel (characteristic of the model)
  • Interior materials (plastics and cloth) can show wear and tear over time

Check before buying

  • Coolant level and signs of overheating (crucial for 2.4L 2AZ-FE)
  • Service history for oil changes (crucial for 3.0L 1MZ-FE to avoid sludge)
  • Steering intermediate shaft (listen for a 'clunk' or 'pop' when turning the wheel at low speeds)
  • Valve cover gaskets and power steering pump for fluid leaks
  • Timing belt replacement history (only applies to 3.0L and 3.3L V6 engines; 4-cylinders use chains)

Official recalls

  • NHTSA Campaign 04V528000 (2004): Curtain shield airbags may deploy improperly due to twisted fabric during assembly.
  • NHTSA Campaign 01V354000 (2001): Steering wheel may be loose due to an improperly tightened steering wheel set nut.
  • Floor Mat Entrapment (Various dates): Unsecured floor mats could trap the accelerator pedal, leading to unintended acceleration (affected multiple Toyota models including XV30).
  • Technical Service Bulletin (TSB) - Steering Clunk: Not a safety recall, but a widespread TSB was issued to re-grease or replace the steering intermediate shaft due to a clunking noise.

Verdict

The Toyota Camry XV30 is a legendary vehicle in the used car market, offering unmatched comfort and longevity for its price point. However, its reputation for being 'bulletproof' comes with two major caveats for this specific generation: the 2.4L head bolt thread issue (mostly 2002-2003) and the 3.0L V6 oil sludge issue. If you are buying a 2.4L, aim for a 2005-2006 facelift model where the block threads were improved. If buying a V6, demand comprehensive service records proving regular oil changes. Avoid neglected examples, but a well-maintained XV30 is one of the smartest, most economical used car purchases you can make.
