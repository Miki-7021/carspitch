Toyota GT86

I (ZN6) · 2012 – 2021

BUY Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The Toyota GT86 (codename ZN6), developed in partnership with Subaru (which markets its twin, the BRZ), marks Toyota's return to the segment of lightweight, rear-wheel-drive sports coupes. A spiritual successor to the legendary Corolla AE86, it prioritizes driving pleasure, chassis balance, and an ultra-low center of gravity over pure power. Equipped with a naturally aspirated Boxer (flat) engine featuring Toyota's D-4S dual injection, it underwent a facelift (Phase 2) in late 2016 / early 2017, bringing chassis improvements, a slight engine revision, and aesthetic touches. It's a purist's car, very reliable if meticulously maintained, but requires particular attention for the very first model years.

Weaknesses 5

  • Pronounced torque dip between 3000 and 4500 rpm (the famous "Torque Dip")
  • Interior plastic quality and sound insulation could be improved
  • Symbolic rear seats (reserved for children or luggage)
  • Spark plug replacement is labor-intensive and costly
  • Fragile paint (prone to stone chips)

Check before buying

  • History of valve spring recall (2012-2013 models): improper application of sealant (RTV) by dealerships during the recall caused engine failures (clogged oil pickup screen).
  • Clutch release bearing on manual gearboxes: listen for a whistling or grinding sound at idle with the pedal released. Replace preventively around 60,000 - 80,000 km.
  • Gearbox synchros (manual): 2nd gear can be stiff when cold. High-quality gearbox oil (e.g., Motul Gear 300) mitigates the issue.
  • Oil seepage at the timing cover (known but minor sealing issue).
  • Signs of intensive track use: check the condition of bushings, shock absorbers, and for brake overheating.

What sets it apart 12

Performance

  • Exceptional Road Handling and Pure Driving Pleasure — The GT86's balanced chassis, precise steering, and ultra-low center of gravity offer outstanding agility and responsiveness. Every journey becomes a rewarding driving experience, with playful and predictable handling, ideal for purists.
  • Powerful and Enduring Braking — Equipped with an efficient braking system, the GT86 offers a firm and progressive pedal, guaranteeing very short stopping distances and perfect control in all circumstances.

Safety

  • High-Level Safety — The Toyota GT86 ensures optimal protection for its occupants, as evidenced by its excellent results in European crash tests.

Reliability

  • Proven Reliability and Innovative Engine — Renowned for its robustness, especially on post-2014 models, the GT86 features an atmospheric Boxer engine with D-4S dual injection, a technology that ensures optimal combustion and prevents valve carbon buildup.

Practicality

  • Unlimited Customization Potential — With a vast catalog of aftermarket parts, the GT86 is the perfect canvas for enthusiasts looking to personalize and optimize their vehicle according to their preferences, whether for aesthetics or performance.

Economy

  • Controlled Maintenance Costs for a Sports Car — Despite its sporty character and performance, the Toyota GT86 stands out for its reasonable maintenance costs, making it accessible to thrill-seekers for daily use.

Alternatives

- **Europe & North Amer.** : Mazda MX-5 (ND) (lighter, convertible, less practical), Nissan 370Z (more powerful, V6, but heavier and more expensive to maintain), Audi TT (front-wheel drive or Quattro, less purist), BMW 2 Series (RWD Coupe, more upscale). - **Japan / Asia** : Subaru BRZ (the technical twin, very slightly different suspension settings), Honda S2000 (as an older used car).

Verdict

The Toyota GT86 is a future collector's item and one of the best modern driving schools. It is a highly recommended purchase for car enthusiasts seeking pure sensations at legal speeds. **However, extreme caution is advised for 2012-2013 models**: if the valve spring recall has been performed, demand proof that the oil pickup screen was subsequently checked. The ideal is to target a model produced after 2014, or better yet, a Phase 2 (2017+) which corrects early issues, improves finish, and slightly smooths out the torque dip.
