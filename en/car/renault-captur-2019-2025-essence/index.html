Summary

The **Renault Captur II** marks a major qualitative leap compared to the first generation. Based on the modular CMF-B platform (shared with the Clio V), it corrects the main flaws of its predecessor: significantly improved interior finish, better sound insulation, more rigorous road handling, and optimized habitability.

Positioned in the ultra-competitive B-SUV segment, it quickly established itself as one of the references in the European market. A significant **facelift (Phase 2)** was carried out in spring 2024, introducing the brand's new visual identity (vertical front fascia, disappearance of 'C' shaped lights) and an update of the multimedia system to OpenR Link (integrated Google).

In terms of reliability, the Captur II particularly shines with its petrol engines (1.0 TCe and 1.3 TCe), which have definitively erased the setbacks of the old 1.2 TCe. The E-Tech versions (hybrids) had capricious beginnings in terms of software, but have been made more reliable over the model years.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable reliability of petrol engines (1.0 TCe and 1.3 TCe)
  • Very relevant and economical LPG version (ECO-G)
  • Interior presentation and finish quality (especially in Intens/Techno/Esprit Alpine trims)
  • Sliding rear bench seat as standard (excellent modularity)
  • Sound road handling and suspension comfort
  • Very responsive OpenR Link multimedia system (on Phase 2)

Weaknesses 5

  • Frequent electronic bugs on early model years (2019-2021): black screens, dashboard resets
  • Random reliability of early E-Tech versions (dog-clutch gearbox)
  • Problematic AdBlue system on rare diesel versions
  • Firmness of suspensions at low speed with 18-inch wheels
  • Catchy manual gearbox on the 1.0 TCe

Check before buying

  • History of software updates (EasyLink multimedia system and engine/gearbox ECUs)
  • Behavior of the EDC7 gearbox during a test drive (absence of juddering on startup or jerks between 1st and 2nd gear)
  • Air conditioning operation (cases of noisy or faulty compressors on 2019-2020 models)
  • Body panel alignment (tailgate sometimes poorly adjusted at the factory on early models)
  • On E-Tech versions: fluidity of thermal/electric transitions

Official recalls

  • - **November 2023**: Fire risk. An internal short circuit of the 12V battery may occur. Concerns models manufactured between mid-2022 and mid-2023.
  • - **May 2022**: Seat belts. Possible incorrect locking of the right rear seat belt. Models produced late 2021.
  • - **December 2021**: E-Tech. Reprogramming of the hybrid control unit to prevent power loss or an unexpected system shutdown.
  • - **July 2020**: Power steering. Risk of loss of assistance due to rubbing of the electrical harness. Models produced between late 2019 and early 2020.
  • - **June 2020**: Sunroof. Risk of the sunroof deflector detaching while driving.

Verdict

The Renault Captur II is an **excellent choice for both used and new cars**, provided you target the right engines. By focusing on petrol, Renault has succeeded in its gamble: the 1.0 TCe and 1.3 TCe units are among the most reliable in their category, offering complete peace of mind compared to some direct competitors (notably Stellantis' 1.2 PureTech). The LPG (ECO-G) version is a boon for purchasing power.

The E-Tech (hybrid) versions are appealing on paper but require caution: prefer a post-2022 model to avoid the electronic and mechanical teething problems of the early days. Diesel is to be avoided except for very high mileage motorway drivers, due to the fragilities of the AdBlue system. In summary: go for a 1.3 TCe 140 hp or a 1.0 TCe LPG.
