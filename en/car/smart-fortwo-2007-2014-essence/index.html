Smart Fortwo

II (451) · 2007 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 58/100 — moyen

All Smart models

Summary

The second-generation Smart Fortwo (Type 451) was launched in 2007 to succeed the pioneering 450. Longer by 19 cm to meet crash-test standards (particularly American ones) and improve comfort, it retains its famous exposed *Tridion* safety cell. Mechanically, it abandons the fragile Mercedes turbocharged engines of the previous generation in favor of naturally aspirated and turbocharged 1.0L 3-cylinder blocks of Mitsubishi origin (3B2 family). While the engine itself is robust, this generation is plagued by two major flaws: a slow *Softouch* (Getrag) automated gearbox prone to actuator failures, and above all the *mhd* (Micro Hybrid Drive - Stop&Start) system, whose faulty design can lead to outright engine failure. Official statistics (TÜV, ADAC, What Car?) rank it among the least reliable vehicles in its class.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable compactness (2.69 m) and exceptional turning radius
  • Mitsubishi petrol engines (excluding mhd) inherently very robust (chain-driven)
  • Astonishing passive safety for its size (Tridion cell)
  • 1.0 Turbo (84 hp) version very versatile and free from the mhd flaw
  • Low depreciation, high demand in the urban used market

Weaknesses 5

  • Softouch automated gearbox: exasperating slowness, jerkiness, and frequent actuator failures
  • Disastrous reliability of the mhd system (engine failure due to belt breakage/overheating)
  • Very high TÜV defect rate at 3 years: 21.5% (among the worst in the segment)
  • Very firm comfort (stiff suspension) and poor soundproofing
  • Cost of maintenance and spare parts (Mercedes-Benz network)

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history of the mhd system (belt, tensioner pulley, alignment shims) on 71 hp versions post-2008
  • Gearbox behavior: gears should shift without loud clunking or excessive slipping
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (headlights, wipers, gauges) to rule out a SAM module failure
  • Condition of the front suspension springs (tendency to break on potholes)
  • On cdi versions: check engine light on (EGR/DPF) and exhaust smoke

Official recalls

  • [2018-04-27] Exhaust / Fire (NHTSA 18V273000): Recall in the United States of 2008-2009 models (Coupe and Cabrio). The rear insulation mat in the engine compartment can deform, deteriorate, and detach over time, touching the exhaust and creating a fire risk.
  • [2008-08-07] Structure / Paint (NHTSA 08V390000): Recall of a specific batch of 43 vehicles (MY 2008) in the United States following the use of a non-compliant batch of paint on the structure, requiring factory rework.
  • [2021-07-23 / 2021-08-27] Windshield and Roof (Rappel Conso France): *Note: Primarily concerns the following generation (453 Series / EQ)*. Risk of the windshield or panoramic roof detaching due to a failing adhesive bond over time. Risk of injury in the event of an accident.

Safety

  • Robust Tridion Safety Cell — Featuring its renowned Tridion safety cell, the Smart Fortwo offers surprising passive protection for its size, as evidenced by its excellent crash-test score.
  • Effective Braking and Electronic Stability — The braking system with standard ABS is perfectly suited for urban use. The integration of ESP significantly enhances dynamic safety and vehicle control in all circumstances.

Reliability

  • Robust Mitsubishi Gasoline Engines — The 1.0L Mitsubishi-sourced gasoline engines (excluding mhd versions) are recognized for their inherent robustness and chain timing, ensuring lasting mechanical reliability.

Performance

  • Driving Pleasure with the 1.0 Turbo — The 84 hp 1.0 Turbo version offers remarkable versatility and brisk acceleration, ideal for city driving and suburban excursions, while benefiting from proven mechanics.

Economy

  • Low Depreciation and High Demand — The Smart Fortwo benefits from low depreciation and high demand in the urban used car market, making it a wise and easy-to-resell investment.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Toyota iQ (more reliable, 4 seats, smoother CVT gearbox), Volkswagen Up! / Skoda Citigo / Seat Mii (larger but infinitely more reliable and refined), Renault Twingo II, Fiat 500.
  • North America: Scion iQ, Fiat 500, Chevrolet Spark.
  • Japan: Local Kei-cars (Honda N-Box, Suzuki Hustler) which offer more space for a similar footprint.

Verdict

The Smart Fortwo II (451) is a vehicle of contrasts. While its format makes it irreplaceable in the city center, its overall reliability record is poor, penalized by an ADAC breakdown rate of 4.1/1000 and a What Car? score of 2/5 (35% breakdown rate over 12 months). The automated gearbox is a constant source of frustration, both for its drivability and its fragility. The absolute critical point is the 1.0 mhd (71 hp) engine: its poorly designed Stop&Start system leads to fatal belt breakages for the engine.

Buying advice: Flee from the *mhd* versions unless the seller provides recent invoices proving the complete update of the system (shims, shaft, reinforced belt). Unhesitatingly favor the 84 hp 1.0 Turbo version (which does not have the mhd system) or the very first 71 hp models from 2007/early 2008 (before the introduction of the mhd).
