Audi A4

B7 (8E) · 2004 – 2008

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The Audi A4 B7 (Type 8E for the sedan/Avant wagon, 8H for the convertible) is not an entirely new generation, but a significant facelift of the B6 (PL46 platform). It introduced the *Singleframe* grille, recalibrated steering, revised suspensions, and new engines (introduction of FSI/TFSI blocks for petrol and evolution of TDIs). Although its interior finish quality remains an absolute benchmark of its time, this generation is marked by a complex technological transition, particularly concerning the 2.0 TDI diesel engines with unit injectors, which severely tarnished the brand's reliability reputation. The overall reliability score is highly polarized: excellent for older engines (1.9 TDI, 1.8T), very risky for newer ones (2.0 TDI, V6 FSI), and catastrophic with the Multitronic continuously variable automatic gearbox.

Strengths 5

  • Interior finish and material quality (ages exceptionally well)
  • Unshakeable reliability of the 1.9 TDI 115 hp engine
  • Efficiency and safety of the Quattro all-wheel drive (Torsen)
  • Pleasure and quietness of the V6 TDI (Common Rail) engines
  • Corrosion resistance (galvanized body)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 2.0 TDI engines (oil pump, cylinder head, injectors)
  • Multitronic automatic gearbox (on front-wheel drive models) extremely fragile and expensive to replace
  • Prohibitive maintenance cost for V6 and V8 engines (timing chain at the rear of the engine)
  • Average rear passenger space and boot volume for the category
  • Premature wear of front suspension arms (complex multi-link setup)

Check before buying

  • **Oil pump history (2.0 TDI)**: Demand an invoice proving the modification or replacement of the hexagonal shaft.
  • **Gearbox**: If automatic, identify the type. Tiptronic (Quattro) = reliable if serviced. Multitronic (Front-wheel drive) = danger, test for a complete absence of jerks on startup and in reverse.
  • **Windscreen scuttle (Plenum)**: Check that the water drainage holes under the battery are not blocked by leaves. Water accumulates and floods the brake booster (Mastervac) or the passenger compartment.
  • **Timing chain noises (V6/V8)**: Cold start is imperative with the bonnet open. A persistent metallic knocking means the timing chain needs to be redone (very expensive).
  • **Front axle**: Listen for knocking noises on degraded roads (upper suspension arm bushings often faulty).

Official recalls

  • - **Airbags (Takata)**: Worldwide recall for the replacement of front airbag gas generators at risk of exploding and projecting metal fragments.
  • - **Injectors (2.0 TDI 170 hp - Action 23K1)**: Replacement of piezoelectric unit injectors prone to short circuits.
  • - **Ignition coils (Petrol engines - Action 28E9)**: Replacement of defective coils causing misfires.
  • - **Brake assistance (2.0 TDI)**: Possible leak on the vacuum pump hose leading to a loss of brake assistance.
  • - **Flywheel (2.0 TDI - 2006)**: Replacement of the dual-mass flywheel (LUK) at risk of disintegrating and causing a fire.

Verdict

The Audi A4 B7 is a car with two faces. If you're looking for an economical and indestructible diesel, the **1.9 TDI 115 hp with a manual gearbox is a golden choice**. Conversely, the very common **2.0 TDI (140 and 170 hp) are true nests of trouble** (oil pump, cylinder head, injectors) and should be avoided unless you have a service history as thick as a phone book proving that all chronic defects have been corrected. The V6 TDIs offer royal driving pleasure but require a premium maintenance budget (beware of the chains). In petrol, the 1.8T remains the safest compromise. Absolute golden rule for this generation: **flee the Multitronic automatic gearbox**, prefer the manual gearbox or the Tiptronic (only associated with Quattro).
