Summary

The first-generation MG ZS (phase 2 / facelift) has established itself as one of the most competitive urban SUVs on the market thanks to a very aggressive pricing policy and a reassuring 7-year or 150,000 km manufacturer's warranty. While the 100% electric version (ZS EV) served as the technological spearhead for the Sino-British brand (SAIC Motor group), the thermal (petrol) versions attracted customers looking for a family-friendly, spacious, and economical vehicle to buy. The 2021 facelift smoothed out the somewhat bland features of phase 1, bringing a modernized hexagonal grille, Full LED headlights, and a revised interior with soft-touch materials and a 10.1-inch touchscreen. Although it doesn't shine in terms of road dynamics compared to European benchmarks (Peugeot 2008, Renault Captur), it offers an almost unbeatable price/space/equipment ratio, positioning itself as a direct rival to the Dacia Duster.

Expert's overall rating: 7.5/10 - A pragmatic and rational choice, secured by a long warranty, despite somewhat thirsty older-generation powertrains.

Strengths 6

  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the new and used car market
  • 7-year or 150,000 km manufacturer's warranty (transferable)
  • Generous rear passenger space and boot volume (448 liters)
  • Interior presentation significantly improved since the facelift (materials, design)
  • Overall very reassuring mechanical reliability (especially the 1.5 naturally aspirated)
  • Smooth and proven Aisin automatic gearbox (on 1.0 T-GDI)

Weaknesses 6

  • Fuel consumption higher than the category average (especially the 1.0 T-GDI)
  • Slow infotainment system, confusing interface, and frequent bugs (Apple CarPlay/Android Auto disconnecting)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (wind and road noise)
  • Clumsy road handling, steering lacking feel
  • Paint quality (thin clear coat, susceptible to micro-scratches and stone chips)
  • Dealership network still developing in some regions

Check before buying

  • Full functionality of the touchscreen (extreme slowness, crashes requiring a restart).
  • Service book: essential to retain the benefit of the 7-year warranty.
  • Condition of interior plastics (scratch easily, especially in the boot and lower door panels).
  • Body panel alignment (factory quality control sometimes inconsistent).
  • Wear of front tires (tendency to understeer causing premature tire wear).
  • Parasitic noises (squeaks/rattles) from the dashboard on degraded roads.

Official recalls

The thermal MG ZS phase 2 has had very few major recalls in Europe, as the mechanics were already proven on phase 1.

  • 2022 (United Kingdom / Asia) - Electrical harness: Risk of an electrical harness rubbing near the steering column, potentially causing a short circuit. Inspection and installation of a protective sleeve.
  • 2023 (Europe) - Software / Multimedia: Service campaign (non-safety related) to update the infotainment module to correct intermittent restarts and loss of Bluetooth connection.

Verdict

The petrol MG ZS (2021-2024) is an eminently pragmatic purchase. It is not aimed at driving enthusiasts, but at families looking for a spacious, well-equipped, and long-guaranteed vehicle, for the price of a used city car.

Which engine to choose? If your budget is tight and you mainly drive in the city or on secondary roads, the 1.5 VTi-Tech is a rock of reliability, albeit anemic. If you are looking for more versatility and the comfort of an automatic gearbox, the 1.0 T-GDI is the best compromise, provided you accept fuel consumption around 7 to 8 L/100 km. Buying a recent used model is very reassuring thanks to the 7-year warranty, but demand a clear service history within the MG network to avoid any unpleasant surprises if you need to claim under warranty.
