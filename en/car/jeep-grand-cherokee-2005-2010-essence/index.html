Jeep Grand Cherokee

III (WK) · 2005 – 2010

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The third-generation Jeep Grand Cherokee (codename WK) marks a turning point for the model by abandoning the solid front axle in favor of independent suspension, greatly improving road handling. Developed under the DaimlerChrysler era, it shares certain components with Mercedes-Benz (notably the NAG1 gearbox and the OM642 diesel V6). While its off-road capabilities remain exceptional thanks to advanced AWD systems (Quadra-Trac II and Quadra-Drive II with ELSD differentials), this generation suffered in its early days from a disappointing interior finish (hard plastics) which was corrected during the 2008 facelift. Data from ADAC and TÜV Report highlight generally very robust mechanics (especially the V8 blocks and the 5-speed gearbox), but reliability heavily penalized by temperamental electronics and numerous recalls.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional off-road capabilities (Quadra-Drive II system with 3 electronic limited-slip differentials)
  • Mechanical robustness of the V8 engines (4.7L and 5.7L Hemi) and the NAG1 automatic gearbox
  • High towing capacity (up to 3,500 kg with the V8s and the CRD)
  • Greatly improved road handling compared to the previous generation (WJ)
  • Performance and exclusivity of the SRT8 version (6.1L)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very temperamental onboard electronics (WIN, TIPM modules, various sensors)
  • Very poor quality of plastics and interior assembly before the 2008 facelift
  • Gargantuan fuel consumption on petrol engines (especially in the city)
  • Disappointing rear seat space and trunk volume compared to the exterior dimensions
  • Numerous manufacturer recalls (NHTSA) indicating a rushed launch

Check before buying

  • Transfer case operation: it is imperative to test shifting into 4-Low (N23 recall often poorly applied causing the actuator to lock up).
  • Onboard electronics: check the operation of all windows, the air conditioning (fragile blend doors), and the absence of warning lights on the dashboard.
  • Engine noise on V8s (Hemi): listen when cold to detect ticking (broken exhaust studs or worn lifters).
  • Oil leaks on the 3.0 CRD: inspect the engine V (under the turbo) to detect an oil cooler leak.
  • Condition of the front axle: wear of the control arm bushings and ball joints, particularly stressed by the weight of the vehicle.

What sets it apart 14

Performance

  • Legendary and Robust V8 Engines — Enjoy the power and renowned reliability of the V8 petrol engines (4.7L and 5.7L Hemi), coupled with a NAG1 automatic transmission of proven robustness, guaranteeing hundreds of thousands of kilometers of driving pleasure.
  • SRT8 Exclusivity and Power — For thrill-seekers, the SRT8 version, with its 426 hp 6.1L Hemi engine, offers exceptional performance and rare exclusivity, transforming this SUV into a true muscle car.

Practicality

  • Unrivaled Off-Road Capabilities with Quadra-Drive II — The Grand Cherokee WK excels off the beaten path thanks to its advanced Quadra-Drive II all-wheel-drive system, featuring three electronic limited-slip differentials, allowing you to tackle the most demanding terrains with absolute confidence.
  • Exceptional Towing Capacity — With a towing capacity of up to 3,500 kg on V8 versions, the Grand Cherokee WK is the ideal partner for towing your caravan, boat, or any other heavy equipment with ease and safety.

Safety

  • Optimal Protection for All Occupants — This vehicle was designed for your peace of mind, achieving a 5-star rating from NHTSA for frontal and side impacts, as well as an 'Good' rating from IIHS, ensuring a high level of safety for the driver and passengers.
  • Effective and Controlled Braking — Equipped as standard with the Anti-lock Braking System (ABS), the Grand Cherokee WK offers adequate stopping distances for its class, generally around 40 to 43 meters from 96 km/h, ensuring optimal control in all circumstances.

Comfort

  • Improved Road Comfort and Stability — Benefit from greatly improved road handling compared to the previous generation, offering a comfortable and stable ride, especially on highways, thanks to its independent front suspension and available Electronic Stability Program (ESP).

Alternatives

The Jeep Grand Cherokee WK competed in a highly competitive segment:

* **Europe:** Volkswagen Touareg (superior finish, high-performance but complex TDI engines), Land Rover Discovery 3 (better interior space, excellent off-road capabilities, but equally hit-and-miss electronic reliability), Volvo XC90 (more family-oriented, 7 seats). * **Japan/Asia:** Toyota Land Cruiser Prado / 120 (legendary reliability, king of the desert, but clumsy road handling), Mitsubishi Pajero. * **North America:** Ford Explorer (more family-oriented, less capable off-road), Chevrolet TrailBlazer, Nissan Pathfinder.

Verdict

The Jeep Grand Cherokee WK (2005-2010) is an automotive paradox. Mechanically, its V8 engines (notably the 5.7 Hemi) and its NAG1 automatic gearbox are incredibly robust, capable of enduring hundreds of thousands of kilometers. Its off-road capabilities with Quadra-Drive II remain stunning. However, it is weighed down by extremely temperamental DaimlerChrysler-era electronics (WIN, TIPM modules, sensors) and an interior finish unworthy of its status before 2008.

**Buying advice:** Only to be considered if you are looking for a true V8 4x4 at a bargain price for towing or off-roading. It is imperative to favor a facelifted (2008-2010) V8 model (305 hp 4.7L or 5.7L Hemi), with a clear history and where recalls (especially the N23 and the ignition switch) have been properly handled. Avoid models displaying a Christmas tree of warning lights on the dashboard.
