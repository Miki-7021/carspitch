Honda NSX

I (NA1/NA2) · 1990 – 2005

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The first-generation Honda NSX (sold under the Acura brand in North America) (NA1/NA2) is a true icon of automotive history. Launched in 1990, it revolutionized the supercar hierarchy by proving it was possible to combine very high-level performance with the reliability and ergonomics of an everyday sedan. Developed with the help of Ayrton Senna, it is the world's first production car to use an all-aluminum monocoque chassis.

**Evolution:** - **NA1 (1990-1996):** 3.0L V6 engine (C30A), 5-speed manual or 4-speed automatic gearbox, pop-up headlights. - **NA2 (1997-2001):** Introduction of the 3.2L V6 (C32B) coupled with a 6-speed manual gearbox. Automatic versions retained the 3.0L. Improved brakes and clutch. - **Facelift (2002-2005):** Pop-up headlights replaced by fixed headlights (aerodynamics and pedestrian safety standards), revised suspension and wider tires.

Today, it is a highly sought-after collector's item, renowned as the 'everyday supercar'.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional mechanical reliability for a supercar
  • Aluminum monocoque chassis (no structural rust)
  • Perfect ergonomics and peripheral visibility (F-16 inspired)
  • VTEC engine with captivating character and metallic sound
  • Reasonable running maintenance cost compared to Ferrari or Porsche
  • Non-existent depreciation (high-value collector's vehicle)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very rapid rear tire wear (aggressive factory geometry with a lot of toe-in)
  • Age-related electronic issues (CCU capacitors, Bose amplifiers)
  • Cost of aluminum body parts (very expensive and difficult to repair)
  • Original braking a bit inadequate for intensive use (especially on NA1)
  • Very low ground clearance (watch out for curbs and speed bumps with the front spoiler)

Check before buying

  • Serial number of the 5-speed manual gearbox (risk of Snap Ring failure on 91-92 models).
  • History of timing belt and water pump replacement (costly operation due to mid-mounted transverse engine).
  • Condition of the 23 hoses in the cooling system (risk of leaks and overheating).
  • Air conditioning function (the CCU module is often faulty).
  • ABS function (the ALB light should not remain on).
  • Alignment of aluminum body panels (a poorly done repair after an accident is a deal-breaker).
  • Oil leaks at the VTEC solenoid gaskets and camshaft O-rings.

Official recalls

  • - **Seat belts (1995):** Recall on certain early 90s models for a defect in the seat belt retractor (Takata supplier, unrelated to the recent airbag scandal). - **Steering box (1990-1991):** Replacement of a gear in the steering rack on a small series of pre-production and first-year models.

What sets it apart 14

Reliability

  • Legendary Mechanical Reliability and Structural Durability — The Honda NSX is renowned for its exceptional mechanical reliability, surpassing many supercars of its era. Its full aluminum monocoque chassis ensures unparalleled structural durability and complete absence of rust, guaranteeing remarkable longevity.

Performance

  • Captivating VTEC Engine and Supercar Performance — Powered by a V6 VTEC engine with a unique character and metallic sound, the NSX delivers very high-level performance. The 3.2L VTEC (C32B) paired with the 6-speed manual gearbox represents the pinnacle of sports engineering, providing optimized power and torque for an exhilarating driving experience.

Handling

  • Exceptional Dynamic Behavior and Surgical Precision — Developed with the help of Ayrton Senna, the NSX benefits from an aluminum monocoque chassis and a mid-engine layout, offering perfect balance and surgically precise steering. Its handling is predictable and communicative, allowing for confident high-speed driving.

Braking

  • Powerful, Enduring, and Controllable Braking — Equipped with ventilated discs on all four wheels and a 4-channel ABS system (standardized from 1991), the NSX ensures powerful, enduring, and highly controllable braking performance, even under intensive use. Its braking distance was competitive for a sports car of its caliber.

Safety

  • Safe Driving Experience for a Supercar — Thanks to its exceptional road holding and ease of driving at the limit, the NSX offers a very safe driving experience. Its predictability and communication with the driver inspire confidence, even at high speeds.

Economy

  • A Collecting Investment with Growing Value — The Honda NSX is a collector's vehicle with no depreciation. Its value is constantly appreciating, making it a wise and exciting automotive investment, with reasonable running maintenance costs compared to its European competitors.

Practicality

  • Perfect Ergonomics and Everyday Usable Supercar — Inspired by an F-16 cockpit, the NSX's ergonomics are perfect, offering exceptional peripheral visibility. It is renowned as the "everyday supercar," combining extreme performance with user comfort for surprising practicality.

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Porsche 911 (Type 964, 993, 996), Ferrari 348, Ferrari F355, Lotus Esprit V8. - **Japan / Asia:** Nissan Skyline GT-R (R32, R33, R34), Toyota Supra Mk4 (A80), Mazda RX-7 (FD3S). - **North America:** Chevrolet Corvette C4 ZR-1, Chevrolet Corvette C5 Z06, Dodge Viper RT/10.

Verdict

The first-generation Honda NSX is the Holy Grail of reliable supercars. It offers a pure analog driving experience, enhanced by a legendary V6 VTEC and a chassis of surgical precision. While the mechanics are virtually indestructible with good maintenance, it's important to remember that the car is over 20 to 30 years old: rubber components (hoses) and electronics (capacitors) require an overhaul. Prioritize a model with a clear history, even if it means paying a premium. NA2 models (3.2L 6-speed manual) are the most refined, but NA1s offer the original experience with the charm of pop-up headlights.
