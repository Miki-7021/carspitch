Hyundai ix35

I facelift (LM) · 2013 – 2015

BUY Reliability 85/100 — excellent

All Hyundai models

Summary

The Hyundai ix35 (known as the Tucson in North America and other markets) received a facelift in late 2013 for its phase 2 (LM generation). This refresh brought LED daytime running lights, new taillights, higher quality interior materials, an updated multimedia system, and, most importantly, improvements to the suspension and steering to correct the handling, which was considered too vague on phase 1. Mechanically, the diesel engines (which represent the overwhelming majority of sales in Europe) were carried over with slight optimizations, while the 2.0 petrol block was replaced by the new 'Nu' generation with direct injection (GDI) in certain markets. It is a pragmatic compact SUV, very reliable in its diesel version, which largely contributed to establishing Hyundai's reputation in Europe thanks to its 5-year warranty (now expired). Overall rating: An excellent used choice if maintenance has been kept up, particularly with the CRDi engine.

Strengths 5

  • Remarkable reliability of the diesel engines (1.7 and 2.0 CRDi)
  • Very advantageous price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • Generous interior space and trunk volume (465 liters)
  • Simple and intuitive control ergonomics
  • Improved handling and soundproofing on this phase 2

Weaknesses 5

  • Quality of some interior plastics (prone to scratching)
  • Slightly high fuel consumption on the 2.0 CRDi 184 hp with automatic gearbox/AWD
  • Electric power steering lacking feel (despite the Flex Steer mode)
  • Damping still a bit firm at low speeds on rough roads
  • Major risks on the 2.0 and 2.4 petrol blocks (North American and Asian markets)

Check before buying

  • History of the DPF and EGR valve on diesels (check engine light, jerking under acceleration)
  • Clunking noise in the steering column when stationary (worn steering flex disc)
  • Clutch operation and absence of vibrations at idle on the 1.7 CRDi (flywheel)
  • Maintenance of the all-wheel drive (AWD): fluid change for the transfer case and rear axle
  • Operation of the reversing camera and touchscreen (some bugs reported)
  • Condition of the leather on the steering wheel and gear knob (tendency to peel)

Alternatives

Alternatives by market

  • Europe: Kia Sportage III (direct technical cousin, same engines), Nissan Qashqai II (more modern, frugal dCi engines but less robust), Volkswagen Tiguan I phase 2 (superior finish, TDI made more reliable in phase 2), Ford Kuga II (excellent chassis), Peugeot 3008 I phase 2.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda CR-V IV (benchmark for petrol reliability), Toyota RAV4 IV, Mazda CX-5 I (beware of Skyactiv-D diesels, prefer petrol).
  • North America: Honda CR-V, Toyota RAV4, Ford Escape, Chevrolet Equinox. (In this market, the Tucson/ix35 is penalized by its Theta II/Nu petrol engines compared to the Japanese).

Verdict

The Hyundai ix35 phase 2 (2013-2015) is an extremely rational and safe used choice, provided you opt for a diesel engine (CRDi). The 1.7 CRDi 115 hp is perfect for economical daily use, while the 2.0 CRDi (136 or 184 hp) proves to be a truly tireless tractor, ideal for high mileage drivers or the mountains in the 4WD version. Problems are rare and are often limited to wear parts (clutch, steering flex disc) or clogging linked to improper use (DPF in the city). On the other hand, for buyers outside Europe (especially in North America), the 2.0 and 2.4 GDI petrol versions should be approached with extreme caution due to major design flaws (connecting rod bearings, oil consumption). In Europe, the 1.6 GDI is decent but lacks power for the size.
