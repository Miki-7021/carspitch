Volvo XC40

I facelift (XZ) · 2022 – 2025

BUY Reliability 88/100 — excellent

Summary

The **first-generation Volvo XC40 (Type XZ)** received a facelift in early 2022. This aesthetic refresh (new bumpers, refined 'Thor's Hammer' headlights with Pixel LED technology) is accompanied by a major technological revolution: the generalization of the **Android Automotive** infotainment system across the entire range.

Although this sheet covers all powertrains, the focus is on the **100% electric version (Recharge, renamed EX40 in 2024)**. This version underwent a spectacular technical evolution in 2023: the abandonment of front-wheel drive (FWD) in favor of rear-wheel drive (RWD) for single-motor versions, with the introduction of new electric motors developed in-house by Volvo and larger capacity batteries (up to 82 kWh). This change radically improved efficiency and driving pleasure. The XC40 remains a benchmark premium compact SUV, combining high-level safety, sleek Scandinavian design, and remarkable comfort.

Strengths 6

  • Major technical evolution in 2023 (RWD propulsion) making the electric version very efficient
  • Highly effective native Google integration (Google Maps, Assistant)
  • Exceptional level of active and passive safety (Pilot Assist)
  • Finish quality and material choice (leather-free on electric versions)
  • Performance of the Twin Motor version (408 hp) worthy of a sports car
  • Spaciousness and clever storage (31L frunk on the electric version)

Weaknesses 5

  • Frequent software bugs on 2022 models (TCAM module, black screens)
  • Highway consumption of the Twin Motor version still a bit high
  • Firm damping, especially with 20-inch wheels
  • Very high weight (over 2.1 tons for the Twin Motor)
  • Gradual disappearance of internal combustion and PHEV powertrains from the catalog

Check before buying

  • TCAM module operation: verify that GPS, 4G/LTE connection, and the SOS button function without error.
  • Tire wear: particularly on the front axle (2022 FWD models) and rear (2023+ RWD/AWD models).
  • Charging flap: check the locking/unlocking mechanism of the T2/CCS cable, which can sometimes get stuck.
  • Brake discs (Electric): check for excessive rust or caliper seizing due to one-pedal driving (One Pedal Drive).
  • OTA update history: ensure the system is not stuck on an old version requiring a visit to the dealership.

Official recalls

  • - **May 2023 (Electric):** Software recall concerning a communication fault in the inverter control module, potentially leading to a loss of motive power while driving. Resolved via OTA.
  • - **February 2023 (All models):** Software issue in the brake control module (BCM2) potentially leading to a loss of braking assistance (brake-by-wire). Resolved via OTA.
  • - **October 2022 (All models):** Software defect preventing the left turn signals from functioning under certain conditions. Resolved via OTA.

  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — Experience excellent, powerful, and stable braking performance, ensuring short and consistent stopping distances for maximum confidence in all situations.

Handling

  • Safe and Comfortable Road Holding — The electric XC40 offers predictable and reassuring road behavior, prioritizing comfort and stability. It instills great confidence in the driver, even in demanding driving conditions.

Economy

Alternatives

- **Europe:** BMW iX1 (very dynamic, excellent efficiency), Audi Q4 e-tron (more spacious), Mercedes EQA (comfortable but less performant), Tesla Model Y (benchmark for efficiency and charging network, but larger). - **Japan / Asia:** Lexus UX 300e (reduced range), Nissan Ariya. - **North America:** Genesis GV60 (ultra-fast 800V charging), Hyundai Ioniq 5, Tesla Model Y. - **Emerging Markets:** BYD Atto 3 (budget alternative), Volvo EX30 (the younger sibling, newer and cheaper).

Verdict

The restyled Volvo XC40 (2022-2025), particularly in its electric variant (Recharge / EX40), is a highly recommendable premium SUV. **The ideal choice is undoubtedly a Single Motor Extended Range (RWD, 82 kWh) version produced from mid-2023.** This technical update has transformed the vehicle, erasing the traction flaws of the old FWD version while drastically increasing real-world range. Early issues related to the Android Automotive system (TCAM module) have been largely eradicated by OTA updates. Mild-hybrid versions (B3/B4) remain excellent alternatives for high-mileage drivers, although the model's future is clearly all-electric.
