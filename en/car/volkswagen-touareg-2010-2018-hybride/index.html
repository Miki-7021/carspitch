Summary

The Volkswagen Touareg II (Type 7P) marks a major evolution compared to the first generation. Nearly 200 kg lighter, it abandons extreme engines (W12, V10 TDI) in favor of more efficient units and introduces a Hybrid version for the first time (sharing its technology with the Porsche Cayenne S Hybrid). Positioned as a premium SUV, it stands out for its build quality, supreme comfort (especially with air suspension), and its very reliable Aisin 8-speed automatic gearbox. The restyling (facelift) occurred in late 2014, bringing aesthetic touches, multimedia updates, and TDI engines meeting the Euro 6 standard. Overall generation note: An excellent road SUV, but the hybrid version, although powerful, poses a significant financial risk on the used market due to the aging of its high-voltage components.

Strengths 6

  • Fit and finish quality worthy of the upper segment
  • Exceptional ride comfort (especially with air suspension)
  • Very reliable and smooth Aisin 8-speed automatic gearbox
  • Towing capacity (up to 3.5 tons)
  • Cabin sound insulation
  • Performance of the Hybrid version (380 hp / 580 Nm)

Weaknesses 5

  • Astronomical repair costs for the Hybrid version (battery, inverter)
  • High weight leading to rapid wear of brakes and tires
  • Aging and slow infotainment system (RNS 850)
  • Disappointing real-world fuel consumption of the Hybrid on the highway (the gasoline V6 is thirsty)
  • Complex mechanical accessibility (especially on V8s and the Hybrid)

Check before buying

  • Hybrid System: Demand a high-voltage battery diagnostic. Test electric/thermal transitions (no jerks or clunks should be felt).
  • Timing (TDI/FSI): Listen carefully to the engine during a cold start (no metallic clicking should last longer than 2 seconds).
  • Air suspension: Check that the vehicle raises and lowers without dashboard errors, and that it does not sag after a night of parking (air spring leaks or compressor fatigue).
  • Water ingress: Lift the front floor mats. Wet carpeting indicates clogged drains, which can destroy electrical harnesses.
  • Transmission: Check that the gearbox oil has been changed (VW says "for life," but Aisin and experts recommend every 60,000 to 80,000 km).

Verdict

The Volkswagen Touareg II (7P) is a formidable flagship, capable of covering miles in royal comfort. However, the Hybrid version should be approached with extreme caution. While it offers undeniable driving pleasure, the complexity of its powertrain (supercharged V6 + electric motor + separation clutch) and the aging of its NiMH battery make it a financial time bomb on the used market. Inverter or battery failures often exceed the vehicle's market value.

Our advice: If you absolutely want the Hybrid, buy a model with a recently replaced battery and inverter, with proof of invoice. Otherwise, the 3.0 V6 TDI (245 or 262 hp) remains the most pragmatic, reliable, and suitable choice for the Touareg's size. For flawless hybrid reliability, turn to a Lexus RX 450h.
