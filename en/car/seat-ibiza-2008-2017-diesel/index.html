Seat Ibiza

IV (6J) · 2008 – 2017

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The fourth-generation Seat Ibiza (Type 6J, then 6P after the 2015 facelift) is based on the Volkswagen Group's PQ25 platform, shared with the VW Polo V and Audi A1. Designed by Luc Donckerwolke, it stands out with its dynamic design ('Arrow Design') and a sportier positioning than its cousins. During its long career, it underwent two facelifts (2012 and 2015) and a major technological transition under the hood. For diesel engines (the focus of this sheet), it started with the robust but rough pump-injector blocks (TDI IP), before switching to common rail (TDI CR) with the EA189 family (infamously known for 'Dieselgate'), then to the EA288 family at the end of its career. It is a versatile city car with rigorous road handling, but its mechanical reliability drastically depends on the chosen year and engine code.

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 1.6 TDI (EGR, injectors, DPF)
  • Very plastic and austere interior finish before the 2015 facelift
  • Poor sound insulation, especially with 3-cylinder TDIs (1.4 IP and 1.2 CR)
  • Firm suspension, compromised comfort on FR and Cupra trims
  • Limited rear passenger space (legroom)

Check before buying

  • History of injectors and EGR valve on the 1.6 TDI (invoices essential).
  • Proof of the 'Dieselgate' update on EA189 engines (1.2, 1.6, 2.0 TDI) and its consequences.
  • Timing chain noise on cold start on 1.2 TSI and 1.4 TSI petrol engines before 2015.
  • Operation of the 7-speed DSG gearbox (DQ200): no juddering, metallic noises, or jerks.
  • Condition of the particulate filter (DPF) on diesels used in the city (warning light on, loss of power).
  • Operation of air conditioning and blower motor (frequent electrical problems).

Official recalls

  • - **November 2016 (Dieselgate)**: Massive recall of EA189 TDI engines (1.2, 1.6, 2.0) for engine management software update to comply with NOx standards.
  • - **May 2017 (Safety)**: Recall concerning the rear door locking system (child safety) which can deactivate itself.
  • - **July 2014 (Fuel)**: Risk of leakage on the fuel filter of 1.2 TDI engines (produced between 2010 and 2014).
  • - **December 2011 (Hood)**: Defect in the engine hood locking system which could open while driving (2008-2011 models).
  • - **DSG7 Gearbox (DQ200)**: Recall campaign (often addressed during revisions) to replace synthetic oil with mineral oil to prevent short circuits in the mechatronics.

Verdict

The Seat Ibiza IV is a city car with two faces. If you're looking for a diesel, caution is advised. Models equipped with the **1.6 TDI** (very common) should be approached with extreme suspicion due to the exorbitant costs associated with EGR and injector failures, often exacerbated by the Dieselgate update. The **1.2 TDI** is to be avoided. However, if you find a well-maintained older **1.9 TDI**, you'll have an indestructible machine. The best diesel compromise remains the **1.4 TDI (EA288)**, which appeared in late 2015 with the second facelift and also benefits from a much better-finished interior. If your annual mileage is less than 20,000 km, you should definitely opt for post-2015 petrol engines (1.0 TSI / 1.2 TSI EA211 with timing belt), which are exemplarily reliable.
