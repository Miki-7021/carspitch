Summary

The fourth-generation Land Rover Range Rover (L405) marked a technical revolution upon its launch in 2012 thanks to the adoption of a 100% aluminum monocoque structure, allowing for spectacular weight savings (up to 420 kg depending on the version). Positioned as the pinnacle of the luxury SUV, it combines limousine-like comfort with extraordinary off-road capabilities (Terrain Response 2 system). A major facelift occurred in 2018, introducing the dual-screen Touch Pro Duo infotainment system, Matrix LED headlights, and the plug-in hybrid powertrain (PHEV P400e). While its road performance and refinement are exceptional, its overall reliability remains its Achilles' heel, with recurring electronic failures and serious mechanical weaknesses on certain engines, notably the diesel V6s.

Strengths 5

  • Imperial ride comfort (standard air suspension)
  • Unmatched off-road capabilities in the category (Terrain Response)
  • Soundproofing and material quality (leathers, woods, aluminum)
  • Space on board, particularly on LWB (long wheelbase) versions
  • Driving pleasure of the diesel V8 (SDV8), rare and torquey

Weaknesses 5

  • Very temperamental overall electronic reliability (screens, sensors, modules)
  • Frequent and documented engine failures on the 3.0 V6 Diesel (TDV6/SDV6)
  • Astronomical maintenance, repair, and insurance costs
  • Substantial weight leading to rapid wear of brakes and tires
  • Steep depreciation, particularly on older diesel engines

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: must be crystal clear, with frequent oil changes (especially on diesels).
  • Air suspension: check that the vehicle raises and lowers without suspicious noise from the compressor, and that it does not sag after a night of parking (air strut leak).
  • Onboard electronics: absolutely test all functions (Touch Pro Duo screens, massaging seats, cameras, power tailgate).
  • Diesel V6 Engine: listen for any dull knocking from the bottom end (warning sign of crankshaft failure).
  • Petrol Engines: check for the absence of coolant smell (leak from the plastic pipes under the supercharger).

Official recalls

The Range Rover L405 has been the subject of numerous official recalls (NHTSA and Rappel Conso). Here are the main ones categorized by theme:

Engine / Fire risk: - *August 2024 (Rappel Conso)*: Possible cracking of the plastic oil filter housing on certain engines, leading to an oil leak and a fire risk. - *September 2024 (Rappel Conso)*: Oil cooler pipe supports improperly tightened (oil leak). - *August 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: Coolant pump malfunction causing rapid overheating and a fire risk. - *July 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: Possible engine seizure leading to external oil leaks or leaks into the intake. - *February 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: DPF mounting frame fittings improperly tightened (fire risk). - *January 2019 (NHTSA 19V040)*: Defective crankshaft pulley on V6/V8 engines.

Running gear / Suspension: - *August 2025 / Nov 2024 (NHTSA 25V514 / 24V840)*: Possible cracking of the front suspension upper knuckle joint (2014-2017 models), which could lead to the detachment of the suspension arm. - *October 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: Possible cracks on the rear subframe that could lead to partial wheel detachment.

Electronics / Cameras / Lighting: - *December 2024 / January 2024 (NHTSA 24V947 / 24V023)*: Water ingress into the rearview camera, making the image unavailable. - *November 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: Pixel LED headlights aimed too high (glare) and failure of the headlamps upon ignition. - *October 2017 (NHTSA 17V679)*: The digital instrument cluster (gauges) may go blank intermittently.

Airbags / Seatbelts / Interior safety: - *July 2024 (Rappel Conso / NHTSA)*: Massive recall related to Takata airbags (propellant degradation that could project metal fragments). - *April 2022 / August 2021 (NHTSA 22V219 / 21V668)*: Driver seatbelt retractor locking defect. - *September 2023 (Rappel Conso)*: Second-row seat frame improperly secured.

Doors / Locks: - *July 2020 / May 2019 (NHTSA 20V387 / 19V392)*: Problem with the keyless entry system (Keyless Latch); doors may open while driving.

  • Prestige and Iconic Design — The Range Rover L405 embodies the pinnacle of luxury SUVs, with a timeless design and a majestic presence that asserts its status as an automotive icon.

  • Legendary Off-Road Capabilities — With its advanced Terrain Response system, the Range Rover L405 offers extraordinary off-road capabilities, allowing you to explore the most demanding terrains with astonishing ease.

  • Cutting-Edge Safety — Designed to protect its occupants, the Range Rover L405 achieved the maximum rating in Euro NCAP tests, ensuring optimal protection for adults and children.

  • Powerful and Efficient Diesel Engines — The Range Rover L405's diesel engines, including the robust SDV8 V8 and the modern Ingenium inline 6-cylinders, offer generous torque, great flexibility, and controlled fuel consumption for long journeys.

Verdict

The Range Rover L405 is a paradoxical vehicle: it is undoubtedly the most majestic and versatile SUV of its generation, but its reliability is unworthy of its rank. Buying a model equipped with the 3.0 V6 Diesel (TDV6/SDV6) is an extremely risky gamble due to frequent crankshaft failures. If you want a diesel, favor the robust 4.4 V8 (SDV8) or the recent Ingenium inline 6-cylinders (post-2020). The petrol versions (V8 Supercharged) are mechanically more solid but require meticulous maintenance of the cooling system. Golden rule: never buy this vehicle without a complete, transparent maintenance history, and ideally covered by a solid mechanical breakdown warranty (such as Land Rover Approved).
