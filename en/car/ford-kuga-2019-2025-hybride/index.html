Summary

The third-generation Ford Kuga (codename C519) marks a major turning point for the compact SUV from the Blue Oval brand. Based on the global C2 platform (shared with the Focus Mk4), it is lighter, longer, and adopts a more dynamic design, closer to a raised wagon. Its main asset lies in its massive electrification offering, quickly becoming the best-selling plug-in hybrid (PHEV) vehicle in Europe. A mid-cycle refresh (facelift) was introduced in early 2024, bringing a redesigned front end, the removal of many physical buttons in favor of the SYNC 4 infotainment system (13.2-inch screen), and a slight optimization of the hybrid powertrains. Overall, the Kuga III is a very accomplished vehicle, offering an excellent compromise between driving dynamics, passenger space, and energy efficiency, particularly in its FHEV and PHEV versions.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional efficiency of hybrid versions (FHEV and PHEV), among the best in the segment.
  • Mechanical reliability of the 2.5L naturally aspirated engine (simple design, Atkinson cycle, chain timing).
  • Dynamic road handling, true to Ford's DNA, despite the weight of the batteries.
  • Generous rear passenger space thanks to the sliding rear bench.
  • Responsive and ergonomic SYNC 3 (and SYNC 4 post-facelift) multimedia system.

Weaknesses 5

  • Interior finish and quality of some plastics are inferior compared to competitors (Peugeot 3008, VW Tiguan).
  • Reduced boot volume on the PHEV version (battery under the floor).
  • Imperfect sound insulation during strong accelerations (eCVT gearbox 'runaway' effect).
  • Capricious 12V battery management on hybrid models (frequent deep discharges).
  • Reliability of diesel powertrains (EcoBlue) marred by emissions control issues.

Check before buying

  • On PHEV (2020): Imperatively check that the High Voltage battery recall has been carried out (complete pack replacement).
  • On PHEV/FHEV: Test the 12V battery. If the vehicle displays random error messages or refuses to start, it is often the cause.
  • On PHEV/FHEV: Check for the presence of drainage holes on the under-engine shield (safety recall).
  • On EcoBlue (Diesel): Service history, illuminated engine light (often related to AdBlue or DPF), and belt noises.
  • Electronics: Test all functions of the SYNC touchscreen, rearview cameras (frequent display bugs requiring a reset or update).

What sets it apart 7

economy

  • Outstanding Hybrid Efficiency — Enjoy optimized fuel consumption thanks to the Kuga's hybrid powertrains (FHEV and PHEV), renowned for their exceptional efficiency and low running costs, among the best in their class.

reliability

  • Proven Mechanical Reliability — The Ford Kuga Hybrid benefits from robust mechanics, notably its 2.5L atmospheric Atkinson cycle engine with a timing chain, ensuring high-level reliability and long-term peace of mind.

safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — Travel with complete peace of mind thanks to the Kuga III's excellent Euro NCAP results, attesting to optimal protection for all occupants.

Performance

  • Dynamic Driving Dynamics — The Kuga III offers an agile and balanced driving experience, true to Ford's DNA. Its precise steering and excellent body control make it one of the most enjoyable SUVs to drive in its category.

practicality

  • Generous Space and Modularity — Appreciate the generous interior space, especially in the rear thanks to its sliding bench seat, offering superior modularity and comfort for all passengers and their luggage.

comfort

  • Intuitive Technology and Modern Comfort — The SYNC multimedia system (3 or 4 depending on the year) is responsive and ergonomic, offering advanced connectivity and a fluid user interface for enhanced driving comfort.

braking

  • Powerful and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from competent and reassuring braking performance, ensuring excellent stopping power and exemplary stability, essential for safety on all journeys.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Toyota RAV4 (hybrid benchmark, more expensive), Peugeot 3008 (better finish, more complex hybrid), Volkswagen Tiguan (more prestigious, powerful PHEV), Hyundai Tucson / Kia Sportage (bold design, excellent hybrid systems).
  • North America (vs Escape): Honda CR-V Hybrid, Toyota RAV4 Hybrid, Subaru Forester, Mazda CX-50.
  • Asia: Honda Breeze/CR-V, Toyota Wildlander/RAV4, Nissan X-Trail.

Verdict

The Ford Kuga III is an extremely relevant choice on the used car market, provided you target the hybrid powertrains (2.5 FHEV or PHEV). The 2.5L engine combined with the eCVT transmission (technology very similar to Toyota's HSD) offers high-level mechanical reliability, controlled consumption, and undeniable driving pleasure. You just need to ensure that 2020 PHEV models have undergone the HV battery recall. On the other hand, diesel powertrains (EcoBlue) are to be avoided except for very high-mileage highway drivers, due to capricious emissions control systems. The 1.5 EcoBoost petrol is a decent choice for small budgets, but does not offer the driving pleasure or fuel economy of the hybrid versions.
