Lexus NX

I (AZ10) · 2014 – 2021

BUY Reliability 92/100 — excellent

Summary

The **first-generation Lexus NX (AZ10)** marked the Japanese premium brand's smashing entry into the highly competitive compact SUV segment. Based on a heavily modified platform derived from the Toyota RAV4 (XA40), the NX stood out with its angular and bold design (trapezoidal grille, 'L'-shaped daytime running lights) and its top-flight interior finish.

Positioned against the Audi Q5, BMW X3, and Mercedes GLC, it bet on non-plug-in hybridization (NX 300h) in the majority of European markets, offering driving smoothness and reliability unmatched in the category. A facelift occurred in late 2017 (2018 Model Year), bringing aesthetic tweaks, a recalibrated suspension (AVS), a larger multimedia screen (10.3 inches), and the generalization of the *Lexus Safety System+*. The NX 200t was renamed NX 300 on this occasion.

**Overall expert rating:** The NX AZ10 is one of the most reliable premium SUVs of its decade. It is a 'peace of mind' purchase par excellence, although its infotainment system is dated.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall reliability (often ranked 1st in satisfaction surveys)
  • Quality of finish and assembly (plush materials, perfect aging)
  • Very low fuel consumption in urban/suburban areas (NX 300h)
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing (especially post-2017 facelift)
  • Lexus Relax warranty (up to 10 years / 160,000 km if maintained in the network)
  • Very high resale value

Weaknesses 5

  • Frustrating infotainment system (Remote Touch) and dated graphics
  • Average trunk volume for the category (high loading sill, sloping rear window)
  • Engine revving effect (rubber-band effect) during hard acceleration (NX 300h e-CVT)
  • Sluggish road handling, focused on comfort rather than dynamism
  • Disappointing highway fuel consumption for the 300h (aerodynamics and weight)

Check before buying

  • Complete maintenance history in the Lexus network (crucial for eligibility for the extended Relax warranty).
  • Condition of the hybrid battery cooling filter (located at the rear seats, it gets clogged with dust/pet hair).
  • Health of the 12V battery (classic) on the 300h, often undersized and prone to rapid discharge.
  • Tire wear (the vehicle is heavy, sometimes asymmetrical wear if the alignment is not perfect).
  • Smooth operation of the electric tailgate (the struts can get tired).

Verdict

The first-generation Lexus NX is an absolute choice of reason. If you are looking for a premium compact SUV and reliability is your number one criterion, there is no better option on the used market. The **NX 300h** version is by far the most recommendable for its smoothness, its economy of use in urban environments, and its legendary robustness. The NX 200t/300 will better suit those who mostly drive on the highway or who are allergic to the e-CVT transmission. Its only real flaw lies in its obsolete multimedia system, a largely acceptable compromise given the almost total absence of costly breakdowns.
