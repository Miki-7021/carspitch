Summary

The first-generation Jaguar XF (codename X250) marks a major stylistic departure for the British brand, abandoning the neo-retro design of the S-Type for modern, fluid lines penned by Ian Callum. Launched in 2007, it received a significant facelift in 2011 (Phase 2), refining its front end (slimmer headlights with LED signature) and modernizing its onboard electronics. Positioned in the premium executive car segment (E-segment), it stands out with a theatrical interior (rotating air vents, retractable cylindrical gear selector) and an excellent compromise between comfort and driving dynamics. While the original petrol engines (4.2 V8) are very robust, the introduction of the 5.0 V8 and V6 diesel units brought their share of reliability challenges. Temperamental electronics are also a point of vigilance on this generation.

Overall reliability rating: Average to Good (highly dependent on the engine).

Strengths 6

  • Timeless and elegant exterior design (especially Phase 2)
  • Spectacular and warm interior presentation
  • Dynamic road behavior and excellent road feel
  • Top-flight soundproofing and suspension comfort
  • Reliability of the 4.2 V8 and 3.0 V6 naturally aspirated petrol engines
  • Smooth and responsive ZF automatic gearboxes (6 and 8-speed)

Weaknesses 6

  • Slow, dated, and unintuitive infotainment system
  • Limited rear roominess and headroom for the segment
  • Disastrous reliability of the 2.7D V6 (crankshaft)
  • Costly timing issues on early 5.0L V8s
  • Temperamental onboard electronics (door locks, window regulators, touch screen)
  • High weight penalizing fuel consumption and brake/tire wear

Check before buying

  • Clear maintenance history, particularly the ZF gearbox oil change.
  • On the 5.0 V8 and 3.0 V6 SC: listen carefully to the engine when cold (timing chain noises or supercharger clunking).
  • On the V8s: inspect the engine bay for traces of dried coolant (water pump or plastic hose leak).
  • Operation of all electrical equipment: motorized air vents (which can jam), rotary gear selector, central locking.
  • On diesels: check the oil level (a level that is too high indicates diesel dilution due to the DPF).

Official recalls

The Jaguar XF (X250) has been the subject of numerous official recalls (NHTSA and Rappel Conso). Here are the main ones:

  • Takata Airbags (Multiple recalls from 2016 to 2026): Degradation of the propellant in the airbag inflator (driver and passenger) over time and with humidity. Risk of deadly metal fragments being projected upon deployment. Affects a large part of production.
  • Fuel System / Tank (Oct 2012, Feb 2016, Feb 2020): On 2010-2012 models equipped with 4.2L and 5.0L engines, the fuel tank outlet flange can crack, leading to a fuel leak and a fire risk.
  • 2.0 GTDi Engine (2013-2016): Several recalls specific to this engine: incorrectly positioned fuel supply hoses (Nov 2016), improperly secured charge air cooler (CAC) hose clamp (Aug 2013, Mar 2014), and power steering retaining bolt (Mar 2016).
  • Rear Suspension (Mar 2014): On 2013-2014 models, the rear suspension toe links can fracture, resulting in a loss of directional control.
  • Braking (Jun 2014): On 2013-2014 models, the right rear brake hose may chafe and leak.
  • Power Steering (Mar 2011, Jul 2014): Power steering pipes supplied with non-compliant zinc-nickel plating (2010) or risk of collision with a PAS system component (XFR-S 2013-2014).
  • Fuel Pump (Jul 2010, Dec 2012, Feb 2015): The electronic modules controlling the fuel pump can shut down, causing the engine to stall while driving.
  • Dashboard (Nov 2008): Breakage of the instrument cluster speaker wires (2009 models).

Verdict

The first-generation Jaguar XF is a characterful sedan that offers an exotic and warm alternative to German classicism. In petrol form, it is a recommendable purchase provided you choose your engine carefully. The 4.2 V8 (NA and supercharged) and 3.0 V6 NA units are highly robust and constitute the safest choices. The spectacular 5.0 V8 offers supercar performance but requires a substantial maintenance budget and strict verification of the timing system (especially before 2013) and the cooling circuit. On the diesel side, flee the 2.7 V6, and only consider the 2.2D and 3.0D if you drive mostly on the highway, with an impeccable maintenance history. In all cases, set aside a budget for the minor electronic quirks inherent to the model.
