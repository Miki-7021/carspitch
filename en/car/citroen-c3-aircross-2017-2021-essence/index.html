Summary

The first-generation Citroën C3 Aircross (A88) is an urban SUV that succeeded the C3 Picasso minivan. Based on the PF1 platform (shared with the Peugeot 2008 I and Opel Crossland X), it stands out with its cheerful design, typical Citroën suspension comfort, and great modularity (sliding rear bench, folding passenger seat). While its family-friendly features are undeniable, its career is heavily marred by major reliability issues with its engines, particularly the 1.2 PureTech petrol units (wet timing belt) and BlueHDi diesels (AdBlue and camshaft chain).

Strengths 5

  • Remarkable suspension comfort for the category
  • Spaciousness and modularity (sliding rear bench by 15 cm, trunk from 410 to 520 L)
  • Original and customizable design
  • Appreciated raised driving position
  • Pleasant PureTech engines to drive (when they work)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 1.2 PureTech petrol engines (wet timing belt)
  • Costly problems with diesel emission control systems (AdBlue)
  • Quality of interior plastics (hard and scratch-sensitive)
  • Slow infotainment system and all-touch ergonomics (air conditioning)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway

Check before buying

  • Service history: Must be 100% up-to-date, within the manufacturer's network, with strict adherence to recommendations (almost zero tolerance for exceeding intervals for warranty coverage).
  • Belt condition (PureTech): Open the oil filler cap and visually inspect the belt (cracks, fraying, swelling).
  • Engine light / Oil pressure: An oil pressure warning light that illuminates, even briefly, indicates a clogged oil strainer. Flee.
  • AdBlue system (BlueHDi): Check for the absence of a UREA light or engine fault. Ask if the tank has already been replaced.
  • Engine noises (1.5 BlueHDi): Metallic rattling at idle = slack camshaft chain.

Official recalls

  • November 2020 (Code HFC): Massive recall of 1.2 PureTech (produced from March 2013 to April 2017, thus the very first C3 Aircross) for timing belt inspection and ECU update to improve oil pressure drop alert.
  • December 2022 (Code JZR): Second massive PureTech recall for ECU reprogramming (braking/oil pressure).
  • October 2019 (Code GNK): Replacement of the particulate filter (FAP) on certain diesel models (risk of emission non-compliance).
  • September 2019 (Code GDD): Battery wiring harness incorrectly positioned, risk of wear due to rubbing.
  • February 2018: Inspection and eventual replacement of the steering column.

Verdict

The Citroën C3 Aircross I is a vehicle full of family qualities, but its mechanical flaws make it extremely risky on the used market. The 1.2 PureTech petrol versions (2017-2021) are to be AVOIDED unless the seller provides invoices proving recent belt replacement, strainer cleaning, and meticulous annual maintenance within the dealership network. If you absolutely must buy one, prioritize a model whose engine has already been made reliable or replaced, and negotiate the price firmly. Diesels are not spared from costly breakdowns (AdBlue).
