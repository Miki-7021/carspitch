Summary

The Ford Focus IV (codename DEH), launched in 2018, is based on Ford's new C2 platform. It marks a break from the previous generation by offering significantly increased interior space, a weight reduction (up to 88 kg), and increased rigidity. True to the model's reputation, it stands out as one of the dynamic benchmarks in its segment thanks to a sharp chassis and precise steering.

A facelift occurred in 2022, bringing a redesigned front end (logo on the grille), the abandonment of physical climate controls in favor of the huge 13.2-inch touchscreen integrating the SYNC 4 system, and a rationalization of the engine range. Production will definitively cease in 2025 with the planned closure of the Saarlouis factory in Germany, marking the end of the Focus lineage in Europe.

Expert's overall rating: An excellent compact car, often underrated, but whose purchase requires extreme vigilance regarding engine choice, especially for gasoline engines (the war between wet belt and chain).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis: one of the best comfort/dynamics compromises in the category
  • Very generous rear passenger space (legroom) and large trunk on the SW version
  • Reliability of 1.5 EcoBoost and 1.0 EcoBoost mHEV (chain-driven) gasoline engines
  • Ergonomics of the SYNC 4 system (on post-2022 facelifted versions)
  • Significantly improved sound insulation compared to the Focus III

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the first 1.0 EcoBoost (non-mHEV) with wet belt
  • Reliability of the 1.5 EcoBlue diesel (timing, leaks)
  • BVA8 automatic gearbox (converter) sometimes brutal and slow when downshifting
  • Quality of some lower interior plastics (pre-facelift)
  • Disappearance of physical climate controls on the facelift (all-touch)

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history (1.0 EcoBoost non-mHEV): Scrupulously check invoices. If the wrong oil was used, flee. Request an inspection of the oil strainer.
  • BVA8 gearbox behavior: During the test drive, simulate traffic jams (1st -> 2nd -> 1st). If violent jerks are felt, the hydraulic block may be worn.
  • Metallic noises (1.5 EcoBlue): Listen to the cold engine. A pronounced clicking sound at the top of the engine indicates a problem with the camshaft chain link.
  • Onboard electronics: Test all touchscreen functions, the rearview camera (which tends to freeze), and the Start&Stop operation.
  • Front tire wear: The sharp front axle can lead to asymmetrical or rapid wear if the alignment is slightly off.

Official recalls

  • May 2018 (Electrical harness): Risk of cable harness rubbing on models manufactured at launch, potentially causing short circuits.
  • August 2018 (Brake pedal): On automatic gearbox models, the brake pedal hinge may break in case of extreme emergency braking.
  • 2019 (Oil separator - 1.0 EcoBoost): Risk of oil leak at the separator, potentially dripping onto the exhaust (fire risk).
  • 2020 (eCall system): Software update because the automatic emergency call system might not transmit GPS coordinates in case of a crash.
  • 2021 (Panoramic roof): Bonding defect of the glass panel on some models, requiring inspection and re-bonding.

Verdict

The Ford Focus IV is a brilliant car on the road, spacious, and well-equipped. However, its reliability record is split in two. We strongly recommend the 1.0 EcoBoost mHEV (mild hybrid) and 1.5 EcoBoost gasoline versions, which benefit from a chain drive and prove to be very reliable. On the other hand, you should avoid the classic 1.0 EcoBoost versions (2018-2020) with a wet belt, which are real ticking time bombs if maintenance hasn't been meticulous, as well as the 1.5 EcoBlue diesel, which is too fragile. Opt for a facelifted model (post-2022) to benefit from the SYNC 4 system and a streamlined engine range.
