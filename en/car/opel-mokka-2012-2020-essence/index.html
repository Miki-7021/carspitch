Summary

The first-generation Opel Mokka (generation A) is a B-segment urban SUV, developed under the General Motors era on the Gamma II platform (shared with the Chevrolet Trax and Buick Encore). Launched in 2012, it received a major facelift in late 2016, adopting the Mokka X designation, with a modernized front end, a streamlined dashboard (ending the button-heavy console), and an updated IntelliLink multimedia system. A heavy vehicle for its category (often over 1.4 tons), it compensates with excellent technological features (AGR ergonomic seats, AFL directional headlights then Full LED) and the availability of genuine all-wheel drive (AWD), rare in this segment. Although demand initially leaned heavily towards diesel, gasoline engines (especially the 1.4 Turbo) are now proving to be the most relevant and reliable choices on the used market.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of gasoline engines (especially the 1.4 Turbo 140 hp with indirect injection)
  • Availability of genuine 4x4 transmission (AWD), rare in the B-SUV segment
  • Improved finish quality and greatly enhanced ergonomics on the Mokka X (post-2016)
  • Premium equipment (AGR ergonomic seats excellent for back support, Matrix LED headlights)
  • Highly appreciated high driving position

Weaknesses 5

  • Excessive weight (penalizes fuel consumption, performance, and brake/tire wear)
  • Disappointing trunk volume for its size (356 liters)
  • Poor sound insulation on diesel versions (especially the 1.7 CDTI)
  • Dashboard of 2012-2016 models overloaded with buttons
  • Costly timing chain issue on the 1.6 CDTI diesel

Check before buying

  • On 1.4 Turbo 140: With the engine running, remove the oil dipstick. If strong suction is felt with a whistling sound, the PCV valve is faulty (valve cover needs changing).
  • On 1.4 Turbo 140: Pink/white traces around the water pump and expansion tank (coolant leaks).
  • On 1.4 Turbo 152: Meticulously check service invoices to ensure Dexos 1 Gen 2 oil was used.
  • On 6-speed Manual Gearbox (M32): During a test drive, shift into 5th and 6th gear at 90 km/h and listen for any whistling or gear lever movement during acceleration/deceleration.
  • On 1.6 CDTI: Listen carefully to the engine during a cold start. A rattling/metallic clanking sound indicates a stretched timing chain (repair > €1500).

Official recalls

  • November 2014: Recall regarding a defective front seatbelt pretensioner (2012-2014 models).
  • April 2015: Reprogramming of the Body Control Module (BCM) due to wiper or lighting bugs.
  • February 2016: Inspection and potential replacement of the brake pedal which could detach from its support (models produced in 2015).
  • December 2017: Recall related to front and side airbags (incorrect deployment) on a limited series of Mokka X.
  • June 2018: Software update to correct non-compliant NOx emissions on certain diesel engines (1.6 CDTI).

safety

  • Certified Maximum Safety — With a 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, the Opel Mokka ensures exceptional protection for its occupants, guaranteeing total peace of mind on the road.
  • Safe and Stable Road Holding — The Mokka offers safe and predictable road behavior, maintaining excellent stability and prioritizing comfort, for serene driving in all circumstances.
  • Effective and Reassuring Braking — Benefit from an effective braking system, with stopping distances considered adequate for its category, contributing to constant active safety.

comfort

  • Superior Comfort and Ergonomics — Enjoy a highly appreciated high driving position and ergonomic AGR seats (available), designed for optimal back support, transforming every journey into a pleasant experience. Mokka X versions benefit from greatly improved interior ergonomics.
  • Advanced Technological Features — Mokka X versions (post-2016) integrate a streamlined dashboard and an updated IntelliLink multimedia system, as well as LED matrix headlights, offering a modern and connected driving experience.

practicality

  • Versatility with All-Wheel Drive — The Opel Mokka offers the rare option of a true 4x4 (AWD) transmission in the urban SUV segment, providing increased grip and off-road capability for remarkable versatility in all conditions.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 2008 I (lighter, larger trunk, but PureTech issues), Renault Captur I (very practical, reliable 1.3 TCE engines, avoid 1.2), Nissan Juke I (divisive design, poor rear legroom), Suzuki Vitara (excellent 4x4 system, very reliable gasoline engines).
  • North America: Honda HR-V (more spacious), Mazda CX-3 (more dynamic but cramped), Subaru Crosstrek (better AWD).
  • Emerging Markets: Hyundai Creta, Ford EcoSport.

Verdict

The Opel Mokka A (and Mokka X) is a robust SUV, whose main flaw is its excessive weight. For used cars, absolutely prioritize gasoline engines, which better suit the vehicle's urban/suburban purpose and prove much more reliable than diesels. The 1.4 Turbo 140 hp (A14NET/B14NET) is the best buy: despite its minor PCV valve defect (easy and inexpensive to repair), it is durable, flexible, and avoids direct injection problems. Avoid the 1.6 CDTI if the chain has not been replaced, and steer clear of the 1.4 Turbo 152 hp if the oil history is not perfect. A Mokka X 1.4 Turbo 140 hp in Elite or Innovation trim with AGR seats is an excellent choice on the used car market.
