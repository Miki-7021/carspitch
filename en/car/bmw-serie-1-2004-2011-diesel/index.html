Summary

The first-generation BMW 1 Series (E87 code for the 5-door version, E81 for the 3-door) marked the Bavarian brand's entry into the premium compact segment. Unique at the time, it was the only car in its class to offer a rear-wheel drive architecture and a longitudinal engine, providing ideal weight distribution (50/50) and unparalleled driving pleasure. The facelift (LCI - Life Cycle Impulse) occurred in March 2007, bringing aesthetic updates, an improved interior finish, and the introduction of 'EfficientDynamics' engines (including the notoriously famous N47 diesel and N43 direct-injection petrol engines). While the chassis is exceptional, the mechanical reliability of this generation is very mixed, particularly for the diesel engines which accounted for the majority of sales in Europe.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling (rear-wheel drive, 50/50 weight distribution)
  • Low and sporty driving position
  • Significantly improved interior finish after the facelift (2007 LCI)
  • Legendary and very reliable 6-cylinder engine (130i)
  • Extremely low fuel consumption of N47 diesels (when they work)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the N47 diesel engine (timing chain)
  • Very limited rear passenger space (legroom and headroom)
  • Limited boot volume (330 liters) and narrow access
  • Very firm suspension, especially with M chassis or Runflat tires
  • Maintenance and spare parts cost (BMW prices)

Check before buying

  • Timing chain noise on N47 diesels (friction/cicada sound between 1500 and 2000 rpm)
  • Presence of invoices proving the removal of intake swirl flaps on M47 diesels
  • Replacement history of injectors, ignition coils, and NOX sensor on N43 petrol engines
  • Steering column operation (red ELV light: steering lock, very costly)
  • Irregular tire wear (sensitive geometry, wear of control arm bushings)
  • Water ingress in the boot (rear light seals or bumper vents)

Official recalls

  • Battery cable (2012 / 2019): Risk of overheating and melting of the positive battery cable connector at the fuse box, potentially leading to engine cut-out or fire.
  • Ventilation wiring harness (2017 / 2019): Deterioration of the blower motor regulator connection, which can cause a short circuit and a risk of fire.
  • Brake assistance (2010): On N47 diesel engines, risk of oil leakage into the brake assistance system (vacuum pump), leading to a loss of assistance.
  • Ignition coils (Various years): Replacement covered for certain 4-cylinder petrol engines (N43).

Verdict

The BMW 1 Series E87 is an enthusiast's car, even in its more common versions. Its rear-wheel drive architecture makes it a unique offering in the used car market. However, purchasing a diesel version requires extreme caution. Phase 1 (M47) models are robust provided the intake swirl flaps are removed. Phase 2 (N47) models, although more powerful and economical, are plagued by the hidden defect of the timing chain. Without a recent timing chain replacement invoice from BMW, avoid the 116d, 118d (143hp), 120d (177hp), and 123d. The sensible choice is a Phase 1 petrol (118i/120i) or, for purists, the magnificent 130i which offers exemplary reliability and first-class mechanical pleasure.
