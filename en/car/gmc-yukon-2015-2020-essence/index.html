GMC Yukon

4th gen (K2XX) · 2015 – 2020

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The 4th generation GMC Yukon (code name K2XX) is an iconic American Full-Size SUV, a technical cousin of the Chevrolet Tahoe/Suburban and the Cadillac Escalade. Positioned as a premium offering between Chevrolet and Cadillac, it is available in standard and extended (Yukon XL) versions. The top-of-the-line **Denali** trim accounts for a massive share of sales thanks to its exclusive 6.2L V8 and luxurious presentation. Although very robust in its body-on-frame chassis design, this generation has been marred by recurring problems with the cylinder deactivation system (AFM) of the 5.3L V8 and numerous electronic and braking recalls.

*Note: Unlike the following generation (T1XX, 2021+) which introduced the excellent 3.0L Duramax 6-cylinder diesel, the K2XX generation was only marketed with V8 gasoline engines.*

Strengths 5

  • Gigantic interior space (especially in the XL version)
  • Exceptional towing capacities (up to 3,800 kg)
  • Reliability and driving pleasure of the 6.2L V8 (Denali trim)
  • Highway ride comfort
  • Very extensive General Motors after-sales network (in North America)

Weaknesses 5

  • Recurring and expensive AFM lifter problems on the 5.3L V8
  • Prohibitive replacement cost of the Magnetic Ride shock absorbers
  • Very high fuel consumption in the city
  • Size unsuited to European roads and parking lots
  • Significant number of manufacturer recalls (braking, steering, airbags)

Check before buying

  • Ticking noise upon startup or while driving on the 5.3L V8 (sign of imminent AFM lifter failure).
  • Stiffness of the brake pedal at low speeds (vacuum pump failure).
  • Oil leaks at the front and rear shock absorbers (Magnetic Ride).
  • Automatic gearbox shifting (look for hesitations or vibrations at a steady speed).
  • Operation of the central touchscreen (CUE/IntelliLink) which can crack or become unresponsive.

Alternatives

* **North America / Middle East**: Chevrolet Tahoe / Suburban (technical twins), Cadillac Escalade (ultra-luxury version), Ford Expedition (better interior space and high-performance twin-turbo V6), Toyota Sequoia (legendary reliability but aging design), Nissan Armada. * **Europe**: Model imported on an individual basis. Local alternatives (although smaller) are the BMW X7, Mercedes-Benz GLS, or Land Rover Range Rover.

Verdict

The 4th generation GMC Yukon is a vehicle with two faces. If you opt for the standard version equipped with the 5.3L V8, caution is advised: AFM lifter problems are frequent, expensive, and the list of recalls (brakes, steering) is long. On the other hand, the **Denali version equipped with the 6.2L V8 is an excellent choice**. This engine proves to be significantly more reliable, offers superior driving pleasure, and justifies the purchase of this behemoth. In all cases, demand a clear maintenance history (frequent oil changes) and verify if the brake vacuum pump and steering recalls have been performed. Also, plan a budget for the inevitable replacement of the Magnetic Ride shock absorbers around 100,000 km.
