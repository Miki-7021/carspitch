Summary

The third-generation Mitsubishi Outlander (chassis code GF/GG/ZJ/ZK/ZL) was launched in 2012, marking a significant evolution from its predecessor. Positioned in the family SUV segment, it emphasized efficiency, comfort, and, above all, innovation with the introduction of the plug-in hybrid (PHEV) version in 2013, a world first for a production SUV. This version quickly propelled the Outlander PHEV to become the best-selling plug-in hybrid vehicle in Europe for several years.

The model benefited from two major facelifts: the first in 2015 (often called 'Dynamic Shield' for its distinctive new front design) and the second in 2018, bringing aesthetic improvements, interior updates, and technical optimizations, particularly for the PHEV version (transition from the 2.0L to the 2.4L engine and increased battery capacity). The Outlander III is recognized for its practicality, generous interior space, and effective S-AWC (Super All-Wheel Control) all-wheel drive. Its general reliability is good, especially for naturally aspirated gasoline engines and the PHEV version, provided rigorous maintenance.

Strengths 6

  • General reliability of gasoline and PHEV powertrains
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (after facelift)
  • Generous interior space and modularity
  • Very effective S-AWC all-wheel drive
  • PHEV version: low running cost if regularly charged, quiet operation
  • Good residual value for the PHEV

Weaknesses 6

  • Perceived interior quality and some materials (before facelift)
  • Modest performance of naturally aspirated gasoline engines with the CVT gearbox
  • High fuel consumption of the 3.0L V6
  • DPF and EGR sensitivity on the 2.2L diesel
  • Battery degradation on early PHEVs (before 2018)
  • Outdated infotainment on early versions

Check before buying

  • PHEV : Battery State of Health (SoH), charging history, operation of hybrid and electric driving modes, condition of charging cables.
  • CVT Gearbox : Absence of abnormal noises, smooth shifts, service history of fluid changes (every 60,000 km).
  • Diesel : DPF condition (no warning light), EGR valve operation, absence of excessive exhaust fumes.
  • General : Tire wear (indicator of poor alignment or worn suspension), air conditioning operation, condition of brake discs and pads (especially on PHEV), presence of rust under the chassis (salted areas).
  • Recalls : Verify that all manufacturer recalls have been performed.

Official recalls

Official recalls for the Mitsubishi Outlander III have mainly concerned the following points:

  • 2014-2015 (PHEV) : Potential issues with the battery charging system, which could lead to overheating or malfunction. Corrected by software update and/or component replacement.
  • 2015-2016 (All models) : Issues related to the braking system (master cylinder) that could reduce braking effectiveness. Master cylinder replacement.
  • 2016-2017 (PHEV) : Risk of corrosion on the rear electric motor wiring, which could lead to power loss. Inspection and replacement of wiring if necessary.
  • 2018-2019 (PHEV) : Issues with the Battery Management Unit (BMU) that could lead to power loss or vehicle shutdown. Software update or BMU replacement.
  • 2019-2020 (All models) : Fuel pump issues, which could lead to engine stalling. Fuel pump replacement.

*It is imperative to check with a Mitsubishi dealership, using the vehicle's VIN, that all applicable recalls have been performed.*

Verdict

The Mitsubishi Outlander III, and particularly its PHEV version, represents a wise choice on the used car market. The naturally aspirated gasoline engines (2.0L and 2.4L) are safe bets for their robustness, provided the CVT gearbox is well maintained. The 3.0L V6 is an excellent choice for power and reliability, but its fuel consumption is a limiting factor.

The PHEV is the strong point of this model. It is ideal for drivers who can recharge regularly and make daily trips shorter than its electric range (approximately 40-50 km in real-world conditions for early versions, 50-60 km for post-2018 versions). It offers very low fuel consumption in this scenario. However, for long journeys without recharging, consumption can be higher than that of an equivalent diesel due to the additional weight of the battery. The reliability of the hybrid system is generally good, but a battery condition check is crucial for older models.

Buying advice : Prioritize a post-2018 PHEV model (2.4L engine and 13.8 kWh battery) if your budget allows, as it offers significant improvements in range and performance. For other powertrains, a complete service history is key. Avoid the 2.2L diesel if your use is primarily urban. The Outlander III is a reliable and practical vehicle, offering excellent value for money on the used market, especially in PHEV version.
