Honda Prelude

IV (BB1/BB4) · 1991 – 1996

BUY Reliability 84/100 — bon

Summary

The 4th generation Honda Prelude (chassis BA8/BA9/BB1/BB2/BB3/BB4) marks a radical stylistic and technological break from its predecessor. Gone are the angular design and pop-up headlights, replaced by fluid, stocky, and aggressive lines. Inside, it stands out with a futuristic, full-width dashboard, blending analog and digital displays (depending on trim levels). Technically, it abandons the mechanical 4WS (4-wheel steering) system for an entirely electronic version. This is also the generation that introduced the legendary H22A VTEC engine, offering high-flying performance. Today, it is a highly sought-after youngtimer, renowned for its overall mechanical reliability, although its advanced age requires strict vigilance regarding corrosion and maintenance history.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional overall mechanical reliability (especially F20/F22 blocks)
  • Sharp road handling, enhanced by the 4WS system (when it works)
  • Legendary H22A VTEC engine (character, sound, performance)
  • Low driving position and sporty ergonomics
  • Futuristic and bold interior design for its time
  • Typical Honda build quality of the 90s

Weaknesses 6

  • Very frequent pervasive corrosion (rear fenders, sills, sunroof)
  • Significant oil consumption on H22/H23 engines (FRM liners)
  • Capricious electronic 4WS system with age (dry solders on the ECU)
  • Original braking lacking endurance in sporty driving
  • Symbolic rear seats (strict 2+2)
  • Body and trim parts have become rare and expensive

Check before buying

  • **Corrosion:** Thoroughly inspect the rear wheel arches (under the plastic protection), sills, trunk floor, and around the sunroof.
  • **4WS System:** Check that the '4WS' light turns off after starting and that the rear wheels steer correctly (often a problem with ECU solders in the trunk or an angle sensor).
  • **PGM-FI Relay:** If the car refuses to start when hot, the main relay (under the dashboard) has cracked solders.
  • **Distributor:** The ignition module (igniter) or coil can fail without warning. Presence of oil in the distributor cap = internal seal failed.
  • **Timing Belt (H22):** Ask for proof of belt replacement AND check if the tensioner has been converted to manual.
  • **Automatic Gearbox:** The 4-speed automatic gearboxes are robust but require regular fluid changes with Honda ATF. Gear shifts should not be harsh.

Verdict

The Honda Prelude 4G is a masterpiece of 90s Japanese engineering. If you're looking for an original and indestructible daily driver, a 2.0i (F20A) version is an excellent choice. For purists and thrill-seekers, the 2.2i VTEC (H22A) is a must-have, offering a rare mechanical experience. However, the purchase must be conditional on a rigorous inspection for rust, the model's true Achilles' heel. Avoid heavily modified examples or those with rear fenders masked with body filler. Prioritize a clear history, even with high mileage, over a low-mileage but neglected car.
