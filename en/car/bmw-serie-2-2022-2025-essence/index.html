Summary

The BMW 2 Series Active Tourer (U06 generation), launched in early 2022, marks a technological and stylistic break from its predecessor (F45). Based on the FAAR platform (shared with the X1 U11), it abandons the manual gearbox and physical iDrive controller in favor of an all-touch interface (iDrive 8 with a curved display). Although it is a compact MPV (a declining segment), BMW has focused on dynamism and efficiency with widespread mild hybridization (48V MHEV) across the core petrol and diesel ranges, as well as plug-in hybrid (PHEV) versions offering up to 90 km of electric range. The petrol engines (B38 and B48) benefit from major technical evolutions (TU2) reinforcing their already excellent reliability.

Strengths 5

  • Mechanical reliability of B38 (3-cylinder) and B48 (4-cylinder) petrol engines is now irreproachable.
  • Significantly improved interior finish quality, worthy of higher segments.
  • Remarkable efficiency of MHEV (220i) versions and real-world range of PHEVs.
  • Dynamic road handling for an MPV (typical BMW).
  • Very refined sound insulation, especially on petrol engines.

Weaknesses 5

  • All-touch ergonomics (iDrive 8): removal of physical climate controls and the iDrive controller.
  • Frequent software bugs on the infotainment system (black screens, CarPlay/Android Auto disconnections) on 2022-2023 models.
  • DKG7 gearbox sometimes hesitant at very low speeds or during maneuvers (especially on non-MHEV versions).
  • Reduced boot volume on MHEV (415L) and PHEV (406L) versions compared to pure internal combustion versions (470L).
  • Very high prices and still expensive options policy.

Check before buying

  • Verify that all software updates (OTA or at the dealership) have been performed (crucial for iDrive 8 and hybrid management).
  • Ensure that the vehicle is not affected by the massive recall of the Continental Integrated Braking System (IBS), or that the part has been replaced.
  • On PHEV versions, test the fluidity of the transition between electric and thermal modes.
  • Inspect front tire wear, as high torque (especially on 223i, 230e, and diesels) tends to wear them prematurely.
  • Test all driving aids (ADAS) during the test drive, as sensors are sensitive.

Official recalls

  • February 2024 (Braking System - IBS): Massive recall (BMW code 0034670200) concerning the integrated braking system supplied by Continental. An electronic defect can lead to loss of braking assistance and ABS/ESP. Affects numerous U06 models produced between June 2022 and January 2024.
  • December 2022 (Seatbelts): Risk of pinching or improper retraction of rear seatbelts. Mechanism replacement.
  • August 2022 (Airbag): On a small series of vehicles produced in early 2022, the driver's knee airbag might not deploy correctly due to a manufacturing defect.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Mercedes-Benz B-Class (W247 - direct competitor, more comfort-oriented), Volkswagen Golf Sportsvan (used car market only), BMW X1 (U11 - SUV alternative on the same technical basis), Audi Q3.
  • Asia: Mercedes-Benz B-Class, Lexus UX (although an SUV, targets a similar clientele looking for compact premium).

Verdict

The BMW 2 Series Active Tourer (U06) is an extremely accomplished vehicle mechanically. The petrol engines (218i, 220i, 223i) rely on B38/B48 blocks whose reliability is now well-proven and among the best in the premium market. The ideal choice is the 220i, which offers the best compromise between performance, consumption, and daily driving pleasure. The main pitfalls of this model are not mechanical, but electronic: iDrive 8 bugs (often resolved by updates) and the recent recall of the IBS braking system. If the targeted vehicle has a clear history and has undergone software corrections and manufacturer recalls, it is a highly recommendable purchase.
