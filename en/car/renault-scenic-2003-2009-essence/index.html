Summary

The Renault Scénic II (codename JM) marked a stylistic break from its predecessor, adopting the divisive design of the Mégane II. While it excelled in its spaciousness, exemplary modularity (individual seats, numerous storage compartments), and ride comfort, it unfortunately remained famous for its disastrous reliability during its first years of commercialization (Phase 1: 2003-2006). Electronic failures (digital dashboard, hands-free card) and mechanical breakdowns (especially on dCi diesels) heavily tarnished its reputation. The late 2006 facelift (Phase 2) corrected most of the early flaws. Today, on the used market, the petrol versions (our focus here) are the only ones to prioritize, offering an economical and significantly less risky alternative than the diesels, provided you target a well-maintained Phase 2.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional spaciousness and modularity (removable seats, flat floor)
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially in petrol)
  • Passive safety (5 Euro NCAP stars, very good for its time)
  • Today's ridiculously low used purchase price
  • Petrol engines generally durable if maintenance is followed

Weaknesses 6

  • Disastrous electronics on Phase 1 (2003-2006)
  • Digital dashboard that goes blank (failing solders)
  • Frequent water ingress (clogged windshield scuttle panel) burning out the UCH
  • Fragile start cards (not recognized)
  • dCi diesel engines (1.5 and 1.9 120hp) with catastrophic reliability
  • Capricious and expensive-to-repair electric parking brake

Check before buying

  • Dashboard: Check that the digital display does not flicker, lose time or mileage.
  • Windshield Scuttle Panel: Lift the grilles under the wipers. If water stagnates, the cabin may have been flooded (check the traps under the feet).
  • Cold Start (Petrol 1.6/2.0): Listen carefully. A rattling noise or stalling indicates a faulty dephaser pulley.
  • Power Windows: Test all 4 windows. The plastic clips break very often.
  • Parking Brake: Ensure it engages and disengages without an error message on the dashboard.
  • Air Conditioning: The compressor or pressure switch are often faulty.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Citroën C4 Picasso (comfortable but similar electronic issues), Volkswagen Touran (more austere, robust but noisy TDI engines), Ford C-Max (more dynamic to drive), Opel Zafira (integrated 7 seats).
  • Asia / Japan: Toyota Corolla Verso (significantly superior reliability, especially in petrol), Honda FR-V (atypical 6 seats, very reliable).

Verdict

The Renault Scénic II is a textbook example of a two-faced car. Absolutely avoid its diesel versions before 2007 (1.5 dCi and 1.9 dCi 120) and its Phase 1 trims riddled with electronic bugs. However, a Phase 2 model (after late 2006) equipped with a petrol engine (1.6 16v or 2.0 16v) is today an excellent deal for small budgets. These petrol versions are mechanically robust, provided you ensure that the timing belt and dephaser pulley have been replaced, and that the water drains are preventively cleaned. It's a sensible purchase, to be firmly negotiated at the slightest electronic defect.
