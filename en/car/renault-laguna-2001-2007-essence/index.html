Summary

The Renault Laguna II (BG0/KG0) is a pivotal vehicle in European automotive history. Launched in 2001, it was the first car in the world to achieve 5 stars in the Euro NCAP crash test, redefining safety standards. It also popularized technologies unheard of at the time: hands-free card, button start, tire pressure monitoring, and advanced multiplexing.

However, this technological ambition came at a price: Phase 1 (2001-2005) suffered from serious reliability issues, both with electronics (faulty cards, capricious sensors) and diesel engines (turbo failures on the 1.9 dCi 120, breakdowns on the 2.2 dCi).

Fortunately, the petrol engines (which are the subject of this report) were largely spared from major mechanical failures, with the notable exception of the disastrous 2.0 IDE. The Phase 2 (March 2005 - 2007) facelift corrected almost all of the initial flaws, transforming the Laguna II into an extremely reliable, comfortable, and now very affordable used sedan.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional passive safety (5 Euro NCAP stars, a historic first)
  • High-level suspension comfort and sound insulation
  • Superb and safe road handling
  • Reliability of petrol engines (excluding 2.0 IDE) and the 2.0 dCi (Phase 2)
  • Extensive equipment for the used car price
  • Bodywork corrosion resistance (composite front wings, aluminum hood)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous electronic reliability on Phase 1 (2001-2004)
  • 2.0 IDE (petrol) and 1.9 dCi 120 / 2.2 dCi (diesel) engines to absolutely avoid
  • Aging interior plastics ('soft touch' coating that peels and becomes sticky)
  • Fragility of starter cards (internal solders fail)
  • Very fragile window regulator mechanisms

Check before buying

  • Full functionality of the hands-free card (opening, closing, starting without inserting the card if option is present).
  • Timing belt history on petrol engines (high cost on the V6).
  • Noise on cold start on 1.6 and 2.0 16v (rattling = faulty dephaser pulley).
  • Functionality of all 4 electric window regulators.
  • Condition of the rear axle silentblocks (rattling on degraded roads).
  • On automatic gearbox: absence of jerks when shifting gears (D to R, and 1 to 2). Demand gearbox oil change invoices.

- 2002: ABS/ESP computer reprogramming (risk of unintended activation).,- 2003: Motorized throttle body wiring harness on petrol engines (risk of power loss or unstable idle).,- 2005: Replacement of the front lower suspension ball joint.,- 2006: Inspection and reinforcement of Isofix anchors on the rear seats.,- 2007: Risk of manual parking brake loosening (recall on models without automatic parking brake).

What sets it apart 6

safety

  • Pioneering and Unrivalled Safety — The Laguna II redefined automotive safety standards by being the first car in the world to achieve the maximum rating in Euro NCAP crash tests, offering exceptional protection to its occupants.

braking

  • Masterful Braking Control — Equipped with an efficient and durable braking system, including ABS and EBD as standard, the Laguna II ensures precise and safe stops, with a reassuring pedal feel.

road_handling

comfort

  • A Cocoon of Comfort — Enjoy high-level suspension comfort and meticulous soundproofing, transforming every journey into a relaxing and silent experience, even over long distances.

reliability

  • Robust Petrol Engines — The petrol engines (excluding 2.0 IDE) of the Laguna II are renowned for their robustness and endurance, offering reliable and powerful mechanics for years of driving pleasure.

  • Generous and Innovative Equipment — The Laguna II offers abundant equipment for its category, including innovations for its time such as the hands-free card and tire pressure monitoring, providing excellent value for money.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 406 / 407 (excellent chassis, reliable petrol engines), Citroën C5 I (hydraulic comfort), Ford Mondeo Mk3 (very reliable in petrol), Volkswagen Passat B5.5 / B6.

Verdict

The Renault Laguna II is a car of extremes. If you buy a Phase 1 diesel, you're heading for serious trouble. However, a petrol Laguna II (1.8 16v, 2.0 16v or 2.0 Turbo) is an excellent used car choice, especially in Phase 2 (after March 2005). These engines are robust, the comfort is superb, and prices are ridiculously low due to the poor reputation of the diesels.

Buying advice: Look for a Phase 2 with the 2.0 16v 135 hp or 2.0 Turbo 170 hp engine. Absolutely avoid the 2.0 IDE (direct injection) from Phase 1. Check that the onboard electronics and window regulators work correctly during the test drive.
