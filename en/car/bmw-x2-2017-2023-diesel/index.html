BMW X2

F39 (2017–2023) · 2017 – 2023

All BMW models

Summary

The BMW X2 (F39 generation) is the 'coupe' and dynamic counterpart to the compact SUV X1 (F48). Based on the UKL2 platform (front-wheel drive or xDrive all-wheel drive), it stands out with a sloping roofline, lowered ground clearance, a firmer chassis, and the presence of the BMW logo on the C-pillars (a nod to the brand's historic coupes). While it sacrifices some interior space and boot volume compared to the X1, it offers superior driving pleasure. In terms of reliability, the F39 generation benefits from the maturity of BMW's modular engines (B37/B47 for diesel, B38/B48 for petrol), turning the page on the serious timing chain issues of the old N47 generation. The X2 is generally a very reliable vehicle, provided that maintenance is scrupulously followed and that recall campaigns (especially for the EGR cooler on diesels) have been carried out.

Strengths 5

  • Overall reliability of the modular B47 (diesel) and B48 (petrol) engines, far superior to older generations.
  • Driving pleasure and chassis dynamism (precise steering, controlled body roll).
  • Interior finish quality and iDrive system ergonomics.
  • Excellent 8-speed Aisin automatic gearbox (smooth and responsive).
  • Very frugal fuel consumption of diesel engines (18d and 20d).

Weaknesses 5

  • Recurrent EGR valve cooler issue on diesels (fire risk, although covered by BMW).
  • Very firm comfort, especially with DirectDrive (M Sport) suspension and 19 or 20-inch wheels.
  • Poor rear visibility (thick C-pillars, small rear window).
  • Rear passenger space and boot volume are reduced compared to the X1.
  • High maintenance and spare parts cost within the official network.

Check before buying

  • Recall history: Demand proof that the EGR cooler recall has been carried out (for diesels).
  • Coolant level: An unexplained drop in a diesel often indicates an internal EGR cooler leak.
  • Tire wear (xDrive models): The difference in wear between the front and rear axles must not exceed 2 mm to preserve the transfer case.
  • Gearbox behavior: No jerks when shifting gears (DKG7 or BVA8).
  • Front axle noises: Clunking on degraded roads may indicate wear of wishbone bushings or the steering rack.

Official recalls

  • EGR Cooler (2018 - 2023): Massive global recall (several successive campaigns) for 4 and 6-cylinder diesel engines produced between 2014 and 2020. Risk of glycol leakage into the intake potentially causing a fire. Replacement of the cooler and EGR valve.
  • High Voltage Battery (August 2020): On plug-in hybrid models (xDrive25e) produced in 2020. Impurities during battery cell manufacturing can cause a short circuit and fire. Replacement of defective modules.
  • Steering Rods (2018): On certain models produced in 2018, risk of steering rod breakage. Replacement covered.
  • Seat Belts (2022): Possible defect of the front seat belt retractor on a small series of vehicles.

Verdict

The BMW X2 (F39) is an excellent choice for those seeking a compact SUV with sporty road handling, even if it means sacrificing some comfort and practicality. In terms of reliability, it is a very reassuring vehicle. The diesel engines (B47), which form the core of the European market, are robust, powerful, and economical. The only real drawback is the EGR valve cooler: it is imperative to check that the vehicle in question has undergone the official recall campaign. If the service history is clear and gearbox/differential oil changes have been anticipated, an X2 18d or 20d can cover high mileages without issues. For low-mileage drivers, petrol versions (18i or 20i) are preferable to avoid DPF clogging.
