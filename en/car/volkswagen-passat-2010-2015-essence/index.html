Volkswagen Passat

B7 (3C) · 2010 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

All Volkswagen models

Summary

The Volkswagen Passat B7 (Type 3C/362 for the sedan, 365 for the SW estate) is not an entirely new generation, but a significant facelift of the B6. It retains the PQ46 platform but considerably improves interior finish quality, sound insulation, and technological equipment (emergency braking, fatigue detection). While it excels in comfort and spaciousness, its mechanical reliability is highly contrasted. The petrol engines (TSI), which are the subject of this report, went through a dark period at Volkswagen (excessive oil consumption, fragile timing chains). Conversely, the diesels switched to common rail technology and proved much more robust than in the previous generation, despite the Dieselgate scandal.

Strengths 5

  • Interior finish quality and material aging
  • Excellent sound insulation (especially with acoustic glazing)
  • Gigantic boot volume (especially in SW estate)
  • Safe and reassuring road handling
  • Reliability of 2.0 TDI (Common Rail) engines compared to the B6

Weaknesses 5

  • Catastrophic reliability of TSI petrol engines (chains, oil) before correction
  • Very fragile DSG7 (DQ200) automatic gearbox (mechatronics and clutches)
  • Very conservative design, little aesthetic evolution compared to the B6
  • High repair costs within the Volkswagen network
  • 'Dieselgate' software update (EA189) sometimes causing EGR/Injector issues

Check before buying

  • For TSI (1.8/2.0): Demand proof of piston ring replacement (invoice for several thousand euros).
  • For TSI (1.4/1.8/2.0): Listen to the cold engine (hood open) to detect a metallic chain noise.
  • For DSG7 gearboxes: Test hill starts and 1st-2nd gear changes (no jerking or juddering tolerated).
  • For DSG6 / Haldex gearboxes: Check oil change frequency (strict 60,000 km).
  • For TDI: Check if the anti-pollution update has been done and if the EGR valve has been replaced since.

Official recalls

  • November 2013 (DSG7 DQ200): Replacement of synthetic gearbox oil with mineral oil to prevent corrosion and short circuits in the mechatronic unit.
  • 2016-2017 (Dieselgate - EA189): Software update (and addition of a flow regulator on the 1.6 TDI) to correct NOx emissions.
  • April 2014 (Lighting): Possible fuse defect affecting exterior lights.
  • Takata Airbags (Various years): Replacement of defective airbag gas generators (mainly concerns North American NMS models).

Verdict

The Volkswagen Passat B7 is an excellent road car, but buying it with a petrol (TSI) engine is like navigating a minefield. The EA111 (1.4) and EA888 Gen2 (1.8, 2.0) engines suffer from major design flaws (timing chain, excessive oil consumption) that can lead to engine destruction. The combination with the DSG7 (DQ200) gearbox worsens the overall assessment.

Buying advice: If you absolutely want a petrol Passat B7, look for a 1.8 or 2.0 TSI model whose engine has been completely rebuilt (pistons, rings, timing) with supporting invoices, or opt for the rare and expensive 3.6 V6. Paradoxically, although this report focuses on petrol, the 2.0 TDI versions (manual or DSG6 gearbox) are infinitely more recommendable for this generation.
