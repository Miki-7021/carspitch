Summary

The third-generation Kia Sorento (UM) marks a true move upmarket for the Korean manufacturer. Gone is the rustic SUV; this iteration positions itself as a large family SUV (available in 5 or 7 seats) with performance close to the premium segment. Based on a platform shared with the Hyundai Santa Fe, it offers remarkable ride comfort, excellent soundproofing, and rich technological features. A facelift occurred in 2018, bringing a new 8-speed automatic gearbox, aesthetic touches, and enhanced driving aids. While the European market was almost exclusively dominated by the excellent 2.2 CRDi diesel, North American, Asian, and Middle Eastern markets benefited from a wide range of gasoline engines. It is on these latter engines that maximum vigilance is required, due to major reliability issues with the Theta II 4-cylinder engine family.

Overall design score: 7.5/10 (Penalized by the 4-cylinder gasoline engines).

Strengths 6

  • Gigantic interior space and modularity (true auxiliary 3rd row)
  • Soundproofing and suspension comfort worthy of the upper segment
  • Exemplary reliability of the 2.2 CRDi diesel engine
  • Pleasure and power of the 3.3L V6 (non-European markets)
  • Unbeatable price/equipment ratio on the used market
  • 7-year manufacturer's warranty (reassuring for end-of-production models)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of 4-cylinder gasoline engines (Theta II)
  • Very high fuel consumption of V6 gasoline versions
  • Imposing size (4.80 m) making urban parking difficult
  • Clumsy road handling, focused on comfort at the expense of dynamism
  • Aging infotainment system on pre-facelift models (2014-2017)

Check before buying

  • Clear service history for oil changes (crucial for all engines, vital for gasoline)
  • Verification of KSDS (Knock Sensor) update application on 2.4 and 2.0T
  • Absence of metallic knocking during acceleration (sign of worn connecting rod bearings on gasoline engines)
  • AWD system operation (central differential lock, absence of jerks)
  • Condition of the trailer hitch wiring harness (subject to a recall for fire risk)
  • Tire and brake wear (heavy vehicle weighing nearly 2 tons)

Official recalls

- Theta II Engines (2017-2021): Massive recalls (especially in North America) for premature connecting rod bearing wear that can lead to engine stall or fire. Engine replacement if damaged, KSDS software installation.,- Trailer Hitch Wiring Harness (2022): Risk of short circuit and fire at the trailer hitch wiring module (even with ignition off). Recommendation to park outdoors before repair.,- High-Pressure Fuel Pump (2018): On 2.0 T-GDI and 3.3 GDI engines, risk of fuel leak at the high-pressure line, increasing fire risk.,- Transmission Lock (2015): The gear lever could be moved from the 'P' position without pressing the brake pedal.

Verdict

The Kia Sorento III is an excellent family SUV, spacious, comfortable, and well-equipped. However, the choice of engine entirely dictates the buying verdict. If you are looking for a gasoline model, absolutely avoid the 2.4 GDI and 2.0 T-GDI 4-cylinders (Theta II) unless the engine has been recently replaced by Kia with an extended warranty. Their design flaws are too risky. Turn to the 3.3L or 3.5L V6, which is very smooth and generally reliable, although you will have to accept gargantuan fuel consumption. Paradoxically, for this heavy SUV, the best engine remains the 2.2 CRDi diesel, which stands out for its reliability, torque, and relative fuel efficiency.
