Summary

The third-generation Ford Mondeo, known by internal codes B4Y (sedan), B5Y (hatchback), and BWY (wagon), was launched in 2000 and marked a turning point for Ford in Europe. Larger, more spacious, and with a more mature design than its predecessor, it aimed to directly compete with D-segment benchmarks like the Volkswagen Passat and Opel Vectra. It was praised for its excellent chassis, offering a remarkable compromise between comfort and dynamism, a typical Ford characteristic.

A minor facelift occurred in 2003 (sometimes referred to as Mk3.5), bringing minor aesthetic changes (headlights, grille, taillights) and interior improvements, particularly in materials and equipment. It was also during this period that the TDCi diesel engines were refined and the 2.2 TDCi made its appearance.

Overall, the Mondeo III is a reliable car, especially in its gasoline versions, offering excellent value for money on the used car market. However, certain diesel engines and some recurring weaknesses require particular attention.

Strengths 6

  • Excellent road handling and suspension comfort
  • Generous interior space and large trunk (especially in wagon)
  • Solid build quality (for its time)
  • Very reliable and economical to maintain Duratec-HE gasoline engines
  • Comprehensive equipment for the price on the used car market
  • Precise and pleasant steering

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of TDCi diesel engines (Delphi injectors, dual-mass flywheel)
  • Susceptibility to rust (rear wheel arches, sills, tailgate)
  • Minor electrical problems (power windows, instrument cluster)
  • High fuel consumption for V6 gasoline engines
  • Difficult access to certain engine parts (V6, diesel injectors)

Check before buying

  • Complete service history, especially for diesel versions (injector invoices, DMF)
  • Condition of rear wheel arches and sills for rust
  • Operation of power windows and instrument cluster
  • Absence of excessive smoke or engine knocking (diesel)
  • Proper functioning of air conditioning (compressor, leaks)
  • Condition of rear suspension bushings and front suspension ball joints
  • Operation of the IMRC control on V6 gasoline engines

Official recalls

Here is a non-exhaustive list of official manufacturer recalls for the Ford Mondeo III (B4Y/B5Y):

  • 2001-2002 : Risk of fuel leak. A fuel hose could rub against a chassis component, leading to a leak. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period).
  • 2002-2003 : Loss of braking assistance. A brake booster vacuum hose could wear out and cause a loss of assistance. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period).
  • 2003-2004 : Steering column failure. The lower universal joint of the steering column could loosen or disengage, affecting steering. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period).
  • 2004-2005 : Front lower suspension ball joint failure. Risk of ball joint separation, potentially leading to loss of control. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period).
  • 2005-2006 : Incorrect deployment of driver's frontal airbag. The airbag module might not deploy correctly in the event of a collision. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period).

It is imperative to check with a Ford dealership using the VIN if the vehicle in question has received the fixes for these recalls.

Verdict

The Ford Mondeo III is an excellent road-going family car, offering driving pleasure superior to the average for its segment and generous interior space. On the used car market, it represents a very interesting deal due to its significant depreciation.

For a worry-free purchase, absolutely prioritize the Duratec-HE gasoline engines (1.8L and especially 2.0L). They are robust, reliable, and their maintenance costs are reasonable. The V6s are pleasant but thirstier and potentially more expensive to maintain (IMRC, spark plugs).

Regarding diesels, caution is advised. TDDi versions are simpler but rare. The TDCi (2.0L and 2.2L) are powerful but their reliability is marred by recurring and costly problems with Delphi injectors and the dual-mass flywheel. A TDCi purchase is only advisable with an impeccable service history, invoices proving recent replacement of these expensive parts, and ideally a warranty.

Whatever the choice, a thorough inspection of the bodywork (rust) and electrical weak points is recommended. If you find a well-maintained example, especially a gasoline one, the Mondeo III remains a wise choice for a reliable and pleasant-to-drive family vehicle.
