Summary

The first-generation Audi A5 (internal code B8 or 8T) made a strong impression upon its launch in 2007. Based on the Volkswagen Group's MLB (Modular Longitudinal Matrix) platform, it shared its foundations with the A4 B8. Initially launched as a coupe, the range quickly expanded with the convertible in 2009 and the very popular Sportback (five-door) also in 2009. Its design, penned by Walter de Silva, is often cited as one of the brand's most successful, combining elegance and sportiness. A significant facelift occurred in late 2011 (for the 2012 model year), bringing aesthetic updates (headlights, grille, bumpers) and technical evolutions, particularly concerning engines and transmissions.

The reliability of the A5 B8 is generally good, but it is highly dependent on the chosen engines, especially the first-generation TFSI petrol units which experienced recurring problems. Diesel versions and newer or larger petrol engines are generally more robust, provided they receive rigorous maintenance. It is a car that ages well aesthetically, and its interior remains of very good quality.

Strengths 6

  • Timeless and elegant design (coupe, convertible, Sportback)
  • High-quality interior finish and premium materials
  • Ride comfort (especially with standard suspension)
  • Balanced and safe road handling (Quattro)
  • Wide choice of engines and transmissions
  • Good sound insulation

Weaknesses 6

  • Reliability of 1.8 TFSI and 2.0 TFSI engines (EA888 Gen 2) before the facelift (oil consumption, timing chain)
  • High maintenance and spare parts cost
  • Limited rear legroom in coupe and convertible
  • Multitronic (CVT) gearbox unreliable and unpleasant
  • Valve carbon buildup on FSI/TFSI engines (direct injection)
  • Used car prices remain strong for well-engined versions

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for TFSI engines).
  • Engine oil level and oil consumption (ask the seller).
  • Timing chain noise on cold start (metallic rattling).
  • Gearbox operation (Multitronic to avoid, S-Tronic/Tiptronic to test).
  • Condition of brake discs and pads (expensive to replace).
  • Operation of air conditioning and onboard electronics (MMI).
  • Presence of leaks (water pump, sump).
  • Condition of tires and rims (firm suspension can wear tires quickly).
  • Check for completed manufacturer recalls.

Official recalls

Official recalls for the Audi A5 B8 (8T) include, but are not limited to:

  • 2010-2011 (Europe/NA): Potential fuel leak issue at the fuel filter, which could lead to a fire risk. Concerns certain 2.0 TFSI and 3.2 FSI models.
  • 2012 (Europe/NA): Risk of power brake system failure (vacuum pump) on certain 2.0 TFSI models, which could reduce braking effectiveness. Concerns models equipped with the 2.0 TFSI engine.
  • 2014 (Europe/NA): Airbag control module issue that could prevent airbag deployment in the event of a collision. Concerns a wide range of Audi models, including the A5 B8.
  • 2015 (Europe/NA): Risk of overheating of the fuel pump control module, which could lead to engine failure or a fire risk. Concerns certain 2.0 TFSI and 3.0 TFSI models.
  • 2016 (Europe/NA): Coolant level sensor issue that could give a false indication and lead to engine overheating. Concerns certain 2.0 TFSI models.
  • 2017 (Europe/NA): Recall related to "Dieselgate" for 2.0 TDI EA189 engines, concerning a software update to reduce emissions. (Concerns 2.0 TDI models).

It is crucial to verify if all applicable recalls have been performed on the desired vehicle via the chassis number (VIN) at an Audi dealership.

Verdict

The Audi A5 B8 is a desirable car, offering an elegant design and impeccable build quality. However, its purchase on the used market requires careful consideration, especially regarding petrol engines.

If your budget allows and you prioritize reliability: * Opt for a post-facelift 2.0 TFSI (CNCD) (from 2012 onwards): This is the best compromise between performance, reliability, and fuel consumption. * The V6 3.0 TDI are also excellent choices for high-mileage drivers, offering torque and driving pleasure, with generally good reliability if well-maintained. * The V6 3.2 FSI and 3.0 TFSI (S5) are good choices for enthusiasts of more powerful engines, but require rigorous maintenance (decoking).

If your budget is more constrained: * Absolutely avoid the pre-facelift 1.8 TFSI (CDHB) and 2.0 TFSI (CDNB/CDNC) (before 2012), unless you have irrefutable proof that the engine has been repaired (piston ring replacement or complete engine replacement) by Audi or a recognized specialist, with invoices to support it. The risk of excessive oil consumption is too high, and the repair is very costly. * The 2.0 TDI are valid options, but check the DPF and EGR history.

Whatever your choice, a complete service history and a thorough test drive are essential. Prefer models with the S-Tronic or Tiptronic gearbox over the Multitronic. The A5 B8 remains an excellent car if you choose the right engine and are prepared to bear premium maintenance costs.
