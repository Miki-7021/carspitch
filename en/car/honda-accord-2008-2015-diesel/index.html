Honda Accord

VIII (CU) · 2008 – 2015

NEGOTIATE Reliability 82/100 — bon

Summary

The eighth-generation Honda Accord (CU code for the sedan, CW for the Tourer wagon) marks a clear move upmarket for the Japanese manufacturer, directly targeting premium German sedans (Audi A4, BMW 3 Series). Pay attention to the nomenclature: this European and Japanese (JDM) model was sold in North America under the premium Acura TSX badge, while North America had a specific Accord (CP/CS), which was larger and less dynamic.

Equipped with a sharp chassis, precise steering, and excellent build quality, the Accord VIII is an underrated but formidable road car. A facelift (Phase 2) occurred in 2011, bringing minor aesthetic touches, improved sound insulation, and, most importantly, crucial optimizations to the thermal and software management of the 2.2 i-DTEC diesel engine to limit DPF issues. While the gasoline engines are absolute benchmarks in terms of reliability, the i-DTEC diesel (which replaces the old i-CTDi) requires particular attention and strictly highway use.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional chassis: perfect compromise between comfort and dynamism (double wishbone front suspension).
  • Outstanding reliability of gasoline engines (2.0 and 2.4 i-VTEC).
  • Interior finish quality and material aging worthy of a premium car.
  • Excellent control ergonomics and driving position.
  • Perfectly smooth 6-speed manual gearbox (typical Honda).

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the 2.2 i-DTEC highly dependent on usage (fickle DPF, fragile clutch).
  • Disappointing rear passenger space for a 4.72 m car.
  • Sedan trunk volume (460L) penalized by intrusive wheel arches.
  • Infotainment system and GPS completely obsolete today.
  • Very high cost of genuine Honda spare parts.

Check before buying

  • On the 2.2 i-DTEC: Oil level (risk of dilution), DPF regeneration history, absence of jerks during acceleration (EGR).
  • On the 2.2 i-DTEC: Test the clutch in 3rd/4th gear under heavy load (slippage = clutch + dual-mass flywheel to be replaced, bill > €1500).
  • On the 2.4 i-VTEC: Listen to the engine during a cold start (a 2-second 'crr-crr' indicates a worn VTC actuator).
  • Steering rack: Check for any knocking or stiff spots when turning the wheel while stationary.
  • Automatic gearbox: Demand oil change invoices (every 60,000 km). An unserviced automatic gearbox can cause jerks.

Official recalls

  • - **Takata Airbags (Multiple years)** : Massive and critical recall for the replacement of front airbag inflators (risk of metallic fragment projection). - **Engine wiring harness (2011)** : On some 2008-2010 models, risk of the electrical harness rubbing under the hood, potentially causing short circuits. - **ECU / DPF Software (2011-2012)** : Software update at dealerships for 2.2 i-DTEC models to correct DPF alert thresholds and modify post-combustion injection strategy. - **Windshield trim (2010)** : Risk of detachment of the side windshield moldings at high speed.

Performance

  • Dynamic and Balanced Road Handling — Its exceptional chassis offers a perfect balance between agility and comfort, with precise steering and controlled body roll for an engaging driving experience.
  • Precise and Enjoyable Manual Gearbox — Appreciate the perfect handling of the 6-speed manual gearbox, typical of Honda, which contributes to a fluid and responsive driving experience.

Comfort

  • Premium Interior Quality and Refined Comfort — The cabin stands out with meticulous finish, high-quality materials, and excellent ergonomics, ensuring superior comfort on all your journeys.

Economy

  • Diesel Engine Optimized for the Road — The 2.2 i-DTEC engine offers smoothness, quietness, and generous torque, with controlled fuel consumption, ideal for long highway trips. Post-2011 models benefit from software optimizations for enhanced performance.

Alternatives

- **Europe** : Mazda 6 (similar dynamics, beware of 2.2 MZR-CD/Skyactiv-D diesels), Toyota Avensis (more placid but very reliable), BMW 3 Series E90/F30 (premium benchmark), Volkswagen Passat B6/B7. - **Japan / Asia** : Toyota Mark X, Nissan Skyline, Subaru Legacy. - **North America (vs Acura TSX)** : Lexus IS 250, Audi A4, Volvo S60.

Verdict

The Honda Accord VIII is a fantastic sedan, often unfairly overlooked in Europe. However, the choice of engine entirely dictates the ownership experience. **If you're aiming for the 2.2 i-DTEC diesel**, caution is advised: it's not inherently bad, but it tolerates short trips very poorly. A clear service history, a recent clutch, and 100% road/highway use are mandatory to avoid costly DPF and dual-mass flywheel problems. On the other hand, **if you opt for a gasoline model (2.0 or 2.4 i-VTEC)**, you're buying one of the most reliable cars of its generation, capable of reaching astronomical mileages with simple routine maintenance.
