Summary

The Buick Encore (first generation) is a premium subcompact crossover SUV built on General Motors' Gamma II platform, sharing its underpinnings with the Chevrolet Trax and Opel/Vauxhall Mokka. Introduced for the 2013 model year, it became a massive sales success, effectively popularizing the premium subcompact SUV segment in North America. Praised for its maneuverability, elevated driving position, and exceptionally quiet cabin (thanks to Buick's 'QuietTuning' acoustic laminated glass and active noise cancellation), the Encore is an excellent urban commuter. However, its long-term reputation is heavily marred by the reliability issues of its base 1.4L turbocharged engine, particularly in the early production years (2013-2016).

Strengths 5

  • Exceptionally quiet cabin (Buick QuietTuning technology)
  • Compact footprint makes it highly maneuverable in urban environments
  • User-friendly and responsive infotainment system (especially 2017+ models)
  • Affordable entry point into the premium crossover segment
  • Good crash test safety ratings

Weaknesses 5

  • Sluggish acceleration with the base 138-hp 1.4L LUV engine
  • Severe mechanical design flaws in early models (PCV system, cooling system)
  • Cramped rear seating and limited cargo capacity compared to rivals
  • Poor fuel economy for a vehicle of its size and weight
  • Faux leather (leatherette) seats prone to cracking on the side bolsters

1.4L Turbo (LE2)AverageBetter performance and generally more reliable than the LUV, but susceptible to direct injection carbon buildup on intake valves and premature battery wear due to the Auto Start/Stop system.

Check before buying

  • Listen for a high-pitched whistling sound at idle (indicates a ruptured PCV diaphragm in the valve cover).
  • Check for oil leaks around the valve cover gasket and the front crankshaft seal (often blown out by PCV pressure).
  • Inspect the coolant reservoir level and look for crusty orange residue around the water pump and plastic coolant return lines.
  • Test the air conditioning system thoroughly; AC compressor failures are common.
  • Check for a Check Engine Light (CEL) indicating code P0299 (Turbo Underboost), which requires turbocharger replacement.

Official recalls

Based on official NHTSA data, the Buick Encore has been subject to several critical recalls:

  • [2017-07-10] 17V437000 (AIR BAGS): Frontal airbags may not deploy properly in certain 2014-2017 models.
  • [2016-09-08] 16V651000 (AIR BAGS): Frontal airbag deployment software issue.
  • [2016-07-05] 16V502000 (IGNITION): Electronic park lock lever may allow the ignition key to be removed without the transmission being in PARK (2016-2017 models).
  • [2015-03-20] 15V162000 (LABELS): Incorrect tire placard labels on 2015 models.
  • [2015-03-13] 15V146000 (STEERING): Loss of electric power steering assist (EPAS) in 2015 models.
  • [2014-08-08] 14V488000 (SEAT BELTS): Front seat belt pretensioners may not lock properly in 2013 models.
  • [2014-07-23] 14V446000 (SEATS): Incomplete welds on seat hook brackets (2013-2014 models).
  • [2014-07-02] 14V417000 (ELECTRICAL): Software issue may cause the infotainment screen to go blank or fail to chime (2013-2014 AWD models).
  • [2014-06-06] 14V298000 (AIR BAGS): Passenger airbag may not deploy properly (2013 models).
  • [2013-03-28] 13V105000 (STEERING): Steering wheel fastener may not have been properly torqued on models with heated steering wheels (2013 models).

Verdict

The Buick Encore is a comfortable, quiet, and well-packaged urban crossover, but it requires extreme caution on the used market. Early models (2013-2016) are plagued by the 1.4L LUV engine's inherent design flaws, specifically regarding the PCV system and cooling components. If you are considering an Encore, strongly prioritize the 2017-2019 models equipped with the upgraded 1.4L LE2 engine (153 hp), which resolves many of the earlier mechanical headaches and offers a vastly improved interior. Avoid early models without a meticulous service history.
