Summary

The fourth-generation Ford Mondeo, known by the internal code BA7, was launched in 2007 and marked a turning point for Ford in the large family car segment. Based on the Ford EUCD platform (shared with the S-Max and Galaxy), it stood out with a more assertive design, inspired by the Iosis concept, and universally praised road handling. Available as a 4-door saloon, 5-door (hatchback), and estate (Clipper/SW), it offered generous interior space and great comfort. A significant facelift occurred in 2010 (often called Mk4.5 or Mondeo FL), bringing aesthetic (front fascia, LED lights) and technical (new, more powerful EcoBoost and TDCi engines) evolutions. Overall, the Mondeo IV is an excellent road car, offering a very attractive price/performance ratio on the used market, provided you choose the right engine and check its service history.

Strengths 6

  • Dynamic and comfortable road handling, excellent chassis.
  • Generous interior space and boot volume, especially in the estate version.
  • Improved finish quality compared to the previous generation.
  • Good price/performance ratio on the used car market.
  • Very reliable and robust 2.0 and 2.3 Duratec petrol engines.
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation on long journeys.

Weaknesses 6

  • 1.6 Duratec petrol and 1.8 TDCi diesel engines a bit underpowered and less reliable.
  • Reliability of 2.5 Duratec Turbo and 2.0 EcoBoost more demanding in maintenance and potentially costly.
  • Onboard electronics sometimes temperamental (multimedia system, various sensors).
  • Large turning radius, making city maneuvers more difficult.
  • Fuel consumption of large petrol engines (2.5T, 2.0 EcoBoost) can be high during dynamic driving.
  • Rapid tire wear on heavy or powerful versions.

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices, especially for turbo engines (petrol and diesel).
  • Condition of the dual-mass flywheel (noises, vibrations on start/stop or at low rpm) on diesels and the 2.5T.
  • Functioning of the air conditioning and all onboard electronics (GPS, Bluetooth, sensors).
  • Absence of leaks (oil, coolant) under the vehicle and in the engine compartment.
  • Condition of tires and brakes (discs and pads) due to the vehicle's weight.
  • Suspicious noises in the timing system (ticking, knocking) on the 1.6 Duratec and 2.0 EcoBoost.
  • Condition of the DPF (diesel particulate filter) on diesels, especially if the vehicle has done a lot of city driving.

Official recalls

Here is a non-exhaustive list of official manufacturer recalls for the Ford Mondeo IV (BA7):

  • 2007-2008 (Europe) : Risk of short circuit and fire due to an electrical harness under the front passenger seat. (Affected population: vehicles produced between February 2007 and March 2008).
  • 2008 (Europe) : Brake pedal attachment problem that could lead to a loss of braking efficiency. (Affected population: vehicles produced during a specific period in 2008).
  • 2011 (Europe) : Risk of corrosion on brake lines, which could lead to brake fluid leakage and reduced braking efficiency. (Affected population: vehicles produced between 2007 and 2010).
  • 2012 (Europe) : Risk of fuel leakage at the fuel filter, which could lead to a fuel smell or fire risk. (Affected population: vehicles equipped with certain diesel engines).
  • 2013 (Europe) : Engine cooling fan control module overheating problem, which could lead to a fire risk. (Affected population: vehicles produced between 2007 and 2011).
  • 2014 (Europe) : Corrosion problem on the electric power assisted steering (EPAS) system that could lead to a loss of steering assistance. (Affected population: vehicles produced between 2007 and 2013).

Verdict

The Ford Mondeo IV (BA7) is an excellent proposition on the used car market for anyone looking for a versatile, comfortable, and dynamic large family car without breaking the bank. Its road handling is one of its major strengths, often placing it above its direct competitors. For a worry-free purchase, prioritize the 2.0 Duratec and 2.3 Duratec petrol engines, which are exceptionally robust and do not suffer from the ailments of more complex turbo engines. They are perfect for versatile use and long journeys.

If you are a high-mileage driver and diesel is essential, the 2.0 TDCi and 2.2 TDCi are good choices, provided you check the service history (dual-mass flywheel, DPF, injectors) and avoid models that have done too much city driving. The 1.6 Duratec petrol and 1.8 TDCi diesel should be considered with caution, as their reliability is more erratic and their performance a bit lacking for the car's size.

As for the 2.5 Duratec Turbo and 2.0 EcoBoost, they offer very enjoyable performance but require more rigorous maintenance and can lead to higher repair costs in case of problems. They are aimed at informed buyers, willing to invest in meticulous mechanical follow-up.

In all cases, a complete service history and a thorough test drive are essential. The Mondeo IV remains a safe bet if the engine choice is judicious and maintenance is respected.
