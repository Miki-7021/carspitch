Summary

The third-generation Porsche Cayenne (Type 9YA), launched in 2018 and heavily facelifted in 2023, is based on the Volkswagen Group's MLB Evo platform (shared with the Audi Q8, Bentley Bentayga, and Lamborghini Urus). This generation marks a historic turning point for the model with the definitive abandonment of diesel engines in favor of a highly advanced plug-in hybrid (PHEV) strategy. Available in classic and 'Coupé' body styles (since 2019), the Cayenne III stands out for its exceptional dynamic behavior for its weight, top-flight finish, and advanced technological integration. The 2023 facelift brought a new interior (inspired by the Taycan), revamped engines (the V8 makes its return on the S version), and higher-capacity hybrid batteries (25.9 kWh). Although overall mechanical reliability is sharply up compared to previous generations (ADAC breakdown rate of 2.8, the best in the historical lineup), the onboard electronics and air suspension remain points of vigilance in the long term.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional road handling, masking the vehicle's weight (especially with rear-wheel steering and PDCC)
  • Benchmark interior finish quality and assembly in the category
  • Highly refined E-Hybrid versions: real electric range (especially post-2023) and seamless thermal/electric transition
  • Overall mechanical reliability sharply up (ADAC rate of 2.8, the best in the model's history)
  • Strong residual value on the used market (particularly PHEV and GTS versions)

Weaknesses 5

  • Excessive weight of E-Hybrid versions (up to 2.6 tons), leading to rapid wear of consumables (tires, brake pads)
  • Recurring software bugs on ADAS systems and the infotainment screen (reported by ADAC and What Car?)
  • Complex and expensive-to-repair air suspension (possible leaks after 100,000 km, a historical weak point highlighted by the TÜV)
  • Prohibitive cost of options and maintenance in the Porsche network
  • TÜV defect rate at 3 years (18.5%) slightly above average, often failing for running gear or electronics details

Check before buying

  • On E-Hybrids: Demand a battery State of Health (SoH) certificate and test charging on a public station and a domestic outlet.
  • Air suspension: Check that the vehicle raises and lowers without excessive compressor noise and does not sag after being parked overnight.
  • Electronics: Test all driving aids (ADAS), the rearview camera (prone to recalls), and the fluidity of the central screen.
  • Running gear: Inspect tire wear (especially on the inside) and the condition of the brake discs (tungsten carbide-coated PSCB brakes cost a fortune to replace).
  • Maintenance history: A Cayenne must have a 100% clear service history (Porsche or recognized specialist) to retain its value and its Porsche Approved warranty.

Verdict

The Porsche Cayenne III (9YA) is arguably the most dynamic and refined luxury SUV in its segment. The E-Hybrid versions, which account for the majority of sales in Europe, offer remarkable versatility, especially since the 2023 facelift that equips the vehicle with a 25.9 kWh battery providing genuine electric range. In terms of mechanical reliability, the V6 and V8 engines are robust (very low ADAC breakdown rate of 2.8). However, the record is tarnished by recurring electronic bugs (infotainment, ADAS, cameras) and an air suspension that, while high-performing, remains a long-term weak point (highlighted by the TÜV). The colossal weight of the hybrid versions also generates significant running costs (tires, brakes). It is a vehicle to **BUY** if you prioritize driving pleasure and benefit from a 'Porsche Approved' warranty, but to **NEGOTIATE** with caution on the used market outside the network, demanding a clear history and a high-voltage battery test.
