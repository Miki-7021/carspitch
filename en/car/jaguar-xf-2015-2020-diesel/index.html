All Jaguar models

Summary

The second-generation Jaguar XF (code name X260) marks a major technical break from its predecessor (X250). Based on the iQ-Al (D7a) modular platform, it is made of 75% aluminum, allowing it to shed up to 190 kg on the scale. This weight loss greatly benefits driving dynamics, making the XF one of the most agile sedans in its segment. However, while the road handling and suspension comfort are praised, the interior finish quality of the early model years disappointed compared to the German trio. Mechanically, this generation introduces the "Ingenium" modular engine family (2.0L 4-cylinder), intended to offer efficiency and performance. Unfortunately, the diesel version (which accounts for the majority of sales in Europe) quickly revealed serious design flaws, tarnishing the model's overall reliability record before its facelift at the end of 2020.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional aluminum chassis (agility and steering precision)
  • Ride comfort and soundproofing at high speeds
  • Elegant and timeless exterior design
  • Drivability of the V6 engines (petrol and diesel)
  • Generous trunk volume (540 liters)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of the 2.0 Ingenium diesel engine (chain, DPF, turbo)
  • Disappointing interior plastic quality on early model years (2015-2017)
  • Slow and buggy InControl Touch infotainment system before 2018
  • Steep depreciation, particularly on diesels
  • Sparse dealer network and cost of spare parts

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: avoid any diesel model that followed the 34,000 km oil change interval.
  • Metallic noise at the rear of the engine on cold startup (sign of a stretched timing chain on the 2.0d).
  • Oil level on the 2.0d: a level above the maximum indicates dilution by diesel fuel (danger to the engine).
  • Operation of the center screen and onboard electronics (frequent black screens).
  • Condition of body panel alignments and rattling noises in the cabin on rough roads.

Official recalls

  • Takata Airbags (Multiple recalls: 2016 to 2026): The propellant in the driver/passenger airbag inflator may degrade over time and with humidity, risking the projection of metal fragments upon deployment.
  • Seat belts (02/09/2022): The pretensioner tubes may have been damaged during assembly, reducing their effectiveness in the event of an impact.
  • Fuel system (01/07/2022): Manufacturing defect on the fuel return pipe (diesel models), risk of leakage.
  • Fuel rail (02/02/2018 - NHTSA 18V090000): On 2.0L petrol engines (2018 models), risk of fuel leak at the fuel rail end caps.
  • Airbags / SRS (15/02/2018 - NHTSA 18V112000): Incorrect calibration of the supplemental restraint system (SRS) on 2018 Sportbrake versions, which could prevent the proper deployment of the frontal airbags.
  • Dashboard (26/10/2017 - NHTSA 17V678000): The digital instrument cluster may intermittently go blank (black screens) on 2017-2018 models.
  • Structure (09/02/2017 - NHTSA 17V084000): Possible cracking of the left inner sill panel on certain 2017 models, compromising protection in the event of a side impact.

Verdict

The Jaguar XF (X260) is a sedan with fantastic road handling, betrayed by the catastrophic reliability of its best-selling engine: the 2.0d Ingenium. Buying a 4-cylinder diesel version is a risky bet that requires a flawless maintenance history (oil changes every 10,000 km) and, ideally, an already replaced timing chain. If you insist on driving an XF, favor the petrol engines (2.0t or the melodious Supercharged V6) or the 3.0d V6 diesel for high mileage drivers. Negotiate prices firmly, as the depreciation of this model is particularly severe due to its reputation.
