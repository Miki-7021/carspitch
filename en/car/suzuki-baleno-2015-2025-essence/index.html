Suzuki Baleno

II · 2015 – 2025

BUY Reliability 91/100 — excellent

Summary

The Suzuki Baleno II marks the return of the nameplate on a versatile city car (B-segment) based on the lightweight *Heartect* platform. Designed to offer an exceptional space-to-footprint ratio, it stands out with its featherlight weight (often under a ton), ensuring very low fuel consumption and excellent agility. While it left the European market at the end of 2019 (a victim of stricter emission standards and internal competition from the Swift), it continues a triumphant career in India (under the Maruti Suzuki badge), Africa, and Latin America, where it received a major facelift in 2022. It is also sold under the names Toyota Glanza or Toyota Starlet in some markets. It is an ultra-rational choice: reliable, spacious (355 L trunk), and economical, although the perceived quality (hard plastics, sound insulation, thin paint) betrays its aggressive pricing.

Strengths 6

  • Rear passenger space worthy of the segment above (legroom)
  • Excellent trunk volume (355 liters)
  • Exceptionally low fuel consumption (especially with the 1.2 Dualjet)
  • Very high overall mechanical reliability
  • Very competitive price/equipment ratio
  • Featherlight weight promoting agility and economy of consumables (brakes, tires)

Weaknesses 6

  • Interior plastic quality (hard and prone to scratches)
  • Poor sound insulation (wind and road noise on the highway)
  • Very thin paint, susceptible to stone chips and micro-scratches
  • Suspension a bit firm at low speeds and jittery on degraded roads
  • Steering lacking feel (vague around the center point)
  • Small fuel tank (37 liters)

Check before buying

  • Suspension: Clunking noises over speed bumps (fragile anti-roll bar bushings or shock absorber mounts).
  • Bodywork: Paint chips on the hood and front bumper, early signs of corrosion on the underbody if the vehicle has been driven in very salty regions.
  • Clutch: On manual gearbox versions, test the biting point (sometimes premature wear in 100% urban use).
  • Electronics: Operation of the Bosch infotainment system (frequent bugs, touchscreen sometimes temperamental, requires updates).
  • Air conditioning: Compressor efficiency (some cases of refrigerant gas leaks reported).

Official recalls

  • May 2016 (Airbags): Software update for the airbag control module (risk of non-deployment or inadvertent deployment).
  • May 2019 (Braking - India/Global): Replacement of a vacuum pump hose for brake assist that could become clogged and stiffen the brake pedal.
  • November 2021 (Fuel Pump): Massive recall at Suzuki (including the Baleno) to replace the low-pressure fuel pump impeller (risk of engine stalling).
  • 2022 (Airbags - India): Recall on facelifted models for a potential airbag controller defect.

Verdict

The Suzuki Baleno II is the archetype of a smart purchase. If you're looking for a prestigious or luxurious vehicle, look elsewhere. However, if you want a city car capable of comfortably accommodating 4 adults, with a large trunk, that never breaks down, and consumes less than 5L/100 km in real-world conditions, it's an unbeatable choice. The 1.2 Dualjet (with or without SHVS) engine is the absolute rational choice. The 1.0 Boosterjet provides real pep for road trips but will require slightly more rigorous maintenance. Buy with your eyes closed after a simple check of the service history and bodywork condition.
