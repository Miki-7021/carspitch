Summary

The Renault Fluence (L30) is a compact three-box sedan based on the Mégane III platform, but with an extended wheelbase. Initially designed for emerging markets, Eastern Europe, Turkey, and South America (where sedans with boots are popular), it was also marketed in Western Europe. It stands out for its generous interior space, suspension comfort, and immense 530-liter boot. The facelift (Phase 2) that occurred in late 2012 / early 2013 gave it Renault's new visual identity (large diamond logo on a black grille). Although its interior finish is a step below the Mégane III (harder plastics), it proves to be a pragmatic and economical choice on the used market, particularly with its fully mature diesel (dCi) engines.

Strengths 5

  • Generous rear passenger space and immense boot volume (530 liters)
  • Excellent suspension comfort, 'pullman' style (ideal on degraded roads)
  • Overall reliability of dCi diesel engines (mature generation)
  • Unbeatable price/performance ratio on the used market
  • Rich equipment on high trim levels (Privilège, Initiale)

Weaknesses 5

  • Interior plastic quality inferior to the Mégane III (easily scratched)
  • Imperfect sound insulation at high speed (wind noise)
  • Capricious and expensive-to-repair EDC automatic gearbox (dual-clutch)
  • Phase 1 design considered clumsy by some
  • Significant depreciation (although an advantage when buying)

Check before buying

  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) maintenance history on dCi models: avoid models used exclusively in the city.
  • EDC gearbox behavior (on 1.5 dCi 110): no jerking, shuddering, or slipping should be felt on startup.
  • Front suspension noises: fragile shock absorber mounts and stabilizer bar links.
  • Air conditioning operation: compressor or condenser sometimes leaky.
  • Onboard electronics: TomTom/R-Link GPS screen bugs, wear of hands-free card buttons.

Official recalls

  • November 2011: Injection computer reprogramming on 1.5 dCi (risk of stalling or loss of power).
  • February 2012: Inspection and possible replacement of the steering column (risk of loss of control, very low volume affected).
  • May 2015: Risk of rupture of a front brake line (friction against a suspension component).
  • 2016 (Specific Markets): Recall for front airbags (campaign related to Takata supplier in certain hot/humid regions).

Verdict

The Renault Fluence is the quintessential rational choice. Shunned in Western Europe for its sedan body style, it nevertheless offers first-rate family performance for a ridiculously low price on the used market. For diesel, it's an excellent choice: the 1.5 dCi (especially the 110 hp manual) and the 1.6 dCi 130 hp are reliable, economical engines perfectly suited to the car's size. However, it is necessary to AVOID the first-generation EDC automatic gearbox, which is a source of significant costs, and to steer clear of the Z.E. electric version, whose battery technology is completely obsolete. Opt for a Phase 2 (after 2013) with a manual gearbox for a worry-free purchase.
