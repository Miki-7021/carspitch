Summary

The third-generation Honda CR-V (code name RE) marked a major turning point for the model. Gone was the adventurer look with the spare wheel on the tailgate: it adopted an urban crossover design, a lowered center of gravity, and road handling much closer to a sedan. Highly popular with families for its exceptional spaciousness and modularity (sliding bench seat), it benefited from a facelift in late 2009 / early 2010. This restyling introduced aesthetic touches, improved sound insulation, and most importantly, the replacement of the 2.2 i-CTDi diesel engine with the new 2.2 i-DTEC, available for the first time with an automatic gearbox. Overall, it is one of the most reliable SUVs of its generation, although diesel versions require rigorous maintenance and appropriate use.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional spaciousness and flat rear floor
  • Sliding and reclining rear bench seat (rare modularity)
  • Overall reliability far superior to the category average
  • Safe road handling and suspension comfort
  • Very pleasant and quiet diesel engines (i-CTDi and i-DTEC)
  • Assembly quality and aging of interior materials

Weaknesses 6

  • Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) very intolerant of urban driving (diesels)
  • Imperfect sound insulation on the highway (road noise, improved after 2010)
  • Hard dashboard plastics prone to scratches
  • Original multimedia/GPS system completely obsolete today
  • Non-permanent 4x4 transmission (reactive but not designed for true off-roading)
  • Sometimes rapid wear of tires and brake pads

Check before buying

  • Exhaust gas smell in the cabin when stationary, hood open (sign of a cracked manifold on the 2.2 i-CTDi).
  • Groaning or rubbing noise from the rear during low-speed maneuvers with full steering lock (requires a rear differential fluid change).
  • Air conditioning operation: if it blows hot after a few minutes, the compressor clutch is likely faulty.
  • Shaking or slipping during strong accelerations in 3rd/4th gear on the i-CTDi (worn dual-mass flywheel and clutch).
  • Oil level on diesels (must not exceed the maximum mark, sign of dilution by diesel fuel).
  • Condition of front shock absorber boots (tendency to tear).

Verdict

The Honda CR-V III is an excellent used car choice for a family looking for a spacious and reliable SUV. If you're aiming for a diesel, the 2.2 i-DTEC (post-2010) is preferable for its better DPF management and compatibility with the automatic gearbox. The 2.2 i-CTDi remains a good engine, but requires checking that the exhaust manifold and clutch have been replaced. Warning: avoid diesels if you primarily drive in the city, the DPF will be very expensive for you. For mixed or urban use, the 2.0 i-VTEC petrol engine is a model of absolute reliability, although a bit thirstier.
