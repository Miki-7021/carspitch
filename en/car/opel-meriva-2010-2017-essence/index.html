All Opel models

Summary

The Opel Meriva B compact MPV, launched in 2010, succeeded the first generation with a bold proposition: FlexDoors rear suicide doors. This unique feature, facilitating access to the cabin, became its signature. Based on the Opel Corsa D and Astra J platform, the Meriva B positions itself as a practical and modular family vehicle, offering generous interior space and the FlexSpace rear seating system. A minor facelift occurred in late 2013 for the 2014 model year, bringing minor aesthetic changes and the introduction of the 1.6 CDTI diesel engine. Overall, the Meriva B is a reliable vehicle, but certain engines, notably the 1.4 Turbo petrol and 1.7 CDTI, have experienced recurring weaknesses that require particular attention during purchase and maintenance.

Strengths 6

  • Interior modularity and FlexSpace system
  • Easy access with FlexDoors rear suicide doors
  • Suspension comfort
  • Good interior space for its category
  • Raised driving position
  • Reliable 1.4 naturally aspirated and 1.6 CDTI (post-2014) engines

Weaknesses 5

  • Imperfect reliability of 1.4 Turbo petrol engines (timing chain)
  • Poor reliability of 1.7 CDTI engines (injectors, DPF, flywheel)
  • Steering sometimes considered too assisted
  • Limited rear visibility
  • FlexDoors design that may deter some buyers

Check before buying

  • 1.4 Turbo Petrol Engines: Timing chain noises on cold start (metallic rattling), water pump leaks, oil consumption. Prioritize models after 2013.
  • 1.7 CDTI Diesel Engines: Excessive smoke, jerking, abnormal noises (injectors, flywheel), DPF warning light on.
  • All Engines: Condition of ignition coils (petrol), operation of EGR valve and DPF (diesel).
  • General: FlexDoors operation (noises, play), tire condition (possible irregular wear), air conditioning function, onboard electronics (screen, GPS, Bluetooth).
  • Complete service history and invoices, especially for timing chain or injector replacements.

Official recalls

Official Recalls (Opel Meriva B)

  • 2010-2011 (Production date): Potential problem with fuel tank mounting. Risk of leakage in case of impact. Verification and reinforcement of the mounting.
  • 2011 (Production date): Problem with the tightening of the flywheel mounting bolt on some vehicles equipped with diesel engines. Risk of loosening and noise. Verification and re-tightening/replacement.
  • 2012 (Production date): Potential malfunction of the electric power steering column control module. May lead to loss of assistance. Reprogramming or replacement of the module.
  • 2013 (Production date): Electrical harness problem at the tailgate, potentially leading to a short circuit or malfunction of the lights. Verification and repair of the harness.
  • 2014 (Production date): Risk of fuel leakage at the fuel filter on some diesel models. Verification and replacement of the filter.

*Note: This list is indicative and not exhaustive. It is imperative to check with an Opel dealership whether the targeted vehicle has been subject to recalls and if the corrective actions have been carried out.*

What sets it apart 6

practicality

  • Smart FlexSpace Modularity and Easy Access — Benefit from a clever interior thanks to the FlexSpace system, allowing quick rear seat configuration to adapt to all your needs. The opposing FlexDoors rear doors offer exceptionally easy access, ideal for families.

comfort

  • Comfortable Suspension and Elevated Driving Position — The Meriva B offers excellent suspension comfort, effectively absorbing road irregularities for pleasant journeys. Its elevated driving position provides better visibility and a sense of control.

reliability

  • Robust 1.4 Atmospheric Petrol Engine (100 hp) — The 100 hp 1.4 atmospheric petrol engine is renowned for its mechanical simplicity, reliability, and reduced maintenance costs, offering appreciable flexibility in urban environments.

safety

  • High-Level Passive Safety — The Meriva B achieved 5 stars in Euro NCAP tests, with impressive scores of 89% for adult protection and 82% for child protection, ensuring peace of mind for the whole family.
  • Effective and Predictable Braking — The Meriva B's braking performance is adequate and predictable for its category, with stopping distances from 100 km/h generally between 39 and 41 meters, ensuring serene control in all circumstances.
  • Stable Road Holding and Agile Steering — The Meriva B prioritizes comfort and stability, offering a smooth and predictable ride. Its light steering facilitates urban maneuvers, while well-controlled body roll ensures a sense of security.

  • Japan/Asia: Honda Jazz/Fit (smaller), Toyota Verso-S/Ractis.

  • Emerging Markets: Dacia Lodgy (larger and more rustic), Chevrolet Spin (South America, larger).

Verdict

The Meriva B is a clever and comfortable compact MPV, ideal for families seeking modularity and easy access. However, engine choice is crucial for long-term reliability. We highly recommend the 1.4 naturally aspirated petrol versions (100 hp) for their simplicity and robustness, or the 1.6 CDTI diesel versions (from 2014 onwards) for their driving pleasure and improved reliability. The 1.4 Turbo petrol and 1.7 CDTI diesel engines should be approached with caution, prioritizing post-2013 models for the 1.4T and demanding an impeccable service history for the 1.7 CDTI. A well-chosen and maintained Meriva B can be an excellent family companion, but a thorough inspection is essential.
