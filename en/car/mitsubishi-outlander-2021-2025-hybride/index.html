Mitsubishi Outlander

IV (2021–2025) · 2021 – 2025

All Mitsubishi models

Summary

The **fourth-generation Mitsubishi Outlander (GN)** marks a complete break from its predecessor. Based on the Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi Alliance's CMF-CD platform (shared with the Nissan Rogue/X-Trail T33), it makes a spectacular leap in perceived quality, spaciousness, and onboard technology.

While North America and Oceania initially received a Nissan-sourced naturally aspirated gasoline engine, it is the **PHEV (Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicle)** version that forms the global core of the range and marks the model's return to Europe in late 2024. This PHEV version retains the robust 2.4L (4B12) engine from the previous generation, but combines it with more powerful electric motors (85 kW front, 100 kW rear) and a significantly larger battery (20 kWh vs. 13.8 kWh), offering a real-world electric range of approximately 70 to 80 km. The S-AWC (Super-All Wheel Control) system remains one of the best electrified all-wheel drive systems on the market.

Strengths 6

  • Extremely mature and reliable PHEV system (Gen 3 heritage)
  • Excellent real-world electric range (20 kWh battery)
  • Highly effective S-AWC all-wheel drive on snow/dirt
  • Spectacular increase in interior finish quality (materials, assembly)
  • Spaciousness and possibility of 7 seats (depending on markets/versions)
  • Often generous manufacturer warranty (up to 5 or 8 years depending on country)

Weaknesses 5

  • Noisy internal combustion engine under strong acceleration (e-CVT 'rubber band' effect)
  • High fuel consumption with empty battery (often > 8 L/100 km on highway)
  • Infotainment system (Nissan-sourced) sometimes slow and prone to bugs
  • Very high weight of the PHEV version (over 2.1 tons)
  • Third row of seats (7-seater) purely symbolic (children only)

Check before buying

  • Traction battery State of Health (SOH) via OBD diagnostic before used purchase.
  • Software update history (crucial for inverter and 12V battery management).
  • Tire wear (high weight and instant torque quickly wear out tires, especially at the front).
  • Functionality of the central screen and rearview camera (frequent bugs on 2021-2022 models).
  • Service book: the PHEV's internal combustion engine requires annual oil changes even if it has run little.

Official recalls

  • - **Rearview Camera (2022)**: The infotainment screen may remain black when shifting into reverse due to a software error. Update required (mainly concerns North America). - **Fuel Pump (2021-2022)**: On 2.5L versions, possible fuel pump failure leading to engine stalling (problem shared with Nissan). - **Inverter (2023)**: On PHEV versions, a software error can lead to a loss of motive power under specific conditions. Inverter ECU reprogramming. - **Seatbelts (2022)**: Possible improper locking of the seatbelt retractor on certain production batches.

Verdict

The Mitsubishi Outlander IV is an excellent choice, particularly in its **PHEV** version. By partnering with Nissan for the chassis and interior, Mitsubishi has corrected the main flaws of the previous generation (dated finish, average soundproofing) while retaining what it does best: its plug-in hybrid powertrain and S-AWC transmission. Mechanical reliability is excellent, with the 2.4L engine being indestructible. The only real pitfalls concern early electronic bugs (infotainment) and high fuel consumption on the highway once the battery is depleted. It is a highly recommendable purchase for families who can charge daily.
