Summary

The fifth-generation Ford Mondeo (internal code CD391), also known as the Ford Fusion in North America and some Asian markets, was launched in Europe in 2014, although it had been available in the United States since 2012. Representing the D-segment, it positions itself as a spacious and comfortable family sedan, offering excellent value for money. It was praised for its modern design (inspired by the Evos concept), dynamic road handling, and well-equipped interior, particularly with the SYNC infotainment system. A minor facelift occurred in 2019, bringing subtle aesthetic changes and technological updates, including the introduction of new diesel powertrains and improvements to the hybrid system. The Mondeo V is a generally reliable car, but certain powertrains, especially the small and medium-displacement EcoBoost engines, have experienced specific issues that warrant particular attention. Its versatility makes it an interesting choice for families and high-mileage drivers.

  • Reliability of 1.0 and 1.5 EcoBoost engines (design issues)

  • SYNC infotainment system sometimes slow or buggy
  • Limited rear visibility on the sedan
  • Fuel consumption of non-hybrid gasoline versions can be high in dynamic driving
  • Decreasing resale value for internal combustion versions

Check before buying

  • Complete service history and invoices (especially for EcoBoost)
  • Coolant level and color (absence of bubbles, sludge, abnormal drop)
  • Engine noises (knocking, tapping, abnormal belt noises)
  • SYNC system operation (fluidity, connectivity)
  • Tire and brake condition (even wear)
  • Air conditioning operation
  • Absence of engine warning lights or dashboard faults
  • For hybrids: traction battery condition (via a Ford diagnostic if possible)
  • For diesels: FAP and EGR valve condition (usage history)

Verdict

The Ford Mondeo V (CD391) is a family sedan that offers an excellent compromise between comfort, dynamism, and spaciousness. Its design remains modern, and its equipment is often generous. However, its reliability record is mixed, mainly due to the small and medium-displacement EcoBoost gasoline engines (1.0L and 1.5L) which have experienced serious design problems, particularly coolant intrusion.

Verdict: NEGOTIATE, or even AVOID for certain versions.

  • BUY: If you opt for the 2.5 L Duratec (NA) versions, the 2.0 L EcoBoost (with clear history and post-2017 for NA), or especially the hybrid versions (2.0 L Duratec Hybrid/Energi). The latter are the most reliable and economical to use. The 2.0 TDCi diesels are also good choices for high-mileage drivers, provided rigorous maintenance.
  • NEGOTIATE: If you are interested in a 1.5 L EcoBoost post-2019 (with engine fixes) and the price is very attractive, after a thorough mechanical inspection and a clear history. For the 1.0 L EcoBoost, negotiation is appropriate, and a preventive belt replacement should be budgeted for.
  • AVOID: The 1.5 L EcoBoost from the first years of production (before 2019), due to the high risk of major engine problems. The risk does not justify the purchase, even at a low price, unless the engine has been replaced or fully overhauled with corrected parts.

In all cases, a complete service history and inspection by a professional are essential. The Mondeo V remains a pleasant car to live with, but the choice of powertrain is paramount for a serene experience.
