Opel Corsa

D · 2006 – 2014

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

All Opel models

Summary

The Opel Corsa D (fourth generation) marks a major stylistic and technical break from its predecessor. Based on the SCCS platform co-developed with Fiat (shared with the Grande Punto), it grew considerably (nearly 4 meters) and gained in interior space, but also gained significant weight (up to 200 kg more than the Corsa C). This excess weight penalizes entry-level engines. It underwent two notable evolutions: a technical update in 2010 (chassis, steering, transition to Euro 5 standards with 'A' engines) and an aesthetic restyling in 2011 (redesigned front end). Well-built and safe, its overall reliability is average, marred by chronic weaknesses on certain peripherals (ignition coils, timing chains) and the notoriously fragile 6-speed manual gearbox (M32).

Weaknesses 5

  • Excessive weight penalizing performance and fuel consumption
  • Easytronic automated manual gearbox is slow, harsh, and unreliable
  • Fragile 6-speed manual gearbox (M32) (bearings)
  • Poor rear visibility (especially in 3-door models)
  • Uneven interior finish (hard plastics prone to scratches)

Check before buying

  • Timing chain noise: A metallic rattling sound on cold start in gasoline engines (1.0, 1.2, 1.4) or the 1.3 CDTI indicates a stretched chain that needs urgent replacement.
  • M32 gearbox (6-speed): On 1.4T, 1.6T, 1.3 CDTI 90 and 1.7 CDTI. Listen for any whistling in 5th and 6th gear. Check if the gear lever moves back and forth during accelerations/decelerations.
  • Cabin ventilation: If the ventilation only works on the maximum speed (4), the blower motor resistor is burnt out (very common but inexpensive failure).
  • Power steering (EPS): Stiff spots in the steering or clunking noises in the steering column (steering angle sensor or faulty electric motor).
  • Easytronic gearbox: Avoid models equipped with this automated manual gearbox (jerks, very expensive clutch actuator failures).

What sets it apart 7

Safety

  • 5-Star Euro NCAP Safety — The Corsa D achieved the maximum 5-star rating in Euro NCAP tests, ensuring optimal protection for occupants, with an 89% score for adults and 82% for children.

Braking

  • Efficient and Controlled Braking — Enjoy a responsive and stable braking system, offering excellent performance for its category. Stopping distances are short, typically around 38-39 meters from 100 km/h, ensuring enhanced safety.

Handling

  • Safe and Predictable Road Handling — The Corsa D offers balanced and secure road handling. Its suspension effectively absorbs irregularities, providing a pleasant drive and good daily stability, even in urban environments.

Practicality

  • Generous Interior Space — Benefit from generous interior space for a city car, offering significant comfort for all passengers, especially in the rear. Ideal for families or long journeys.

Economy

  • A Smart Investment — The Corsa D represents an exceptional opportunity in the used car market, offering a very attractive acquisition cost for a well-equipped and versatile vehicle.

Reliability

  • 1.4 Twinport Engine: The Choice for Peace of Mind — Opt for the 1.4 Twinport engine (90 or 100 hp) with a 5-speed manual gearbox, renowned for its robustness and excellent balance between performance and reliability. Ideal for versatile use.

Comfort

  • Everyday Driving Comfort — Appreciate correct sound insulation, especially on high-end versions, and a suspension that effectively filters road imperfections, making every journey more enjoyable.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Renault Clio III (more comfortable), Peugeot 207 (better chassis), Ford Fiesta Mk7 (more dynamic), Volkswagen Polo V (better finish), Fiat Grande Punto (technical cousin).
  • Asia / Japan: Toyota Yaris II/III, Honda Fit/Jazz, Suzuki Swift.
  • North America: Not marketed (Chevrolet Sonic/Aveo in the same segment).
  • Emerging Markets: Chevrolet Agile (South America, modified Corsa B base).

Verdict

The Opel Corsa D is a versatile city car that has aged well aesthetically, but its reliability record is mixed. For a worry-free gasoline purchase, opt for the 1.4 Twinport engine (90 or 100 hp) with a 5-speed manual gearbox, ideally a post-2010 (Euro 5) model. It offers the best reliability/performance ratio to counteract the car's high weight. The 1.2 is sufficient for city driving but suffers from the same minor ailments (ignition coils, chain). Absolutely to avoid: the 1.0 engine (anemic), the Easytronic automated manual gearbox (a hotbed of breakdowns), and any version equipped with the 6-speed manual gearbox (M32) whose bearings have not been replaced or regularly drained.
