Summary

The Fiat 500 (generation 312) is an icon of modern automotive design. Launched in 2007, it resurrected the 'Cinquecento' myth with phenomenal commercial success, based on the Fiat Panda II platform. It received a major facelift (Phase 2) in 2015. Although its primary purpose is urban, Fiat made the choice (very popular at the time in Europe) to equip it with the small 1.3 MultiJet diesel engine. While this unit is intrinsically robust and very economical on the road, its association with purely urban use has generated numerous reliability problems (DPF, EGR valve, timing chain). This report focuses on the diesel versions while providing an exhaustive overview of other powertrains to help you make the right choice.

Strengths 5

  • Timeless design and unparalleled charm
  • 1.3 MultiJet engine, very torquey and economical on open roads
  • 1.2 Fire petrol engine with exemplary reliability
  • Ideal size for parking (3.57 m)
  • Very low cost of spare parts (excluding TwinAir and Diesel DPF)

Weaknesses 5

  • Diesel engine (1.3 MJT) catastrophic if used only in the city (DPF, EGR, Chain)
  • Imperfect driving position (steering wheel not depth-adjustable)
  • Very limited rear passenger space and boot volume (185 liters)
  • Dualogic automated gearbox slow, prone to jerks and actuator failures
  • Rapid wear of front wishbone bushings and shock absorber mounts

Check before buying

  • On the 1.3 MultiJet: Oil level (must not exceed MAX), chain noise on cold start, DPF/Engine warning light.
  • On the 1.3 MultiJet: Oil change history (must be annual, even if mileage not reached).
  • On the 0.9 TwinAir: Smooth operation without misfires (otherwise MultiAir module is faulty).
  • Running gear: Clunking over speed bumps (wishbones, anti-roll bar links).
  • Tailgate wiring harness: Wires cut in the sheath over time (no more rear wiper or license plate lights).

What sets it apart 6

aesthetics

  • Iconic and Timeless Design — The Fiat 500 captivates with its inimitable retro-chic style and unparalleled charm, making it a car that never goes out of fashion.

practicality

  • Urban Mastery — With its compact size of just 3.57 meters and light steering (often with 'City' mode), the Fiat 500 is the queen of parking and effortlessly navigates through traffic.

economy

  • Fuel Efficiency and Torque on the Road — The 1.3 MultiJet engine offers generous torque and remarkably low fuel consumption on road trips, ensuring economical and enjoyable journeys.

safety

  • Maximum Protection — The Fiat 500 achieved 5 stars in Euro NCAP tests, with high scores for adult (87%) and child (86%) protection, ensuring peace of mind for its occupants.

braking

  • Safe and Balanced Braking — Equipped with ABS and EBD as standard, the Fiat 500 offers adequate braking performance for its category, ensuring controlled stops in all circumstances.

handling

  • Agile Road Behavior — Its agile chassis and precise steering give the Fiat 500 excellent maneuverability in the city and predictable, safe road behavior, even at higher speeds.

Alternatives

  • Europe: Renault Twingo (rear-wheel drive, very agile), Peugeot 108 / Citroën C1 / Toyota Aygo (very reliable in petrol), Volkswagen Up! (better finish, more spacious), Ford Ka (generation 2, shares the same chassis and engines as the 500).
  • North America: Mini Cooper Hatch, Chevrolet Spark.
  • Japan / Asia: Honda N-One (retro-look Kei car), Suzuki Alto.

Verdict

The Fiat 500 is an emotional purchase that can prove very rational, provided you choose the right engine. Regarding the 1.3 MultiJet Diesel: it's an excellent engine for a Punto or a Tipo that drives a lot, but it's nonsensical in a 500 intended for the city. If you buy a used 1.3 MJT, make sure the previous owner drove on the open road and absolutely check the condition of the timing chain. For 90% of Fiat 500 buyers, the modest but indestructible 1.2 Fire 69 hp (or the recent 1.0 hybrid) is the absolute preferred choice. Avoid TwinAir versions and the Dualogic automated gearbox.
