Summary

The 2012 Fiat Punto (often called Punto III or Punto 2012) is the second and final facelift of the Grande Punto (Type 199) which appeared in 2005, succeeding the Punto Evo. It drops the suffixes to revert to the simple name 'Punto'. Positioned in the B-segment (versatile city cars), it stood out for its generous interior space and very aggressive pricing at the end of its career. However, its aging design resulted in technological shortcomings and a catastrophic 0-star Euro NCAP score in 2017 (mainly due to the absence of modern driving aids). In terms of reliability, the reports are poor: the ADAC notes a higher-than-average breakdown rate (6.8 breakdowns/1000 veh/year), the TÜV points to an alarming defect rate of 23.5% at 3 years (among the worst in the segment), and What Car? gives it a rating of 2/5. While the basic FIRE petrol engines are mechanically robust, the model is plagued by fragile peripherals, capricious electronics, and long-term susceptibility to corrosion.

Strengths 5

  • Extremely low used purchase price (significant depreciation)
  • Generous interior space for the B-segment (rear legroom)
  • Very durable FIRE petrol engines (1.2 and 1.4 8V) if maintained
  • Reduced maintenance and insurance costs
  • Giugiaro-designed exterior that ages quite well

Weaknesses 6

  • Very high breakdown and defect rates (ADAC: 6.8/1000, TÜV: 23.5% at 3 years)
  • Capricious electronics (Blue&Me multimedia system, power windows, central locking)
  • Fragile 'Dualdrive' electric power steering (steering angle sensor, column replacement needed)
  • Notorious structural corrosion on examples over 100,000 km (sills, wheel arches, subframe)
  • Obsolete passive and active safety (0 stars Euro NCAP in 2017)
  • 1.3 MultiJet engine unsuitable for city driving (DPF, EGR, timing chain)

Check before buying

  • Power steering operation: turn from lock to lock while stationary, no stiff points or 'red steering wheel' warning light should appear.
  • Timing belt history on 1.2 and 1.4 FIRE engines (invoices required).
  • Inspection of the underbody, sills, and engine subframe to check for perforating rust.
  • Operation of all electrical equipment (air conditioning, windows, wipers, radio).
  • For MultiAir and TwinAir: demand invoices proving the use of the exact oil recommended by Fiat.
  • On the 1.3 MultiJet: absence of metallic knocking on cold start (chain).

Verdict

The 2012-2018 Fiat Punto is a purchase justified **only by its price**. With a TÜV defect rate of 23.5% at 3 years and a What Car? score of 2/5, it is among the least reliable city cars of its generation, not because of its basic engines, but due to fragile electronics, problematic power steering, and a propensity for rust.

**If you must buy one:** Avoid the 1.3 MultiJet diesels (too many DPF/chain problems) and complex engines (TwinAir, MultiAir) which require meticulous maintenance. Exclusively favor the **1.2 8V (69 hp)** or **1.4 8V (77 hp)** versions with a manual gearbox. These FIRE engines are indestructible if the timing belt is replaced on time. Thoroughly inspect the electronics, steering, and underbody before purchase. Negotiate firmly.
