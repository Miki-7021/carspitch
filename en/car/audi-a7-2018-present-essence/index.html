Summary

The second-generation Audi A7 Sportback (internal designation Type 4K8) was introduced in 2018. Built on the Volkswagen Group's MLB Evo platform, it shares much of its underlying architecture, powertrains, and technology with the A6 and the flagship A8. The A7 distinguishes itself with its sleek, coupe-like sloping roofline and a practical 5-door liftback design. It features advanced 48V mild-hybrid (MHEV) technology across most of its engine lineup, a highly digitized dual-screen MMI infotainment system, and sophisticated driver assistance systems. While it offers exceptional long-distance cruising comfort and premium build quality, its heavy reliance on complex electronics and emissions systems requires meticulous maintenance.

Strengths 5

  • Stunning exterior design with practical Sportback tailgate
  • Exceptional ride quality and cabin insulation
  • High-quality interior materials and futuristic dual-screen layout
  • Strong and efficient V6 diesel engines (for long-distance drivers)
  • Quattro all-wheel-drive system provides outstanding grip

Weaknesses 5

  • 48V Mild-Hybrid system (BSG) failures are common and expensive
  • Noticeable throttle/transmission lag on V6 TDI models
  • Dual-screen MMI can be distracting to use while driving and prone to software glitches
  • High depreciation and expensive out-of-warranty repair costs
  • Intrusive active lane assist system

Reliability by engine

EngineReliability Notes
2.0 TFSI (40/45)Generally reliable. The EA888 Gen 3/4 engine has resolved older oil consumption issues. Main vulnerabilities lie in the 12V/48V mild-hybrid system components rather than the mechanical engine itself.
3.0 V6 TFSI (55)Robust mechanicals. However, the 48V Belt Starter Generator (BSG) is a known weak point that can fail, leading to electrical system collapse and stalling. Water pump leaks can occur at higher mileage.
PHEV (50/55 TFSI e)The internal combustion engine is solid, but the high-voltage battery system has been subject to recalls due to overheating risks during charging. Complex integration means higher diagnostic costs out of warranty.
2.0 TDI (40)Very reliable for high-mileage highway driving. Prone to DPF and EGR clogging if used primarily for short city trips. Water pump and timing belt should be monitored closely.
3.0 V6 TDI (45/50/S7)Excellent torque and longevity. Suffers from noticeable turbo lag (especially the 50 TDI) due to strict WLTP emissions tuning. AdBlue system sensors and NOx sensors are common failure points.
2.9 V6 / 4.0 V8 (S7/RS7)High-performance engines requiring strict maintenance. The 4.0 V8 is generally robust but PCV valve failures and turbo oil strainer issues (though mostly resolved in this generation) should be monitored. High running costs are a given.

Check before buying

  • 48V System Health: Check if the Belt Starter Generator (BSG) has been replaced under warranty. A failing BSG will trigger an 'Electrical System Fault' warning.
  • Infotainment Stability: Test both MMI screens for responsiveness. Freezing or black screens require software updates or module replacements.
  • Suspension: If equipped with adaptive air suspension, listen for compressor strain and check for uneven ride height indicating air strut leaks.
  • Tire Wear: Inspect rear tires for uneven wear, which indicates rear axle alignment issues (subject to a known recall).
  • Transmission Smoothness: The 7-speed S tronic should shift smoothly. Jerkiness at low speeds may require a software calibration or fluid change.

Official recalls

Based on official safety recalls (Rappel Conso / European authorities):

  • Seatbelt Buckle (Dec 2025): Defect in the seatbelt buckle and anchor, leading to insufficient restraint effect. Risk of injury.
  • PHEV Battery Overheating (Nov 2025 & Apr 2025): High-voltage battery cell modules may overheat during charging, posing a fire risk.
  • Rear Axle Alignment (Sep 2022): Alteration of toe and/or camber values on the rear axle leads to increased and uneven tire wear.
  • Rear Axle Control Arm (Nov 2021): Eccentric bolt nuts of the rear axle spring link control arm may break due to stress corrosion.
  • Tow Bar (Nov 2021): Defective locking pin on the pivoting tow bar mechanism.
  • Airbag Sensors (Nov 2021): Defective crash sensors for airbag restraint systems installed in the front doors.
  • Dashboard/Airbag (Aug 2021): Manufacturing defect in the dashboard could hinder passenger airbag deployment.
  • Emissions Software (Aug 2021): Vehicles equipped with engine control software considered unauthorized by competent authorities.
  • Belt Starter Generator / BSG (Jul 2021 & May 2020): Moisture can penetrate the belt starter generator, causing short circuits, local overheating, and a fire risk.

Alternatives

  • Mercedes-Benz CLS: The original four-door coupe. Offers a plusher ride and smoother powertrains, but less practical trunk space.
  • BMW 8 Series Gran Coupe: Sportier handling dynamics and excellent inline-6 engines, but significantly more expensive.

Verdict

The Audi A7 C8 is a masterclass in design and long-distance touring comfort. Its interior is a technological tour de force, and the practicality of the liftback makes it a versatile grand tourer. However, its reliability is marred by the complex 48V mild-hybrid system, specifically the Belt Starter Generator (BSG), which has left many owners stranded. Software glitches in the MMI and emissions-related lag on diesel models are also notable drawbacks. It is a fantastic car to drive, but buying one out of warranty without a comprehensive service history is a financial risk. Ensure all recalls have been performed, particularly concerning the electrical systems and PHEV batteries.
