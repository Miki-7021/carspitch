Summary

The third-generation Kia Rio (codename UB) marks a major turning point for the Korean manufacturer. Designed under the guidance of Peter Schreyer, it abandons the low-cost appearance of its predecessors for an assertive European design, a significantly improved finish, and rich equipment. Positioned in the B-segment (versatile city cars), it shares its platform with the Hyundai i20. A mid-life facelift occurred in late 2014 / early 2015 (redesigned bumpers, updated multimedia system). Highly praised for its 7-year manufacturer's warranty (now expired for this generation), it particularly shines with the reliability of its naturally aspirated gasoline engines, which are the focus of this report.

Strengths 6

  • Remarkable reliability of naturally aspirated gasoline engines (1.25 and 1.4 CVVT/MPI)
  • Timing chain on all gasoline engines
  • Generous interior space for the category (rear legroom)
  • Very comprehensive standard equipment (especially on high-end Premium/Active trims)
  • Exterior design that has aged very well
  • Overall low maintenance cost

Weaknesses 5

  • Imperfect sound insulation (road and aerodynamic noise on the highway)
  • Firm suspension, degraded comfort on bumpy roads (especially with 16/17-inch wheels)
  • Hard interior plastics prone to scratches
  • Naturally aspirated gasoline engines lacking punch at low revs (need to be revved)
  • Variable reliability of diesels in urban use (DPF/EGR)

Check before buying

  • Clunking or play in the steering (wear of the electric power steering column coupler).
  • Maintenance history: check if annual services have been respected to maintain the former 7-year warranty.
  • Clutch biting point on manual gasoline versions (premature wear in city driving).
  • Operation of onboard electronics (radio, Bluetooth, steering wheel controls).
  • On 1.6 GDI versions (import or North America): unstable idle or loss of power (sign of valve fouling).

Official recalls

  • April 2013 (Brake light switch): Replacement of the brake pedal switch which could prevent brake lights from illuminating or deactivate cruise control (2011-2012 models).
  • July 2012 (Airbags): Reprogramming of the airbag control module due to a risk of non-deployment of curtain airbags in the event of a side impact.
  • 2015 (North America - Fuel leak): Risk of cracking of a fuel hose near the engine due to heat (1.6 GDI models).
  • 2017 (Child safety): Possible defect in the rear door locking mechanism (handle cable).

Alternatives

  • Europe: Peugeot 208 I (more comfortable but problematic PureTech engines), Renault Clio IV (more dynamic, lower finish before facelift), Volkswagen Polo V (better finished, sometimes capricious chain-driven TSI engines), Hyundai i20 II (technical cousin), Toyota Yaris III (ultra-reliable hybrid alternative).
  • North America: Honda Fit (more practical/spacious), Ford Fiesta (dynamic but PowerShift automatic gearbox to avoid), Hyundai Accent.
  • Emerging Markets (Russia/Asia): Hyundai Solaris, Volkswagen Polo Sedan, Lada Vesta.

Verdict

The Kia Rio III (UB) is an excellent used car choice if you target the naturally aspirated gasoline engines (1.25 and 1.4 CVVT/MPI). These engines, though not particularly powerful, shine with their robustness and timing chain, offering rare peace of mind in this segment. The North American 1.6 GDI requires more rigorous monitoring (fouling). Diesel versions (CRDi) are to be avoided except for high-mileage extra-urban drivers, otherwise significant costs (DPF, EGR) may arise. Despite a somewhat firm ride and basic plastics, it's a rational, economical, and very reliable city car in its gasoline variants.
