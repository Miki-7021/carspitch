Honda CR-V

IV (RM) · 2012 – 2018

BUY Reliability 90/100 — excellent

Summary

The fourth-generation Honda CR-V (code name RM) marks an evolution towards a more road-oriented and family-friendly use compared to its predecessors. Unveiled in late 2011 and launched in Europe in late 2012, it stands out for its record interior space, immense trunk (589 liters), and high level of ride comfort. The 2015 facelift brought aesthetic touches, improved sound insulation, a new infotainment system (Honda Connect), and, most importantly, the replacement of the aging 2.2 i-DTEC with an all-new 1.6 i-DTEC bi-turbo 160 hp engine paired with a ZF 9-speed automatic gearbox. Although demand here focuses on the diesel (very popular in Europe for this model), the CR-V has been a global best-seller thanks to its notoriously indestructible i-VTEC gasoline engines. Overall, it is one of the most reliable SUVs in its category.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional overall reliability, far superior to the segment average
  • Giant interior space and trunk volume (589 to 1669 liters) with 'Magic Seats' system
  • Very fuel-efficient i-DTEC diesel engines (especially the 1.6 120 hp) and powerful
  • Suspension comfort and sound insulation (especially after the 2015 facelift)
  • Ergonomics of controls and driving position

Weaknesses 5

  • Dated, slow, and unintuitive infotainment system (even the post-2015 Honda Connect)
  • Quality of some interior plastics (hard and prone to scratches)
  • 5-speed automatic gearbox (on 2.2 i-DTEC and 2.0 i-VTEC) slow and detrimental to fuel consumption
  • Non-permanent all-wheel drive (Real Time AWD) not well suited for true off-roading
  • Relatively fragile body paint (prone to stone chips)

Check before buying

  • Maintenance history: Honda engines require strict adherence to service intervals and oil grades.
  • Diesel particulate filter (DPF) on diesels: check for absence of warning light and inquire about the previous owner's driving habits.
  • Clutch and dual-mass flywheel on the 2.2 i-DTEC with manual gearbox (test for absence of slipping in 4th/5th gear and vibrations).
  • Smooth operation of the ZF 9-speed automatic gearbox (1.6 i-DTEC 160): no jerks on downshifts.
  • Tire wear: the CR-V is heavy, check for even wear (alignment).

Verdict

The 4th generation Honda CR-V is an absolute sensible choice. If you are looking for a used family diesel SUV, it is one of the safest purchases on the market. The **1.6 i-DTEC 120 hp** is the best diesel buy for its frugality and flawless reliability, provided you drive enough to preserve its DPF. For those who need all-wheel drive or an automatic gearbox, the **1.6 i-DTEC 160 hp** (post-2015) is preferable to the aging 2.2 i-DTEC, although the latter remains a mechanical rock. Simply avoid diesel models that have only been driven in the city. If your annual mileage is low (< 15,000 km), opt without hesitation for the 2.0 i-VTEC gasoline, which is literally indestructible.
