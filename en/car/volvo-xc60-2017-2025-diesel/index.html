Volvo XC60

II (UZ) · 2017 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 74/100 — bon

Summary

The **second-generation Volvo XC60 (internal code UZ, SPA platform)** is the Swedish brand's global best-seller. Positioned as an intermediate premium SUV (D-segment), it stands out for its sleek Scandinavian design, exceptional ride comfort, and advanced safety arsenal. Initially launched with pure internal combustion engines (VEA 2.0L 4-cylinder family), it gradually integrated 48V mild-hybridization ('B' range) and plug-in hybridization ('Recharge' or 'T' range).

A facelift occurred in late 2021 (Model Year 2022), marking the abandonment of 100% internal combustion engines in favor of MHEV/PHEV, minor aesthetic retouches, and especially the replacement of the 'Sensus' infotainment system with a system based on **Android Automotive (Google)**.

**Overall reliability score:** Good, but diesel engines (D3, D4, D5) require rigorous monitoring due to known weaknesses in the EGR circuit and intake, particularly in urban use.

Strengths 5

  • High-level suspension comfort (especially with the air suspension option) and sound insulation.
  • Interior finish quality and seat ergonomics (among the best on the market).
  • Exceptional active and passive safety (very well calibrated Pilot Assist).
  • Bowers & Wilkins audio system (optional) of rare quality.
  • Very wide range of engines, adapted to all markets.

Weaknesses 5

  • Reliability of the EGR circuit and intake on diesel engines (D3/D4/D5).
  • Recurring PowerPulse hose issue on 235 hp diesels (D5/B5).
  • High vehicle weight, leading to rapid wear of brakes and tires.
  • Aging Sensus infotainment system (before 2022) and Android Automotive very buggy at its launch (2022).
  • ERAD (rear electric motor) failure on early plug-in hybrid models.

Check before buying

  • Recall history: imperatively check that the recall on the intake manifold (diesels) has been carried out.
  • On D5/B5: open the hood and check the condition of the large black PowerPulse system hose (grease marks or dislodgement).
  • On PHEVs (T6/T8): listen carefully to the rear axle in 100% electric (Pure) mode. A whistling or groaning indicates a faulty ERAD bearing.
  • Air suspension (if equipped): check that the vehicle rises and lowers without excessive compressor noise and does not sag after a night of parking.
  • Panoramic sunroof: check for water ingress at the A-pillars and front floor (clogged drains).

Official recalls

  • - **July 2019 (4-cylinder Diesels 2014-2019):** Risk of deformation or melting of the plastic intake manifold due to EGR soot. Software update and modification of the cooling circuit.
  • - **July 2020 (Seat belts):** Premature wear of the steel cable securing the front seat belts. Replacement with a more robust attachment. Concerns almost all production up to 2020.
  • - **March 2020 (AEB Emergency Braking):** The automatic emergency braking system may not detect certain obstacles due to a software bug. Update required.
  • - **July 2021 (Fuel pump):** The low-pressure fuel pump fuse may blow (change from a 15A to a 20A fuse).
  • - **October 2023 (Steering):** On some 2022-2023 models, possible loss of power steering assistance due to a software defect.

Verdict

The Volvo XC60 II is an exceptional family SUV, excelling in comfort and safety. **However, purchasing a diesel version (D3, D4, D5) requires caution.** These (VEA) engines are susceptible to clogging (EGR/Intake) if they don't undertake regular long journeys. The D5 adds the annoying fragility of its PowerPulse hose. If you drive a lot, prioritize a recent diesel model (B4/B5 MHEV) with a clear service history and up-to-date recalls. For mixed or urban use, you should definitely opt for the gasoline (B4/B5) or plug-in hybrid (Recharge) versions, avoiding PHEVs from before 2021 to steer clear of costly ERAD failures.
