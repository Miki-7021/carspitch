Summary

The first-generation Volkswagen Touareg (internal code 7L) marked the German brand's entry into the premium SUV segment. Launched in 2002, it shared its platform with the Porsche Cayenne and, later, the Audi Q7, demonstrating its high-end ambitions and serious off-road capabilities. Positioned as a versatile vehicle, capable of towing heavy loads, tackling difficult terrain, and offering high road comfort, the Touareg 7L was a commercial success.

A facelift occurred in 2007, bringing minor aesthetic changes (headlights, grille, bumpers) and technical evolutions, notably the introduction of new engines (3.6 V6 FSI, more powerful 3.0 V6 TDI, 4.2 V8 FSI).

**Overall reliability score:** The Touareg 7L is a complex and heavy vehicle, which translates into potentially high maintenance and repair costs. Its reliability is highly dependent on the chosen engine and maintenance history. Atmospheric V6 and V8 gasoline versions are generally more robust than the V10 TDI diesels or V6 FSI, but all versions can suffer from problems related to air suspension and electronics.
