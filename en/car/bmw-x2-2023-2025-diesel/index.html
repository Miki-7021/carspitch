Summary

The second-generation BMW X2, known by the internal code U10, was launched in late 2023, marking a significant stylistic departure from its predecessor. Abandoning the more traditional compact SUV silhouette, the U10 adopts a more assertive "Sports Activity Coupé" (SAC) line, with a fastback profile reminiscent of the X4 and X6. Based on the FAAR platform (shared with the X1 U11 and 2 Series Active Tourer), it offers a comprehensive range of powertrains: petrol, diesel (with mild-hybridization for some versions), and a 100% electric variant (iX2). Larger and more technological, it targets a young and dynamic clientele. As a very recent model, specific reliability data for this generation is still limited, but we can rely on the reputation of the engine blocks and technologies shared with other recent BMW models.

Strengths 6

  • Modern and technological interior (iDrive 9, curved dual screen)

  • Improved suspension comfort compared to the previous generation

Weaknesses 6

  • Limited rear visibility due to the sloping roofline
  • High purchase price, especially with options
  • Rear passenger space and boot volume slightly inferior to a classic SUV (X1)
  • Long-term reliability of new technologies (mild-hybridization, iDrive 9) still unknown
  • Complexity of iDrive 9 menus for some users
  • Absence of plug-in hybrid (PHEV) powertrain at launch

Check before buying

  • Operation of all electronic and software systems (iDrive 9, ADAS)
  • History of software updates (OTA)
  • Condition of tires and brakes (especially on M35i versions)
  • For diesels: service history of DPF and AdBlue system
  • For mild-hybrid versions: proper functioning of the 48V system
  • Absence of parasitic noises or unusual vibrations (especially on early models)

Official recalls

As a very recent model (launched late 2023), no major and widespread manufacturer recalls have been reported to date for the BMW X2 U10. It is common for the first few months of commercialization to reveal minor adjustments via service campaigns or software updates, but not yet large-scale official recalls. It is always recommended to check the chassis number (VIN) with a BMW dealer to ensure that no specific recall is pending on a given vehicle.

Verdict

The BMW X2 U10 is a bold and modern proposition in the premium compact SUV coupé segment. Its assertive design, technological interior, and proven powertrains (petrol and diesel) make it an attractive choice. However, as a first-year production model, some caution is advised regarding the long-term reliability of the new onboard technologies (mild-hybridization, iDrive 9). The B38, B48, and B47 engine blocks are generally very reliable, which is an excellent starting point.

Buying advice: * For high-mileage drivers and long journeys: Diesel versions (sDrive18d, xDrive20d) are to be preferred for their fuel efficiency and torque, with excellent historical reliability of the B47 block. The xDrive20d offers an excellent compromise with all-wheel drive and mild-hybridization. * For versatile and dynamic use: The sDrive20i offers a good balance, while the M35i xDrive will delight performance enthusiasts. * For urban and ecological use: The iX2 is a relevant option, but be aware of the constraints related to charging and the novelty of BMW's electric technology on this model.

Whatever the choice, prioritize a vehicle with a complete and up-to-date service history. For early models, an extended warranty can be a wise precaution. The X2 U10 is an excellent vehicle, but as with any new product, early adopters sometimes experience the teething problems of software adjustments.
