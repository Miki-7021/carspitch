BMW Série 3

E46 · 1998 – 2005

NEGOTIATE Reliability 72/100 — bon

Summary

The BMW 3 Series E46 is often considered the pinnacle of the 3 Series in terms of balance between dynamism, comfort, and build quality. Replacing the E36, it introduced a timeless design and modernized onboard electronics (CAN bus).

**Generations and body styles :** - Sedan (1998), Coupe and Touring (1999), Convertible (2000), Compact (2001). - **Phase 1 (1998-2001)** : Softer lines, M43/M52TU (gasoline) and M47/M57 (diesel) engines. - **Phase 2 / LCI (2001-2005)** : Redesigned front end (headlights, grille), modified taillights, stiffened running gear, introduction of N42/N46 (gasoline) and M47TU/M57TU (diesel) engines.

Although this report focuses on **diesel engines (very popular in Europe)**, the E46 is globally recognized for its inline 6-cylinder gasoline engines. In the used car market, the E46 diesel requires extreme vigilance: while the M57 blocks are intrinsically robust, peripherals (swirl flaps, turbos, GM automatic gearboxes) can turn the purchase into a financial black hole if the history is unclear.

**Overall generation rating: 7.5/10** (Penalized by chassis and diesel peripheral issues, but saved by its exceptional driving pleasure).

Strengths 5

  • Driving pleasure and chassis dynamism (50/50 weight distribution)
  • 6-cylinder diesel engines (M57) with remarkable torque and sound
  • Interior finish quality and driver-oriented ergonomics
  • Timeless design that ages extremely well
  • Massive availability of spare parts and strong support community

Weaknesses 5

  • Tearing of the floor at the rear subframe mounting points (major design flaw)
  • Swirl flaps on diesels (M47TU/M57TU) that can destroy the engine
  • Fragile GM automatic gearbox (5L40-E) on diesels, especially if not serviced
  • Chronic fragility of the cooling system (exploding expansion tank)
  • Rapid wear of front control arm bushings (FCAB) and rear trailing arm bushings (RTAB)

Check before buying

  • Imperatively inspect the rear floor (under the trunk/differential) for cracks or torn welds.
  • Request proof (invoice or photos) of swirl flap deletion on diesels.
  • Check the automatic gearbox service history (if automatic). Flee if the oil is original at over 150,000 km.
  • On 320d 150hp: listen for turbo whistling during acceleration and check for absence of blue/white smoke.
  • On 330d 204hp: check for absence of exhaust smell in the cabin (cracked manifold).
  • Test road behavior: vibrations during braking or vagueness in steering indicate worn control arm bushings.

Verdict

The BMW 3 Series E46 is an enthusiast's car that requires an informed buyer, particularly for diesel models. **The verdict is NEGOTIATE or BUY UNDER STRICT CONDITIONS.**

If you are looking for a diesel, aim for a **330d (184 or 204 hp) with a manual gearbox**. It is a monument in automotive history that can exceed 400,000 km if meticulously maintained. However, purchase is strongly advised against if the swirl flaps have not been removed, if the rear subframe is cracked, or if you opt for an unserviced GM automatic gearbox. The 320d 150hp should be approached with great caution due to the fragility of its turbo. Always plan a reserve budget (€1,000 to €2,000) to refresh the running gear and cooling system.
