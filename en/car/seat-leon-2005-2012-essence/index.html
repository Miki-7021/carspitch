Summary

The second-generation Seat Leon (type 1P) is based on the excellent Volkswagen Group PQ35 platform, shared with the Golf V/VI and Audi A3 8P. Designed by Walter de Silva, it stands out with a sporty line (hidden rear door handles) and dynamic positioning. The 2009 restyling (facelift) brought a notable improvement in interior plastic quality, new technological equipment, and a revamp of the engine range (transition to common rail diesels and introduction of TSI engines). While its chassis is praised for its agility, its mechanical reliability is very heterogeneous, particularly for the TSI petrol engines of this era (EA111 and EA888 generations) which require absolute vigilance before purchase.

Strengths 5

  • Dynamic chassis and sharp front axle (PQ35 platform)
  • Exterior design that has aged very well
  • Decent interior space and good boot volume (341 L)
  • Very high-performance and engaging FR and Cupra versions
  • Exemplary reliability of older naturally aspirated petrol engines (1.6 MPI)

Weaknesses 5

  • Very disappointing interior plastic quality before the 2009 facelift
  • Disastrous reliability of early TSI engines (chain, piston rings)
  • Average sound insulation (road and wind noise)
  • Very firm suspension, especially in FR/Cupra trim or with 18-inch wheels
  • Poor rear and three-quarter rear visibility

Check before buying

  • ABS/ESP unit (Teves Mk60): The G201 pressure sensor often fails (ESP light on). Costly repair if complete replacement.
  • Air conditioning compressor: Tendency to seize (central pulley screw loosens or breaks).
  • Door locks: Micro-switches fail, preventing central locking/unlocking.
  • Water ingress: Faulty door seals leading to water under the floor mats.
  • TSI service history: Demand invoices proving timing chain replacement or engine overhaul (oil consumption).

Verdict

The Seat Leon II is a compact car with an assertive style and enjoyable road handling. However, buying a used petrol version is like navigating a minefield. The TSI engines (1.2, 1.4, 1.8) of this era suffer from major design flaws (timing chain, excessive oil consumption) that can ruin the buyer. If you're looking for a reliable petrol car on a budget, the placid 1.6 MPI 102 hp is the only 100% worry-free choice. For sports enthusiasts, the Cupra versions (2.0 TFSI EA113) are robust provided they receive meticulous maintenance. For diesel, it is imperative to prioritize the Common Rail (TDI CR) versions that appeared after the 2009 facelift.
