Ford Escape

III (C520) · 2013 – 2019

All Ford models

Summary

The 3rd generation Ford Escape (code name C520, sold as the Ford Kuga II in Europe) marks a stylistic and technical break from its predecessors, adopting the global C1 platform (shared with the Focus).

⚠️ CLARIFICATION ON THE HYBRID: Although targeted by this search, the Gen 3 Ford Escape (2013-2019) was NOT produced in ANY hybrid version. Ford made the strategic choice at the time to reserve its hybrid powertrains for the Ford C-Max. The hybrid only made its return to the Escape with the 4th generation (2020+). This sheet therefore focuses on the internal combustion engines (EcoBoost, Duratec, TDCi) actually available on the Gen 3, while including context data on the Gen 4 hybrid.

The model received a facelift in late 2016 (2017 model year) introducing the SYNC 3 system and the 1.5L EcoBoost engine. Overall, the reliability of this generation is rated below average by ADAC, Consumer Reports, and J.D. Power, dragged down by problematic 1.5L and 1.6L engines and an avalanche of recalls (door latches, transmission, fire risks).

Strengths 5

  • Dynamic road handling and precise steering (Focus chassis)
  • Highly reliable 2.5L Duratec engine (North America)
  • High-performance 2.0L EcoBoost engine (240+ hp)
  • Highly responsive SYNC 3 multimedia system (from 2017)
  • Generous cargo space for the category

Weaknesses 5

  • Total absence of a hybrid powertrain on this generation
  • Catastrophic reliability of the 1.5L and 1.6L EcoBoost engines (coolant leaks, failures, fires)
  • 6F35 automatic gearbox sometimes harsh and prone to linkage failures
  • Interior plastic quality and uneven fit and finish
  • Record number of safety recalls (doors opening while driving, fire risks)

Check before buying

  • Coolant level (CRUCIAL on 1.5L and 1.6L EcoBoost)
  • Recall history: imperatively check if the door latches and transmission bushing have been replaced
  • Automatic gearbox behavior (jerks between 2nd and 3rd gear)
  • Infotainment system operation (especially on 2013-2015 models with SYNC 2)
  • Signs of oil or coolant leaks under the vehicle

Official recalls

The Gen 3 Ford Escape / Kuga II has suffered an exceptionally high number of recalls. Here are the most critical ones:

  • Fire / Engine Risks:
  • 17V209000 (March 2017): 1.6L EcoBoost. Overheating due to a lack of coolant circulation that can crack the cylinder head, leak oil, and cause a fire.
  • 26V011000 (Jan 2026/NHTSA): Engine block heater on 2.0L. Can crack, leak, and create a short circuit/fire.
  • Rappel Conso (May 2021): Kuga PHEV (Gen 4). High-voltage battery charging defect that can vent hot gases and cause a fire.
  • Transmission (Risk of rolling without driver):
  • 22V413000 (June 2022), 22V254000, 18V471000: The bushing connecting the shifter cable to the transmission degrades. The vehicle may display 'P' (Park) but be in another gear and roll away on its own.
  • Door Latches:
  • 20V331000 (June 2020), 16V643000 (Sept 2016): A door latch component can break. The door may open unexpectedly while driving.
  • Electrical / Safety:
  • 14V495000 (August 2014): Wiring harness (2.0L). Defective splices causing engine stalls.
  • 14V237000 (May 2014): Restraint Control Module (RCM) requiring reprogramming.
  • 16V617000 / 26V374000: Excessive power window force (defective anti-pinch).

What sets it apart 5

Safety

  • Top-tier Safety for the Whole Family — The Ford Escape III achieved maximum ratings in safety tests, effectively protecting its occupants through robust design and advanced protection systems. It has also been recognized for its safety by independent organizations.

Braking

  • Confident and Responsive Braking — Enjoy excellent and competitive braking performance, delivering powerful and progressive deceleration for enhanced peace of mind in all driving situations.

Handling

  • Dynamic and Agile Road Handling — Benefit from an engaging driving experience thanks to a chassis inherited from the Focus, offering precise steering, excellent body control, and surprising agility for an SUV.

Practicality

  • Generous Cargo Space — The Escape III stands out with its spacious cargo volume, ideal for family trips, hobbies, or transporting bulky items, offering great everyday versatility.

technologie

  • Intuitive Connectivity with SYNC 3 — From 2017 models onwards, the SYNC 3 multimedia system offers a highly responsive interface, compatible with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, for seamless smartphone integration and easy access to your applications.

Alternatives

  • If you absolutely want a Hybrid (2013-2019): Toyota RAV4 Hybrid (the absolute benchmark in reliability), Ford C-Max Hybrid (same platform as the Escape but in a compact MPV format).
  • North America: Honda CR-V (very spacious, reliable in 2.4L), Mazda CX-5 (excellent reliability, superior finish), Toyota RAV4.
  • Europe: Peugeot 3008 (watch out for the 1.2 PureTech), Volkswagen Tiguan, Nissan Qashqai, Kia Sportage.

Verdict

The 3rd generation Ford Escape (2013-2019) is an SUV with pleasant road handling, but whose reliability is undermined by disastrous mechanical choices in the core lineup. If you were looking for a hybrid, walk away: it does not exist on this generation.

If you buy an internal combustion model: Absolutely avoid the 1.5L and 1.6L EcoBoost engines, which are veritable ticking time bombs (coolant intrusion, engine failures, fires). The only recommendable purchase is the 2.5L Duratec version (often found in the base S trim in North America) which is indestructible, or the 2.0L EcoBoost (thirstier but solid). In Europe, the 2.0 TDCi remains a rational diesel choice. Imperatively verify that all recalls (transmission, doors) have been performed.
