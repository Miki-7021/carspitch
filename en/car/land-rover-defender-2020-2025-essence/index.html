Land Rover Defender

II (L663) · 2020 – 2025

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The second-generation Land Rover Defender (L663) marks a radical departure from its rustic predecessor. Based on the D7x aluminum unibody platform, it abandons the ladder frame to offer road handling worthy of a luxury SUV while retaining exceptional off-road capabilities. Available in three wheelbases (90, 110, and 130), it positions itself as an ultra-technological premium off-roader. While the mechanical design is robust, overall reliability remains marred by recurring electronic and software issues, typical of Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) productions. Data from the TÜV Report (22% defect rate at 3 years) and What Car? (2.5/5) confirm below-average reliability, requiring particular vigilance on early model years (2020-2021).

Strengths 5

  • Unrivaled off-road capabilities in its class (Terrain Response 2)
  • Exceptional ride comfort with the air suspension
  • Highly successful neo-retro design and practical/washable interior
  • Drivability of the inline 6-cylinder engines (P400 petrol and D300 diesel)
  • Impressive onboard technology (3D cameras, ClearSight)

Weaknesses 5

  • Capricious electronic and software reliability (ADAC breakdown rate 4.5, What Car? 33% fault rate)
  • High defect rate at technical inspection (TÜV Report) at 3 years: 22%
  • Excessive weight (often over 2.3 tons) penalizing fuel consumption and wear of consumables
  • Jaguar Land Rover after-sales network often overwhelmed and expensive
  • Complex ergonomics for certain functions via the touchscreen

Check before buying

  • Complete history of software updates (Pivi Pro, ABS sensors, cameras)
  • Smooth operation of the air suspension (raising/lowering without suspicious compressor noise)
  • Absence of oil leaks (oil filter housing, camshaft cover on petrol engines)
  • Condition of the underbody (vehicle potentially used in intensive off-roading)
  • Operation of the central screen at startup (known black screen issue)

Verdict

The Land Rover Defender L663 is a brilliantly engineered vehicle, offering a spectrum of capabilities (on-road/off-road) that is almost unique on the market. However, purchasing one must be done with full awareness: electronic and software reliability is capricious, as evidenced by statistics from ADAC (4.5 breakdowns/1000), the TÜV Report (22% defect rate at 3 years), and the long list of official recalls.

**Buying advice:** Absolutely avoid the 2020 and early 2021 model years, which suffered the teething problems of the new software platform. Favor a 2022 or later model, **exclusively with a manufacturer's warranty or a solid extended warranty**. In petrol, the 6-cylinder P400 is the best compromise. The P300 is a bit underpowered given the weight, and the PHEV P400e adds a layer of complexity to an already sensitive vehicle. Scrupulously verify that all recalls (especially the oil filter housing and PCM updates) have been performed.
