Peugeot 308

I (4A/C) · 2007 – 2013

NEGOTIATE Reliability 68/100 — moyen

Summary

The first-generation Peugeot 308 (code name T7 / 4A/C) succeeds the 307, from which it inherits the platform, but corrects many early flaws, particularly in terms of electronics. Launched in 2007, it stands out with a significantly improved finish, imperial road handling typical of Peugeot, and generous interior space. It benefited from a facelift (Phase 2) in spring 2011, marking the introduction of e-HDi engines (with an excellent Stop&Start system via a starter-alternator) and the transition to Euro 5 standards. While its dynamic qualities are undeniable, the mechanical reliability of this generation is very heterogeneous. It introduced the 'Prince' petrol engines (VTi/THP) co-developed with BMW, notoriously known for their timing chain issues, and featured the first-generation 1.6 HDi 16v (DV6) diesels, prone to turbo failures. The choice of engine and year is therefore absolutely crucial to avoid financial pitfalls.

Strengths 5

  • First-class road handling (comfort/road holding compromise)
  • Remarkable reliability of 2.0 HDi and 1.6 HDi 8v engines (post-2011)
  • Significantly improved finish and material quality compared to the 307
  • Generous interior space and boot volume
  • Ultra-fast, silent, and vibration-free Stop&Start system (e-HDi)

Weaknesses 5

  • Disastrous reliability of THP petrol engines and early 1.6 HDi 110hp
  • High weight of the car (especially in Phase 1), penalizing smaller engines
  • Very prominent front overhang (easily scrapes on speed bumps)
  • Aging and sometimes buggy multimedia systems (WIP Nav, WIP Com 3D)
  • Average sound insulation on early diesels when cold

Check before buying

  • On 1.6 HDi 16v (2007-2010): Presence of black tar at the base of the injectors (leak) and pronounced turbo whistling.
  • On all DPF diesels: Eolys additive level ('DPF clogging risk' message on the dashboard) and refill history.
  • On 1.6 HDi 110 / 2.0 HDi 136: Vibrations in the clutch pedal or knocking at idle (worn dual-mass flywheel).
  • On VTi / THP: Timing chain noise when cold (metallic rattling) and oil level (ask the seller about the frequency of top-ups).
  • Front axle: Dull noises on degraded roads (worn wishbone bushings or anti-roll bar drop links).

What sets it apart 14

Reliability

  • Reliability of Benchmark Diesel Engines — The 2.0 HDi (136 to 163 hp) engines are renowned for their robustness and endurance, ideal for high-mileage drivers. The 1.6 e-HDi 8-valve versions (from 2011 onwards) also offer significantly improved reliability and excellent driving pleasure.

Handling

  • Imperial Road Handling — Enjoy first-class road holding, offering a perfect balance between comfort and dynamism. The 308 I absorbs road imperfections brilliantly and provides a serene and precise driving experience.

Safety

  • Enhanced Safety — With a 5-star Euro NCAP crash test rating (92% for adult protection and 76% for child protection), the Peugeot 308 I ensures optimal protection for all occupants.
  • Powerful and Enduring Braking — Benefit from a highly effective braking system, capable of stopping the vehicle over short distances, typically around 38 to 40 meters from 100 km/h, with excellent pedal feel.

Comfort

  • Interior Comfort and Quality — The 308 I's cabin stands out with significantly improved finish quality and materials, offering a pleasant and well-designed environment for your journeys.

Practicality

  • Generous Space and Boot Volume — Enjoy a spacious interior and a generous boot volume, making this compact car particularly practical for families and long trips.

Economy

  • Advanced e-HDi Stop&Start Technology — On e-HDi versions, the Stop&Start system is ultra-fast, quiet, and vibration-free, contributing to optimized fuel consumption in urban driving.

Alternatives

In **Europe**, competition is fierce with the **Volkswagen Golf VI** (superior finish, TDI engines made reliable with Common Rail, but fragile TSI), the **Renault Mégane III** (excellent reliability of the 1.5 dCi from 2010 and formidable chassis), and the **Ford Focus II/III** (which also shares the 1.6 and 2.0 diesel engines with the 308). In **Asia/Japan**, the **Honda Civic VIII** offers mechanical reliability (notably the 2.2 i-CTDi and VTEC blocks) far superior to PSA's Prince/DV6 engines. In **emerging markets**, the **Chevrolet Cruze** or **Toyota Corolla** offer often more robust naturally aspirated alternatives for local conditions.

Verdict

The Peugeot 308 I is an excellent compact car dynamically, but it requires a rigorous selection of its engine. **If you are looking for a diesel**, avoid the 1.6 HDi 16v (90 and especially 110 hp) from before 2011, unless you have a meticulous service history proving the replacement of injector seals and very regular oil changes. Absolutely prioritize the restyled versions (Phase 2 from March 2011) equipped with the **1.6 e-HDi 112/115 hp (8 valves)**, which are much more reliable and pleasant. For high-mileage drivers, the **2.0 HDi (136 to 163 hp)** is a true workhorse, capable of devouring kilometers without flinching. On the petrol side, caution is advised: the VTi and THP are trouble spots (timing chain, oil consumption). To be bought with full knowledge of the facts, and with a contingency budget.
