Summary

The first-generation Volvo S60 (P2 platform) was introduced in 2000 as a sporty, mid-size executive sedan designed to compete with the German trio (BMW 3 Series, Audi A4, Mercedes C-Class). Penned by Peter Horbury, its coupe-like roofline and pronounced shoulders marked a radical departure from Volvo's traditional boxy designs. Renowned for its exceptionally comfortable seats, outstanding safety credentials, and characterful inline-5 cylinder engines, the S60 remains a highly desirable modern classic.

**Generational Updates:** - **2000-2004 (Phase 1):** Introduction of the model. Early models suffered from teething issues, notably with the Magneti Marelli Electronic Throttle Module (ETM) and Aisin AW55-50SN automatic transmission valve bodies. - **2005-2010 (Facelift / Phase 2):** Significant mid-cycle refresh. Introduced body-colored moldings, clear-lens headlights, upgraded interior materials, a faster CAN-bus electrical system, and the switch to more reliable Bosch throttle bodies across the board. The T5 engine was upgraded from 2.3L to 2.4L, and the D5 diesel received a power bump.

Overall, the P2 S60 is a robust highway cruiser. While the engines themselves are capable of extreme mileages (often exceeding 300,000 miles / 500,000 km), the surrounding ancillaries (PCV system, suspension bushings, electronics) require diligent maintenance.

Strengths 6

  • Exceptional seat comfort (often cited as the best in the industry)
  • Outstanding passive and active safety for its era (SIPS, WHIPS)
  • Characterful and highly durable inline-5 petrol engines
  • Excellent rust protection and paint quality
  • High-quality audio systems (Premium Sound with Dolby Pro Logic II)
  • Timeless exterior design that has aged very well

Weaknesses 6

  • Large turning circle (especially on T5 and R models with larger wheels)
  • Front suspension components (control arm bushings, strut mounts, end links) wear out quickly due to the heavy front end
  • Aisin 5-speed automatic transmission can be problematic on early models (2001-2003)
  • Cramped rear legroom compared to rivals
  • High maintenance costs for the S60 R (4C suspension, AWD components)
  • PCV system requires replacement every 100k miles (labor-intensive)

Check before buying

  • PCV System: Perform the 'glove test' to check for positive crankcase pressure.
  • Transmission: Test drive an automatic until fully warm. Look for harsh engagements (especially P to D, or 2nd to 3rd gear flare).
  • Timing Belt: Must be changed every 10 years or 100,000 miles (160,000 km), including the water pump and tensioners. Interference engines will be destroyed if it snaps.
  • Suspension: Listen for clunks over bumps (indicates worn spring seats, sway bar links, or control arm bushings).
  • Dashboard (DIM): Ensure the clock doesn't run backward and all gauges/message centers work perfectly.
  • Alarm Siren Module: Located in the front right fender. The internal battery leaks acid onto the circuit board, causing 'Alarm System Service Required' messages and sunroof malfunctions.

Verdict

The Volvo S60 Mk1 is a brilliant, characterful alternative to the default German executive sedans. If you are looking for a petrol model, the **post-2005 facelift 2.5T** is the absolute sweet spot, offering modern electronics, a reliable Bosch throttle body, and a refined, punchy engine. The naturally aspirated 2.4 is slower but virtually indestructible.

**Buying Advice:** Avoid 2001-2002 automatic models unless the transmission has been explicitly replaced or shifts flawlessly when hot. Always check the PCV system and timing belt history. The S60 R is a fantastic performance car but should be treated as a high-maintenance enthusiast vehicle, not a cheap daily driver. Buy on condition and maintenance history rather than mileage.
