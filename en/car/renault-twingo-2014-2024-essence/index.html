Summary

The Renault Twingo III (AH8 generation) marks a radical break in the model's history. Developed in partnership with Daimler (sharing its platform with the Smart Fortwo III and Forfour II), it adopts a rear-engine, rear-wheel-drive architecture, offered exclusively as a 5-door. This technical choice gives it an exceptional turning radius (4.30 m), ideal for urban use.

A facelift (Phase 2) occurred in 2019, bringing a C-shaped light signature, a redesign of the multimedia system (Easy Link), and an update of the engines to meet Euro 6d-Temp standards. In 2020, the range was expanded with a 100% electric version (Z.E., then E-Tech). Although its rear design limits trunk volume and makes it prone to heating, the Twingo III remains a formidable city car. Its overall reliability is satisfactory, although complex mechanical accessibility makes some interventions costly in terms of labor.

Strengths 5

  • Unbeatable turning radius (4.30 m) facilitating parallel parking and U-turns
  • Compact exterior (3.61 m) while offering 5 doors as standard
  • Pleasing design and numerous customization options
  • Enjoyable EDC dual-clutch automatic gearbox (rare in this segment)
  • E-Tech electric version very relevant for city use

Weaknesses 5

  • Difficult mechanical accessibility (engine under the trunk floor) increasing labor costs
  • Trunk prone to heating (poor engine thermal insulation)
  • Marked sensitivity to crosswinds on the highway (despite the Crosswind Assist system)
  • Light sound insulation at high speeds
  • Limited trunk volume (188 to 219 liters) and high loading sill

Check before buying

  • Front tire wear: the extreme steering angle prematurely wears the outer shoulders.
  • Coolant level: particularly on TCe engines (leak at the thermostat housing).
  • Metallic noises on cold start (TCe): indicates a loose timing chain.
  • Front hood operation: the plastic sliding mechanism is fragile and can get stuck.
  • R-Link / Easy Link multimedia system: check for fluidity and absence of intermittent restarts.

Official recalls

  • April 2015: Risk of non-conformity of welds on front shock absorbers (models manufactured in 2014).
  • November 2015: Injection computer reprogramming (risk of stalling on SCe 70).
  • May 2016: Replacement of rear seat belt buckles (risk of detachment in case of impact).
  • February 2017: Inspection and possible replacement of the front hood (risk of flying off at high speed due to defective locking).
  • October 2019: Inspection of the steering column (risk of loss of control on a small series).
  • 2023 (E-Tech): Reprogramming of the traction control unit to prevent sudden power loss.

Verdict

The Renault Twingo III is a pure city car. Its atypical architecture makes it the undisputed queen of city centers thanks to its unparalleled maneuverability. For a used car, the verdict is favorable, provided you choose the right engine for your use. For 100% urban use, the 1.0 SCe (ideally the 75 hp Phase 2) is the most rational and reliable choice. If you regularly drive outside urban areas, the 0.9 TCe 90/95 is essential, but requires a clear maintenance history (monitoring of the chain and cooling system). The E-Tech electric version is an excellent deal for recent used cars for commuting. Be aware, however, of maintenance costs: the rear-engine architecture extends labor time for simple operations.
