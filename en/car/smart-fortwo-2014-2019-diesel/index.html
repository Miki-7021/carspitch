Smart Fortwo

III (453) · 2014 – 2019

NEGOTIATE Reliability 62/100 — moyen

Summary

The third-generation Smart Fortwo (code name 453) marks a major turning point in the model's history. Developed in partnership with Renault (it shares about 70% of its components with the Twingo III), it retains its rear-engine, rear-wheel-drive architecture, as well as its iconic length of 2.69 m. Its record turning circle (8.69 m) makes it the undisputed queen of the city. The most notable improvement is the abandonment of the disastrous single-clutch automated manual gearbox (Softouch) in favor of a manual gearbox or an excellent dual-clutch gearbox (Twinamic).

**⚠️ CRUCIAL NOTE ON THE DIESEL ENGINE:** Unlike the 450 and 451 generations which offered the famous 0.8 cdi engine, **the Smart Fortwo 453 was NEVER marketed in a diesel version**. The Daimler group abandoned diesel on this model due to anti-pollution standards (Euro 6) being too expensive to meet for a micro-city car, and the partnership with Renault whose petrol engines (SCe/TCe) were adopted. Buyers absolutely looking for a diesel will have to turn to the previous generation (451) or to competitors. The sheet below therefore details the petrol and electric engines actually available on the 453.

Despite its urban qualities, overall reliability remains mixed according to inspection organizations (TÜV, What Car?), although the ADAC notes a clear improvement compared to previous generations.

Strengths 5

  • Exceptional turning circle (8.69 m), unbeatable in the city
  • Twinamic dual-clutch gearbox (finally a true, smooth, and responsive automatic gearbox)
  • Surprising front-seat roominess for its size
  • Much safer and more comfortable road handling than the 451 generation
  • Still iconic customization and design

Weaknesses 6

  • Total absence of a diesel engine (for heavy urban drivers/fleets)
  • Very high TÜV defect rate at 3 years (21.5%), among the worst in the segment
  • Mediocre What Car? reliability score (2/5, 35% of faults reported)
  • Tiny and impractical trunk (engine located underneath, heating the floor)
  • Soundproofing on highways (wind and road noise)
  • High purchase price and maintenance costs (Mercedes-Benz network)

Check before buying

  • Cooling system on 0.9 Turbo engines (leaks, fluid level) - black spot raised by the ADAC
  • Smooth operation of the Twinamic gearbox (no excessive juddering upon starting should be tolerated)
  • Condition of the front axle (ball joints, silent blocks) heavily stressed by extreme steering in the city
  • Mounting of the panoramic or solid roof (subject to an official recall)
  • Operation of the multimedia system (Smart Media-System / R-Link), often slow or buggy

Alternatives

- **Europe:** Renault Twingo III (its technical cousin, cheaper and offering 4 seats), Toyota Aygo / Peugeot 108 / Citroën C1 (very reliable), VW Up! / Skoda Citigo / Seat Mii (better finished, excellent in electric), Fiat 500 (for the neo-retro style). - **North America:** Chevrolet Spark, Fiat 500, Mini Cooper (although larger). - **Asia:** Japanese Kei cars (Honda N-Box, Suzuki Hustler) which offer much more space for a similar footprint.

Verdict

The Smart Fortwo III (453) is a paradoxical city car. In terms of driving pleasure, it corrects the worst flaws of its ancestors: the Twinamic gearbox is a delight compared to the old Softouch, and comfort takes a leap forward. However, **for buyers looking for a diesel, this model is a dead end**, as the engine was permanently abandoned.

In terms of reliability, the results are very mixed. While the ADAC notes an improvement in immobilizing breakdowns (4.1/1000) compared to the 450/451 generations, the German TÜV severely penalizes it with a defect rate of 21.5% at 3 years, and What Car? gives it a very poor 2/5. Cooling problems on the 0.9 TCe and structural recalls (roof, windshield, steering) urge caution.

**Buying advice:** Avoid the early production 2014-2015 models. Favor a 2017 or newer 0.9 90 hp Twinamic model, with a clear maintenance history (ideally outside the Mercedes network to reduce costs). The electric version (EQ) is excellent for the city, provided you check the condition of the onboard charger.
