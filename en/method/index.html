Method and sources

Every report rests on identifiable public sources. Here is which ones, how they are combined, and what the result does not claim to be.

The sources

Reports are built from public publications and databases, not from tests we carried out ourselves:

  • Breakdown statistics — ADAC Pannenstatistik (Germany) and TÜV/DEKRA reports: frequency and nature of failures by model and year.
  • Reliability surveys — What Car? Reliability Survey (UK), L'Argus and Auto Plus (France): owner feedback.
  • Official recalls — EU Safety Gate (formerly RAPEX), KBA (German federal motor transport authority), manufacturer campaigns.
  • Technical documentation — service intervals, parts costs and availability.

Every report lists its sources at the bottom of the page.

The reliability score

The score, out of 100, combines three elements:

  • frequency of reported failures for this model and generation;
  • severity — an immobilising breakdown weighs more than a faulty sensor;
  • repair cost, relative to the value of the car.

Reading guide: 85 and above — among the most reliable in its class; 70 to 84 — good, a few points to watch; 55 to 69 — average, careful servicing essential; below 55 — recurring risks, for informed buyers only.

The score applies to the model, never to a specific car. A reliable model can have been badly maintained.

The verdicts

  • Buy — solid reliability, affordable parts, no known structural flaw.
  • Negotiate — recommended, but with an identified weak point that justifies a discount or an inspection.
  • Avoid — recurring costly defects or insufficient safety for everyday use.

The verdict ignores price: it describes the model, not the deal.

What our reports do not say

  • They do not replace a technical inspection or a test drive.
  • They do not guarantee the condition of a specific used car: mileage, servicing and history matter as much as the model.
  • They do not cover every engine or trim.
  • Prices are indicative and vary by market, region and condition.

Updates and corrections

Reports are revised when a source publishes new data — annual statistics, a recall campaign, a manufacturer correction. A report found to be wrong is corrected as soon as the error is identified.

Editorial independence

No manufacturer, garage or dealer pays to appear here or to obtain a favourable verdict. The ranking is not for sale.

The site may contain affiliate links to services useful at purchase (car finance, insurance). Those links change neither the score, nor the verdict, nor the selection.
